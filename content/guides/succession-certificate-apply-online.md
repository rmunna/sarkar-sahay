A **succession certificate** is one of the most important legal documents you may need after the death of a family member — especially when banks, insurance companies, or mutual fund houses refuse to release assets without court authorisation. This guide explains the complete process to apply for a succession certificate in India, including online filing options, required documents, court fees by state, and expected timelines.

## What Is a Succession Certificate?

A succession certificate is a legal order issued by a **district civil court** under **Sections 370–390 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925**. It authorises the certificate holder (a legal heir) to:

- Collect **debts** owed to the deceased (bank fixed deposits, salary dues, provident fund)
- Receive **securities** belonging to the deceased (shares, debentures, mutual fund units, government bonds)

> **Key point:** A succession certificate does **not** transfer immovable property like land or houses. For property transfer, you need [property registration and mutation](/guide/property-registration-online).

## When Do You Need a Succession Certificate?

You need a succession certificate when:

- The deceased **died intestate** (without a valid will)
- Banks or financial institutions demand a court order to release deposits, especially for **amounts above ₹1–2 lakh**
- You need to claim **insurance proceeds, PPF, shares, or mutual funds**
- Multiple legal heirs exist and a single authorised person needs to collect the assets

### Succession Certificate vs Legal Heir Certificate

| Feature | Succession Certificate | Legal Heir Certificate |
|---|---|---|
| **Issued by** | District civil court | Tehsildar / Municipal authority |
| **Legal basis** | Indian Succession Act, 1925 | State revenue rules |
| **Purpose** | Authorises collection of debts & securities | Establishes list of legal heirs |
| **Validity** | Nationwide, accepted by all institutions | Limited; many banks don't accept for large sums |
| **Time to obtain** | 3–6 months (uncontested) | 15–30 days |
| **Cost** | 2%–7.5% of asset value (court fee) | ₹50–₹500 |

A [legal heir certificate](/guide/death-certificate-apply-online) is simpler and cheaper but may not suffice for high-value claims. Banks routinely insist on a succession certificate for deposits above ₹1 lakh.

### Succession Certificate vs Probate

- **Probate** applies when the deceased **left a valid will** — it validates the will through court.
- **Succession certificate** applies when there is **no will** (intestate succession).
- In cities like Mumbai, Chennai, and Kolkata, probate is **mandatory** for wills under Section 213 of the Indian Succession Act.

## Which Court to Apply To

File the petition in the **district civil court** that has jurisdiction over:

- The place where the **deceased ordinarily resided** at the time of death, **or**
- The place where any **property of the deceased** is situated

You cannot file in just any court — jurisdiction matters and an incorrectly filed petition will be returned.

## How to Apply for Succession Certificate Online

### Step 1: Obtain the Death Certificate

Before anything else, you need the [death certificate of the deceased](/guide/death-certificate-apply-online). This is a mandatory document for the petition.

### Step 2: Prepare the Petition

Draft a petition (through a lawyer) under Section 372 of the Indian Succession Act, including:

- **Details of the deceased** — full name, date of death, last residence, religion
- **Details of the petitioner** — name, relationship with the deceased
- **Details of all legal heirs** — names, ages, addresses, relationship
- **List of debts and securities** — bank accounts, FDs, shares, insurance policies with approximate values
- **Statement that no will exists** (or that the will does not cover these assets)
- **Prayer** — requesting the court to grant a succession certificate

### Step 3: File Online via e-Courts Portal

The **e-Courts** platform enables electronic filing in many districts:

1. Visit **[services.ecourts.gov.in](https://services.ecourts.gov.in)**
2. Register as a litigant or through your advocate's credentials
3. Select the appropriate **district court** and case type (succession petition)
4. Upload the petition, supporting documents, and court fee receipt
5. Submit and note the **case number** for tracking

> **State-specific portals:** Some states like Delhi, Karnataka, and Maharashtra have their own e-filing systems. Check your state high court website for local options.

### Step 4: Pay Court Fees

Court fees are calculated as a **percentage of the total value** of debts and securities claimed. Rates vary by state:

| State | Court Fee Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Delhi | 2% of asset value | Subject to minimum ₹100 |
| Maharashtra | 3% of asset value | Additional stamp duty may apply |
| Karnataka | 5% of asset value | Capped for certain categories |
| Tamil Nadu | 7.5% of asset value | Among the highest rates |
| Uttar Pradesh | 2%–5% (slab-based) | Varies by asset value |
| West Bengal | 3% of asset value | Probate mandatory in Kolkata |

Pay via **challan, demand draft, or e-payment** (where available). Keep the receipt — it must be filed with the petition.

### Step 5: Court Issues Public Notice

After admitting the petition, the court:

1. Issues a **public notice** in a local newspaper (and sometimes the Official Gazette)
2. Sets a **45-day objection period** during which anyone can file objections
3. If no objections are received, the case proceeds as **uncontested**

### Step 6: Court Hearing and Order

- **Uncontested cases:** After the 45-day notice period, the court verifies documents, hears the petitioner, and grants the certificate — typically within **3 to 6 months** total.
- **Contested cases:** If an heir or creditor objects, the case enters trial. This can take **1 to 3 years** depending on the nature of the dispute and court backlog.

Once granted, the succession certificate specifies:
- The debts and securities covered
- The name of the authorised person
- Any limitations or conditions

## Documents Required for Succession Certificate

Gather these documents before filing:

- **Death certificate** of the deceased ([apply here](/guide/death-certificate-apply-online))
- **Identity proof** of the petitioner (Aadhaar, PAN, voter ID)
- **Proof of relationship** — [marriage certificate](/guide/marriage-certificate-apply-online), birth certificate, family ration card
- **Details of assets** — bank passbooks, FD receipts, share certificates, insurance policy documents, mutual fund statements
- **List of all legal heirs** with addresses
- **Affidavit** stating that no will exists and the information provided is true
- **No Objection Certificate (NOC)** from other legal heirs (if available — speeds up the process)
- **Court fee receipt**

## Contested vs Uncontested Cases

### Uncontested

All legal heirs agree on who should receive the certificate. The process is straightforward:

- File petition → public notice → 45-day wait → hearing → certificate granted
- **Timeline:** 3–6 months
- **Cost:** Court fee + lawyer fee (₹5,000–₹25,000 typically)

### Contested

One or more heirs or a creditor of the deceased files an objection. Common disputes include:

- **Disputed heirship** — someone claims to be an heir
- **Competing petitions** — multiple heirs file separately
- **Creditor objections** — outstanding debts of the deceased

In contested cases, the court conducts a **full trial** with evidence and cross-examination. Timeline can extend to **1–3 years**, and legal costs rise significantly.

> **Tip:** Get NOCs from all legal heirs before filing. This single step can prevent your case from becoming contested and save you years of litigation.

## Important Points to Remember

- A succession certificate is **valid across India** — no institution can refuse it
- The certificate can be **revoked** if obtained through fraud or misrepresentation
- You can apply for succession certificate for **specific assets** — you don't need to list everything
- **NRIs** can apply through a power of attorney holder in India
- The certificate is **not a title document** — it only authorises collection of debts and securities
- For **immovable property** (land, house), you need separate mutation and [registration](/guide/property-registration-online)

## Track Your Application

After filing, track your case status online:

1. Visit [services.ecourts.gov.in](https://services.ecourts.gov.in)
2. Use **Case Status** → search by case number, party name, or advocate name
3. Check hearing dates, orders, and case progress

## Summary

Getting a succession certificate involves filing a petition in the district civil court, paying state-specific court fees, waiting through a 45-day public notice period, and attending hearings. While the process takes 3–6 months for uncontested cases, having all documents ready and NOCs from co-heirs significantly speeds things up. For high-value bank deposits, insurance claims, and securities, this certificate is often the only document financial institutions will accept.
