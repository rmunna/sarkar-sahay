# Home Loan Prepayment vs Investment India 2026 — The Complete Decision Guide

One of the most common financial dilemmas in India: you have surplus money at the end of the month (or year). Should you **prepay your home loan** or **invest in equity mutual funds** (or other instruments)?

The correct answer depends on multiple factors — your loan rate, tax regime, income slab, risk tolerance, and loan tenure remaining. Here's a framework to decide.

## The Core Trade-off

| Prepaying Home Loan | Investing in Equity MF |
|--------------------|------------------------|
| Guaranteed return = home loan rate (8.5–9.5%) | Expected return ≈ 12–14% CAGR (not guaranteed) |
| No risk — debt reduced with certainty | Market risk — can be negative in 1–2 year periods |
| Emotional benefit: debt-free sooner | Wealth creation: larger corpus over time |
| Tax benefit REDUCED (less interest to deduct) | Tax on gains (LTCG 12.5% on equity) |

## The Tax Angle — This Changes Everything

### Old Tax Regime

Under the old tax regime, **home loan interest is deductible up to ₹2 lakh** under Section 24b (self-occupied property).

If you're in the 30% slab, this ₹2L deduction saves: ₹2,00,000 × 30% = **₹60,000/year**.

**Effective home loan rate = Stated rate − Tax saving**:
- Home loan at 9%: After 30% tax benefit on ₹2L = effective cost of 9% drops significantly
- If interest < ₹2L: Full interest deducted → effective rate lower than stated
- If interest > ₹2L: Only ₹2L deducted → partial benefit; the excess interest has no deduction

**Implication**: Under old regime, keep the home loan as long as interest is ≤ ₹2L/year (or close to it). The tax deduction makes the effective rate much lower.

### New Tax Regime

Under the new tax regime, **home loan interest deduction (Section 24b) is NOT available** for self-occupied property.

**Effective home loan rate = Full stated rate (no tax saving)**.

At 9% interest, you're paying 9% without any tax offset.

**Implication**: Under new regime, **prepaying makes more sense** — there's no tax benefit to lose by reducing loan outstanding. The comparison is now: 9% guaranteed (prepay) vs 12–14% equity returns.

## Comparison Framework

### Step 1: Identify Your Effective Loan Rate

| Regime | Tax Bracket | Home Loan Rate | Effective Rate After Tax Saving |
|--------|-------------|----------------|--------------------------------|
| Old | 30% | 9% | ~6–7% (if interest < ₹2L) |
| Old | 20% | 9% | ~7–8% (if interest < ₹2L) |
| New | Any | 9% | 9% (no deduction) |

Under old regime at 30% slab with < ₹2L annual interest: effective rate can be as low as 6.3%. Equity at 12% CAGR easily beats 6.3% — invest.

Under new regime at full 9%: Equity's 12% expected return vs guaranteed 9% — it's closer, and depends on risk appetite.

### Step 2: Compare with After-Tax Investment Returns

Equity mutual fund returns are also taxed:
- LTCG on equity (held > 12 months): 12.5% above ₹1.25L
- Pre-tax CAGR 12%: After 12.5% LTCG on gains, effective net return ≈ 10.5–11%

**Comparison**:
- Old regime, 30% bracket: Effective loan rate 6–7% vs net equity return 10–11% → **Invest in equity**
- New regime: Effective loan rate 9% vs net equity return 10–11% → **Marginal case; risk preference decides**
- Risk-averse (new regime): Prepay for guaranteed 9% vs uncertain 10–11%

### Step 3: Loan Tenure Remaining

**Long remaining tenure (15+ years)**:
- Compounding benefit of equity is highest over long periods
- 12% CAGR over 15 years = 5.35x returns
- 9% savings over 15 years = significant but less dramatic
- **Usually: Invest in equity for long remaining tenure**

**Short remaining tenure (5 years or less)**:
- Shorter compounding window for equity (and more STCG risk)
- Prepayment gives guaranteed closure and emotional benefit
- **Often better: Prepay for short remaining tenure**

### Step 4: Psychological Factor

Being debt-free provides real psychological peace and life flexibility:
- Job security concerns → debt-free is less stressful
- Planning for early retirement → closed loan means lower monthly outgo
- Family stability → no EMI burden in family crisis

If the guaranteed, debt-free feeling is worth 2–3% per year in expected return difference, factor that into your decision.

## Hybrid Approach — Best of Both

Instead of binary choice:
- **Keep ₹2L of annual interest** to maximize Section 24b deduction (old regime users)
- Prepay excess principal that takes interest above ₹2L
- Simultaneously invest additional surplus in equity MF

**Example**:
- Outstanding loan: ₹60L at 9%
- Annual interest: ₹5.4L (much above ₹2L deduction limit)
- You have ₹3L surplus
- Option: Prepay enough principal to bring annual interest to ₹2L (need ~₹37L prepayment — not immediately possible)
- Practical hybrid: Prepay ₹1.5L and invest ₹1.5L in equity SIP

## Home Loan Prepayment — How It Works

### Partial Prepayment
- Most banks allow partial prepayment (typically minimum ₹5,000–₹10,000)
- You can reduce either EMI (keep tenure same) or tenure (keep EMI same)
- **Reducing tenure**: More interest saved over loan life
- **Reducing EMI**: More cash flow each month

### Prepayment Charges
- **Floating rate home loan**: No prepayment penalty (RBI directive)
- **Fixed rate home loan**: Penalty up to 2% of prepaid amount
- Most home loans in India are floating rate → no prepayment charges

## When Prepayment Clearly Wins

1. **Risk-averse investor**: The guaranteed 8.5–9% return of prepayment beats FDs, debt MFs, and bonds — all without volatility.

2. **New regime + high loan rate**: Without Section 24b benefit, effective loan rate = stated rate. Prepaying 9% beats debt instruments and is close to equity returns without the risk.

3. **Near retirement**: Building a debt-free base before retirement reduces financial stress significantly. Preserve equity investments for long-term corpus.

4. **Income uncertainty**: Unstable job? Reduce debt. A smaller EMI or no EMI gives more flexibility if income drops.

## When Investment Wins

1. **Old regime + 30% bracket + significant interest**: With 30% deduction, effective home loan rate drops to ~6.3%. Long-term equity at 12% CAGR easily beats this.

2. **Long remaining tenure (15+ years)**: Time in market dominates in long tenures. Equity compounding exceeds loan interest savings.

3. **Employer NPS contribution or 80CCD(2)**: If you can reduce taxable income via NPS and stay in old regime, maximizing deductions while investing surplus in equity gives best outcome.

4. **Emergency fund first**: Before prepaying, ensure 6 months' expenses in liquid fund. Prepaying and then needing a loan for emergencies is inefficient.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My home loan EMI is ₹40,000/month and I save ₹15,000 extra each month. What should I do?

Split: ₹5,000–₹7,500 toward prepayment (for psychological progress and interest saving) and ₹7,500–₹10,000 into equity SIP. This hybrid gives both debt reduction satisfaction and equity wealth creation.

### I'm in the new regime and don't get 24b benefit. My loan rate is 8.75%. Should I prepay everything?

The 8.75% guaranteed return from prepayment vs equity's expected 12% (after LTCG) is a personal decision: 3.25% more expected return from equity, with risk. If you have stable income, 10–15 year investment horizon, and can sleep with short-term market drops: invest. If you're risk-averse or closer to retirement: prepay.

### I have ₹20 lakh in cash savings. Close the home loan fully or invest?

If fully closing the loan brings genuine peace of mind AND your investment portfolio is already well-funded (separate from this ₹20L), closing the loan may be the right move. But if this ₹20L represents most of your savings, closing the loan entirely leaves you with no financial buffer — keep at least 12–18 months of expenses liquid.
