# Salary Slip — How to Read, Download, and Understand Your Pay Slip

A **salary slip** (also called a pay slip or paycheck stub) is a document issued by your employer every month that shows the detailed breakdown of your salary — what you earned, what was deducted, and what you received in hand. Whether you need it for filing ITR, applying for a loan, or simply understanding your compensation, knowing how to read your salary slip is essential.

## What Is a Salary Slip?

A salary slip is a formal document that contains a detailed summary of your monthly salary. It typically includes:

- **Employee details** — name, employee ID, designation, department, PAN, bank account number
- **Pay period** — the month and year the salary covers
- **Earnings** — all components that add to your pay
- **Deductions** — all components subtracted from your gross pay
- **Net pay** — the final amount credited to your bank account

Under the **Payment of Wages Act, 1936** and various state Shops & Establishments Acts, employers are legally required to provide salary slips to employees. You have the right to request one if your employer does not issue it automatically.

## Salary Slip Components Explained

### Earnings (Credits)

| Component | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Basic Salary** | The core fixed component, usually 40–50% of CTC. PF, gratuity, and other benefits are calculated on this. |
| **House Rent Allowance (HRA)** | Allowance to cover rent expenses. Partially or fully exempt from tax if you live in rented accommodation. |
| **Dearness Allowance (DA)** | Paid mainly to government employees to offset inflation. Revised periodically. |
| **Special Allowance** | A flexible component that fills the gap between CTC and other defined components. Fully taxable. |
| **Conveyance Allowance** | Covers commuting expenses. Exempt up to ₹1,600/month under the old tax regime. |
| **Medical Allowance** | Fixed monthly allowance for medical expenses. Taxable under the new regime. |
| **Leave Travel Allowance (LTA)** | Covers domestic travel expenses. Tax-exempt for actual travel twice in a block of 4 years. |
| **Bonus / Performance Pay** | Variable pay based on performance, paid monthly, quarterly, or annually. |

### Deductions (Debits)

| Component | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Provident Fund (PF)** | Employee contributes 12% of basic salary (+ DA) to EPF. Employer matches this contribution. |
| **ESI (Employee State Insurance)** | Applicable if gross salary is ≤ ₹21,000/month. Employee pays 0.75%, employer pays 3.25%. |
| **TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)** | Income tax deducted monthly by employer based on your declared investments and tax regime. |
| **Professional Tax** | State-level tax, capped at ₹2,500/year. Varies by state (e.g., ₹200/month in Maharashtra, Karnataka). |
| **Loan Recovery / Advances** | Deduction for any salary advance or company loan repayment. |

## How to Read Your Salary Slip — Example Breakdown

Here is a sample salary slip for an employee with a monthly CTC of ₹60,000:

**Earnings:**

| Component | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Basic Salary | 25,000 |
| HRA | 10,000 |
| Special Allowance | 13,150 |
| Conveyance Allowance | 1,600 |
| Medical Allowance | 1,250 |
| **Gross Earnings** | **51,000** |

**Deductions:**

| Component | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| PF (Employee 12%) | 3,000 |
| Professional Tax | 200 |
| TDS | 2,500 |
| **Total Deductions** | **5,700** |

**Net Salary (In-Hand) = ₹51,000 − ₹5,700 = ₹45,300**

> **Note:** The remaining ₹9,000 from CTC (₹60,000 − ₹51,000) goes toward employer PF contribution, gratuity, and insurance — these don't appear in your in-hand salary.

### Key Things to Check Every Month

- Verify your **basic salary** matches your offer letter
- Check if **PF is calculated correctly** (12% of basic + DA)
- Confirm **TDS** aligns with your tax declarations
- Look for any **unexpected deductions** (loan recovery, loss of pay)
- Ensure **total working days** and **leave balance** are accurate

## How to Download Your Salary Slip

Most companies provide salary slips through their HRMS (Human Resource Management System) portal. Here's how to access yours:

### Common HRMS Portals

- **Keka HR** — Login → My Finances → Payslips → Select month → Download PDF
- **greytHR** — Login → Payroll → Pay Slips → Select month → Download
- **Zoho People** — Self-service → Pay Slips → Select period → Download
- **Darwinbox** — Compensation → Payslips → Download
- **SAP SuccessFactors** — Employee Central → Payroll → Pay Statements

### For Government Employees

- **CPAO (Pay & Accounts)** — Available through PFMS or departmental portals
- **iHRMS** — Used by several state governments (Haryana, Punjab, etc.)
- **SPARK** — Kerala government employee portal
- **BhaVishyavani / AIMS** — For specific central government departments

### Steps to Download

1. Log in to your company's HRMS portal or ESS (Employee Self Service)
2. Navigate to **Payroll** or **My Pay** section
3. Select the **month and year**
4. Click **View** or **Download** to get the PDF
5. Save it for your records

> If your employer does not provide digital salary slips, request them in writing. You are legally entitled to receive one.

## Why Your Salary Slip Is Important

Your salary slip is not just a monthly statement — it serves as a critical document for:

### 1. Filing Income Tax Returns (ITR)
Your salary slip shows TDS deducted, HRA received, and PF contributions — all needed for [filing your ITR online](/guide/income-tax-return-file-online). Cross-check these with your [Form 16](/guide/form-16-download-guide).

### 2. Loan Applications
Banks require **3–6 months of salary slips** for home loans, personal loans, and car loans. It helps them verify your income and repayment capacity.

### 3. Visa Applications
Embassies require salary slips as proof of stable employment and income when processing visa applications.

### 4. Employment Verification
When switching jobs, your new employer may ask for recent salary slips to verify your current compensation.

### 5. PF and ESI Claims
Your salary slip helps verify [EPF contributions](/guide/epf-passbook-balance-check-online) and ESI benefits. Match your PF deductions with your EPF passbook regularly.

## Gross Salary vs Net Salary vs CTC

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **CTC (Cost to Company)** | Total annual cost the employer spends on you — includes salary, PF (employer share), gratuity, insurance, bonuses. |
| **Gross Salary** | CTC minus employer-side contributions (employer PF, gratuity, insurance). This is your pre-deduction salary. |
| **Net Salary (In-Hand)** | Gross salary minus employee deductions (PF, TDS, professional tax). This is what you actually receive. |

**Formula:**
```
CTC = Gross Salary + Employer PF + Gratuity + Insurance + Other Benefits
Net Salary = Gross Salary − Employee PF − TDS − Professional Tax − Other Deductions
```

## How to Calculate Your In-Hand Salary

Follow these steps to estimate your monthly in-hand salary from your CTC:

1. **Find your annual CTC** from your offer letter
2. **Subtract employer contributions** — employer PF (12% of basic), gratuity (4.81% of basic), employer ESI (if applicable), group insurance
3. **Result = Annual Gross Salary** → divide by 12 for monthly gross
4. **Subtract employee deductions** — employee PF, TDS, professional tax
5. **Result = Monthly Net Salary (In-Hand)**

### Quick Example

| | Annual (₹) | Monthly (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| CTC | 6,00,000 | 50,000 |
| Minus Employer PF | 28,800 | 2,400 |
| Minus Gratuity | 11,538 | 962 |
| **Gross Salary** | **5,59,662** | **46,638** |
| Minus Employee PF | 28,800 | 2,400 |
| Minus Professional Tax | 2,400 | 200 |
| Minus TDS (approx.) | 15,600 | 1,300 |
| **Net Salary (In-Hand)** | **5,12,862** | **42,738** |

## Important Tips

- **Keep salary slips for at least 3 years** — useful for ITR verification, loan applications, and employment disputes
- **Compare with Form 16** — ensure TDS shown on salary slips matches your Form 16 at year-end
- **Declare investments early** — submit your tax-saving declarations (80C, 80D, HRA) at the start of the financial year to reduce monthly TDS
- **Check PF passbook regularly** — verify your employer is depositing PF contributions correctly
- **Understand your tax regime** — old regime allows deductions (HRA, 80C, etc.); new regime offers lower rates but fewer exemptions
- **Request salary slip if not provided** — it is your legal right under labour laws

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is salary slip mandatory by law?
Yes. Under the Payment of Wages Act and state Shops & Establishments Acts, employers must provide a salary slip showing the breakdown of wages and deductions.

### What is the difference between salary slip and Form 16?
A salary slip is a monthly document showing your pay breakdown. Form 16 is an annual TDS certificate issued by your employer summarizing total salary, deductions, and tax paid for the financial year.

### Can I get a salary slip after leaving the company?
Yes, you can request salary slips from your previous employer. Most HRMS portals allow access for a limited period after exit. If access is revoked, write to HR.

### How many months of salary slips do banks need for a loan?
Most banks require **3 to 6 months** of recent salary slips along with bank statements and Form 16 for loan processing.

### What should I do if my salary slip has errors?
Immediately raise it with your HR or payroll department in writing. Common errors include wrong PF deduction, incorrect TDS, or missing allowances. Keep a copy of your communication.

### Is salary slip needed for ITR filing?
While not mandatory to upload, salary slip details help you accurately fill in salary breakdowns, HRA exemption, and verify TDS in your ITR form. Your Form 16 and AIS/TIS are the primary documents.

### How is professional tax calculated?
Professional tax rates are set by state governments. Most states charge ₹150–₹200 per month (capped at ₹2,500/year). Some states like Rajasthan and Delhi do not levy professional tax.
