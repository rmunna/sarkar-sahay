# Lottery Prize Money Tax India 2026 — 30% Flat Tax, No Deductions

Won a lottery? Got prize money from a game show or quiz? Cashed out winnings from an online gaming platform? The tax treatment is straightforward but harsh: **flat 30% tax** with no deductions, regardless of your income.

## The Tax Rate — Section 115BB

Under **Section 115BB** of the Income Tax Act:
- **Flat 30% tax** (plus applicable surcharge and cess) on winnings from:
  - Lottery (state lotteries like Kerala, Sikkim, etc.)
  - TV game shows (KBC, Bigg Boss, reality shows)
  - Crossword puzzles
  - Card games / gambling (legal)
  - Horse racing (net winning per race)
  - Online gaming (Budget 2023 specifically added this)
- **No basic exemption**: Even if your total income is below ₹4L (new regime) or ₹2.5L (old regime), lottery winnings are taxed at 30% flat
- **No deductions**: You cannot deduct 80C, 80D, home loan interest, or any other deduction against this income
- **Separate slab**: This income doesn't merge with your regular income for slab calculation — it's taxed separately at 30%

**Effective rate with cess**: 30% + 4% Health & Education Cess = **31.2%**
Higher surcharges apply for very large winnings (above ₹50L or ₹1 crore).

## TDS Before Payout

The prize distributor (lottery company, TV channel, race club, gaming platform) deducts **TDS at 30%** on winnings above ₹10,000 before paying you.

**TDS threshold**: ₹10,000 per winning.
- Win ₹8,000: No TDS deduction, but you must self-declare and pay tax in ITR
- Win ₹50,000: 30% TDS deducted = ₹15,000 TDS; you receive ₹35,000 net

For games shows: Prizes in kind (cars, jewellery, appliances) — TDS is calculated on the market value of the prize.

## Online Gaming — Budget 2023 Specific Rules

Budget 2023 added Section 115BBJ specifically for "Net Winnings from Online Games":

| Condition | TDS Rate |
|-----------|---------|
| Net winnings from each withdrawal | **30% TDS** |
| No minimum threshold | Applies from ₹1 (no ₹10,000 floor) |
| If platform is non-gaming (like lucky draws) | ₹10,000 threshold applies |

**Online gaming platforms (Dream11, MPL, RummyCircle, Junglee Poker, etc.)**:
- Must deduct TDS at 30% on net winnings when you withdraw
- "Net winnings" per withdrawal = Amount withdrawn − Amount deposited in that session
- Tax is levied at 30% on these net winnings

**Practical impact**: If you deposited ₹1,000 on Dream11 and withdrew ₹2,500:
- Net winning: ₹1,500
- TDS: 30% × ₹1,500 = ₹450
- Net amount: ₹2,050

## State Lottery — Additional State-Level Deductions

State lotteries often have additional local deductions before payout. For example, Kerala lottery prizes:
- Above ₹1L: Face value minus agent commission minus income tax TDS
- Payout is the net after all deductions

When filing ITR, declare the **gross prize amount** (before TDS), not what you received.

## ITR Filing for Prize Money

Winnings are declared under **"Income from Other Sources"** in your ITR. Even if TDS was deducted by the organizer, you must declare the gross amount.

**Which ITR form**: If you have winnings + salary, use ITR-2. If additionally have business income, use ITR-3.

In Schedule OS (Other Sources): There's a specific section for "Income chargeable at special rates (winnings from lottery/crossword/etc.)." Enter the gross winning amount here.

The portal automatically applies the flat 30% rate (not your slab rate) on this income.

## Horse Racing — Different Treatment

Horse racing has specific rules:
- **Individual race winning**: Taxed at 30% flat
- Stakes paid (losing bets) in the same meeting can be set off against winnings (unlike lottery where no deduction is allowed)
- From a licensed horse racing club only — illegal betting has different treatment

## Winnings in Kind (Non-Cash Prizes)

If you win a car, jewellery, or appliances:
- Tax is on the **market value** of the prize
- The organizer must calculate TDS on market value
- If organizer paid TDS on your behalf (sometimes done for big prizes): That TDS is income for you and also taxable!

**Example**: Win a ₹5L car on a game show.
- Tax: 30% × ₹5L = ₹1.5L
- Organizer may pay this TDS themselves rather than asking you to pay
- But the ₹1.5L paid by organizer on your behalf is itself a "benefit" and taxable in your hands

Some game shows gross up the prize to cover TDS — verify with the show's production company.

## KBC (Kaun Banega Crorepati) Prize

KBC prize money (₹1 crore for the grand prize):
- Tax at 30% = ₹30L TDS
- Plus surcharge on income above ₹50L (10% surcharge) and above ₹1 crore (15% surcharge)
- You receive approximately ₹67–68L net after all deductions

Smaller prizes on KBC: Same 30% TDS.

## Can You Invest Prize Money in 80C to Save Tax?

**No** — the 30% flat rate under Section 115BB cannot be reduced by 80C investments, home loan deductions, or any Chapter VI-A deductions. The flat rate is applied on the gross winning before any deductions.

The only way to avoid this tax: Don't win (not practical advice). The law gives no relief mechanism.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I won ₹50,000 in a fantasy sports app (Dream11). They deducted TDS. Do I need to declare in ITR?

Yes — always declare in ITR even if TDS was deducted. The TDS is only an advance deduction; you must report the gross winning in Schedule OS under "Online Games" income. The 30% TDS is credited against your total tax liability. File ITR-2 (if salaried with this additional income).

### I won ₹8,000 in a state lottery. No TDS was deducted. Do I pay tax?

Yes — the ₹10,000 TDS threshold only means the lottery organizer doesn't deduct TDS below ₹10,000. You still owe 30% tax on ₹8,000 = ₹2,400. Declare it in ITR under Other Sources (lottery income category) and pay self-assessment tax.

### My grandmother gifted me her lottery prize money of ₹2L. Is the gift taxable for me?

If the gift is from a relative (grandmother is a relative under gift tax rules): Gift is tax-free for you. But the grandmother would have already paid 30% tax on winning the prize. The subsequent gift to you (a relative) is covered under Section 56(2)(x) exemption for gifts from relatives.
