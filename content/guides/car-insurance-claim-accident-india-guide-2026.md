# Car Insurance Claim After Accident India 2026 — Complete Guide

The 30 minutes after a car accident determine whether your claim gets approved or delayed. Most claim rejections happen because of documentation errors or wrong steps right after the accident. Here's exactly what to do.

---

## Immediate Steps After an Accident

### First 30 Minutes

**Step 1: Safety first**
Move people away from the vehicle if it's a road hazard. Call 112 if anyone is injured.

**Step 2: Take photographs** (BEFORE moving the car)
- All angles of your damaged car
- Position of both vehicles (if third-party involved)
- Close-up of all damage points
- Registration plates of all vehicles
- The accident scene (road, weather, skid marks)
- Dashcam footage if available (save immediately — it may overwrite)

**Step 3: Don't admit liability**
Don't say "it was my fault" or sign anything at the scene. Let the insurance investigation determine liability.

**Step 4: Get the other driver's details** (if third-party accident)
- Name, phone number, address
- Vehicle registration number
- Insurance company + policy number
- Driving license number

**Step 5: File FIR if needed**
- Third-party property damage or injury: FIR usually required by insurer
- Major own-damage (major structural damage): Get FIR or Motor Accident Report from police
- Minor own-damage (parking scratch, minor dent): FIR not always required — check your policy

---

## Within 24 Hours — Inform Your Insurer

**Call your insurer's 24/7 claim helpline** (number on your policy document or insurance app).

Information you'll need:
- Policy number
- Date, time, location of accident
- Nature of damage
- Vehicle registration number
- Whether third party is involved

**Why 24 hours?** Most policies require "prompt" notification. Delays can give insurers grounds to reduce/reject the claim. Some policies explicitly require notification within 24–48 hours.

## Two Types of Claims — Choose Correctly

### 1. Cashless Claim (Recommended)

**How it works**: Your car goes directly to an authorized (network) garage. Insurer pays the garage directly. You pay only the deductible/depreciation amount.

**When to use**: Whenever the damage is significant and the insurer has a network garage near you.

**Process**:
1. Call insurer → request cashless claim + surveyor visit
2. Insurer sends surveyor (within 24–48 hours) to assess damage
3. Surveyor approves repair list
4. Garage repairs car using approved parts
5. Insurer pays garage directly
6. You pay: Deductible (mandatory excess) + depreciation amount (unless zero-dep add-on) + any non-covered items

**Finding network garages**: Your insurer's app/website has a garage locator. Also available on IRDAI website.

### 2. Reimbursement Claim

**How it works**: You pay the garage, insurer reimburses you later.

**When to use**: Emergency (network garage not available), or minor damage at a trusted non-network garage.

**Process**:
1. Get car repaired at any garage
2. Collect: detailed invoice (part-wise), payment receipt, surveyor's assessment report
3. Submit claim documents to insurer
4. Insurer reimburses within 7–30 days (after deducting depreciation, deductible, etc.)

**Disadvantage**: You bear upfront cost; reimbursement can be slow (15–30 days).

## What Insurer DEDUCTS From Your Claim

Even in a valid claim, you don't get 100% of repair cost back:

### 1. Mandatory Deductible (Compulsory Excess)

Regulatory minimum deductible (as set by IRDAI):
- Cars up to 1,500cc: ₹1,000
- Cars above 1,500cc: ₹2,000

You always pay this regardless of add-ons.

### 2. Depreciation on Parts

Parts have a depreciation schedule — insurers don't pay full replacement cost for parts because they had prior use:

| Part | Depreciation (>5-year-old car) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| Rubber/plastic parts | 50% |
| Fiber parts | 30% |
| Metal parts | 10–15% |
| Glass (windshield) | 0% |
| Engine parts | 0–15% |

**Example**: Replacing a rubber bumper for ₹10,000 → insurer pays ₹5,000 (50% deduction for depreciation); you pay ₹5,000 out of pocket.

**Solution — Zero Depreciation (Zero Dep) Add-On**: Pay slightly higher premium to eliminate depreciation deduction. Insurer pays full replacement cost. Essential for new cars.

### 3. Consumables Not Covered (by default)

Engine oil, nut-bolts, coolant, lubricants used in repairs — not covered in standard policy. 
**Solution**: "Consumables add-on" covers these.

## NCB (No Claim Bonus) — How It Works

NCB rewards you for not filing claims with premium discounts at renewal:

| Claim-Free Years | NCB Discount on Own Damage Premium |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------|
| 1 year | 20% |
| 2 years | 25% |
| 3 years | 35% |
| 4 years | 45% |
| 5+ years | 50% |

**If you file any own-damage claim**: NCB resets to 0% at renewal.

**Impact example**: 5-year NCB gives 50% discount on own-damage premium. If your OD premium is ₹10,000 → you pay ₹5,000. After a claim, NCB resets → you'll pay ₹10,000 next year.

**Decision: Claim or pay out of pocket?**
If repair cost ≤ next year's NCB loss, pay out of pocket and preserve NCB.

**Example**: Minor dent repair costs ₹4,000. NCB reset increases your premium by ₹6,000 next year. Better to pay ₹4,000 yourself than lose ₹6,000 in NCB savings.

**NCB Protect Add-On**: Allows one claim per policy year without resetting NCB. Worth buying if you have 35%+ NCB.

## Third-Party Claim vs Own-Damage Claim

| Type | What It Covers | Effect on NCB |
|------|---------------|---------------|
| Own Damage (OD) | Your car's damage | Resets NCB |
| Third-Party (TP) | Damage you caused to others | Does NOT affect NCB |

If you damage someone else's vehicle → your third-party coverage pays them. Your NCB is unaffected.

If someone else damages your car and admits fault → their third-party pays you. Your NCB is unaffected.

## Why Claims Get Rejected — Common Reasons

| Rejection Reason | Prevention |
|-----------------|-----------|
| Driving without valid license | Ensure license is valid, not expired |
| Drinking and driving (DUI) | Zero coverage for accidents under influence |
| Policy expired at time of accident | Check renewal date in insurer app |
| Car used for commercial purpose | Personal policy doesn't cover commercial use |
| Delayed intimation to insurer | Call within 24 hours, always |
| Driving without RC | Keep RC in car |
| Modified car (unauthorized) | Declare all modifications at policy time |
| Wear-and-tear damage (not accident) | Not coverable — must be sudden, external event |

## If Claim Is Rejected — How to Fight Back

1. **Request written rejection letter** with specific reason
2. **File complaint on IRDAI's Bima Bharosa portal**: [bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in](https://bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in)
3. **Insurance Ombudsman**: For amounts up to ₹30 lakh — file at [ioins.gov.in](https://ioins.gov.in) — free, resolved in 3 months typically
4. **Consumer Forum**: [edaakhil.nic.in](https://edaakhil.nic.in) for deficiency of service
5. **NCDRC**: For large claims

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I file FIR for a minor fender-bender?

Not mandatory for minor damage without third-party injury. However, if you later need to claim and the insurer asks for FIR, having one is safer. For scratches under ₹5,000, often better to skip claim (preserve NCB) and skip FIR.

### The other driver wants to settle privately (not involve insurance). Should I?

Get their payment first (UPI/bank transfer), in writing. Confirm no injury involved. For significant damage (>₹10,000), insist on insurance claim — private settlements can fall through.

### My car was damaged in a flood. Is it covered?

Covered under standard comprehensive policy if you have "Act of Nature" cover (included in most comprehensive policies). Don't start the flooded car — hydrostatic lock can increase damage and complicate claims. Call insurer immediately.

### Can I repair first and claim later?

For reimbursement claims: yes, but keep all original bills and photos. For cashless: no — surveyor must assess before repair.
