## Why Vehicle Insurance is Mandatory in India

Under the **Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (Section 146)**, every vehicle running on Indian roads must have at least a valid **third-party insurance** policy. Driving without insurance is a punishable offence.

### Penalties for Driving Without Insurance

| Offence | Fine |
|---------|------|
| First time caught without insurance | ₹2,000 and/or 3 months imprisonment |
| Repeat offence | ₹4,000 and/or 3 months imprisonment |

> **Important:** Without valid insurance, you cannot renew your vehicle's Registration Certificate (RC) or get a fitness certificate.

## Types of Vehicle Insurance in India

There are three main types of motor vehicle insurance:

### 1. Third-Party Insurance (Mandatory)

This is the **minimum legal requirement**. It covers damage or injury you cause to **other people, vehicles, or property** in an accident.

**What it covers:**
- Injury or death of a third person
- Damage to someone else's vehicle or property
- Legal liability if you are at fault

**What it does NOT cover:**
- Damage to your own vehicle
- Theft of your vehicle
- Personal injury to the driver/owner

### 2. Comprehensive Insurance (Recommended)

This is the **most complete protection**. It includes everything in third-party insurance **plus** coverage for your own vehicle.

**What it covers:**
- Everything in third-party insurance
- Damage to your own vehicle (accident, fire, natural disaster)
- Theft of your vehicle
- Personal accident cover for owner-driver (₹15 lakh)

**Optional add-ons available:**
- Zero depreciation cover
- Roadside assistance
- Engine protection
- Return to invoice cover
- Key replacement

### 3. Own Damage (OD) Insurance

This covers **only your own vehicle** — no third-party liability. You can buy this separately if you already have a standalone third-party policy.

**What it covers:**
- Accidental damage to your vehicle
- Fire, explosion, natural calamities
- Theft, burglary, riots

### Quick Comparison

| Feature | Third-Party Only | Comprehensive | Own Damage Only |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Third-party liability | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Own vehicle damage | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Theft protection | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Personal accident cover | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Legally sufficient? | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ (need TP too) |

## How to Buy or Renew Vehicle Insurance Online

### Method 1: Directly from Insurer Websites

Visit any IRDAI-approved insurer's website:

1. Go to the insurer's website (e.g., ICICI Lombard, HDFC ERGO, Bajaj Allianz, New India Assurance)
2. Select **"Motor Insurance"** → Car or Two-Wheeler
3. Enter your vehicle registration number
4. Vehicle details will auto-fetch from VAHAN database
5. Choose policy type (Third-Party / Comprehensive)
6. Select add-ons if needed
7. Pay online via UPI, debit card, credit card, or net banking
8. Policy document is sent instantly to your email

### Method 2: Through Insurance Aggregators

Aggregator platforms let you compare quotes from multiple insurers:

- **PolicyBazaar** — policybazaar.com
- **Acko** — acko.com
- **Digit Insurance** — godigit.com
- **Coverfox** — coverfox.com

These platforms show you side-by-side price comparisons and help you pick the best deal.

### Method 3: Through Parivahan / e-Vahan Portal

When performing vehicle-related services on the **Parivahan** portal, you may be prompted to update or link your insurance details. The VAHAN system now integrates with insurance databases for verification.

### For Renewal

- If your policy has **not lapsed** (within 90 days of expiry): You can renew online directly without inspection.
- If your policy has **lapsed** (more than 90 days): The insurer may require a **vehicle inspection** before issuing a new policy. Some insurers allow self-inspection via app (photo/video of vehicle).

## Documents Required

### For Buying New Insurance
- Vehicle Registration Certificate (RC)
- Aadhaar card or PAN card (for KYC)
- Vehicle chassis number and engine number
- Previous insurance policy (if any)

### For Renewal
- Previous policy number or copy
- Vehicle RC number
- KYC document (Aadhaar/PAN)

### For Lapsed Policy (New Policy After Gap)
- All of the above
- Vehicle inspection report (insurer will arrange)
- Photos of the vehicle (front, back, sides, odometer, chassis number)

## How to Check Vehicle Insurance Status Online

You can verify if any vehicle has valid insurance using these methods:

### Method 1: VAHAN Portal

1. Visit **[vahan.parivahan.gov.in](https://vahan.parivahan.gov.in)**
2. Enter the vehicle registration number
3. The portal shows basic vehicle details including insurance validity

### Method 2: IIB (Insurance Information Bureau) Portal

1. Visit **[iib.gov.in](https://iib.gov.in)**
2. IIB maintains a database of all motor insurance policies in India
3. Note: Direct public search may not be available; this database is primarily used by enforcement agencies and insurers

### Method 3: mParivahan App

1. Download the **mParivahan** app from Google Play Store or Apple App Store
2. Enter your vehicle number or scan the RC QR code
3. View RC details along with insurance status

### Method 4: Contact Your Insurer

Call your insurance company's toll-free number or log in to their website/app to check your policy status.

## How to Make an Insurance Claim

### For Accident / Damage

1. **Inform your insurer immediately** — Call the toll-free number or use the app
2. **File an FIR** if it involves a third party or major accident
3. **Get a claim number** from the insurer
4. **Take the vehicle to a network garage** (cashless) or any garage (reimbursement)
5. **Submit documents:** Claim form, RC copy, driving license copy, FIR (if applicable), photos of damage
6. **Survey:** Insurer sends a surveyor to assess damage
7. **Repair and settlement:** Cashless claims are settled directly with the garage; reimbursement claims are paid to you after you submit bills

### For Theft

1. **File an FIR** at the nearest police station immediately
2. **Inform your insurer** within 24 hours
3. **Submit:** FIR copy, RC original, all vehicle keys, claim form, KYC documents
4. **Non-traceable certificate:** If vehicle is not found in 30-60 days, police issues this certificate
5. **Transfer RC:** You need to transfer the RC to the insurer's name via RTO
6. **Claim settlement:** Insurer pays the IDV (Insured Declared Value) of the vehicle

## No-Claim Bonus (NCB) — Save Money on Renewal

NCB is a **discount on your insurance premium** for every claim-free year. It rewards safe driving.

### NCB Discount Rates (IRDAI Standard)

| Claim-Free Years | NCB Discount |
|-----------------|--------------|
| After 1 year | 20% |
| After 2 years | 25% |
| After 3 years | 35% |
| After 4 years | 45% |
| After 5+ years | 50% |

### Important NCB Rules

- NCB applies only to the **Own Damage (OD) premium**, not third-party premium
- NCB belongs to the **owner**, not the vehicle — you can transfer it to a new vehicle
- If you make a claim, your NCB resets to 0%
- You can protect your NCB with an **NCB Protector add-on**
- NCB is valid for **90 days** after policy expiry — renew within this window to retain it

## How to Transfer Insurance When Selling Your Vehicle

When you sell your vehicle, the insurance policy should be transferred to the new owner.

### Steps to Transfer

1. **Seller informs the insurer** about the sale (in writing or online)
2. **Buyer applies for transfer** with the insurer, submitting:
   - Sale deed / Form 29 and Form 30
   - New RC in buyer's name (or transfer receipt)
   - Buyer's KYC documents
   - Copy of existing insurance policy
3. **Insurer processes the transfer** — usually takes 7-15 days
4. **New policy is issued** in the buyer's name for the remaining period

> **Tip:** Do not let the insurance lapse during [vehicle RC transfer](/guide/vehicle-rc-transfer-online). The vehicle must be insured at all times.

### If You Buy a New Vehicle and Want to Transfer NCB

- Submit a letter to your old insurer requesting NCB certificate
- Provide this certificate to your new insurer when buying the new policy
- NCB transfers to you, not to the vehicle

## IRDAI Rules You Should Know

- **Third-party insurance is mandatory** for all vehicles (Section 146, Motor Vehicles Act)
- **Long-term third-party policies:** For new cars, 3-year TP is mandatory; for new two-wheelers, 5-year TP is mandatory
- **Personal accident cover** of ₹15 lakh for owner-driver is compulsory with every motor policy
- **IRDAI regulates all premiums** for third-party insurance — rates are fixed and same across all insurers
- **OD premiums are market-driven** — compare across insurers for the best rate
- Insurers cannot refuse to issue a third-party policy

## Tips for Getting the Best Deal

1. **Compare quotes** on aggregator websites before buying
2. **Maintain your NCB** — avoid small claims to keep the discount
3. **Choose a higher voluntary deductible** to lower your premium
4. **Buy add-ons wisely** — zero depreciation is worth it for new vehicles
5. **Renew before expiry** — don't let your policy lapse
6. **Install approved anti-theft devices** — some insurers offer a discount
7. **Bundle long-term policies** if available for better rates

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I buy vehicle insurance without RC?
No, the Registration Certificate is mandatory for buying insurance. For brand new vehicles, the dealer arranges temporary insurance before registration.

### What happens if my insurance lapses?
You will need to buy a fresh policy. The insurer may require vehicle inspection. You also lose your NCB if the gap exceeds 90 days.

### Is online insurance as valid as offline?
Yes, online policies are equally valid. The e-policy document (PDF) is legally accepted everywhere including by traffic police and RTOs.

### Can I cancel my vehicle insurance?
Yes, you can cancel and get a pro-rata refund for the unused period, minus any claims made.

### How quickly is the policy issued online?
Instantly. You receive the policy document on your email and can download it immediately after payment.
