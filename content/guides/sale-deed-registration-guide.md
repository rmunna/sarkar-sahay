# Sale Deed Registration: Process, Documents & Stamp Duty

A **sale deed** is the most important legal document in a property transaction. It transfers ownership of immovable property from the seller to the buyer. Under the Indian Registration Act, 1908, registration of a sale deed is **mandatory** — an unregistered sale deed has no legal validity for transferring property ownership.

This guide covers the complete process of drafting, stamping, and registering a sale deed in India.

---

## What is a Sale Deed?

A sale deed (also called a **conveyance deed**) is a legal document that records the sale of property between a seller (vendor) and a buyer (vendee). It contains:

- Details of the buyer and seller
- Property description (location, area, boundaries, survey number)
- Sale consideration (price)
- Terms and conditions of the sale
- Declaration that the seller has clear title
- Transfer of all rights to the buyer

---

## Step 1: Drafting the Sale Deed

### Key Contents of a Sale Deed

1. **Date and place** of execution
2. **Parties' details** — full names, father's name, age, address, [Aadhaar](/guide/aadhaar-card-apply-online)/PAN of buyer and seller
3. **Recitals** — how the seller acquired the property
4. **Property description** — survey/plot number, area, boundaries (east/west/north/south), built-up area
5. **Sale consideration** — total price, advance paid, balance amount
6. **Payment details** — mode of payment (cheque, NEFT, demand draft)
7. **Title guarantee** — seller's declaration of clear, marketable title
8. **Encumbrance declaration** — property is free from mortgages, liens, disputes
9. **Possession clause** — when possession is handed over
10. **Indemnity clause** — seller indemnifies buyer against title defects

### Tips for Drafting

- Get the deed drafted by a **property lawyer** or experienced document writer
- Verify property description matches the actual survey/revenue records
- Mention the **PAN numbers** of both parties (mandatory for transactions above ₹10 lakh)
- Include the **encumbrance certificate** reference

---

## Step 2: Stamp Duty Calculation

Stamp duty is a tax levied by the state government on property transactions. It must be paid **before or at the time of registration**.

### How Stamp Duty is Calculated

Stamp duty is calculated on the **higher of**:
- The actual sale consideration (price mentioned in the deed), OR
- The **circle rate / guideline value / ready reckoner rate** of the property

### Stamp Duty Rates (Major States)

| State | Stamp Duty (General) | Women Concession |
|-------|---------------------|------------------|
| Maharashtra | 5%–6% (varies by area) | 1% less in some areas |
| Karnataka | 5% (3% for SC/ST) | 2%–3% |
| Uttar Pradesh | 7% | 5% (if sole woman buyer) |
| Rajasthan | 5%–6% | 4%–5% |
| Delhi | 4%–6% | 4% |
| Tamil Nadu | 7% | 7% (1% concession in some cases) |
| West Bengal | 5%–7% | 3%–5% |
| Gujarat | 4.9% | 4.9% |

> **Note:** Rates change periodically. Check the [stamp duty calculator](/guide/property-valuation-stamp-duty) or your state's registration portal for current rates.

### Registration Charges

In addition to stamp duty, a **registration fee** of **1%** of the property value is charged in most states (subject to a maximum cap).

### How to Pay Stamp Duty

1. **E-stamping** — Buy e-stamp certificates from authorized centres or [shcilestamp.com](https://shcilestamp.com/)
2. **Franking** — Pay at authorized banks and get the deed franked
3. **Physical stamp paper** — Purchase non-judicial stamp paper of the required value (being phased out in many states)

## Step 3: Registration Process

### Before the Visit

1. Book an **appointment** on your state's registration portal (mandatory in many states)
2. Get **e-stamp certificate** or franked stamp paper of the required value
3. Arrange **two witnesses** with their ID proofs
4. Get **passport-size photographs** of buyer, seller, and witnesses

### At the Sub-Registrar Office

1. **Submit the sale deed** along with supporting documents
2. **Biometric verification** — fingerprints and photographs of all parties
3. **Witness verification** — both witnesses sign and provide ID
4. **Pay the registration fee** (online or at the counter)
5. **Receive acknowledgement** with the document number
6. **Collect the registered deed** — typically available within 7–15 days (or downloaded online in many states)

### Online Registration (Available in Select States)

Some states now offer **e-registration**:
- **Maharashtra:** [igr.maharashtra.gov.in](https://igr.maharashtra.gov.in/) — complete online booking and token system
- **Karnataka:** [kaveri2.karnataka.gov.in](https://kaveri2.karnataka.gov.in/) — online slot booking and document upload
- **Uttar Pradesh:** [igrs.up.gov.in](https://igrs.up.gov.in/) — online appointment and registration

## Documents Required for Registration

### From the Seller

- Original title deed (previous sale deed or allotment letter)
- Encumbrance certificate (last 13–30 years)
- Property tax paid receipts
- NOC from housing society/authority (if applicable)
- [PAN card](/guide/pan-card-apply-online)
- Aadhaar card
- Passport-size photographs

### From the Buyer

- PAN card (mandatory for transactions above ₹10 lakh)
- Aadhaar card
- Address proof
- Passport-size photographs
- TDS challan (if applicable — 1% TDS on property above ₹50 lakh)

### Other Documents

- **Two witnesses** with Aadhaar and photographs
- **Power of Attorney** (if any party is represented by an agent)
- **Society NOC** (for flats in cooperative housing societies)
- **Bank NOC** (if property has an existing home loan)
- **Approved building plan** (for newly constructed property)

---

## TDS on Property Purchase

If the property value is **₹50 lakh or above**, the buyer must:

1. Deduct **1% TDS** on the sale consideration
2. Deposit TDS using **Form 26QB** on [tin.tin.nsdl.com](https://onlineservices.tin.egov-nsdl.com/etaxnew/tdsnontds.jsp)
3. Issue **Form 16B** to the seller

## Important Tips

1. **Always verify the title** — get a title search done by a lawyer for at least 13 years of ownership history
2. **Check the encumbrance certificate** — ensures no pending mortgages or legal claims
3. **Pay through banking channels** — avoid cash payments; keep full trail of payments for IT purposes
4. **Get mutation done** after registration — see our [mutation guide](/guide/mutation-of-property-apply)
5. **Keep multiple copies** — store the registered deed safely and keep certified copies

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a sale deed and an agreement to sell?

An agreement to sell is a promise to sell the property in the future under certain conditions. A sale deed is the final document that actually transfers ownership. The agreement to sell does not transfer ownership — the sale deed does.

### Is registration of a sale deed mandatory?

Yes. Under Section 17 of the Indian Registration Act, 1908, any document that transfers immovable property valued above ₹100 must be registered. An unregistered sale deed cannot be used as evidence of title transfer in any court.

### How many witnesses are needed for sale deed registration?

Two witnesses are required. They must be present at the sub-registrar office with their original ID proofs (Aadhaar card) and passport-size photographs.

### Can I register a sale deed online?

The registration still requires physical presence at the sub-registrar office for biometric verification in most states. However, you can book appointments, upload documents, and pay fees online in states like Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Uttar Pradesh.

### What happens if I pay less stamp duty than required?

If the stamp duty paid is less than the required amount, the registrar will ask you to pay the deficit. If discovered later, the government can impose a penalty of up to 10 times the deficit amount plus the original deficit.

### Can a sale deed be cancelled?

A registered sale deed can only be cancelled by mutual consent of both parties (through a cancellation deed) or by a court order. Unilateral cancellation is not possible.

### What is the validity of a sale deed?

A registered sale deed is valid permanently. There is no expiry date. It remains valid until the property is sold again or transferred through another legal document.
