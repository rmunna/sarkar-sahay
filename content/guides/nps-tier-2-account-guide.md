# NPS Tier 2 Account: How to Open, Benefits & Withdrawal Rules

The **NPS Tier 2 account** is a voluntary savings account that works alongside your NPS Tier 1 (pension) account. Unlike Tier 1, it has **no lock-in period** — you can deposit and withdraw anytime. This makes it a flexible investment option with the same professional fund management as NPS Tier 1.

## What is NPS Tier 2 Account?

NPS Tier 2 is an **open-ended investment account** under the National Pension System. It offers the same fund managers, asset classes, and low-cost structure as Tier 1, but without withdrawal restrictions. Think of it as a mutual fund alternative with very low fund management charges.

## NPS Tier 2 vs Tier 1: Key Differences

| Feature | Tier 1 (Pension) | Tier 2 (Savings) |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Retirement savings | Voluntary investment |
| Lock-in period | Until age 60 | **No lock-in** |
| Minimum opening amount | ₹500 | ₹1,000 |
| Minimum per contribution | ₹500 | ₹250 |
| Minimum annual contribution | ₹1,000 | No minimum annual requirement |
| Withdrawal | Restricted (partial after 3 years) | **Anytime, any amount** |
| Tax benefit (80CCD) | Yes — up to ₹2 lakh | **No** (except central govt employees) |
| Tax on withdrawal | 60% tax-free at maturity | Taxed as per income slab |
| Prerequisite | None | Active Tier 1 account required |
| Annuity requirement | 40% at maturity | No annuity required |

## Who is Eligible?

- Any Indian citizen with an **active NPS Tier 1 account**
- You **cannot open Tier 2 without Tier 1**
- Age: 18–70 years (same as Tier 1)

## How to Open NPS Tier 2 Account Online

### If You Already Have a Tier 1 Account

#### Step 1: Log in to CRA Portal
Visit [https://www.npscra.nsdl.co.in/](https://www.npscra.nsdl.co.in/) and log in with your PRAN and password.

#### Step 2: Request Tier 2 Activation
Go to **"Transaction"** → **"Tier 2 Activation"** or **"Open Tier 2 Account"**.

#### Step 3: Select PFM and Investment Choice
- Choose your **Pension Fund Manager** (can be different from Tier 1)
- Select **Active Choice** or **Auto Choice** for asset allocation
- Choose nominee (can be different from Tier 1)

#### Step 4: Make Initial Contribution
- Minimum ₹1,000 for opening
- Pay via **net banking, debit card, or UPI**

#### Step 5: Tier 2 Account Activated
Your Tier 2 account is activated under the same PRAN. You can start transacting immediately.

### If Opening Both Tier 1 and Tier 2 Together
During [NPS registration on eNPS](/guide/nps-account-open-online), select **both Tier 1 and Tier 2** at the time of registration. Make the combined minimum contribution (₹500 for Tier 1 + ₹1,000 for Tier 2).

## Investment Options in Tier 2

The asset classes and fund managers are identical to Tier 1:

### Asset Classes
| Class | Description | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|
| **E (Equity)** | Invests in stocks (index funds) | High |
| **C (Corporate Bonds)** | Corporate debt securities | Moderate |
| **G (Government Securities)** | Government bonds | Low |
| **A (Alternate Assets)** | REITs, InvITs, CMBS | Moderate-High |

### Investment Choices
- **Active Choice:** You decide the allocation. Max 75% in equity (no age-based cap in Tier 2).
- **Auto Choice:** Lifecycle-based allocation (Aggressive/Moderate/Conservative).

> **Tip:** Since Tier 2 has no lock-in, you can use Active Choice with higher equity allocation for potentially better returns, but be mindful of market risk.

## Withdrawal Rules — Tier 2

- **No lock-in period** — withdraw anytime (exception: central govt employees, see below)
- **No minimum balance** required after account opening
- **No limit on withdrawal amount** — you can withdraw partial or full balance
- **Withdrawal credited to bank** linked to your PRAN within 3–4 working days
- Process: Log in to CRA portal → Transaction → Withdrawal Tier 2 → Enter amount → OTP → Submit

### Exception: Central Government Employees
Central government employees who claim tax benefit under **Section 80CCD(1)** on Tier 2 contributions face a **3-year lock-in** on those contributions. This was introduced in Budget 2020.

## Tax Treatment of NPS Tier 2

| Aspect | Tax Treatment |
|---|---|
| Contribution | **No tax deduction** (except central govt employees under 80CCD) |
| Capital gains on withdrawal | **Taxed as per income slab** (no separate capital gains treatment) |
| Dividend/interest earned | Included in corpus, taxed on withdrawal |
| No TDS | TDS is not deducted; you must self-report in ITR |

**Key point:** Tier 2 withdrawals are added to your income for the year and taxed at your applicable slab rate. There is no long-term capital gains benefit like mutual funds.

## Fees and Charges

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Tier 2 account opening | Free |
| CRA annual maintenance | ₹50–₹75 per year (combined for Tier 1 + 2) |
| PFM investment management fee | 0.01%–0.09% of AUM |
| Withdrawal processing | Free |
| Transaction charges | ₹3.75 per transaction (approx) |

> The fund management charges are among the **lowest in India** — significantly cheaper than mutual funds (which charge 0.5%–2.5%).

## When Should You Use NPS Tier 2?

✅ **Good for:**
- Parking surplus money with professional fund management at ultra-low cost
- Medium-term goals (3–5 years) where you want equity exposure without lock-in
- As an alternative to liquid/debt mutual funds with lower expense ratios

❌ **Not ideal for:**
- Retirement savings (use Tier 1 for tax benefits and discipline)
- Emergency fund (bank savings account is more liquid)
- Short-term needs (3–4 day withdrawal time vs instant in bank)

## Important Tips

1. **Tier 2 is NOT a replacement for Tier 1** — you must maintain Tier 1 to keep Tier 2 active. If Tier 1 is frozen, Tier 2 stops too.
2. **No tax benefit for most subscribers** — don't invest in Tier 2 expecting tax deductions (only central govt employees get 80CCD benefit on Tier 2)
3. **Compare with direct mutual funds** — while NPS Tier 2 has lower fees, mutual funds offer better tax treatment (LTCG exemption up to ₹1.25 lakh) and instant redemption
4. **You can choose different PFMs for Tier 1 and Tier 2** — optimize by selecting best-performing PFM for each
5. **Maintain Tier 1 minimum contribution** — if your Tier 1 is frozen due to non-contribution (₹1,000/year minimum), your Tier 2 will also be affected

## FAQs

### Q1. Can I open Tier 2 without Tier 1?
No. An active NPS Tier 1 account is mandatory to open and maintain a Tier 2 account.

### Q2. Is there any lock-in for NPS Tier 2?
No lock-in for most subscribers. Central government employees who claim tax benefits on Tier 2 face a 3-year lock-in.

### Q3. What is the minimum balance in NPS Tier 2?
There is no minimum balance requirement. You can withdraw the entire amount. However, if the balance becomes zero and no contribution is made, the account may become dormant.

### Q4. Can I transfer money from Tier 2 to Tier 1?
No. You cannot transfer between Tier 1 and Tier 2. They are separate accounts with separate withdrawal rules.

### Q5. What returns can I expect from NPS Tier 2?
Returns depend on your asset allocation. Historically, NPS equity (E) funds have delivered 10–14% CAGR, corporate bonds (C) 8–10%, and government securities (G) 7–9%. Past performance doesn't guarantee future returns.

### Q6. Is NPS Tier 2 better than FD?
NPS Tier 2 offers potentially higher returns (especially with equity allocation) and lower charges. However, it carries market risk and withdrawals take 3–4 days. FDs offer guaranteed returns and immediate liquidity. Choose based on your risk appetite.

### Q7. How do I check my Tier 2 balance?
Log in to [CRA portal](https://www.npscra.nsdl.co.in/) with your PRAN or use the NSDL NPS mobile app. Your Tier 1 and Tier 2 balances are shown separately.
