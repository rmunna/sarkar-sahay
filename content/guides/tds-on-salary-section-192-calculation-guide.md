# TDS on Salary (Section 192) India 2026 — How It's Calculated, How to Reduce It, Form 16

If you're salaried, your employer deducts **TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)** on your salary every month under **Section 192** of the Income Tax Act. This is not optional for either side — employers who don't deduct TDS face penalties.

Understanding how TDS on salary is calculated helps you:
- Submit correct investment declarations to minimize monthly TDS
- Verify your Form 16 is accurate
- Avoid surprises when filing your ITR

## How Section 192 TDS Works

The employer is responsible for calculating and depositing your income tax with the government on your behalf, spread across the financial year.

**Calculation approach**:
1. Estimate total annual income (salary + all components)
2. Apply applicable deductions (HRA, investments declared, professional tax, etc.)
3. Calculate total tax for the year
4. Divide by 12 = monthly TDS amount
5. Deduct that amount from each month's salary

The tax is deposited by the 7th of the following month. The accumulated TDS is reported to you in **Form 16** by June 15 of the assessment year.

## Step-by-Step TDS Calculation

### Step 1: Total Gross Salary

All components are included:
- Basic salary
- HRA (before HRA exemption)
- Conveyance, medical, special allowance
- Bonus, incentive (as and when paid — reshuffles the monthly TDS)
- Value of perquisites (company car, ESOPs, etc.)

### Step 2: Exempt Allowances

Deduct these from gross salary:
- **HRA exemption**: Minimum of (actual HRA, 50%/40% of basic, rent paid minus 10% of basic)
- **Leave travel allowance (LTA)**: Exempt twice in a 4-year block for actual travel costs
- **Standard Deduction**: ₹75,000 for all salaried employees under new regime; ₹50,000 under old regime
- **Professional Tax**: Deducted from salary (up to ₹2,400/year)

### Step 3: Add Employer Declared Perquisites

- Company car (₹1,800–₹2,400/month perquisite value)
- Rent-free accommodation (15% of salary as perquisite)
- ESOPs exercised during the year (FMV − Strike price as perquisite)
- Education reimbursement above ₹100/month per child

### Step 4: Net Salary Income After Step 2 and 3

This is your **Salary Head income** for the year.

### Step 5: Deductions Based on Investment Declaration (Form 12BB)

Under **old regime**, reduce salary income by:
- **80C**: Up to ₹1,50,000 (PPF, ELSS, life insurance premium, children's tuition, home loan principal)
- **80D**: Up to ₹25,000 self/family health insurance; ₹50,000 if parents are senior citizens
- **80CCD(1B)**: Up to ₹50,000 NPS contribution
- **80G**: Donation to approved charities
- **Home loan interest (Section 24b)**: Up to ₹2,00,000 for self-occupied property
- **HRA exemption** (if not already excluded from gross)

Under **new regime**: No deductions except Standard Deduction ₹75,000.

### Step 6: Calculate Tax on Net Income

Apply slab rates to the net income after all deductions:

**New regime slabs (FY 2025-26)**:
| Income Slab | Rate |
|-------------|------|
| Up to ₹3,00,000 | 0% |
| ₹3L–₹7L | 5% |
| ₹7L–₹10L | 10% |
| ₹10L–₹12L | 15% |
| ₹12L–₹15L | 20% |
| Above ₹15L | 30% |

(Plus 4% cess on tax, plus surcharge for income above ₹50L)

### Step 7: Divide by 12 for Monthly TDS

Tax calculated above ÷ 12 = monthly TDS.

**Adjustment throughout the year**: When bonus is paid (increases income), or when December investments are submitted (reduces income), the employer recalculates and adjusts TDS in subsequent months.

## Form 12BB — Investment Declaration to Reduce TDS

Form 12BB is the declaration you submit to your employer listing:
- HRA rent payment details (address of rented accommodation, rent amount, landlord's PAN if >₹1L/year)
- LTA claims and travel details
- Section 80C investments (PPF statements, ELSS proofs, insurance receipts)
- Section 80D health insurance premiums
- Home loan interest certificate (from bank)
- NPS contributions under 80CCD(1B)

**When to submit**: Typically April (beginning of financial year) for the full year estimate, then actual proofs in January–March.

**If you don't submit Form 12BB**: Your employer deducts TDS without any deductions — maximum possible TDS. You get refund when filing ITR, but you've given the government a free loan throughout the year.

## Old Regime vs New Regime Declaration to Employer

From FY 2023-24, employees must declare to their employer whether they want **old or new regime**:
- **New regime is default**: If you don't declare anything, employer applies new regime
- To claim old regime benefits (80C, HRA, home loan interest): Explicitly inform HR/payroll

You can switch regime when filing your ITR (if the employer applied the wrong one), but this causes a mismatch and may require you to pay/claim refund when filing.

## What Form 16 Contains

Form 16 has two parts:

**Part A**: 
- Employer's TAN
- Your PAN
- Total salary paid to you
- Total TDS deducted, month-by-month
- TDS deposited with government

**Part B** (detailed):
- Gross salary breakdown (all components)
- Exempt allowances (HRA, LTA)
- Perquisites value
- Standard deduction
- Chapter VI-A deductions (80C, 80D, etc.)
- Net taxable income
- Tax calculated and TDS deducted

**Form 16 must be issued by June 15** after the financial year ends. If your employer doesn't give it, you can get TDS credit from Form 26AS and file ITR without Form 16 (using payslips for income details).

## Common TDS on Salary Problems

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Too much TDS deducted | No investment declaration submitted | Submit Form 12BB; get refund at ITR filing |
| TDS mismatch between Form 16 and AIS | Employer filed incorrect TDS return | Ask employer to file revised Form 24Q |
| Employer hasn't deposited TDS | Employer cash flow issue | TDS shown in Form 16 but not in Form 26AS | Contact employer; report to TRACES if not resolved |
| Perquisite value not included in Form 16 | Payroll error | Get corrected Form 16; affects ITR filing |

## Employer Not Deducting TDS — Risk to You

Even if your employer fails to deduct TDS, **YOU (the employee) are still liable for the tax**. The Income Tax Department will ask you to pay the tax when filing ITR, with interest (234B/234C) if advance tax wasn't paid.

If your employer didn't deduct TDS, you should:
1. Pay advance tax yourself in installments (June 15, Sept 15, Dec 15, March 15)
2. Or at least pay the full tax by March 31 to avoid 234B interest

## Freelancers and Section 192 vs Section 194J

| You Are | TDS Section | Rate |
|---------|-------------|------|
| Salaried employee | Section 192 | At slab rate (variable, not a fixed rate) |
| Freelancer/consultant (technical services) | Section 194J | 10% TDS |
| Freelancer (professional services) | Section 194J | 10% TDS |

Section 192 TDS is personalized to your income — the rate varies based on your income level. Section 194J is a flat 10%.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I joined a company mid-year. My TDS seems very high this month. Why?

When you join mid-year, your employer estimates income for the remaining months of the financial year and calculates annual tax on that. But for the standard deduction (₹75K/₹50K), it's given for the full year even for a partial year of employment. The recalculation may cause a spike in early months. It evens out by March.

### My employer deducted TDS under new regime but I want old regime. What do I do?

Switch in Form 12BB — inform your employer to apply old regime from the next month. For TDS already deducted under new regime, the adjustment is reflected in subsequent months. You can also simply file your ITR under old regime (if old regime is better) and claim the difference back as refund — even if employer deducted under new regime.

### My company went bankrupt mid-year and didn't deposit TDS they deducted. Do I get credit?

No — the IT Department's TRACES system shows only TDS actually deposited by the employer. If TDS was deducted from your salary but not deposited, it won't show in Form 26AS and you cannot claim it as credit. You'd need to pay that tax again and sue the employer for the deducted amount. This is a legal remedy — contact a lawyer and the appropriate labor/company law authorities.
