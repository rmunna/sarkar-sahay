# Section 80G Donation Tax Deduction 2026 — Complete Guide

Donations to government funds, temples, hospitals, universities, and approved NGOs can be claimed as a deduction from your taxable income under **Section 80G** of the Income Tax Act. The deduction can be **100%** or **50%** of the donated amount, with or without a cap.

> **Key rule changed in 2021**: The NGO/fund must now submit a statement to the IT Dept (Form 10BD), and donors receive **Form 10BE** as the donation certificate. Without Form 10BE, your 80G claim may be rejected.

## Four Categories of 80G Deductions

| Category | Deduction | Cap |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| **A** | 100% of donation | No cap |
| **B** | 50% of donation | No cap |
| **C** | 100% of donation | Up to 10% of Adjusted Gross Total Income (AGTI) |
| **D** | 50% of donation | Up to 10% of AGTI |

**AGTI (Adjusted Gross Total Income)**: Your gross total income minus long-term capital gains, short-term capital gains (Section 111A), income for minors, and deductions under Chapter VIA other than 80G.

The cap of 10% AGTI applies only to Categories C and D. For A and B, you can donate any amount and claim the full deduction without a cap.

## The ₹2,000 Cash Donation Limit

**Cash donations above ₹2,000 are NOT eligible for 80G deduction**.

| Mode | Maximum donation eligible |
|------|--------------------------|
| Cash | ₹2,000 per donation |
| Cheque / Bank transfer / NEFT / UPI | No limit |
| Online payment (debit card, credit card) | No limit |

**This is not optional** — even if the NGO gives you a receipt for a ₹50,000 cash donation, you cannot claim 80G on it. The deduction is disallowed.

## Form 10BE — The New Mandatory Certificate

From FY 2021-22 onwards, simply having a receipt from the NGO is not enough. The NGO must:
1. File **Form 10BD** (Donation Statement) with the IT Department
2. Issue you **Form 10BE** (Donation Certificate) based on the 10BD filing

**Without Form 10BE, your 80G deduction can be rejected.**

Where to get Form 10BE:
- The NGO sends it by email, or
- Download from incometax.gov.in → Login → e-File → Income Tax Forms → View Form 10BE

**If the NGO hasn't filed 10BD**: Your 80G claim is at risk. Ask the NGO for their 10BE certificate. If they say they haven't filed 10BD, your deduction may be denied.

## How to Claim 80G in ITR

1. Collect **Form 10BE** from each NGO/fund you donated to
2. In your ITR, go to **Schedule 80G** (Deductions under Chapter VIA)
3. For each donation, enter:
   - Name of donee (NGO/fund)
   - PAN of donee
   - Type (A/B/C/D — 100% or 50%, with or without cap)
   - Amount donated
   - Eligible deduction (automatically calculated)
4. The total deduction is subtracted from your taxable income

**ITR-1**: Has a simplified 80G section for Category A/B donations.
**ITR-2 and ITR-3**: Full Schedule 80G for all categories.

## Calculating Your Actual Tax Benefit

**Example**:
- Gross taxable income: ₹12,00,000
- Donated ₹1,00,000 to an approved NGO (50% with 10% AGTI cap)
- AGTI: ~₹11,00,000 (simplified)
- 10% of AGTI = ₹1,10,000 → Cap is ₹1,10,000 (donation ₹1L is within cap)
- 80G deduction: 50% × ₹1,00,000 = **₹50,000**
- Taxable income after 80G: ₹12,00,000 − ₹50,000 = **₹11,50,000**
- Tax saving (at 20% slab): ₹10,000

**Example with PM CARES (100%, no cap)**:
- Donated ₹5,00,000 to PM CARES Fund
- Deduction: 100% × ₹5,00,000 = **₹5,00,000** (no AGTI cap)
- Tax saving (at 30% slab): ₹1,50,000

## Corporate Donations — CSR vs 80G

For companies donating under their **CSR obligation** (Section 135), the donation is:
- Deductible as a business expense under Section 37 (not under 80G)
- **NOT deductible under 80G** for amounts mandatorily spent under CSR

For **voluntary donations** by companies beyond CSR requirements: 80G is available.

## How to Verify If an NGO Is 80G Approved

Check the IT Department's NGO portal:
- incometax.gov.in → Quick Links → Verify Form 10BD/10BE or search for the institution
- The NGO's approval should show as "active" under Section 80G

An NGO's 80G approval needs periodic renewal (now every 5 years under new rules). Expired approvals mean your donation may not qualify.

## 80G vs 80GGA vs 80GGC — Other Donation Sections

| Section | For | Deduction |
|---------|-----|-----------|
| **80G** | Charitable trusts, government funds | 50% or 100% |
| **80GGA** | Scientific research / rural development (only for non-business income taxpayers) | 100% |
| **80GGC** | Donations to political parties | 100% (non-business taxpayers) |
| **80GGB** | Corporate donations to political parties | 100% |

Individuals can claim 80GGA for donations to research institutions and rural development. Salaried employees (ITR-1/ITR-2) can claim 80GGA.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I donated through a crowdfunding platform for a medical emergency. Is it eligible for 80G?

Only if the crowdfunding platform is itself a registered charity with 80G approval AND the specific campaign/fund has approval. Personal crowdfunding campaigns for an individual's medical expenses are generally NOT 80G eligible — you're donating to a specific person, not a charitable institution.

### My employer deducted my charitable donation from salary and sent it directly. Can I claim 80G?

Yes — if your employer deducted from salary and donated to an approved 80G institution on your behalf, you can claim 80G. Your employer should provide you a Form 10BE or certificate. This deduction should ideally be reflected in your Form 16 Part B.

### I donated ₹1 lakh in cash to my temple. Can I claim 80G?

Only ₹2,000 of the cash donation is eligible. Even if the temple is an approved 80G institution (many are, in Category C or D), the cash amount above ₹2,000 is disqualified. You'd get 80G benefit only on ₹2,000, not on the full ₹1 lakh.

### Is a donation to a foreign NGO eligible for 80G?

No — Section 80G only covers donations to institutions registered and approved in India. Donations to foreign organizations (Oxfam international, Red Cross, etc.) are not eligible for 80G, even if the India affiliate is registered.
