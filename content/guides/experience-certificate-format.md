# Experience Certificate — Format, Sample, and How to Get One

An experience certificate is one of the most important documents in your professional life. Whether you are switching jobs, applying for higher education, or appearing for a government recruitment process, this certificate serves as formal proof of your work history. This guide covers everything you need to know — what it is, the standard format, a ready-to-use sample, and how to get one from your employer.

## What Is an Experience Certificate?

An experience certificate is an official letter issued by an employer that confirms an individual worked at the organisation for a certain period. It typically includes:

- The employee's full name
- Job title or designation
- Duration of employment (joining and last working date)
- A brief remark on conduct and performance

It is printed on the company's official letterhead, signed by an authorised person (usually from HR), and stamped with the company seal. This document acts as verifiable proof of your professional experience.

## When Do You Need an Experience Certificate?

You may need an experience certificate in several situations:

- **Joining a new job** — Most employers ask for it during the onboarding process to verify your previous employment.
- **Government job applications** — Recruitments by UPSC, SSC, state PSCs, railways, and banks require experience certificates at the document verification stage.
- **Higher education or MBA admissions** — Some programmes ask for proof of work experience.
- **Visa applications** — Certain countries require employment history documentation.
- **Professional licensing** — Fields like law, medicine, and chartered accountancy may require proof of practical experience.
- **Loan or credit applications** — Banks may request employment proof for processing loans.

## Key Details in an Experience Certificate

A properly drafted experience certificate should contain the following elements:

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Company letterhead** | Official name, logo, and address of the employer |
| **Certificate number/reference** | A unique reference number for record-keeping |
| **Date of issue** | The date the certificate is issued |
| **Employee's full name** | As per official records |
| **Designation** | Job title held during employment |
| **Department** | The department or team (if applicable) |
| **Date of joining** | When the employee started |
| **Last working day** | When the employee was relieved |
| **Performance remark** | A brief, positive note on conduct and performance |
| **Signatory details** | Name, designation, and signature of the authorised person |
| **Company seal/stamp** | Official stamp of the organisation |

## Standard Format and Sample Template

Below is a standard experience certificate format widely accepted across Indian companies and government bodies.

### Sample Experience Certificate

```
[Company Letterhead]

[Company Name]
[Company Address]
[City, State — PIN Code]
[Phone Number | Email]

Date: [DD/MM/YYYY]
Ref No: [Certificate Number]

TO WHOM IT MAY CONCERN

This is to certify that Mr./Ms. [Full Name of Employee] was employed
with [Company Name] as [Designation] in the [Department Name]
department from [Date of Joining] to [Last Working Date].

During his/her tenure with us, Mr./Ms. [Last Name] demonstrated
sincerity, dedication, and a professional approach to work. His/her
conduct and performance were found to be satisfactory/good/excellent.

We wish him/her all the best in future endeavours.

This certificate is issued upon request for whatever purpose it may serve.

Authorised Signatory:

[Name]
[Designation]
[Company Name]
[Company Seal/Stamp]
```

> **Tip:** Customise the performance remark based on actual feedback. Avoid vague or generic language if you are drafting this for someone.

## How to Request an Experience Certificate from Your Employer

Follow these steps to request your experience certificate smoothly:

1. **Submit a formal request** — Write a polite email or letter addressed to your HR department or reporting manager. Mention your employee ID, designation, department, and dates of employment.

2. **Time your request** — Ideally, request the certificate during your notice period or on your last working day. Many companies issue it along with the relieving letter and final settlement documents.

3. **Follow up** — If you do not receive it within 7–15 days of your last working day, send a polite follow-up email. Keep all communication in writing.

4. **Collect and verify** — When you receive the certificate, verify that all details (name, dates, designation) are accurate. Request corrections immediately if anything is wrong.

### Sample Request Email

```
Subject: Request for Experience Certificate — [Your Name], [Employee ID]

Dear [HR Manager's Name],

I am writing to request my experience certificate. My last working day
with [Company Name] was [Date]. Below are my details for your reference:

  Full Name: [Your Name]
  Employee ID: [ID]
  Designation: [Your Designation]
  Department: [Department]
  Date of Joining: [DD/MM/YYYY]
  Last Working Day: [DD/MM/YYYY]

Kindly issue the experience certificate at the earliest. I am happy to
complete any pending formalities required for the same.

Thank you.

Regards,
[Your Name]
[Contact Number]
```

## What If Your Employer Refuses to Issue an Experience Certificate?

Unfortunately, some employers delay or refuse to issue experience certificates. Here is what you can do:

- **Send a written request** — Always put your request in writing (email is best) so you have documented proof.
- **Escalate internally** — If HR is unresponsive, escalate to a senior manager or the company's grievance cell.
- **File a complaint with the Labour Commissioner** — Under Indian labour laws, you can approach the local Labour Commissioner's office and file a written complaint against the employer. The office can mediate and direct the employer to issue the certificate.
- **Send a legal notice** — If all else fails, consult a labour lawyer and send a legal notice to the employer demanding the certificate.
- **Use alternative proof** — In the interim, gather supporting documents such as your appointment letter, payslips, Form 16, PF statements (from EPFO), and bank salary credit statements to establish proof of employment.

## Experience Certificate vs Relieving Letter

These two documents are often confused. Here is the difference:

| Aspect | Experience Certificate | Relieving Letter |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Confirms work experience, role, and performance | Confirms the employee has been formally relieved |
| **When issued** | On or after the last working day | On the last working day |
| **Content** | Name, designation, tenure, performance remark | Acceptance of resignation, last working date |
| **Used for** | New job applications, government jobs, education | Proving you left the previous job on good terms |
| **Mandatory?** | Not legally mandated but widely expected | Not legally mandated but widely expected |

> **Note:** Some small companies issue a single combined letter. If you need separate documents, specify that in your request.

## Important Tips

- **Request early** — Do not wait months after leaving. The sooner you request, the easier it is to get.
- **Keep copies** — Store digital and physical copies. Scan and save a PDF.
- **Check accuracy** — Verify every detail before accepting. Errors in dates or designation can cause problems later.
- **Get it on letterhead** — A certificate without the company letterhead or seal may not be accepted by future employers or government bodies.
- **Maintain professionalism** — Even if your exit was difficult, keep your request polite and professional. It increases the likelihood of a timely response.
- **Know your rights** — Indian labour laws support an employee's right to documentation of employment. Do not hesitate to escalate if necessary.
