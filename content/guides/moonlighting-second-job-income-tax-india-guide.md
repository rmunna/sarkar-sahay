# Moonlighting Tax India 2026 — Declaring Second Income in ITR

You have a full-time job. You also do weekend consulting, freelance design, or a side business. Your employer deducts TDS on salary — but that TDS covers **only** your salary income. The additional income from moonlighting is separate and must be declared.

Here's how to handle the tax.

## The Core Rule

**Income from any source = taxable**. Whether it's your regular employer, a freelance client, or a side project:
- Employer's TDS covers your salary income (Form 16 captures this)
- Any other income — freelance, consulting, tuition, content creation, part-time work — is YOUR responsibility to declare and pay tax on

**Your employer doesn't know about your side income**. The IT Department, however, can see your bank deposits, TDS credits from clients (if any), and AIS data.

## What ITR Form to Use

| Income Situation | ITR Form |
|-----------------|---------|
| Salary only (income ≤ ₹50L, no capital gains) | ITR-1 |
| Salary + capital gains (any amount) | ITR-2 |
| Salary + freelance/consulting income (any amount) | **ITR-3** (regular) or **ITR-4** (if using Section 44ADA/44AD presumptive) |
| Salary + business income | ITR-3 |

**Important**: Once you have freelance/professional income, you cannot use ITR-1 or ITR-2. You must use ITR-3 or ITR-4.

## Declaring Freelance Income in ITR

### Option 1: Section 44ADA (Professionals — 50% Presumptive)

If you're a doctor, CA, lawyer, engineer, IT consultant, architect, or other specified professional:
- Declare **50% of gross receipts** as income (no need to track actual expenses)
- Use **ITR-4 (Sugam)** — simpler filing

**Example**: ₹5L in freelance consulting receipts
- Declared income under 44ADA: ₹2.5L
- Add this ₹2.5L to your salary income for total tax calculation

### Option 2: Actual Income Minus Expenses (Regular)

Track your actual expenses and declare the net profit:
- Equipment, software subscriptions, internet, travel to client sites — all deductible
- Use **ITR-3**

**Example**: ₹5L receipts, ₹1.5L expenses
- Net income declared: ₹3.5L
- This gives lower tax than 44ADA if your margin is below 50%

Choose the option that gives lower income declaration — which depends on your actual expense ratio.

## Combined Tax Calculation

**Salaried + Freelance Example (FY 2025-26, New Regime)**:

| Income Source | Amount |
|--------------|--------|
| Salary (gross) | ₹12,00,000 |
| Standard deduction (new regime) | − ₹75,000 |
| Taxable salary | ₹11,25,000 |
| Freelance receipts | ₹4,00,000 |
| Declared under 44ADA (50%) | ₹2,00,000 |
| **Total taxable income** | **₹13,25,000** |

Tax (new regime slabs):
- ₹0–₹4L: 0%
- ₹4L–₹8L: 5% = ₹20,000
- ₹8L–₹12L: 10% = ₹40,000
- ₹12L–₹13.25L: 15% = ₹18,750
- **Total tax**: ₹78,750

Employer's TDS (on salary only): ~₹48,750
**Additional tax to pay**: ~₹30,000

Since additional tax > ₹10,000: Must pay **advance tax** (by March 15 of FY).

## Advance Tax for Moonlighters

If your additional tax liability (beyond employer's TDS) exceeds ₹10,000:
- Pay **advance tax** before year-end
- For freelancers under Section 44ADA: Only **one installment due by March 15** (simplified)
- If not paid by March 15, pay as **self-assessment tax** with 1%/month interest (Section 234B/C)

**Practical approach**: Most moonlighters simply pay in March. The interest is modest (1%/month on the shortfall × few months). But if you earn year-round, it's better to pay quarterly.

## Does Your Employer Need to Know?

**Tax**: Your employer doesn't need to know about side income. Tax is your personal obligation — you declare all sources in ITR, which your employer never sees.

**Employment Contract**: Separately — many employers in India prohibit moonlighting (especially for same-sector work). Check your employment agreement. This is a legal/contractual matter, not a tax one. Tax-wise, income is income regardless.

**ESOP / Conflict of Interest**: If your freelance work competes with your employer or involves confidential information, consult a lawyer. Tax declaration doesn't affect this risk.

## TDS from Freelance Clients

If a client pays you more than ₹30,000 in a financial year for professional services:
- Client deducts **10% TDS** under Section 194J
- You receive 90% cash
- The 10% TDS appears in Form 26AS
- Claim this TDS credit in ITR against your total tax liability

Even if a client doesn't deduct TDS (smaller amounts, or a client who doesn't comply), YOU must declare the income and pay tax.

## GST Consideration for Freelancers

If your annual freelance receipts exceed **₹20L** (₹10L for special category states):
- You must register for **GST**
- Charge 18% GST to clients
- File monthly/quarterly GST returns

This is separate from income tax — both GST registration AND income tax compliance are required once you cross the GST threshold.

For foreign clients (export of services), GST applies at 0% (zero-rated), but GST registration may still be required.

## Freelance Income Received from Foreign Client (Remittance)

If you're providing services to a foreign company:
- Income is taxable in India (Indian resident's worldwide income is taxable)
- Foreign currency remittance shows up in bank account and potentially in AIS
- No automatic TDS (foreign client doesn't know Indian TDS rules)
- You declare the full amount in ITR under business/professional income
- May claim Section 44ADA if eligible (50% presumptive)

## Documents to Maintain for Moonlighting

Keep for potential scrutiny:
1. Invoices raised to each client
2. Payment receipts / bank statements showing credits
3. Contracts or agreements with clients
4. Expense receipts (if claiming actual expenses in ITR-3)
5. TDS certificates (Form 16A) from clients who deducted TDS

You don't need to share these with the IT Department proactively — but keep them 6–7 years.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I earn ₹3L from tutoring students on weekends. My main salary is ₹8L. Do I need to declare the tutoring income?

Yes — all income is taxable. Tutoring is "professional income" (if you're a qualified teacher) or "income from other sources" (if hobby/informal). Add ₹3L to your ₹8L salary for total tax. Use ITR-4 if tutoring > ₹1L and you want Section 44ADA; otherwise include in ITR-2 as income from other sources.

### I write content for US clients and get paid in USD via PayPal. Is this taxable in India?

Yes — as an Indian resident, your worldwide income is taxable. Dollar income received in India = taxable professional/business income. Convert to INR at the exchange rate on the date of receipt. If you're exporting services (clients are foreign), GST is zero-rated but income tax applies normally.

### My company policy prohibits moonlighting. Will my employer find out if I declare side income in ITR?

Your ITR is private — employers don't have access to your income tax returns. The Income Tax Department's data sharing with employers is limited to Form 16 TDS data. Your secondary income declaration in ITR is not visible to your employer.
