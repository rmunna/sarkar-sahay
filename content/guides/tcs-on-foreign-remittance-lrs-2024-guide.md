# TCS on Foreign Remittances (LRS) India 2026 — 20% Rate, Exemptions, and ITR Credit

**TCS (Tax Collected at Source)** on foreign remittances under the **Liberalized Remittance Scheme (LRS)** applies when you send money abroad. Budget 2023 significantly changed TCS rates — most remittances now attract 20% TCS. Here's the current rate chart and how to recover it.

## TCS Rates on LRS Remittances (Effective October 1, 2023)

| Purpose of Remittance | TCS Rate |
|----------------------|---------|
| **Overseas tour packages** (booked through any travel agent) | **20%** |
| **Remittance for investment abroad** (stocks, property, savings) | **20%** |
| **Gift money sent abroad** | **20%** |
| **Other remittances** (not education/medical/investment) | **20%** |
| **Education abroad — own funds** (not via loan) | **5%** above ₹7 lakh |
| **Education abroad — education loan from financial institution** | **0.5%** above ₹7 lakh |
| **Medical treatment abroad** | **5%** above ₹7 lakh |
| **International credit/debit card spending (transactions up to ₹7L/year)** | **0%** (exempt under LRS threshold) |

## The ₹7 Lakh Threshold

LRS remittances up to **₹7,00,000** per financial year are **exempt from TCS** for most categories.

TCS applies only on the **amount exceeding ₹7 lakh** in a financial year (aggregate across all remittances):

| Total Annual LRS Remittance | TCS Status |
|--------------------------|-----------|
| Up to ₹7,00,000 | ❌ No TCS |
| Above ₹7,00,000 (for most purposes) | ✅ 20% TCS on excess above ₹7L |

**Exception**: Overseas tour packages — TCS applies from the **first rupee** (no ₹7L exemption for tour packages).

## International Credit/Debit Card Spending — What's Covered

This was a major confusion point in 2023:

**Pre-Oct 2023 (old rule)**: International credit card spending was NOT under LRS — effectively TCS-free for foreign card spends.

**Budget 2023 amendment**: International credit card spending was included under LRS, triggering 20% TCS concerns.

**Actual implementation**: The government has given relief:
- International credit/debit card spends up to **₹7 lakh per year** are still exempt from TCS
- Only amounts above ₹7L in card spends + other remittances combined attract TCS
- Most individual travelers don't exceed ₹7L in a year, so effectively no TCS for normal tourists

## Forex Card — TCS at Reload

When you load money onto a **forex card** for foreign travel:
- Up to ₹7L aggregate remittances in the year: No TCS
- If you've already remitted ₹7L+ earlier in the year, additional forex card loading triggers 20% TCS (or 5% for education purpose)

**Example**: You loaded ₹3L on forex card in June, ₹3L in August, and ₹3L in October. Total = ₹9L.
- First ₹7L: No TCS
- Remaining ₹2L: 20% TCS = ₹40,000

## Overseas Tour Package — 20% from First Rupee

If you buy a **holiday package** through any Indian travel agent (MakeMyTrip, SOTC, Thomas Cook, etc.):
- **20% TCS from first rupee** — no ₹7L exemption
- Applies to: flight + hotel packages, group tours, visa assistance included packages

**Example**: You book a ₹2 lakh Bali holiday package.
- TCS: ₹2,00,000 × 20% = **₹40,000** collected upfront by travel agent
- You pay ₹2,40,000 total (₹2L package + ₹40K TCS)

**For self-booking (without package agent)**:
- Booking flights directly = not "tour package" = subject to ₹7L threshold exemption (falls under general LRS)

## Education Abroad — 0.5% or 5% TCS

| How You're Funding | TCS Rate |
|-------------------|---------|
| **Education loan** from RBI-approved financial institution (bank, NBFC) | **0.5%** on amount above ₹7L |
| **Own funds** (savings, parents' funds, personal loan) | **5%** on amount above ₹7L |

**Example**:
- Tuition fee abroad = ₹20 lakh/year via education loan
- Threshold: ₹7L exempt
- TCS on ₹13L: 0.5% = **₹6,500**

This is designed to make education loans more attractive (0.5% TCS vs 5% for self-funding).

## Medical Treatment Abroad — 5% TCS

Remittances for medical treatment abroad (surgery, specialist consultations):
- 5% TCS on amounts above ₹7L
- Must declare medical purpose to the bank/remittance service

## Who Collects TCS?

| Remittance Channel | TCS Collected By |
|-------------------|----------------|
| Forex card/wire transfer through bank | Your **bank** |
| Overseas tour package | **Travel agent** |
| International credit card above threshold | **Card-issuing bank** |

TCS is deposited to the government in your PAN. You see it in **Form 26AS** as TCS credit.

## How to Claim TCS Credit Back

TCS is NOT an additional tax — it's an advance tax credit.

When filing your ITR:
1. The TCS amount appears in your **Form 26AS** (under Part C: Tax Collected at Source)
2. ITR pre-fill automatically picks this up
3. If your actual tax liability is less than TCS collected: **You get a refund**
4. If your tax liability is more: TCS reduces your additional payment

**Example**:
- TCS deducted on forex card and tour package: ₹60,000
- Your total income tax after all deductions: ₹45,000
- Refund: ₹15,000

The 20% TCS is high specifically because the government wants to track high LRS remittances — taxpayers who declare income honestly get it back as refund.

## What Is LRS?

Under RBI's **Liberalized Remittance Scheme**, resident Indians can send up to **USD 2,50,000** (approximately ₹2 crore) per financial year abroad for any permitted current or capital account transaction. LRS covers:
- Foreign education
- Medical treatment abroad
- Holiday abroad
- Gift/donations to foreign recipients
- Investment in foreign stocks/property

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I booked flights directly (not through a tour package). Is 20% TCS applicable?

Booking flights/hotels directly (without a bundled tour package) is a regular LRS remittance subject to the ₹7L threshold. No TCS if your total annual LRS remittance stays below ₹7L.

### I paid for Netflix/Amazon Prime in USD via credit card. Is that under LRS/TCS?

Small international subscriptions were historically excluded from LRS. RBI has issued clarifications — spends on international cards are now under LRS, but the ₹7L annual threshold means most individuals are not affected.

### My company sent me abroad on a business trip and I used my personal card. Do I owe TCS?

Business travel reimbursed by your employer may not count as personal LRS. Consult with your employer's accounts team — typically business reimbursements are handled differently from personal LRS.
