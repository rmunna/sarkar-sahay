# Section 80C India 2026 — All Options, Returns, and Lock-In Compared

Section 80C is India's most popular tax deduction — letting you reduce taxable income by up to ₹1,50,000 per financial year. But most people know only 2–3 options. Here's the complete picture of all qualifying investments and expenses.

## The ₹1.5L Cap — How It Works

Section 80C has a **combined limit of ₹1,50,000** across all qualifying instruments. Whether you invest ₹50K in PPF + ₹1L in ELSS, or ₹1.5L entirely in LIC premium — the total deductible is capped at ₹1.5L.

**Available only under old tax regime**. Under the new regime, Section 80C deductions are not applicable.

## All 80C Options — Complete List

### Investments

#### 1. ELSS (Equity Linked Saving Scheme)
- **Type**: Equity mutual fund with 3-year lock-in
- **Returns**: 12–18% historically (market-linked, not guaranteed)
- **Lock-in**: 3 years per SIP installment
- **Tax on maturity**: LTCG at 12.5% above ₹1.25L per year
- **Best for**: Wealth creation + tax saving; younger investors

#### 2. PPF (Public Provident Fund)
- **Type**: Government-backed fixed income
- **Returns**: 7.1% p.a. (revised quarterly, currently 7.1%)
- **Lock-in**: 15 years (extendable in 5-year blocks)
- **Tax on maturity**: Completely tax-free (EEE status — exempt at investment, accumulation, and withdrawal)
- **Best for**: Safe, long-term, tax-free returns

#### 3. NPS (National Pension System) — 80CCD(1)
- **Type**: Market-linked pension fund
- **Returns**: 10–15% (equity portion; mixed with debt)
- **Lock-in**: Until age 60
- **Tax on maturity**: 60% lump sum tax-free; 40% annuity taxable
- **Note**: Within 80C limit as 80CCD(1); additional ₹50K via 80CCD(1B) outside 80C
- **Best for**: Long retirement horizon, market-linked growth

#### 4. EPF (Employees' Provident Fund)
- **Type**: Salary deduction; auto-invested
- **Returns**: 8.15% p.a. (FY 2025-26 rate)
- **Lock-in**: Until age 58 (or job tenure)
- **Tax on maturity**: Tax-free if service > 5 years
- **Note**: Employee EPF contribution is automatically 80C; no additional action needed
- **Best for**: Salaried employees (automatic — don't need to invest separately)

#### 5. NSC (National Savings Certificate)
- **Type**: Post Office fixed income
- **Returns**: 7.7% p.a. (5-year NSC, reviewed quarterly)
- **Lock-in**: 5 years
- **Tax on maturity**: Principal + interest = taxable; but accrued interest each year is also eligible for 80C deduction
- **Best for**: Conservative investors who want higher post-office rates than PPF

#### 6. 5-Year Bank FD (Tax-Saving FD)
- **Type**: Fixed deposit with 5-year lock-in
- **Returns**: 6.5–8.5% (varies by bank; small finance banks may offer more)
- **Lock-in**: Strictly 5 years; CANNOT be broken prematurely
- **Tax on maturity**: Interest is taxable at slab rate (TDS deducted)
- **Best for**: Those who want bank's guarantee + DICGC insurance

#### 7. SCSS (Senior Citizen Savings Scheme)
- **Type**: Post Office / bank scheme for 60+ year olds
- **Returns**: 8.2% p.a. (currently)
- **Lock-in**: 5 years (extendable once by 3 years)
- **Tax on maturity**: Interest is taxable; 80C deduction on principal
- **Who**: Only for 60+ years (or 55+ for VRS retirees)
- **Maximum**: ₹30L total investment cap

#### 8. SSY (Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana)
- **Type**: Post Office scheme for girl children (under 10 years)
- **Returns**: 8.2% p.a. (currently)
- **Lock-in**: Until girl turns 21 (or marriage after 18)
- **Tax on maturity**: Completely tax-free
- **Who**: Only for parents/guardians of girl children below 10 years

#### 9. ULIP (Unit Linked Insurance Plan)
- **Type**: Life insurance + investment
- **Returns**: Market-linked (depends on fund)
- **Lock-in**: 5 years
- **Tax**: Complicated — read our ULIP tax guide
- **Caution**: High charges; most financial planners recommend term + MF over ULIP

#### 10. Life Insurance Premium (LIC / other policies)
- **Type**: Insurance premium payment
- **Returns**: Maturity value (for endowment/money-back policies)
- **Lock-in**: Policy term
- **What qualifies**: Premium for life insurance on self, spouse, or children
- **Condition**: Premium ≤ 10% of sum assured (for post-April 2012 policies)
- **Note**: Premium for your parents' life insurance does NOT qualify for 80C

### Expenses

#### 11. Home Loan Principal Repayment
- **Type**: Principal portion of EMI
- **Returns**: Equity in your home
- **Lock-in**: Effective lock-in (selling within 5 years from possession: 80C benefit reversed)
- **What qualifies**: Principal portion of home loan EMI; stamp duty and registration (one-time, year of payment)
- **No separate investment needed**: Part of your existing EMI automatically qualifies

#### 12. Children's Tuition Fees
- **Type**: School/college fees for children
- **Returns**: Education (no financial return)
- **Who**: For your own children (up to 2 children)
- **What qualifies**: Tuition fees only (NOT development fees, transportation, hostel, donations)
- **For**: Schools, colleges, universities in India (not foreign institutions)

### Other Instruments

#### 13. Post Office Time Deposit (5-year)
- **Type**: Post Office FD equivalent
- **Returns**: 7.5% p.a. (5-year POTD currently)
- **Lock-in**: 5 years
- **Tax**: Interest taxable

#### 14. Infrastructure Bonds (if any active)
- Occasional; government issues long-duration infrastructure bonds with 80C benefit
- Not always available; check for current issuances

#### 15. Subscription to PSU Bonds / NaBFID Bonds
- NABARD, NHB, SIDBI bonds — if issued with 80C benefit
- Check for current issuances; not regularly available

## Which 80C Options Are Best?

**Best for wealth creation**: ELSS (highest returns if you can tolerate 3-year lock-in and market volatility)

**Best for safety**: PPF (government-backed, tax-free, 15-year compounding at 7.1%)

**Best overall combination (working professional below 45)**:
1. ELSS: ₹1,00,000 (equity growth)
2. PPF: ₹50,000 (safe, tax-free)
3. Total: ₹1,50,000 (maxed 80C)

Plus separately: ₹50,000 in NPS under 80CCD(1B) for ₹2L total deduction.

**For senior citizens**: SCSS (8.2%) + NSC + PPF (partial).

**Don't need to do anything extra for EPF**: Your employer automatically deducts 12% of basic salary for EPF — this is already your 80C contribution. If basic salary is ₹12,500+/month, your EPF alone covers ₹18,000+/year. Most salaried people have partial 80C covered automatically via EPF.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My EPF covers ₹60K/year automatically. How much more should I invest?

Calculate: ₹1,50,000 − ₹60,000 (EPF) = ₹90,000 remaining. Invest this ₹90K in ELSS or PPF to maximize 80C. Choose based on your risk tolerance and investment horizon.

### My LIC premium is ₹80,000/year. Do I still need to invest more?

₹1,50,000 cap − ₹80,000 LIC = ₹70,000 more to max 80C. Add ELSS or PPF for the remaining ₹70K. Note: Check if your LIC policy qualifies (premium ≤ 10% of sum assured for post-2012 policies).

### Is term insurance premium 80C eligible?

Yes — term insurance premium (for life insurance on self, spouse, or children) qualifies under Section 80C. It doesn't matter whether it's term or endowment — any life insurance premium qualifies. However, term insurance is purely protection (no maturity) so the "investment" angle is different.
