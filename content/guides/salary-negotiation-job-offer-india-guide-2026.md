# Salary Negotiation India 2026 — Complete Playbook

Most Indians leave significant money on the table because they don't negotiate. Data consistently shows 70–80% of employers expect candidates to counter their first offer. Here's exactly how to negotiate — for new job offers and annual appraisals.

---

## The Core Principle: Always Counter

The first offer is rarely the best offer. Employers build negotiation room into their offers:
- They expect 1–2 rounds of negotiation
- Saying yes immediately signals you don't know your market value
- The worst response to a counter is "no" — which puts you back where you started

**Exception**: Government jobs (fixed pay scales), entry-level roles at MNCs with standardized bands. For all other situations — negotiate.

---

## Part 1: Negotiating a New Job Offer

### Step 1: Know Your Number Before the Interview

Research market compensation:
- **AmbitionBox** ([ambitionbox.com](https://www.ambitionbox.com)): India's best salary database by company and role
- **Glassdoor** ([glassdoor.co.in](https://www.glassdoor.co.in)): Good for MNCs and startups
- **LinkedIn Salary**: For mid-senior roles
- **Levels.fyi** ([levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi)): For tech roles at MNCs and startups
- **Industry connections**: Ask peers and ex-colleagues what they make

**Target range**: Have a specific number, not a vague sense. If market pays ₹18–22L for your role, your target is ₹21–22L.

### Step 2: Let Them Make the First Offer

If asked "what's your expected CTC?":
- Redirect: "I'm open to the right offer — what's the range budgeted for this position?"
- Or: "I'd like to understand the full role before we discuss compensation — can we come back to this?"
- If they insist: Give a range, not a specific: "I'm targeting ₹20–23L based on my research"

**Don't reveal your current salary** — this anchors negotiations unfavorably. In some states, employers legally cannot compel you to share current salary (Karnataka, Telangana have drafted such rules; check your state).

### Step 3: Counter the First Offer

When they make an offer:
1. **Thank them** and express genuine interest in the role
2. **Ask for 24 hours** to review the details
3. **Counter at 10–20% above their offer** (higher if your market research supports it)

**Script for counter-offer**:
> "Thank you for the offer — I'm very excited about this opportunity. Based on my research and the market rate for this role, I was targeting ₹22L. Given my [3 specific value points — years of experience, specific skill, measurable impact], could you move to ₹22L?"

### Step 4: The Components to Negotiate

CTC = Fixed pay + Variable pay + Benefits. Negotiate each:

| Component | What to Push For |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Fixed pay** | Higher base → permanent; affects all future hikes |
| **Variable pay %** | Lower % of variable (variable is not guaranteed) |
| **Joining bonus** | Compensate for loss of unvested ESOPs/bonus at previous employer |
| **ESOPs** | Especially at startups — understand vesting schedule, cliff, exercise price |
| **Notice period buyout** | Ask company to pay your notice period if you're asked to join immediately |
| **Remote work clause** | In offer letter, not verbal assurance |
| **Increment date** | If you join in September, next increment may be 16 months away — negotiate a 6-month review |

### Step 5: Use Competing Offers as Leverage

If you have competing offers:
> "I have another offer at ₹23L. I prefer your company because [genuine reason]. If you can match ₹22.5L, I can commit by Friday."

This is powerful and is the strongest negotiating position (BATNA — Best Alternative to No Agreement).

## Part 2: Annual Appraisal Negotiation

### Typical Appraisal Cycles in India

- January–March (most IT companies)
- April (financial year end companies)
- Random anniversary date (some companies)

Know your company's cycle and start preparation 3 months before.

### Step 1: Build Your Case (3 Months Before Appraisal)

**Track your accomplishments**:
- Specific deliveries with measurable impact: "Delivered X feature that reduced support tickets by 30%"
- Revenue impact: "Acquired 15 enterprise clients, contributing ₹2Cr ARR"
- Team contributions: "Mentored 3 juniors; 2 promoted this year"
- Savings: "Optimized infra cost from ₹10L/month to ₹7L/month"

**Keep a "wins" document** — update it monthly. Memory is unreliable at appraisal time.

### Step 2: Research Market Rate Annually

Your value in the market increases each year with experience. Research your current market rate every year:
- What would a company pay to hire someone with your exact experience today?
- This is your baseline for negotiation

If your current CTC is ₹18L and market rate is ₹24L — you're being underpaid 33%. That's your leverage.

### Step 3: The Appraisal Conversation

**What NOT to say**:
- "I have personal expenses" — irrelevant to your employer
- "My colleague gets more" — may be false, also risky
- "I've been here X years" — tenure alone isn't a valid argument

**What TO say**:
- "Based on my market research, professionals with my experience and skills are at ₹24–26L. My current ₹18L is significantly below market."
- "This year I delivered [accomplishment 1], [accomplishment 2], [accomplishment 3] which had [measurable impact]. I'm requesting a hike that brings me closer to market value."
- "I want to continue growing with this company — I need to understand the path to ₹24L over the next 12–18 months."

### Step 4: Typical Hike Ranges in India (2026)

| Situation | Typical Hike |
|-----------|-------------|
| Average performer, same role | 8–15% |
| Good performer, same role | 12–18% |
| Promotion to next level | 15–30% |
| Market-correction hike (underpaid) | 20–40% |
| Job change | 30–50% (industry benchmark) |

**The hard truth**: Job change typically yields 30–50% hike; internal appraisal typically 8–18%. If you're significantly below market, the fastest path to market rate is often a job change.

### Step 5: Countering a Low Offer at Appraisal

If offered lower than expected:
1. Ask for the reasoning: "How was this hike calculated?"
2. Present your market research and accomplishments
3. Request a reconsideration: "I appreciate the offer. Given my market research showing ₹24L for my role, and the [specific accomplishments] this year, I'd like to request ₹21L as a baseline correction."
4. If still no: "Can we agree to a 6-month review with a defined increment if I meet [specific measurable goals]?"

## Decoding CTC vs Take-Home

| Component | Notes |
|-----------|-------|
| CTC (Cost to Company) | Employer's total cost — includes PF contribution, gratuity, insurance |
| Gross Salary | Monthly before TDS |
| Take-home (In-hand) | After TDS deduction |
| PF deduction | 12% of basic salary (your contribution + employer contribution = both in CTC but your share is deducted from salary) |
| Gratuity | Funded by employer; part of CTC but you receive only after 5 years of service |

**Ask for the salary slip format** when evaluating offers — a ₹24L CTC may give very different take-home depending on how the structure is split (basic vs allowances vs variable).

**Optimal structure**: Higher allowances (HRA for city employees, LTA, food coupons) → lower taxable income → higher take-home.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My employer says "no salary discussion before performance review." What do I do?

Ask for a calendar invite for the review and ensure your documented accomplishments are shared with your manager in writing before the meeting. Show, don't just tell.

### They gave me 10% but I asked for 20%. Should I accept?

Counter once more: "I appreciate the offer. Can we bridge this to 15%?" Getting 15% is better than accepting 10% with one counter. Worst they say is no.

### I have a competing offer but like my current company. How do I use it?

Be honest: "I have an offer at ₹24L. My preference is to stay — I've built strong relationships and believe in the product. If you can match ₹22L, I'd like to continue." This is the most powerful single-sentence negotiation tool.

### Is it professional to negotiate?

Completely — negotiation is expected, not rude. Hiring managers and HRBPs are accustomed to it. A polite, well-researched counter-offer is respected, not frowned upon.
