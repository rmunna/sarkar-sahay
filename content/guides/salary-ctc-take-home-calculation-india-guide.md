# CTC to Take-Home Salary India 2026 — The Complete Breakdown

You got an offer letter saying "₹12 Lakh CTC." But your bank account shows ₹78,000 a month — not ₹1,00,000. Why the gap?

Because **CTC ≠ Salary in Hand**. CTC (Cost to Company) is the total cost the employer bears for you, including components you never see in your bank account.

## What Is CTC?

**CTC = All cash components + Non-cash benefits + Employer-borne obligations**

It includes things like:
- Your basic salary and allowances
- Employer's contribution to your EPF (12% of basic)
- Gratuity provision (4.81% of basic)
- Medical insurance premium (if company-paid)
- Any other perquisites

You pay tax, EPFO contributions, and professional tax — the employer pays their EPF share and gratuity.

## Typical CTC Breakup (₹12L CTC Example)

| Component | Annual (₹) | Monthly (₹) | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|------------|-------|
| **Basic Salary** | 4,80,000 | 40,000 | Usually 40-50% of CTC |
| **House Rent Allowance (HRA)** | 2,40,000 | 20,000 | Usually 40-50% of Basic |
| **Special Allowance** | 1,92,000 | 16,000 | Flexible component |
| **Leave Travel Allowance (LTA)** | 40,000 | 3,333 | Tax-exempt up to actual travel cost |
| **Medical Allowance** | 15,000 | 1,250 | Taxable (old medical allowance exemption removed) |
| **Employer EPF Contribution** | 57,600 | 4,800 | 12% of Basic — NOT in your hand |
| **Gratuity Provision** | 23,077 | 1,923 | 4.81% of Basic — accrued but not monthly |
| **Group Medical Insurance** | 12,000 | 1,000 | Company pays premium |
| **Total CTC** | **11,99,677** | **~₹1,00,000** | |

**Your gross salary (what you receive before deductions)**: ₹9,67,000 (Basic + HRA + Special + LTA + Medical Allowance)

**Not in your salary slip**: Employer EPF, Gratuity, Insurance = ₹92,677/year

## Deductions From Your Gross Salary

| Deduction | How Calculated | Monthly (₹) |
|-----------|---------------|------------|
| **Employee EPF** | 12% of Basic (₹40,000) | 4,800 |
| **Professional Tax** | State-specific; typically ₹150–₹200/month | 200 |
| **Income Tax TDS** | Calculated on taxable income / 12 | ~4,500–12,000 (depends on regime + deductions) |
| **Group Medical Insurance** (sometimes) | Employee share of premium | 0–2,000 |

## What You Actually Get (Take-Home)

Using the ₹12L CTC example, assuming new tax regime and no other deductions:

**Gross Monthly Salary**: ₹80,583
(CTC monthly ₹1,00,000 minus employer EPF ₹4,800 and gratuity provision ₹1,923 that don't appear in your slip)

**Deductions**:
- Employee EPF: ₹4,800
- Professional Tax: ₹200
- Income Tax TDS: ~₹6,800 (new regime; calculated below)
- **Total deductions**: ~₹11,800

**Take-Home**: ₹80,583 − ₹11,800 = **~₹68,783/month**

Not ₹1,00,000. About 68% of the headline CTC number.

## Income Tax Calculation on ₹12L CTC (New Regime)

Annual gross salary: ₹9,67,000
Standard deduction (new regime): ₹75,000
Taxable income: ₹8,92,000

New regime tax slabs (FY 2025-26):
- ₹0–₹4L: 0% = ₹0
- ₹4L–₹8L: 5% = ₹20,000
- ₹8L–₹8.92L: 10% = ₹9,200
- **Total tax**: ₹29,200/year
- **Monthly TDS**: ₹2,433

With 87A rebate: If taxable income ≤ ₹7L (it's ₹8.92L here), no rebate applies. Full ₹29,200 is payable.

Wait — ₹8.92L taxable income under new regime:
- ₹4L × 0% = ₹0
- ₹4L–₹8L at 5% = ₹20,000
- ₹8L–₹8.92L at 10% = ₹9,200
- Total: ₹29,200

Take-home is more like **₹72,000/month** (correcting the earlier estimate).

## HRA Exemption — For Renters Under Old Regime

If you pay rent and are using the old tax regime, HRA exemption reduces taxable salary:

HRA exempt = Minimum of:
1. Actual HRA received (₹20,000/month)
2. 50% of basic salary (metro cities) = 50% × ₹40,000 = ₹20,000
3. Actual rent paid minus 10% of basic = (rent - ₹4,000)

If rent paid = ₹18,000/month:
- Option 3 = ₹18,000 − ₹4,000 = ₹14,000
- Exempt HRA = ₹14,000/month (₹1,68,000/year)
- This reduces taxable salary by ₹1,68,000

**Under the new regime**: HRA exemption is NOT available. Your full HRA is taxable.

## EPF: Where Does Your Money Go?

Monthly EPF deduction: 12% of basic = ₹4,800 (from your salary)
Employer EPF: 12% of basic = ₹4,800 (from their pocket, not your salary)

Of the employer's 12%:
- 8.33% goes to **EPS (Employees' Pension Scheme)** — for pension
- 3.67% goes to your **EPF account**

So your EPF account receives:
- Your contribution: ₹4,800
- Employer EPF portion: ₹1,468 (3.67% of ₹40K)
- **Total EPF credit per month**: ₹6,268

EPF earns 8.15% interest per year (FY 2025-26 declared rate).

Your EPS (pension) account gets ₹3,332/month — this builds your pension entitlement, not a lump-sum withdrawal fund.

## How to Negotiate CTC for Better Take-Home

### Optimize Salary Structure:

1. **Maximize HRA** (if you pay rent): Get 50% of basic in HRA (metro) — this is tax-free if you pay rent (old regime)

2. **LTA**: Up to ₹40K per year tax-free (old regime) for actual travel

3. **Flexi benefits**: Many companies allow allocation to fuel reimbursement, meal cards, phone/internet reimbursement — tax-exempt components

4. **NPS employer contribution**: Some companies offer NPS contribution under Section 80CCD(2) — this reduces taxable salary by up to 14% of basic (entirely outside 80C)

5. **Less gratuity exposure for new joiners**: Gratuity only vests after 5 years — but it's part of CTC from day 1. Ask for a restructured CTC that reduces gratuity provision and increases cash components.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My CTC is ₹10L but I get only ₹65K/month. Is my employer cheating?

Probably not — this is normal. ₹10L CTC includes employer EPF (~₹4,800/month) and gratuity provision (~₹1,600/month) that don't appear in your account. Plus income tax TDS of ~₹2,500–₹6,000/month depending on your regime. Work backwards: gross salary ÷ 12, then subtract employee EPF + PT + TDS.

### I'm switching jobs. The offer says ₹15L CTC. How do I compare to my current ₹12L CTC?

Compare "Gross Annual Salary" (CTC minus employer EPF, gratuity, insurance) rather than headline CTC. Ask HR for the salary structure/breakup before accepting. Also check if the new company has better benefits (better insurance, NPS, etc.) that offset lower take-home.

### What happens to employer EPF contribution? I thought EPF was MY money.

The employer's EPF contribution does go to your account — but part of it goes to EPS (Employees' Pension Scheme), not the withdrawable EPF balance. When you leave before 10 years of service, you can withdraw the EPF balance (including employer 3.67% share) but the EPS portion is either transferred to a new employer scheme or paid out as a reduced withdrawal.
