# Soil Health Card Scheme – How to Get Your Soil Health Card

The Soil Health Card (SHC) scheme is a Government of India initiative that provides farmers with a printed report on the nutrient status of their soil. The card carries crop-wise recommendations for fertilisers and nutrients to help farmers improve soil health and increase crop productivity. The scheme is implemented by the Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare.

## What is a Soil Health Card?

A Soil Health Card is a printed document issued to farmers every **2 years** that contains:

- **12 parameters** of soil health — 6 chemical (N, P, K, OC, pH, EC) and 6 physical/biological indicators
- **Crop-wise fertiliser recommendations** based on your soil's nutrient deficiency
- **Suggestions for soil amendments** — lime, gypsum, organic manure, etc.
- **Colour-coded nutrient status** — deficient (red), sufficient (green), surplus (blue)

The aim is to promote **balanced use of fertilisers** and reduce wasteful overuse.

## Who is Eligible?

- **All farmers** in India — landowners, tenant farmers, sharecroppers
- No income or landholding restriction
- The scheme covers **all states and union territories**
- **Free of cost** — no charges for soil testing or card issuance

## How to Get Your Soil Health Card

### Online Method (Check/Download)

1. Visit the Soil Health Card portal at [soilhealth.dac.gov.in](https://soilhealth.dac.gov.in/)
2. Click on **"Farmer's Corner"** → **"Print Your Soil Health Card"**
3. Select your **State, District, Sub-District, and Village**
4. Enter your **name** or **SHC number** (if available)
5. Click **Search** — your soil health card will be displayed
6. Download or print the card for your reference

### Offline Method (Through Agriculture Department)

1. Contact your **village-level agriculture extension worker** or **Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK)**
2. Request soil sampling from your farm
3. The agriculture officer will **collect soil samples** from your field (typically from multiple spots at 15-20 cm depth)
4. Samples are sent to the nearest **Soil Testing Laboratory (STL)**
5. Lab analyses the 12 parameters and generates recommendations
6. Your **Soil Health Card is printed and delivered** to you through the agriculture department
7. A new card is issued every **2 years** (one cycle)

### Via Common Service Centres

1. Visit your nearest **CSC/Jan Seva Kendra** with your [Aadhaar card](/guide/aadhaar-card-apply-online)
2. The CSC operator can help you check your soil health card status online
3. Download and print the card at the centre

## Understanding Your Soil Health Card

| Parameter | What It Measures | Ideal Range |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| **pH** | Soil acidity/alkalinity | 6.5 – 7.5 |
| **EC** | Electrical conductivity (salinity) | < 1 dS/m |
| **Organic Carbon (OC)** | Soil organic matter | > 0.5% |
| **Nitrogen (N)** | Available nitrogen | 280-560 kg/ha |
| **Phosphorus (P)** | Available phosphorus | 10-25 kg/ha |
| **Potassium (K)** | Available potassium | 120-280 kg/ha |
| **Sulphur (S)** | Available sulphur | > 10 ppm |
| **Zinc (Zn)** | Micronutrient | > 0.6 ppm |
| **Iron (Fe)** | Micronutrient | > 4.5 ppm |
| **Copper (Cu)** | Micronutrient | > 0.2 ppm |
| **Manganese (Mn)** | Micronutrient | > 1.0 ppm |
| **Boron (B)** | Micronutrient | > 0.5 ppm |

## Key Benefits

- **Free soil testing** — no cost to the farmer
- **Personalised recommendations** — crop-specific fertiliser advice based on your soil
- **Reduces input cost** — balanced fertiliser use saves money on unnecessary inputs
- **Improves crop yield** — right nutrients in right quantities boost productivity
- **Environmental benefit** — reduces soil degradation from excessive chemical fertiliser use
- **Issued every 2 years** — regular monitoring of soil health changes
- **Digital access** — check your card online anytime

## Important Tips

1. **Collect soil samples correctly** — take samples from multiple spots in a zigzag pattern, mix them, and send a representative sample
2. **Avoid sampling** near bunds, trees, manure heaps, or waterlogged areas — these give inaccurate results
3. **Follow the recommendations** on your card — don't apply excess urea or DAP without checking if your soil needs it
4. **Use organic manure** as suggested — it improves long-term soil health beyond what chemical fertilisers can do
5. **Get a new card every 2 years** to track how your soil health is changing over time

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the Soil Health Card free?
Yes. The entire process — soil sampling, laboratory testing, and card issuance — is completely free for all farmers.

### How often is a new Soil Health Card issued?
A new card is issued every **2 years** (one cycle). The government conducts soil sampling in cycles across all districts.

### Can I check my Soil Health Card online?
Yes. Visit [soilhealth.dac.gov.in](https://soilhealth.dac.gov.in/), go to Farmer's Corner, and search by your name, village, and district.

### What should I do if my soil shows nutrient deficiency?
Follow the crop-specific fertiliser recommendations printed on your card. You can also consult your local agriculture extension officer or KVK for guidance.

### Does the Soil Health Card cover all types of soil?
Yes. The scheme covers all types of agricultural land across all states. Both irrigated and rainfed farmland are eligible.

### How is the soil sample collected?
Agriculture department staff collect soil from 15-20 cm depth at multiple spots in your field. The samples are mixed to create a composite sample, which is sent for lab analysis.
