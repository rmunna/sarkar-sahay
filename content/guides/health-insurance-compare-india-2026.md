## Individual vs Family Floater — Which to Buy?

| Factor | Individual Plan | Family Floater |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| **Cover** | Each person gets own sum insured | Shared sum insured for all family members |
| **Premium** | Higher total (separate for each) | Lower total (shared) |
| **Risk** | Safer — each person's own cover | Risk of multiple claims exhausting shared pool |
| **Best for** | Senior parents (own separate policy) | Nuclear family (spouse + 2 kids) |

**Recommendation:** 
- Nuclear family (you + spouse + 2 kids under 20): Family Floater of ₹10–15L
- Parents (60+): Separate senior citizen plan (Star Senior Citizen Red Carpet, etc.)

## What to Look For When Comparing (5 Critical Factors)

1. **No Room Rent Sub-limit** — Many plans cap room to ₹3,000–₹5,000/day. If you take a higher room, proportionate deduction applies. Choose plans with no room rent cap
2. **No Disease-wise Capping** — Some old plans cap specific diseases (cataract, hernia at ₹25,000). Modern plans don't have this
3. **Pre-existing Disease Waiting Period** — 2–4 year waiting period for PEDs (diabetes, BP, etc.). Choose plan with shortest waiting period (1–2 years for network hospitals)
4. **Restoration** — When claims exhaust sum insured, does it restore? Once or unlimited? Niva Bupa offers unlimited; most others give once
5. **Cashless vs Reimbursement** — Cashless at network hospitals is essential. Check if your nearest/preferred hospital is in the network

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is employer health insurance enough?**
No — employer insurance typically covers only ₹3–5 lakh and stops when you leave the job. Always have a personal health insurance policy in addition to employer cover.

**What is the waiting period for pre-existing diseases?**
Most plans have 2–4 year waiting period. For conditions like diabetes or hypertension, the plan will not cover hospitalisation related to these for 2–4 years after policy start. Niva Bupa's waiting period is 2 years at network hospitals.

**Can I get health insurance if I have diabetes?**
Yes — most insurers now cover people with diabetes, hypertension etc. (pre-existing conditions) with 2–4 year waiting period. Declare it honestly — hiding PED = claim rejection.

**Is health insurance premium eligible for tax deduction?**
Yes — under **Section 80D**:
- Self, spouse, children: Up to ₹25,000/year deduction
- Parents (below 60): Additional ₹25,000
- Parents (60+): Additional ₹50,000
- Maximum: ₹75,000 (if you + senior parents both have separate policies)

**What is the difference between cashless and reimbursement claims?**
Cashless: Hospital directly settles with insurer (TPA); you pay nothing at discharge except non-covered items.
Reimbursement: You pay hospital, then submit bills to insurer and get money back (takes 2–4 weeks). Always prefer cashless — faster and no cash needed.
