# Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana — Withdrawal Rules, Partial Withdrawal & Maturity Claim

**Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)** is the government's flagship scheme for the girl child — 8.2% annual interest (currently the highest among all small savings schemes), tax-free returns, and 80C deduction. But many families are unclear on when and how they can withdraw.

> **Quick answer**: Partial withdrawal (50% of balance) is allowed when daughter turns 18. Full maturity (100%) is at 21 years from account opening date.

## SSY at a Glance — Current Rules

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Interest rate FY 2025-26** | **8.2% p.a.** (compounded annually) |
| **Eligible** | Girl child below 10 years at account opening |
| **Maximum deposit** | ₹1,50,000 per year |
| **Minimum deposit** | ₹250 per year |
| **Lock-in (full maturity)** | 21 years from account opening date |
| **Partial withdrawal** | After daughter turns 18 |
| **Tax** | Section 80C deduction + interest tax-free + maturity tax-free (EEE) |
| **Where** | Post office or authorized bank branches |

## Maturity — When Does SSY Mature?

SSY matures **21 years from the date of account opening** — NOT 21 years after the daughter's birth, and NOT when she turns 21.

**Example**:
- Account opened: April 15, 2010 (daughter was 5 years old)
- Maturity date: April 15, 2031 (daughter will be 26 at maturity)

The account does **not** need to have fresh deposits for all 21 years. Deposits are mandatory for only the first **15 years** from account opening. After 15 years, no further deposits required — the account continues earning interest until maturity at 21 years.

## Partial Withdrawal — After Daughter Turns 18

**Rules**:
- Allowed **only after** daughter turns 18 OR passes Class 10 (whichever is earlier)
- Maximum withdrawal: **50% of the balance** at the end of previous financial year
- Purpose: Higher education OR marriage expenses
- Only **one partial withdrawal** allowed during the scheme

**Required documents for partial withdrawal**:
- Daughter's Aadhaar card (age proof showing 18+)
- Original SSY passbook
- For education: Admission letter / fee receipt from institution
- For marriage: Marriage certificate or invitation (if for marriage)

**How to apply**: Visit the post office / bank where account is held, fill withdrawal form, submit documents.

## Full Maturity Withdrawal (21-Year Closure)

When the 21-year period completes, the full balance (principal + accumulated interest) is payable.

**Process**:
1. Go to the **post office / bank branch** where account is held
2. Carry: Original passbook + daughter's identity proof (Aadhaar/Passport) + Bank account details
3. Fill **SSY closure/withdrawal form**
4. Amount credited to daughter's bank account (not guardian's)
5. If daughter is not present: She can authorize the parent/guardian with a signed letter

**The account is closed** once the maturity amount is withdrawn. It does not continue beyond 21 years.

## Premature Closure — Allowed in These Cases

SSY cannot be closed before 21 years EXCEPT in these specific situations:

| Situation | Allowed? | Conditions |
|-----------|---------|-----------|
| **Marriage of daughter** (18+ years) | ✅ Yes | Up to 1 month before or 3 months after marriage date |
| **Death of daughter** | ✅ Yes | Account closed, balance paid to guardian/parent |
| **Life-threatening illness of daughter** | ✅ Yes | Requires medical documentation |
| **Death of guardian** | ✅ Yes | Account passed to daughter or closed |
| **Extreme compassionate grounds** | ✅ Yes (at government discretion) | AO approval required |

**Premature closure for other reasons**: Not allowed without the above conditions.

## What If Deposits Stop?

If you miss making a deposit in a year, the account becomes "discontinued" (defaults) after the minimum ₹250 isn't deposited. To revive:
- Pay a penalty of **₹50 per year of default** + the minimum deposit for each defaulted year
- Account can be revived any time before 15-year deposit period ends

If you never revive, the account still earns interest (at Post Office Savings Account rate of 4% instead of 8.2%) until maturity.

For smaller deposits:
- ₹50,000/year × 15 years → ~₹22–25 lakh at maturity
- ₹1,000/year (minimum meaningful) × 15 years → ~₹40,000–45,000

## Tax Treatment of SSY

SSY enjoys the highest tax benefit — **EEE status**:
- **E**xempt: Annual deposit deductible under Section 80C (up to ₹1.5L, old regime)
- **E**xempt: Interest earned is fully tax-free each year
- **E**xempt: Maturity amount is fully tax-free

Under the new tax regime, Section 80C deduction is not available, but interest and maturity continue to be tax-free regardless of regime.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My daughter is 18 and wants to withdraw 50% for college. What proof is needed?

Carry: admission letter from the college/university + daughter's Aadhaar (showing age 18+) + original passbook. Visit the post office/bank. They'll process withdrawal within 7-15 working days. Amount goes to daughter's bank account.

### The account was opened in 2012. The 21-year maturity is 2033. Can I withdraw before?

Only if one of the premature closure conditions applies (marriage, illness, guardian death). Otherwise, you must wait until 2033 for full maturity. You can take 50% partial withdrawal after daughter turns 18 (for education/marriage).

### My daughter's SSY account is at a post office but she lives abroad for studies. Can she claim maturity online?

SSY maturity claim currently requires in-person visit or authorized representative. She can authorize you (the guardian) via a signed power of attorney to claim on her behalf. The amount will go to her Indian bank account.

### I want to transfer the SSY account from post office to bank. Is that allowed?

Yes — SSY accounts can be transferred between post offices and authorized banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, etc.). Submit a transfer request at the originating branch with the original passbook.
