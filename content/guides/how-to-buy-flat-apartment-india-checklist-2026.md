# How to Buy a Flat in India 2026 — Complete Checklist

Buying a home is the largest transaction most Indians ever make. A few hours of verification before booking can save you from fraud, delays, or property disputes. Here's the complete checklist.

---

## Step 1: Define Your Budget Correctly

Before looking at any property, calculate your realistic budget:

| Component | Calculation |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Max EMI** | 40% of net monthly take-home (FOIR rule) |
| **Loan eligibility** | Typically 60x monthly income (varies by bank) |
| **Down payment needed** | 10–25% of property value (bank pays 75–90% LTV) |
| **Registration + stamp duty** | 4–7% of property value (state-dependent) |
| **Interior, moving, other costs** | ₹2–5L for a ₹50L flat |

**Total cash needed at purchase** = Down payment + Stamp duty/registration + Interior costs

**Example**: ₹60L flat | Down payment (20%) = ₹12L | Stamp duty (6%) = ₹3.6L | Total = **₹15.6L cash needed** before you touch the loan.

## Step 2: Ready-to-Move vs Under-Construction

| Feature | Ready-to-Move | Under-Construction |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|
| Price | 10–20% higher | Cheaper (early stage) |
| What you see | Actual unit | Just floor plan + model flat |
| Delivery risk | None | Significant (delays, cancellations) |
| GST | 0% (GST-exempt after OC) | 5% (on affordable housing 1%) |
| Possession | Immediate | 2–5 years |
| Tax benefit on home loan | From day 1 | Pre-EMI interest claimed in 5 installments after possession |
| Risk | Lower | Higher |

**When to choose under-construction**: Only if you have time, RERA-registered project, good builder track record, 15%+ price advantage, and don't need immediate possession.

## Step 3: RERA Verification — Non-Negotiable

**Check RERA registration before booking any flat.**

How to check:
1. Go to your state's RERA portal (Maharashtra: maharera.mahaonline.gov.in, Delhi: reras.delhi.gov.in, Karnataka: rera.karnataka.gov.in, etc.)
2. Search by project name or builder name
3. Verify: Registration status, Expected completion date, % of construction completed, Number of units sold vs total, Court orders / complaints if any

**RERA not registered?** Don't book. The project is either illegal or in a jurisdiction where RERA has exemptions. Ask why.

**Check RERA-filed documents**: RERA requires builders to upload approved plans, title deed, commencement certificate. Read them.

---

## Step 4: Title Deed Verification

The most critical legal check. The seller must have clear, undisputed ownership.

**What to verify**:
- **Chain of title**: How did this seller get the property? Was each transfer legal? Go back 30 years if possible.
- **Encumbrance Certificate (EC)**: Shows all transactions on this property for a period. Download from state's registration portal. Any mortgage, lien, court attachment should appear here.
- **Current owner match**: Seller on paper should match the actual person selling.
- **No joint ownership disputes**: If multiple names on title deed, all owners must consent to sell.

**Where to check**:
- Encumbrance certificate: State registration portal (e.g., igr.maharashtra.gov.in, kaveri.karnataka.gov.in)
- Sale deed: Can be searched in Sub-Registrar records by property address

**Hire a lawyer**: For high-value properties, pay a property lawyer (₹5,000–₹25,000) to review title documents. Cheap insurance against a ₹50–₹100L mistake.

---

## Step 5: Approved Plan vs What's Being Built

Builder must have approved plan from local authority (BBMP, BMC, HMDA, MDA, DDA, etc.).

**What to check**:
- Is the plan sanctioned by the municipal authority?
- Is the floor you're buying in the approved plan? (Illegal top-floor additions are common)
- Has plan been modified? (Modifications need re-approval)
- Is the FSI (Floor Space Index) used correctly? (Illegal extra construction)
- Is the land use zone correct? (Residential vs agricultural vs commercial)

**Common fraud**: Illegal construction on approved residential plans (extra floors), selling flats in buildings without proper approval.

---

## Step 6: Completion Certificate / Occupancy Certificate

**Occupancy Certificate (OC)** / **Completion Certificate (CC)**: Issued by local municipal authority after construction is complete and all safety standards are met.

**Why it matters**:
- No OC = building is technically illegal
- Utility connections (electricity, water, gas) may not be given officially without OC
- Banks won't give home loans for resale without OC
- Municipality can demolish unauthorized construction

**For under-construction**: Get a commitment (in sale agreement) that OC/CC will be obtained before possession.

**For ready-to-move**: Demand to see the OC certificate. If builder says "it's pending" — wait until it's obtained before paying final installment.

## Step 7: Builder Track Record

Research the builder:
- Past projects — delivered on time or delayed?
- RERA complaint history at state RERA portal
- Court cases (search builder name + consumer forum / NCDRC)
- Customer reviews (magicbricks.com, 99acres.com review sections)
- LinkedIn — how old is the company? Key promoters' background?

**Red flags**: Builder is first-timer without established track record; website is new; no social media presence; can't show you completed projects.

---

## Step 8: Sale Agreement / Builder-Buyer Agreement (BBA)

Before booking, you'll be asked to sign an agreement and pay advance. Read it carefully:

**Key clauses to verify**:
- **Possession date**: Clear date committed, not "approximately 2027"
- **Grace period**: Most agreements give builder 6-12 months grace beyond committed date
- **Penalty for delay**: If builder misses possession: should be ₹5–₹10/sq.ft./month (RERA default if not specified)
- **Your penalty for non-payment**: Forfeiture clause — how much you lose if you cancel?
- **Super built-up area vs carpet area**: RERA mandates carpet area disclosure. The "super built-up" always sounds bigger.
- **Maintenance charges**: Who fixes defects in year 1? Who pays common area maintenance?
- **Force majeure**: Broad force majeure clauses can delay possession indefinitely without penalty

**Carpet area vs super built-up**: RERA requires pricing on carpet area. Calculate price per sq.ft. of carpet area (not super built-up). 1,200 sq.ft. super built-up = ~750–900 sq.ft. carpet.

## Step 9: Home Loan — Compare Before Committing

Most builders have "preferred lenders" — don't just go with them. Compare:

| Bank | EBLR Rate (Approx) | Effective Rate |
|------|-------------------|----------------|
| SBI | 8.25–8.75% | Check current RBI linked rates |
| HDFC Bank | 8.35–8.85% | Floating EBLR |
| ICICI Bank | 8.35–8.90% | Floating |
| Kotak Mahindra | 8.65–9.10% | Floating |

**Important terms**: All rates are floating (EBLR-linked) — they change with RBI repo rate. Check current rates at time of application.

**Processing fee**: ₹5,000–₹15,000. Negotiate — often waivable.

**Pre-payment penalty**: Most floating rate loans have zero pre-payment penalty. Confirm.

## Step 10: Stamp Duty and Registration

After deciding to buy, you'll pay stamp duty + registration:

| State | Stamp Duty | Registration |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| Maharashtra | 5–6% | 1% |
| Karnataka | 5% | 1% |
| Delhi | 4% (women) / 6% (men) / 5% (joint) | 1% |
| Tamil Nadu | 7% | 4% |
| West Bengal | 6–7% | 1% |
| Gujarat | 4.9% | 1% |

**Women buyers get discount in many states** — if buying in wife's name or jointly, check for reduced stamp duty.

**Circle rate vs agreement value**: Stamp duty is paid on the HIGHER of agreement value or government circle rate (ready reckoner rate). Don't agree to undervalued sale deed (to reduce stamp duty) — illegal and causes problems at resale.

## Red Flags — Walk Away From These

| Red Flag | Why Dangerous |
|---------|--------------|
| RERA not registered | Could be illegal; no legal protection |
| Cash component demanded | Black money transaction; illegal |
| OC not available for ready property | Building may be unauthorized |
| Title deed with multiple claims | Ownership dispute; could lose property |
| "Just sign now, read later" | Pressure tactics for bad agreements |
| Unregistered sale agreement | No legal standing |
| Builder refuses EC / title documents | Hiding encumbrances |
| Price "too good to be true" (30%+ below market) | Distressed asset or fraud |
| Building plan not matching actual construction | Illegal deviation |

---

## Step 11: Final Payment and Registration

**Final payment only after**:
- You've seen the flat (actual unit, not model flat)
- OC/CC is ready (or firm commitment for under-construction)
- All punch list items from joint inspection are completed
- No outstanding dues on property (society charges, property tax)

**Registration**: Sign sale deed in the presence of Sub-Registrar with seller + witnesses. Both buyer and seller must physically appear (or via power of attorney).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### The builder says RERA registration is pending. Should I book?

No — don't pay advance before RERA registration is obtained. RERA registration gives you legal protection. An "applied for RERA" claim is not the same as "registered."

### Builder wants 20% in cash (black money). What are the risks?

Paying unaccounted cash: you can't claim that portion in capital gains calculation (lower cost = higher tax when selling), you have no legal proof for the full amount paid, and it's illegal. Walk away.

### Should I hire a property lawyer?

For amounts above ₹30L — yes, absolutely. A property lawyer reviewing documents for ₹10,000–₹25,000 can save you from lakhs in losses.

### Is it safe to buy flats through real estate agents?

Agents are useful for finding properties but represent the seller, not you. They earn commission from the seller. Don't rely on agent's "verification" — do your own.
