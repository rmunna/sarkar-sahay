# Advance Tax India 2026 — Who Must Pay, Due Dates & How to Calculate

**Advance tax** means paying your income tax in installments during the financial year itself, rather than as a lump sum when filing your ITR. If you have income that isn't fully covered by TDS (like freelance income, capital gains, rental income, crypto gains, or business income), you must calculate and pay advance tax on schedule.

> **Key threshold**: If your **estimated tax liability** for the year exceeds **₹10,000** (after crediting TDS), you must pay advance tax.

## Who Must Pay Advance Tax

| Category | Must Pay Advance Tax? |
|----------|----------------------|
| **Salaried employee** (employer deducts TDS fully) | ❌ Usually No — TDS covers it |
| **Salaried employee with side income** (freelance/rent/interest) | ✅ Yes — for income beyond TDS coverage |
| **Freelancer / Consultant** | ✅ Yes |
| **Business owner / Self-employed** | ✅ Yes |
| **Capital gains (stocks, mutual funds, property)** | ✅ Yes — after the quarter gains are realized |
| **Rental income owner** | ✅ Yes |
| **Crypto gains** | ✅ Yes |
| **NRI with Indian income** | ✅ Yes |
| **Senior citizen (60+) — pension/salary only** | ❌ Exempt under Section 207 |
| **Senior citizen with business income** | ✅ Yes — not exempt if has business income |

### The Salaried Employee Exception

If you are **only salaried** and your employer deducts TDS every month fully, advance tax is already being paid on your behalf (through TDS). You don't need to pay separately.

However, if you have **additional income** that the employer doesn't know about — freelance work, Zerodha capital gains, rent from a property, crypto profits, FD interest above TDS threshold — that additional income may push you past the ₹10,000 threshold and require advance tax.

## Advance Tax Due Dates 2025-26 (AY 2026-27)

| Installment | Due Date | % of Total Tax to Pay |
|-------------|----------|----------------------|
| 1st | **June 15, 2025** | At least 15% |
| 2nd | **September 15, 2025** | At least 45% (cumulative) |
| 3rd | **December 15, 2025** | At least 75% (cumulative) |
| 4th (final) | **March 15, 2026** | 100% |

> **Important**: Percentages are cumulative. By September 15, you must have paid 45% of estimated full-year tax total (not just 45% more).

**Presumptive taxation (Section 44AD / 44ADA / 44AE)**: If you opted for the presumptive taxation scheme, you pay **all advance tax in one shot by March 15** — no quarterly installments.

## How to Calculate Advance Tax

### Step 1: Estimate Your Total Income for the Year

Add up all expected income:
- Salary (check your CTC/Form 16 from employer)
- Freelance / business income (estimate based on Q1-Q2 actuals)
- Capital gains (actual gains realized so far + expected)
- Rental income
- Interest income (FD, savings)
- Crypto gains

### Step 2: Subtract Deductions

- Section 80C (up to ₹1.5L): PPF, ELSS, LIC, NPS, EPF, housing loan principal
- Section 80D (health insurance): Up to ₹25,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizen parents)
- Section 80CCD(1B) (NPS): Additional ₹50,000
- HRA exemption (if in old regime)
- Standard deduction: ₹75,000 (new regime) or ₹50,000 (old regime)

### Step 3: Calculate Tax on Taxable Income

Apply the tax slabs (new regime by default for FY 2025-26):

| Income Range | Tax Rate (New Regime 2025-26) |
|-------------|-------------------------------|
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | 0% |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

Add 4% health & education cess on tax.

Note: If income ≤ ₹12,00,000 (₹12,75,000 with std deduction for salaried), Section 87A rebate makes tax = zero. No advance tax needed in that case.

### Step 4: Subtract TDS Already Deducted

Check Form 26AS or AIS for TDS deducted so far by:
- Employer (salary TDS)
- Bank (FD interest TDS)
- Crypto exchange (1% TDS)
- Property buyer (TDS on rent/sale)

Subtract TDS from your total estimated tax.

### Step 5: Compare with ₹10,000 Threshold

If `(Estimated Tax − TDS)` > ₹10,000 → **You must pay advance tax.**
If < ₹10,000 → No advance tax required; pay at filing time.

### Practical Example

Freelance developer, FY 2025-26:
- Expected annual income: ₹18,00,000
- Deductions: ₹50,000 standard deduction (new regime) = taxable income ₹17,50,000
- Tax: ₹0 on first ₹4L + 5% on ₹4L (₹20,000) + 10% on ₹4L (₹40,000) + 15% on ₹1.5L (₹22,500) = ₹82,500 + cess ₹3,300 = **₹85,800**
- TDS deducted by clients (10% on professional fees): ₹25,000
- Net advance tax payable: ₹85,800 − ₹25,000 = **₹60,800**

**Advance tax schedule**:
- June 15: 15% of ₹60,800 = **₹9,120**
- September 15: 45% total = ₹27,360, so pay **₹18,240** more
- December 15: 75% total = ₹45,600, so pay **₹18,240** more
- March 15: 100% = pay **₹15,200** remaining

## How to Pay Advance Tax Online

1. Go to **[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/)** → "E-Pay Tax"
2. OR directly at [eportal.incometax.gov.in](https://eportal.incometax.gov.in/) → "e-Pay Tax"
3. Select challan type: **Challan No. 280 — Income Tax (Other than Companies)**
4. Under "Type of Payment": Select **Advance Tax (100)**
5. Enter Assessment Year: AY 2026-27 (for FY 2025-26 income)
6. Enter PAN and fill in the amount
7. Pay via net banking, debit card, UPI, or credit card
8. Save the challan receipt (BSR Code + Challan Serial Number)

## Interest for Late/Short Advance Tax Payment

### Section 234C — For Missing Quarterly Installments

If you don't pay the required % by each due date:

| Shortfall | Interest |
|-----------|---------|
| 234C interest rate | **1% per month** on the shortfall amount |
| Duration | 3 months per quarter |

**Example**: Should have paid ₹9,000 by June 15 but paid ₹0. Interest = ₹9,000 × 1% × 3 = **₹270** for Q1.

> **Exception**: If the shortfall in any quarter is due to **capital gains or exceptional income** that you couldn't predict, 234C interest isn't charged for that specific quarter — provided you pay all remaining tax in subsequent installments.

### Section 234B — For Not Paying at Least 90% by March 31

If you haven't paid at least 90% of your total tax by March 31:

| 234B Penalty | Rate |
|--------------|------|
| Interest rate | **1% per month** (or part of month) |
| Calculation period | From April 1 of Assessment Year until date of payment |

**Example**: Total tax = ₹1,00,000. Paid only ₹60,000 by March 31. Shortfall from 90% = ₹30,000. If you file ITR in July and pay then: 234B = ₹30,000 × 1% × 4 months = **₹1,200**.

## Adjusting Advance Tax for Capital Gains

Capital gains are hard to predict in advance. The IT rules allow:

- **Short-term capital gains**: Include in the quarter they are realized
- **Long-term capital gains from equity/MF**: For gains realized after a quarter's due date, you have until the next due date to pay advance tax on those gains
- **Property sale gains**: Treat as income of the quarter in which the sale closes; pay the installment for that quarter accordingly

If you sell property or shares in Q3 (Oct–Dec 2025), include the capital gains when calculating your Q3 (December 15) installment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I'm salaried and my Form 16 shows ₹95,000 TDS. I made ₹20,000 from crypto in August. Do I need advance tax?

Yes. Calculate the tax on ₹20,000 crypto profit at 30% = ₹6,000 + cess ₹240 = ₹6,240. Since ₹6,240 < ₹10,000, you're **below the threshold** — no advance tax needed. Pay ₹6,240 as self-assessment tax while filing ITR.

But if your crypto gain was ₹40,000: tax = ₹12,000 + cess = ₹12,480. That's > ₹10,000 → you must pay advance tax by the next due date after the gain was realized.

### I missed the September 15 installment. What now?

Pay the amount you should have paid (45% cumulative) immediately via Challan 280, Advance Tax (100). The 234C interest on the shortfall for 3 months will be calculated when you file your ITR — you don't need to calculate it separately now. ITR pre-fill does this automatically.

### I estimated income wrong and paid too much advance tax. What happens?

If you paid more advance tax than your final tax liability, the **excess is refunded** when you file ITR (with interest at 6% per annum from April 1 of Assessment Year). There is no penalty for overpaying advance tax.

### Can I pay all advance tax at once in March?

Technically, the March 15 deadline is the final installment (100% cumulative). If you pay everything on March 15 in one shot, you'll get a 234C notice for the June, September, and December installments (interest for 3 months each). However, if your total tax is < ₹10,000, you can pay entirely at ITR filing time without any interest.

### Is advance tax required for presumptive taxation (44AD/44ADA)?

If you opted for 44AD (small business) or 44ADA (professionals with ≤ ₹75 lakh revenue), **all advance tax must be paid by March 15** — but you can pay it in one shot without quarterly breakdowns. Missing March 15 triggers 234B/234C.
