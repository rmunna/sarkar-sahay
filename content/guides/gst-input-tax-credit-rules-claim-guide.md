# GST Input Tax Credit (ITC) — Complete Rules and How to Claim

**Input Tax Credit (ITC)** means using the GST you paid on your business purchases to reduce the GST you owe to the government on your sales. It's the fundamental mechanism that prevents double taxation in the GST chain. But ITC has strict conditions — misclassification or wrong claims attract heavy penalties.

## How ITC Works — Simple Example

You're a manufacturer:
- You buy raw materials: Pay ₹18,000 GST
- You sell finished goods: Collect ₹30,000 GST from customers

Without ITC: Pay ₹30,000 GST to government  
**With ITC: Pay ₹30,000 − ₹18,000 = ₹12,000** to government

The ₹18,000 paid on purchases is the ITC you claim.

## 4 Mandatory Conditions to Claim ITC (Section 16(2))

ALL four conditions must be satisfied:

| Condition | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **1. Tax Invoice** | Must have a valid tax invoice (with supplier GSTIN, your GSTIN, GST amount) |
| **2. Goods/Services Received** | You must have actually received the goods or services |
| **3. Supplier Filed GSTR-1** | Supplier must have filed their GSTR-1 (outward supplies) — it appears in your GSTR-2B |
| **4. Tax Paid to Government** | The supplier must have actually paid the tax to the government |

**Critical rule on Condition 3**: Even if you have a valid invoice and received goods, you can only claim ITC for what appears in your **GSTR-2B** auto-populated portal. If your supplier didn't file GSTR-1, the invoice won't appear — you cannot claim ITC.

## GSTR-2B — The ITC Matching Statement

**GSTR-2B** is a monthly auto-generated statement showing all ITC eligible for you (based on supplier's GSTR-1 filings).

- Available by the 14th of each month for the previous month
- Shows: Invoices where supplier has filed GSTR-1
- Claim ONLY what appears in GSTR-2B under regular flow

**If an invoice is not in GSTR-2B**: Either supplier hasn't filed GSTR-1 yet, or they filed without your GSTIN correctly. Chase supplier to file/correct.

## What GST Expenses Are Eligible for ITC?

### Eligible for ITC

| Expense | ITC Available? |
|---------|---------------|
| Raw materials, goods for resale | ✅ Yes |
| Services used for business (professional fees, IT services, logistics) | ✅ Yes |
| Capital goods (machinery, computers) used in business | ✅ Yes (spread over multiple years in some cases) |
| Office supplies, stationery | ✅ Yes |
| Business travel (hotel, flight) for employees | ✅ Yes |

### Blocked ITC (Section 17(5)) — Cannot Claim

| Expense | ITC Blocked? |
|---------|------------|
| **Motor vehicles (< 13 seats)** | ❌ Blocked (unless used for transport of goods/passengers commercially) |
| **Food, beverages, outdoor catering** | ❌ Blocked (unless part of the supply chain — e.g., restaurant) |
| **Health services, life/medical insurance** | ❌ Blocked (unless mandatory for employees) |
| **Works contract for construction of immovable property** | ❌ Blocked |
| **Goods/services for personal use of directors/officers** | ❌ Blocked |
| **Club memberships, health/fitness centres** | ❌ Blocked |
| **Travel benefits for employees** | ❌ Blocked (unless on duty) |

## How to Claim ITC in GSTR-3B

ITC is claimed monthly in **GSTR-3B** (Table 4):

| Table | What to Enter |
|-------|-------------|
| **4(A)(5)**: All other ITC | ITC from GSTR-2B for B2B purchases |
| **4(B)**: Ineligible/Blocked ITC | ITC on blocked categories (must be reversed) |
| **4(C)**: Net ITC available | 4(A) minus 4(B) = your eligible ITC |

**Steps**:
1. Login to [gst.gov.in](https://gst.gov.in/) → File Returns → GSTR-3B
2. Go to Table 4
3. Pre-fill from GSTR-2B (portal suggests amount — verify before accepting)
4. Adjust for blocked credits
5. Net ITC reduces your GST payable

## ITC Reversal — When You Must Return ITC

| Situation | Reversal Required |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Supplier didn't pay tax within 180 days | Reverse ITC (Rule 37) |
| Goods returned or rejected | Reverse proportionate ITC |
| Used goods for personal use | Reverse ITC |
| Capital goods disposed of | Reverse remaining ITC |
| Exempt supplies (mixed supply) | Reverse proportionate ITC |

### Rule 37A — Supplier Default (Important)

If you claimed ITC in GSTR-3B but the **supplier didn't pay tax** (GSTR-3B not filed):
- You must **reverse the ITC** in the subsequent month
- If supplier pays later (within 2 years), you can re-claim the ITC
- This prevents fraudulent ITC claims from fake supplier invoices

## Time Limit to Claim ITC

| Item | Time Limit |
|------|-----------|
| Claim ITC for any invoice | By November 30 of the Assessment Year, OR before filing the September return of next year, whichever is earlier |
| FY 2025-26 ITC last date | By November 30, 2026 (or September 2026 GSTR-3B, whichever earlier) |

**Critical**: Don't leave ITC unclaimed — it lapses after this deadline.

## Common ITC Rejection / Notice Reasons and Fixes

### Notice: "Excess ITC Claimed vs GSTR-2B"

**Cause**: You claimed more ITC in GSTR-3B than what GSTR-2B shows.

**Fix**:
- Reconcile GSTR-2B with your purchase register
- Find invoices where supplier hasn't filed GSTR-1
- Reverse the excess in the next GSTR-3B, or
- Chase supplier to file GSTR-1 so the invoice appears in GSTR-2B

### Notice: "ITC on Blocked Credit Category"

**Cause**: ITC claimed on cars, food, construction, etc. (Section 17(5) items).

**Fix**:
- Reverse the blocked ITC in GSTR-3B Table 4(B)
- Pay the interest (18% per annum) on the wrongly claimed ITC
- Response to notice: Acknowledge and provide evidence of reversal

### Notice: "Fake ITC — Suppliers Didn't Exist"

**Cause**: GSTIN flagged as suspicious/fake by GST officers.

**Fix**:
- This is serious — file a detailed response
- Provide all documents (invoice, payment proof, delivery challan, bank transfer)
- If supplier genuinely didn't supply: Reverse the ITC immediately

## ITC and Annual Reconciliation (GSTR-9)

In the **GSTR-9 Annual Return**, reconcile:
- Total ITC claimed monthly in GSTR-3B
- Total ITC available as per GSTR-2B annual data
- Any differences must be explained or corrected via GSTR-9C (Reconciliation Statement, if applicable)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My supplier went bankrupt and never paid GST. I already claimed ITC. Must I reverse it?

Yes — under Rule 37A, if supplier didn't pay GST (GSTR-3B not filed), you must reverse ITC. You can re-claim if the supplier pays later. If you face a tax demand due to this, you can argue force majeure but reversal is safer.

### Can I claim ITC on GST paid on office renovation?

Partially — GST on furniture, fittings, electrical work inside the office is eligible ITC (as these are not "immovable property" construction). Structural work (civil construction, building the office walls/floors) is blocked under Section 17(5). Draw the line at structural vs non-structural.

### I am on Composition Scheme. Can I claim ITC?

No — Composition dealers cannot claim ITC at all. The composition scheme's low tax rate is in exchange for forfeiting ITC.
