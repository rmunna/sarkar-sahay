# Section 80GG India 2026 — Rent Deduction When Your Employer Doesn't Give HRA

If you're a **freelancer, self-employed professional, or salaried person without HRA**, and you pay rent for accommodation, Section 80GG lets you claim a deduction even without an employer-provided HRA.

This is the lesser-known alternative to the HRA exemption for people without it.

## Who Can Claim Section 80GG?

**Eligible**:
- Salaried employees who do NOT receive HRA (or receive zero HRA in their salary structure)
- Self-employed individuals (freelancers, consultants, business owners)
- Any individual paying rent for accommodation

**Not eligible**:
- Anyone who receives HRA from their employer (they use the regular HRA exemption route)
- Anyone who owns a house at any place in India (in your name, spouse's name, or minor child's name)
- New regime taxpayers (80GG only available under old tax regime)

## Section 80GG Deduction Limit

**Deduction = Lowest of these three**:

1. **₹5,000 per month** (₹60,000 per year) — the monetary cap
2. **25% of Total Income** (before this deduction)
3. **Actual rent paid MINUS 10% of Total Income**

**"Total Income"** here means your total income before Section 80GG deduction but after other deductions (80C, 80D, etc.).

## Worked Example

**Freelance designer, FY 2025-26, old regime**:
- Gross professional income: ₹8,00,000
- 80C investments: ₹1,50,000
- Total income (before 80GG): ₹6,50,000
- Rent paid: ₹12,000/month = ₹1,44,000/year

Calculation:
1. Monthly cap: ₹5,000 × 12 = ₹60,000
2. 25% of total income: 25% × ₹6,50,000 = ₹1,62,500
3. Rent minus 10% of income: ₹1,44,000 − (10% × ₹6,50,000) = ₹1,44,000 − ₹65,000 = ₹79,000

**Deduction = Minimum of ₹60,000 / ₹1,62,500 / ₹79,000 = ₹60,000**

Tax saving at 20% slab: ₹60,000 × 20% = **₹12,000**

## Conditions to Claim Section 80GG

### Must Pay Rent
You must actually pay rent — to a landlord, PG owner, or family member (though family rent can attract scrutiny; best to have formal rental agreement).

### Cannot Own a House
**No house owned anywhere in India** — in your name, your spouse's name, your minor child's name. If you own a house anywhere (even in your hometown), you cannot claim 80GG.

This is the most common disqualification: people who own a house in their home city but rent in a metro for work cannot claim 80GG.

### No HRA From Employer
If your salary structure includes HRA (even if it's a small amount), you cannot use Section 80GG. You must use the HRA exemption calculation instead (which may be better anyway).

### File Form 10BA
Before claiming 80GG, you must file **Form 10BA** (declaration that you pay rent for your accommodation and don't own a house). File this online on the income tax portal before or while filing ITR. Without Form 10BA, the deduction can be disallowed.

## How to File Form 10BA

1. Go to incometax.gov.in → My Account → e-File → Income Tax Forms → Form 10BA
2. Fill:
   - Name and address of landlord
   - Amount of rent paid
   - Period for which rent was paid
   - PAN of landlord (if rent > ₹1L/year)
   - Declaration that no house is owned
3. Submit electronically (no physical submission needed)
4. In your ITR, claim deduction under Section 80GG

## Section 80GG vs HRA — Which Is Better?

| | Section 80GG | HRA Exemption |
|-|-------------|--------------|
| Who can claim | No HRA / self-employed | Salaried with HRA component |
| Maximum deduction | ₹60,000/year | Potentially much higher |
| House ownership | Cannot own any house | Can own houses elsewhere |
| Ease | Simple (Form 10BA) | Calculation needed |

**HRA exemption is almost always larger**: HRA exemption can be ₹1–4L+ for metro city renters with high HRA. Section 80GG is limited to ₹60,000.

But 80GG is the only option if you don't have HRA.

## Higher Rent but Limited Deduction

If you're paying ₹25,000/month rent but have no HRA, Section 80GG still caps your deduction at ₹5,000/month = ₹60,000/year regardless of actual rent.

The ₹60,000 cap hasn't been updated in many years — this limit was set when rents were lower. For high-rent metros, it's a limited relief.

## Self-Employed With High Income — 80GG May Help Little

At high income, the "actual rent minus 10% of income" formula reduces the deduction:
- Income: ₹30L, Rent: ₹3,60,000/year
- 10% of income: ₹3,00,000
- Rent minus 10%: ₹3,60,000 − ₹3,00,000 = ₹60,000
- Still ₹60,000 (matches the monthly cap) — fine here

- Income: ₹50L, Rent: ₹4,20,000/year
- 10% of income: ₹5,00,000
- Rent minus 10%: ₹4,20,000 − ₹5,00,000 = **Negative** (less than zero)
- **No Section 80GG deduction** when 10% of income exceeds your annual rent

At very high income levels, 80GG provides zero benefit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I'm a software freelancer paying ₹18,000/month rent. Can I claim 80GG?

Yes — if you don't own any house and your income qualifies. Deduction capped at ₹5,000/month (₹60,000/year). File Form 10BA before claiming. Keep your rent receipts and rental agreement.

### My parents own a house in my hometown. I'm renting in Bengaluru. Can I claim 80GG?

Yes — the condition is that YOU (or your spouse or minor child) don't own a house. Your parents' house doesn't disqualify you. You're renting in Bengaluru and don't own any house — 80GG applies.

### I'm salaried and my CTC includes "HRA" on paper but the amount is ₹0 (or it's merged into special allowance). Can I claim 80GG?

If your salary slip shows HRA = ₹0 (even if the structure mentions HRA), technically you're not receiving HRA. You may claim 80GG. But ideally, get written confirmation from HR that no HRA is being given, in case of scrutiny.
