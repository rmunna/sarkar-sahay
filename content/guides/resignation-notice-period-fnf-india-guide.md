# Resignation & Notice Period India 2026 — Complete Guide

Resigning in India seems simple but the notice period, FnF settlement, and document handover process has many traps. This guide covers your rights so you don't get cheated out of salary or stuck without documents at your new job.

---

## Notice Period — What's the Law?

**There is no single mandated notice period** in central Indian law for white-collar employees. Your notice period is what's written in your **employment contract / offer letter**.

**Typical notice periods by industry**:
| Industry | Common Notice Period |
|----------|---------------------|
| IT/Software | 60–90 days |
| Banking/Finance | 30–60 days |
| Manufacturing | 30 days |
| Startups | 30–60 days |
| Senior management (VP+) | 90–180 days |

**Factory workers and shop establishment employees**: The Shops and Establishments Act (state-wise) mandates 30 days' notice for employees with 1+ year of continuous service.

---

## How to Calculate Notice Period

Notice period starts from the **day you submit your resignation** (not the day your manager approves it).

**Example**:
- You resign on June 1st
- 60-day notice period
- Last working day = July 31st

**Important**: If your offer letter says "30 days written notice," the clock starts when HR receives the written resignation — not from verbal discussion.

## Notice Period Buy-Out — Can You Leave Early?

Yes. If your new employer wants you to join faster, you (or your new employer) can pay the "notice period buy-out" — equivalent salary for the notice period not served.

**Notice period buy-out calculation**:
Gross Monthly Salary ÷ 30 × Remaining Notice Days

**Example**: ₹1,00,000 gross salary, 60-day notice, want to leave in 30 days:
Buy-out = ₹1,00,000 ÷ 30 × 30 = **₹1,00,000**

**Who pays the buy-out?**
- You pay your current employer
- Some new employers offer "joining bonus" to compensate — verify in your new offer letter
- Some new employers directly negotiate with old employer (rare)

**Tax on notice period buy-out received** (if employer waives your notice period and pays you instead): Taxable as salary income. Tax on buy-out you *pay* is non-deductible.

## FnF — Full and Final Settlement

After your last working day, your employer must complete "Full and Final" (FnF) settlement — paying all dues and recovering any advances.

### What Is Included in FnF?

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Salary for days worked** | Salary for the current month up to last working day |
| **Notice period salary** | If notice period was served |
| **Pending reimbursements** | Medical, travel, internet claims |
| **Leave encashment** | Earned leave balance (if company policy allows encashment at resignation) |
| **Gratuity** | If worked 5+ years (payment within 30 days) |
| **Bonus** | Pro-rata if eligible based on company policy |
| **PF** | Employer ensures UAN is updated; you apply for withdrawal/transfer separately |

### What Can Employer Deduct from FnF?

| Deduction | Valid? |
|-----------|--------|
| Notice period salary (if you short-served) | Yes — employer can deduct equivalent of unserved notice days |
| Outstanding advance/loan recovery | Yes — only if documented in salary advance agreement |
| Equipment damage | Only if proven and documented |
| "Bond" amount (service agreement) | Enforceable only if terms are reasonable and courts have upheld specific bond |

### How Long Does FnF Take?

**Common practice**: 30–60 days after last working day.
**Legal requirement under Payment of Wages Act**: Wages for the final period must be paid by the usual payday or within 2 days of termination, whichever is earlier.

In practice, full FnF (including leave encashment, gratuity) takes 30–90 days. If no FnF after 90 days, you have grounds to escalate.

## Relieving Letter and Experience Letter — Your Rights

**You are legally entitled to both.** These are not "favors" from your employer.

**Relieving Letter**: States your last working day, that you've been "relieved from duties," and your designation. Required by most new employers for background verification.

**Experience Letter**: Confirms your period of employment, role, and responsibilities. Used for future job applications, visa applications, loans, etc.

**Can employer withhold relieving/experience letter?**

No. There is no legal provision allowing an employer to withhold your service certificate. While no single central law explicitly mandates it, several state Shops and Establishments Acts do require employers to issue service certificates. Courts have consistently held that withholding such documents is bad faith.

If your employer is withholding documents as leverage (for notice period, bond, etc.), the threat itself can be legally challenged.

## What To Do If Employer Withholds Documents or Salary

### Step 1: Email HR Formally

Send a written email to HR and manager stating:
- Your last working day
- Requesting FnF payment within 30 days
- Requesting relieving letter and experience letter
- Citing that you've fulfilled all obligations

Keep the email — this is your paper trail.

### Step 2: Escalate to Senior HR / CHRO

If no response in 14 days, escalate to Head of HR or CHRO. CC your manager.

### Step 3: Legal Notice (via Advocate)

If no resolution in 30 days after last working day, send a legal notice through an advocate. Legal notice via registered post is formal and prompts quick response in most cases. Cost: ₹3,000–₹10,000 for an advocate's notice.

### Step 4: Labour Commissioner / Labour Court

**File complaint at**: State Labour Commissioner's office
- Complaint: Non-payment of wages under Payment of Wages Act, 1936
- If salary is withheld: Labour court can order payment + interest + penalty
- Time to file: Within 1 year of violation

**Online portal**: [shramsuvidha.gov.in](https://shramsuvidha.gov.in) → Grievance section

### Step 5: Civil Court

For large FnF amounts or pattern of bad faith (withholding documents + not paying), civil suit for breach of contract. Slower but recovers full amount + damages.

## Service Agreement / Bond — Are They Enforceable?

Many IT companies have "2-year service bonds" with penalty clauses (₹1–5 lakh). The enforceability depends on:

1. **Was it a training bond?** If the company spent money training you and the bond covers training costs — partially enforceable.
2. **Is the amount proportional?** ₹5 lakh penalty for ₹30K/month salary after 18 months of 24-month bond = courts often reduce this as "unreasonable restraint of trade."
3. **Did you receive special benefit?** (Sponsored education, overseas training, certification paid by employer) — bond for those costs is more enforceable.

**Practical reality**: Most employers bluff about bond enforcement. Suing an ex-employee is expensive and unlikely unless the bond amount is large (₹5L+) and the company is large enough to have legal bandwidth.

**What to do**: Don't panic about bonds. If threatened, consult a labor lawyer before paying — many bonds are either unenforceable or negotiable.

## PF After Resignation — What Happens?

1. **Transfer PF**: If joining a new company with PF, transfer your PF balance via UAN (online) to new employer's PF account — no tax, no penalty.
2. **Withdraw PF**: If not joining another job, withdraw after 2 months of unemployment. If worked <5 years, withdrawal is taxable.
3. **Pension (EPS) withdrawal**: Form 10C — if worked <10 years and withdrawing. If 10+ years, you get a pension at age 58 (don't withdraw, keep it).

## Tax on FnF Components

| FnF Component | Tax Treatment |
|--------------|--------------|
| Salary for days worked | Taxable as salary |
| Leave encashment (government employee) | Fully exempt |
| Leave encashment (private employee) | Exempt up to ₹25 lakh (limit raised from ₹3L in 2023) |
| Gratuity (after 5 years) | Exempt up to ₹20 lakh |
| Bonus | Taxable as salary |
| Notice period buy-out received | Taxable as salary |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My company says I need to "clear handover" before getting relieving letter. How long?

Handover is a reasonable requirement. However, it must be for a specific, defined period (1–2 weeks). Indefinite handover pending is not valid. Set a clear deadline in writing.

### New employer is losing the offer if I don't join in 15 days but my notice is 90 days.

Options:
1. Negotiate notice period with current employer (60/45/30 day waiver)
2. Pay buy-out for remaining days
3. Ask new employer to extend joining date (some are flexible, especially for good candidates)
4. Request your new employer negotiate directly with old employer

### Can my employer terminate me instead of accepting my resignation?

If you resign and the employer terminates you instead (to avoid paying notice period), they must pay you termination benefits. This is actually better for you — they're playing with fire. Get the termination in writing if this happens.

### My company is withholding PF saying I haven't served full notice.

Employer cannot withhold your PF balance. EPF is a statutory deposit — it's your money. The employer has no legal right to withhold it regardless of notice period. Raise a grievance at epfindia.gov.in if PF is not credited within 15 days of resignation.
