# Gift Tax Rules India 2026 — When Gifts Are Taxable vs Exempt

India does not have a separate "gift tax" anymore (the old Gift Tax Act was abolished in 1998). Instead, gifts received are taxed under **Section 56(2)(x)** as **"Income from Other Sources"** when they exceed certain thresholds. Gifts from certain relatives and specific occasions are fully exempt.

> **Quick rule**: Gifts from relatives = always exempt. Gifts from non-relatives > ₹50,000 in a year = taxable at your income tax slab rate.

## The ₹50,000 Annual Threshold

If you receive gifts from all non-relatives combined during a financial year:
- Up to ₹50,000 total: **Tax-free**
- Above ₹50,000 total: The **entire amount** (not just the excess) is taxable

**Example**:
- Friends gifted you ₹60,000 on your birthday
- Since ₹60,000 > ₹50,000, the **full ₹60,000** is taxable (not just ₹10,000)
- Tax: ₹60,000 at your slab rate

## Gifts That Are ALWAYS Exempt (No Tax)

| Situation | Exemption |
|-----------|----------|
| Gift from specified **relatives** | ✅ Fully exempt, any amount |
| Gift received on **marriage** | ✅ Fully exempt (from anyone, any amount) |
| Gift received **under Will** or by inheritance | ✅ Fully exempt |
| Gift from **local authority** (government bodies) | ✅ Exempt |
| Gift from **registered charitable trust / educational institution** | ✅ Exempt |
| Gift in **contemplation of death** (deathbed gift) | ✅ Exempt |

## The Relatives Exemption — Complete List

Gifts from these relatives are **fully exempt regardless of amount**:

- **Spouse**
- **Brother and sister** (of you or your spouse)
- **Brother or sister of parents** (your uncle/aunt, spouse's uncle/aunt)
- **Parents** (mother, father)
- **Parents' parents** (grandparents)
- **Children and children's spouses** (son, daughter, son-in-law, daughter-in-law)
- **Children's children** (grandchildren)
- **Lineal ascendants and descendants** (great-grandparents, etc.)

**Key relatives NOT in the exempt list**:
- Cousins (not included)
- Nieces/nephews (not explicitly included for all purposes)
- Friends (never exempt above ₹50,000)

> **Note**: "Spouse of brother/sister" is included (brother-in-law, sister-in-law). Check carefully — the legal definition of "relative" is specific.

## Types of Gifts and Tax Treatment

### Cash Gifts

| Giver | Amount | Tax? |
|-------|--------|------|
| Relative (as above) | Any amount | ❌ Exempt |
| Non-relative | ≤ ₹50,000/year | ❌ Exempt |
| Non-relative | > ₹50,000/year | ✅ Full amount taxable at slab rate |

### Immovable Property (Land, House)

If you receive immovable property as gift:

| Situation | Tax Treatment |
|-----------|-------------|
| Gift from relative | ❌ Exempt |
| Gift from non-relative, property FMV > ₹50,000 | ✅ FMV of property taxable (slab rate) |
| Gift at less than stamp duty value (inadequate consideration) | ✅ Difference between stamp duty value and consideration is taxable |

**FMV**: Fair Market Value as determined by a registered valuer, or stamp duty value as a proxy.

### Movable Property (Jewellery, Shares, Art, etc.)

If you receive shares, jewellery, bullion, artwork, antiques, paintings:

| Situation | Tax |
|-----------|-----|
| From relative | ❌ Exempt |
| From non-relative, FMV ≤ ₹50,000/year | ❌ Exempt |
| From non-relative, FMV > ₹50,000 | ✅ Full FMV taxable |
| Received at below FMV from anyone | ✅ Difference between FMV and price paid is taxable |

### Crypto Gifts

- Crypto is a VDA (Virtual Digital Asset) — taxed at **30%** on FMV at time of receipt (if non-relative, above ₹50,000)
- From relatives: Exempt at receipt, but when you sell, your cost basis = FMV at receipt

## When You Buy Something Below Market Price

If you buy property (or other assets) from someone at below FMV, the **difference** may be taxable under Section 56(2)(x):

**Example**:
- Friend sells you a flat worth ₹50 lakh at ₹35 lakh
- Difference: ₹15 lakh — this is a "deemed gift" to you
- If it's ₹15 lakh from a non-relative: Taxable as income (slab rate)

Exception: Transactions between relatives — fully exempt.

## Tax on Using Gifts You Received

When you **later sell** property or assets received as gift:
- Cost of acquisition = the price the original owner paid (stepped-up basis is NOT given in India for gifts)
- Exception: Property received before April 1, 2001 — you can use FMV as on April 1, 2001 as cost

**Holding period for LTCG**: Includes the period of holding by the original giver.

## How to Report Gifts in ITR

### Taxable Gifts

- Report under **"Income from Other Sources"** in Schedule OS of your ITR
- Include in your total taxable income

### Exempt Gifts

- No need to report in ITR if they're from relatives (exempt) or below ₹50,000 (exempt)
- However, large gifts (>₹2 lakh from anyone) may appear in **AIS (Annual Information Statement)** and the IT department may ask for explanation

## Clubbing of Income on Gifts to Spouse

If you transfer assets to your spouse as a "gift" and those assets earn income:
- The income is **clubbed back to you** for tax purposes (Section 64)
- Example: You give ₹5 lakh FD to your spouse — interest is taxable in YOUR hands, not spouse's

**Exception**: Gifts made before marriage, or gifts that are bona fide and consideration exists.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My parents gave me ₹15 lakh for a home down payment. Is it taxable?

No — parents are on the exempt relatives list. Gifts from parents are fully tax-free regardless of amount. Keep a record (family statement or bank transfer description) for documentation if queried.

### I received jewellery worth ₹3 lakh at my wedding from friends. Is it taxable?

No — wedding gifts from anyone (including friends/non-relatives) are fully exempt regardless of amount. Document the wedding date and that gifts were received at the wedding.

### My office colleagues collectively gifted me ₹20,000. Taxable?

If ₹20,000 is the total from all non-relatives in the financial year: below ₹50,000 threshold → tax-free. But if you received other gifts from non-relatives in the same year pushing the total above ₹50,000, the entire amount becomes taxable.

### I received a house as inheritance from grandparents. Is it taxable?

No — inheritance is explicitly exempt from gift tax. No tax at the time of inheritance. When you later sell the inherited property, you pay capital gains tax on the profit (with original owner's cost and holding period used for calculation).
