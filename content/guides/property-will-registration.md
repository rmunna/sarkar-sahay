# How to Register a Will: Process, Documents & Fees

A will (also called a testament) is a legal document that specifies how a person's property and assets should be distributed after death. While registration of a will is **not mandatory** in India, a registered will carries stronger legal validity and is harder to challenge. This guide covers the complete process.

## What is a Will?

A will is governed by the **Indian Succession Act, 1925**. Key legal requirements:

- The person making the will (**testator**) must be of **sound mind** and **above 18 years**
- It must be made **voluntarily**, without coercion or undue influence
- It must be **signed** by the testator
- It must be **attested** by at least **two witnesses** (who must also sign)
- It can be **revoked or modified** at any time during the testator's lifetime

**Important:** A will takes effect only **after the death** of the testator. Until then, the testator retains full ownership and control of the property.

---

## Types of Wills in India

### 1. Simple / Unprivileged Will
- The most common type
- Must be signed by the testator and attested by two witnesses
- Can be handwritten or typed
- Registration is optional but recommended

### 2. Privileged Will
- Available only to soldiers in active service, mariners at sea, and airmen
- Can be oral or written
- Fewer formalities required
- Valid under Sections 65–67 of the Indian Succession Act

### 3. Joint Will
- Made by two or more persons (typically spouses)
- Operates as separate wills for each person
- Takes effect on the death of each testator for their respective share

### 4. Conditional Will
- Takes effect only upon fulfilment of a specified condition
- Must not impose conditions that are illegal or immoral

### 5. Holographic Will
- Entirely handwritten by the testator
- No typing or printing
- Carries strong authenticity but still needs witnesses

## Registered vs Unregistered Will

| Feature | Registered Will | Unregistered Will |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|
| Legal validity | Valid — stronger evidence | Valid — but easier to challenge |
| Registration | Filed at Sub-Registrar's office | Not filed anywhere |
| Proof of existence | Government record exists | May be disputed |
| Tampering risk | Very low — stored in official records | Higher — can be altered or destroyed |
| Cost | Nominal (₹200 – ₹1,000) | Free |
| Probate still needed? | Yes (in applicable cities) | Yes (in applicable cities) |

**Recommendation:** Always register your will. The cost is minimal and it significantly strengthens the document's legal standing.

## Documents Required for Will Registration

- **The will** — typed or handwritten on plain paper (stamp paper is NOT mandatory for wills in most states)
- **ID proof of testator** — [Aadhaar card](/guide/aadhaar-card-apply-online), PAN card, passport, voter ID
- **Address proof** of testator
- **Passport-size photographs** of testator (2) and witnesses (2 each)
- **ID proof of two witnesses** — Aadhaar card, voter ID
- **Property documents** (for reference — sale deed, title deed, etc.)
- **Medical certificate** (recommended, especially for elderly testators — to prove sound mind)

## Step-by-Step Registration Process

### Step 1: Draft the Will
- Clearly mention the testator's full name, address, and age
- List **all properties and assets** — immovable property, bank accounts, investments, jewellery, etc.
- Name the **beneficiaries** clearly with their relationship
- Specify the **share or distribution** for each beneficiary
- Appoint an **executor** to carry out the will's instructions
- Include a clause revoking all previous wills
- Date the will

### Step 2: Sign and Attest
- The testator signs (or puts thumb impression) on every page
- Two witnesses sign on every page in the testator's presence
- Witnesses must not be beneficiaries of the will

### Step 3: Book Appointment at Sub-Registrar's Office
- Visit the Sub-Registrar's office in the jurisdiction where the testator resides
- Some states offer online appointment booking through their IGR portals

### Step 4: Visit Sub-Registrar's Office
- The testator and both witnesses must be physically present
- Carry original ID proofs and the will document
- The Sub-Registrar verifies the identity of all parties

### Step 5: Pay Registration Fee
- Registration fee is nominal — typically ₹200 to ₹1,000
- No stamp duty is required for will registration in most states
- Pay at the counter or online (where available)

### Step 6: Registration
- The Sub-Registrar registers the will and assigns a registration number
- A copy is retained in the official records
- The original registered will is returned to the testator

## Probate of a Will

**What is probate?** Probate is a court order certifying that the will is valid and authentic. It is granted by the High Court or District Court.

**When is probate mandatory?**
- Mandatory in Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai (under the Indian Succession Act)
- Optional but advisable in other cities
- Required by some banks and institutions before releasing assets

**Probate process:**
1. The executor files a petition in the appropriate court
2. Court issues notices to all interested parties
3. If no objections, court grants probate
4. Timeline: 3–12 months
5. Court fee: ad valorem (based on estate value)

## Codicil — How to Modify a Will

A **codicil** is a supplement to an existing will that makes minor modifications without replacing the entire will.

**Key points:**
- Must be executed with the same formalities as a will (signed, witnessed)
- Should be registered if the original will was registered
- Can add new beneficiaries, change shares, or update property details
- Multiple codicils can be added over time
- For major changes, it's better to draft a new will entirely

---

## Important Tips

1. **Keep the will updated** — review and update after major life events (marriage, birth, divorce, property purchase)
2. **Get a medical certificate** — especially if the testator is elderly; this prevents "unsound mind" challenges
3. **Don't make beneficiaries witnesses** — this is a common mistake that can invalidate bequests to them
4. **Inform the executor** — make sure your appointed executor knows the will exists and where it is stored
5. **Register the will** — the cost is minimal and the legal protection is significant

## FAQs

### Is it mandatory to register a will in India?
No, registration is optional under Section 18 of the Registration Act. However, a registered will is harder to challenge and serves as strong evidence of authenticity.

### Can a registered will be changed?
Yes, a testator can revoke or modify a registered will at any time by:
- Making a new registered will (which automatically revokes the old one)
- Adding a codicil
- Destroying the original will with intent to revoke

### Who can be a witness to a will?
Any person of sound mind and above 18 years. However, **beneficiaries should not be witnesses** — while it doesn't invalidate the will, the bequest to a witness-beneficiary becomes void under Section 67.

### Is stamp paper required for a will?
In most states, a will can be written on plain paper. Stamp paper is not mandatory. However, some people use ₹10–₹100 stamp paper as an additional precaution.

### What happens if someone dies without a will?
The property is distributed as per **personal succession laws** — Hindu Succession Act (for Hindus), Indian Succession Act (for Christians and others), or Muslim Personal Law. Legal heirs may need a [succession certificate](/guide/succession-certificate-apply) or [legal heir certificate](/guide/legal-heir-certificate-apply).

### Can an NRI make a will for property in India?
Yes, NRIs can make a will for their Indian property. It can be executed in India or abroad. If executed abroad, it should comply with Indian law for Indian property.

### How long does probate take?
Typically 3–12 months, depending on court workload and whether objections are raised.
