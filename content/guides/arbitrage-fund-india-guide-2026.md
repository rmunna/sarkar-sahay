# Arbitrage Fund India 2026 — Complete Guide

Arbitrage funds are the quiet winners for high-tax-bracket investors parking short-term money. They earn FD-like returns (7–8%) but are taxed like equity funds — which means far lower tax for anyone in the 20–30% bracket.

---

## What Is an Arbitrage Fund?

Arbitrage funds exploit the **price difference between the cash market (buying stocks on NSE/BSE) and the futures market (selling the same stocks in F&O)**.

**Example**:
- HDFC Bank trades at ₹1,700 on NSE cash segment
- HDFC Bank July futures contract trades at ₹1,714
- Price difference: ₹14 per share
- Arbitrage fund: Buy 1,000 shares at ₹1,700 (cash) + Sell 1,000 HDFC Bank futures at ₹1,714
- On expiry, buy back futures and sell cash: Lock in ₹14 × 1,000 = ₹14,000 profit (risk-free)

Since both buy and sell are simultaneous and locked in, the return is essentially risk-free, irrespective of where HDFC Bank's price moves.

**Returns come from**: The spread between cash and futures prices, which is influenced by:
- Interest rates in the economy (higher rates → larger futures premium → better arbitrage returns)
- Market volatility (higher volatility → wider spreads → better opportunities)

## Arbitrage Fund Tax — The Key Advantage

This is why arbitrage funds are popular: **SEBI classifies them as equity funds** (because they hold >65% in equity/equity derivatives). So:

| Holding Period | Tax Rate |
|----------------|---------|
| <12 months | STCG @ 20% |
| >12 months | LTCG @ 12.5% (above ₹1.25L/year) |

**Compare to FD**:
- FD interest is taxed at your **slab rate** (30% for highest bracket)
- Arbitrage fund held 12+ months: LTCG @ 12.5%

**Post-tax return comparison for 30% bracket investor**:

| Instrument | Pre-Tax Return | Tax Rate | Post-Tax Return |
|------------|---------------|---------|----------------|
| FD (6-month to 2-year) | 7.5% | 30% | **5.25%** |
| Liquid Fund | 7% | 30% (slab) | **4.9%** |
| Arbitrage Fund (12+ months) | 7.5% | 12.5% LTCG | **6.56%** |
| Arbitrage Fund (<12 months) | 7.5% | 20% STCG | **6.0%** |

Even the STCG rate (20%) beats FD post-tax at 30% slab. For <6 month money, liquid funds are simpler; for 12+ months, arbitrage funds win clearly.

## Risk in Arbitrage Funds — Is It Really Safe?

Arbitrage funds are considered **very low risk** because:
1. Every buy in the cash market has a simultaneous sell in futures — no directional bet
2. The return is locked in at the time of trade (futures settlement is guaranteed)
3. SEBI mandates strict hedging — no unhedged equity exposure

**Risks that do exist (small)**:
- **Execution risk**: Prices move between clicking buy and sell (milliseconds). Modern funds use algorithms, minimizing this.
- **Settlement risk**: Rare but NSE/BSE settlement failures (not happened in India in decades)
- **Spread compression**: During low-volatility markets, cash-futures spread narrows, returns drop to 5–6%
- **Very short-term NAV fluctuation**: On settlement days (F&O expiry), NAV may show small negative intraday moves — but 1-day blips, not losses

**Practical summary**: In 20+ years of Indian arbitrage funds, there has never been a calendar year with negative returns. The lowest full-year return was ~4.5% (during demonetization low-volatility period).

## Arbitrage Fund vs Liquid Fund vs FD — When to Use Each

| Time Horizon | Best Option | Reason |
|-------------|-------------|--------|
| <3 months | Liquid Fund or Savings Account | Arbitrage = short-term gains taxed at 20%; liquid = slab but returns are identical; for very short, exit load in some arbitrage funds |
| 3–12 months | Arbitrage Fund (STCG 20%) or Liquid Fund | Arbitrage wins if you're in 30% bracket |
| 12–36 months | **Arbitrage Fund** | Clear winner — LTCG 12.5% vs FD at slab rate |
| 3+ years | Debt funds or Balanced Advantage | Longer horizon → can take more risk for better returns |

**Exit load**: Most arbitrage funds have 0.25% exit load if redeemed within 30 days. After 30 days, no exit load. Very liquid compared to FDs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is arbitrage fund better than overnight or liquid fund?

For holding periods under 3 months: liquid fund is simpler (no exit load, same effective tax for <30% bracket). For 3–12+ months in 20–30% bracket: arbitrage fund wins on post-tax return.

### What happens when F&O expiry is in a particular month?

On NSE monthly expiry day (last Thursday), open futures contracts settle. The fund rolls over positions to next month's contract. This causes slight NAV fluctuation on expiry day — normal, not a loss.

### Can I set up SIP in an arbitrage fund?

Yes, but it's unusual. Arbitrage funds work best as lumpsum parking vehicles (you have a lump sum; you park it). SIP of ₹5,000/month in an arbitrage fund is technically fine but you'd be better served by a debt fund or liquid fund for systematic investing.

### Is arbitrage fund SEBI-regulated and safe from AMC bankruptcy?

Yes — SEBI regulates all mutual funds in India. Mutual fund assets are held in a separate trust, not on AMC's balance sheet — even if the AMC shuts down, your fund assets are intact.

### Why don't more people use arbitrage funds?

Lack of awareness. FDs are the default for "safe parking" in India. Once investors understand the tax math (especially in 20–30% brackets), arbitrage funds become the obvious choice for 1–3 year money.
