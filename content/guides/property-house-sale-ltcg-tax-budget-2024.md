# Property Sale LTCG Tax India 2026 — Complete Guide with Budget 2024 Changes

Selling a house or plot in India triggers **Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)** tax if you held it for more than **24 months**. Budget 2024 made a significant change to how this tax is calculated — and for most property sellers, the change requires comparing two options to see which saves more tax.

> **Budget 2024 key change**: LTCG rate reduced from 20% to 12.5%, BUT indexation (inflation adjustment) removed. For properties bought before July 23, 2024, you can choose between old (20% + indexation) and new (12.5%, no indexation) — pick whichever gives lower tax.

## Property LTCG — Basic Rules

| Rule | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Holding period for LTCG** | More than **24 months** (2 years) |
| **Tax rate** | 12.5% (no indexation) or 20% with indexation (for property bought before July 23, 2024) |
| **Holding period for STCG** | Up to 24 months |
| **STCG tax rate** | At your income tax **slab rate** (not 20%) |
| **Cess** | 4% health & education cess on LTCG/STCG |

## Budget 2024 Changes — What Changed?

**Before July 23, 2024**:
- LTCG on property: **20% with indexation**
- Indexation = adjust purchase price for inflation using Cost Inflation Index (CII)

**After July 23, 2024**:
- New rate: **12.5% WITHOUT indexation**
- Indexation benefit removed for new property purchases

**For properties purchased BEFORE July 23, 2024**:
- Special relief provided — you can choose the option that gives lower tax:
  - Option A: 20% with indexation
  - Option B: 12.5% without indexation

**For properties purchased ON OR AFTER July 23, 2024**:
- Only Option B applies: 12.5%, no indexation

## How to Calculate Property LTCG

### Step 1: Determine Cost of Acquisition (Purchase Price)

If held for many years, you may need to use **Fair Market Value (FMV) as on April 1, 2001** for property purchased before that date (instead of actual purchase price).

### Step 2: Apply Indexation (For Option A — Old Rate)

- Indexed Cost = Purchase Price × (CII of Year of Sale ÷ CII of Year of Purchase)
- CII values are published by the government each year

**CII Reference Values (selected years)**:
| Year | CII |
|------|-----|
| 2001-02 | 100 |
| 2010-11 | 167 |
| 2015-16 | 254 |
| 2020-21 | 301 |
| 2023-24 | 348 |
| 2024-25 | 363 |
| 2025-26 | 376 (provisional) |

### Step 3: Calculate LTCG

**Option A (20% with indexation)**:
`LTCG = Sale Price − Indexed Cost − Transfer Expenses`
`Tax = LTCG × 20%`

**Option B (12.5% without indexation)**:
`LTCG = Sale Price − Actual Purchase Price − Transfer Expenses`
`Tax = LTCG × 12.5%`

### Worked Example

Bought flat in 2015 for ₹50,00,000. Sold in 2026 for ₹1,20,00,000.

**Transfer expenses** (stamp duty, brokerage): ₹3,00,000

**Option A (20% + indexation)**:
- CII 2015-16: 254; CII 2025-26: 376
- Indexed Cost = ₹50L × (376/254) = ₹74,02,000
- Gain = ₹1,20L − ₹74,02,000 − ₹3L = **₹42,98,000**
- Tax = ₹42,98,000 × 20% = **₹8,59,600** (+ cess ₹34,384 = ₹8,93,984)

**Option B (12.5%, no indexation)**:
- Actual Cost = ₹50,00,000
- Gain = ₹1,20L − ₹50L − ₹3L = **₹67,00,000**
- Tax = ₹67,00,000 × 12.5% = **₹8,37,500** (+ cess ₹33,500 = ₹8,71,000)

**Winner: Option B** — saves ₹22,984 in this case. But the difference varies. Always calculate both.

> **Rule of thumb**: The longer you held the property and the higher inflation was during that period, the more likely Option A (indexation) saves more tax.

## When to Choose Option A vs Option B

| Holding Period | Likely Better Option |
|---------------|---------------------|
| Bought 2001-2010, sold 2026 | Option A (20% + indexation) likely wins — high inflation period |
| Bought 2015-2020, sold 2026 | Calculate both — roughly equal |
| Bought after July 23, 2024 | Only Option B available |
| Bought at very low price long ago | Option A wins (indexation multiplies heavily) |

## How to Save Tax on Property LTCG

### 1. Section 54 — Invest in Residential Property

If you sell a **residential property** and buy/construct another **residential house**:
- **Full exemption** if LTCG invested in new property
- **Partial exemption** if only part of LTCG invested (proportionate)
- Timeline: Buy 1 year **before** or 2 years **after** sale, or construct within 3 years of sale
- Only 1 property (previously; Budget 2023 allowed up to 2 properties if LTCG ≤ ₹2 crore — one-time option)
- Property must be in India

**Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS)**: If you can't use the gain before ITR filing, deposit the LTCG amount in a Capital Gains Account at a bank. This preserves the Section 54 exemption while you search for property.

### 2. Section 54EC — Invest in Capital Gains Bonds

Invest LTCG in **NHAI/REC bonds** (Section 54EC bonds):
- Investment limit: ₹50,00,000 (₹50 lakh)
- Lock-in: 5 years
- Interest rate: ~5.25% (taxable at slab rate)
- Must invest within **6 months** of property sale

**Catch**: Not very tax-efficient since bond interest is taxable. Works if you don't want to buy another property.

### 3. Section 54F — Sell Non-Residential Property, Buy Residential

If you sell **land or commercial property** and buy a **residential house**:
- Exemption proportionate to amount invested in new house vs net consideration (sale price)
- All net consideration invested = full exemption

### 4. Offset Against Capital Losses

LTCG from property can be offset against:
- Long-term capital losses from other assets (stocks sold at loss, etc.)
- No STCG set-off against LTCG

## Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on Property Sale

When you sell property for **more than ₹50 lakh**, the buyer must deduct **1% TDS** on the sale price:
- Buyer files Form 26QB (not you)
- TDS certificate: Form 16B (buyer gives you this)
- This TDS is credited against your LTCG tax

If the buyer doesn't deduct TDS, it's their legal responsibility. But ensure you check Form 26AS after the deal closes.

## Inherited / Gifted Property

| Situation | Tax Treatment |
|-----------|-------------|
| Inherited property | No tax at inheritance; when you sell, LTCG based on original owner's purchase date and price |
| Gifted property | No tax at gift receipt; selling taxed based on donor's purchase date and cost |
| Holding period | Includes previous owner's holding period for LTCG classification |

## ITR Filing for Property LTCG

- File **ITR-2** (salaried with capital gains) or **ITR-3** (business owner)
- Report in **Schedule CG** → Long-Term Capital Gains from property
- For Option A: Report in "Property with indexation" column
- For Option B: Report in "Property without indexation" column
- Section 54/54EC/54F exemption: Mention in Schedule CG under exemption claimed

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I sold ancestral property my grandfather bought for very little in 1980. How to calculate cost?

Use **Fair Market Value as on April 1, 2001** as the cost of acquisition (the government allows this as the base for all properties held before that date). Get a registered valuer's certificate for the April 1, 2001 value. Then apply indexation from 2001-02 CII (100) to current year.

### I'm reinvesting in a new property but the deal isn't done by July 31 ITR deadline. What to do?

Open a **Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS)** at a bank before July 31. Deposit the LTCG amount. ITR filing shows CGAS deposit as proof you're utilizing Section 54. You then use the CGAS balance to buy property within the 2-year/3-year window.

### Is there any exemption limit for LTCG on property (like the ₹1.25L for equity MF)?

No — unlike equity mutual funds (₹1.25L annual LTCG exemption), there is **no exemption limit** for property LTCG. The entire LTCG is taxable unless you use Section 54/54EC/54F.

### My property was in joint names (myself and spouse). How is LTCG split?

LTCG is split based on the ownership proportion stated in the sale deed (usually 50-50 for joint property). Each co-owner reports their share in their respective ITRs.
