# Intraday Trading Tax India 2026 — How Stock Market Income Is Taxed

Stock market income in India doesn't have a single tax rate — the treatment depends on how you traded: intraday (buy-sell same day), delivery-based (hold overnight), or F&O (futures and options). Each has completely different tax rules.

## Three Types of Stock Market Income

| Trading Type | Tax Classification | Tax Rate |
|-------------|-------------------|---------|
| **Intraday equity trading** | Speculative Business Income | Slab rate (0%–30%) |
| **Delivery-based equity (short-term)** | Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG) | 20% (Budget 2024 change) |
| **Delivery-based equity (long-term >12 months)** | Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG) | 12.5% above ₹1.25L |
| **F&O (Futures and Options)** | Non-Speculative Business Income | Slab rate (0%–30%) |
| **Intraday currency/commodity trading** | Speculative Business Income | Slab rate |

**Budget 2024 Change**: STCG on listed equity was raised from 15% to **20%** effective July 23, 2024.

## Intraday Equity Trading — Speculative Business

When you buy and sell a stock **within the same trading day** (no delivery taken):
- This is **speculative business income** under Section 43(5)
- Taxed at your **income tax slab rate** (0%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, or 30% depending on new/old regime and total income)

**Speculative loss**: Can only be set off against speculative income. If intraday trading results in a loss:
- Cannot be set off against salary income, rental income, or capital gains
- Can be set off against speculative profits in the same year
- Carry forward: 4 years (only against future speculative income)

**Key point**: Even if you're salaried, intraday trading makes you a "trader" — you must file **ITR-3** (cannot use ITR-1 or ITR-2).

## Delivery-Based Equity — Capital Gains

When you buy shares and hold them **overnight or longer**:
- Short-term (held ≤ 12 months): **STCG at 20%** (Budget 2024, previously 15%)
- Long-term (held > 12 months): **LTCG at 12.5%** above ₹1.25L threshold (Budget 2024 raised from ₹1L at 10%)

**LTCG with ₹1.25L threshold**: Your first ₹1.25L of LTCG per year is exempt. Only gains above ₹1.25L are taxed at 12.5%.

**Important**: LTCG is taxed even in new tax regime. Section 87A rebate does NOT apply to LTCG (income tax.gov.in confirmed this from AY 2024-25).

For delivery trading: Use **ITR-2** if only delivery (no intraday/F&O). If you also have intraday or F&O: Use **ITR-3**.

## F&O (Futures and Options) — Non-Speculative Business

F&O trading is classified as **non-speculative business income** (not speculative, even though it sounds like speculation):
- Taxed at income tax slab rate (same as salary — added to your total income)
- F&O profit + loss from different contracts in the same year can be netted

**F&O losses**:
- Can be set off against any non-speculative business income (not speculative income though)
- Can be set off against salary? → **No** — business loss generally cannot be set off against salary
- But can offset capital gains, rental income, and other business income
- Carry forward: 8 years (offset against business income)

**F&O turnover for audit purposes**: 
- Turnover = Sum of (positive differences + negative differences) on all F&O contracts
- NOT the total contract value
- Tax audit required if F&O turnover > ₹10 crore (for non-cash) OR profit < 6%/8% of turnover (presumptive taxation not available for F&O)

## Turnover Calculation for Tax Audit

| Trading Type | Turnover Definition |
|-------------|---------------------|
| **Intraday equity** | Absolute profit + absolute loss (sum of positive + negative differences) |
| **Delivery equity** | Full sale value of shares sold |
| **F&O** | Absolute positive + negative differences on each contract settlement |

**Tax audit requirement** (Section 44AB):
- Turnover > ₹10 crore (if 95%+ transactions are digital): Audit required
- Turnover > ₹2 crore (below 95% digital): Audit required
- Profit < 6%/8% of turnover (presumptive won't apply to traders): Regular books + audit if turnover > ₹2C

Most small traders don't reach ₹10 crore in F&O turnover. But professional traders do.

## ITR Form for Stock Traders

| Trading Activity | ITR Form |
|-----------------|---------|
| Only delivery-based (no intraday/F&O) | ITR-2 |
| Intraday trading (any amount) | ITR-3 |
| F&O trading (any amount) | ITR-3 |
| Combination of delivery + intraday/F&O | ITR-3 |

**Can a stock trader use ITR-4 (Sugam/Presumptive)?** No — Section 44AD/ADA presumptive schemes do not apply to trading income. Must use ITR-3.

## Expenses Allowed for F&O / Intraday Traders

As a "business income" taxpayer, you can deduct actual business expenses:
- Brokerage and transaction charges
- Internet charges (proportionate)
- Computer/software subscriptions used for trading
- Advisory/research subscriptions
- Home office expenses (proportionate)
- STT (Securities Transaction Tax) paid — deductible against business income (not capital gains)

**For capital gains (delivery trading)**: Only cost of acquisition is deductible. No brokerage or other expenses.

## STT — Securities Transaction Tax

**STT** is levied at the exchange level:
- Equity delivery buy: 0.1%; Equity delivery sell: 0.1%
- Equity intraday sell: 0.025%
- F&O — Futures sell: 0.02% on sell side; Options sell: 0.1% on premium

STT is NOT an income tax. It's automatically collected by your broker.

For capital gains tax calculations: STT is NOT deductible from capital gains.
For business income (intraday/F&O): STT is deductible as a business expense.

## Tax-Loss Harvesting in Equity

If you have unrealized LTCG (on stocks held >12 months), selling and immediately rebuying to realize the ₹1.25L annual exemption is called **tax-loss harvesting** (technically gain harvesting here):
- Sell stocks with ₹1.25L unrealized LTCG, buy them back immediately
- Reset cost basis to current price
- First ₹1.25L of LTCG is exempt — save 12.5% × ₹1.25L = ₹15,625
- India has NO wash-sale rule (unlike the US) — you can sell and repurchase immediately

## Practical Example

**Salaried person (₹15L salary) with trading activity**:
- Intraday profit: ₹30,000
- F&O loss: ₹1,50,000
- Delivery LTCG: ₹2,00,000 (>12 months)
- Delivery STCG: ₹80,000

**Tax computation**:
- Intraday profit ₹30,000: Speculative business income — taxable at slab rate
- F&O loss ₹1,50,000: Non-speculative business loss — can offset intraday profit? No (intraday is speculative, F&O is non-speculative, they cannot offset each other). Carry forward F&O loss for 8 years.
- LTCG ₹2,00,000: ₹1,25,000 exempt; ₹75,000 taxable at 12.5% = ₹9,375
- STCG ₹80,000: 20% = ₹16,000

Total additional tax from trading: ₹9,375 + ₹16,000 + slab rate tax on ₹30,000 (intraday)

**F&O losses cannot offset salary income** — common misconception. F&O is business income; salary is a separate head. Losses from business can be carried forward but not set off against salary.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I lost ₹5L in F&O trading this year. My salary is ₹20L. Can I reduce my tax?

No — F&O business losses cannot be set off against salary income. Your ₹5L loss is carried forward for 8 years (against future non-speculative business income). Your ₹20L salary is taxed normally. This is the "trap" many traders fall into — they expect to net losses against salary, but the law doesn't allow it.

### I do delivery trading and have only 3-4 sell transactions a year. Is it still "business income"?

With only 3-4 transactions, delivery trading is typically "investment" (capital gains). The IT Department uses the frequency, holding period, and intent to determine if it's business or investment. Occasional buy-hold-sell is capital gains. Frequent churning with investment intent is treated as business. Rare delivery trades → Capital gains (ITR-2).

### My broker shows STT deducted. Does this reduce my LTCG tax?

No. STT is a separate tax and does not reduce your LTCG calculation. STT is deducted at source by the exchange; you don't need to separately track or deduct it from capital gains for tax purposes.
