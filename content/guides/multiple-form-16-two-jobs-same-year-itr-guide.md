# Multiple Form 16 India 2026 — How to File ITR When You Changed Jobs

Changing jobs during the financial year is common — but it creates a tax complication most people don't expect: **each employer deducts TDS independently without knowing what the other paid you**.

This almost always leads to a **TDS shortfall** and a **tax demand notice** if you don't handle it correctly.

## The Core Problem: Dual Employer TDS

**Employer A** (Jan–June): Pays you ₹10L, deducts TDS as if that's your only income for the full year.
**Employer B** (July–March): Pays you ₹14L, also deducts TDS as if that's your only income for the full year.

Each employer gives you the benefit of the full basic exemption (₹3L for new regime or ₹2.5L for old regime) and full standard deduction (₹75K).

**The result**: You've been given double basic exemption + double standard deduction, and your combined ₹24L income has been taxed at lower effective rates than it should.

**When you file ITR**, the IT Department sees your total income as ₹24L and calculates the actual tax — creating a demand for the difference.

## How Much Is the TDS Shortfall? (Example)

**FY 2025-26, New Regime, 0–5 bracket**

| | Employer A's Calculation | Employer B's Calculation | Correct Calculation |
|-|------------------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| Income | ₹10,00,000 | ₹14,00,000 | ₹24,00,000 |
| Standard deduction | ₹75,000 | ₹75,000 | ₹75,000 (once) |
| Taxable income | ₹9,25,000 | ₹13,25,000 | ₹23,25,000 |
| Tax (new regime) | ~₹67,500 | ~₹1,47,500 | **~₹3,47,500** |
| Total TDS deducted | ₹67,500 + ₹1,47,500 = ₹2,15,000 | — | ~₹3,47,500 |
| **Shortfall** | — | — | **~₹1,32,500** |

You'll owe approximately ₹1.32L when filing ITR. Plus Section 234B/C interest if advance tax wasn't paid.

## Disclosing Previous Employer Salary to New Employer (The Right Move)

**Section 192(2)**: When joining a new employer, you **can (and should) give them your Form 12B** — declaring your previous employer's salary details. The new employer then deducts TDS on the combined income, avoiding the shortfall.

**How to disclose**:
1. Get a Form 16 or salary slip showing the income from previous employer
2. Fill **Form 12B** (Declaration of salary from previous employer)
3. Submit to new employer's payroll/HR team

Most employees skip this — which means a tax demand at ITR time. If you've already changed jobs without disclosing, you'll need to handle it at ITR filing time.

## How to File ITR with Two Form 16s

### Step 1: Collect Both Form 16s
- **Part A**: TDS data (tax deducted, PAN, TAN of employer)
- **Part B**: Income breakdown (salary, perquisites, deductions, taxable amount)

Both employers must issue Form 16 by **June 15** of the next FY.

### Step 2: Go to incometax.gov.in — Pre-Filled ITR

The Income Tax portal pre-fills ITR with:
- Salary income from both employers (from annual information returns employers file)
- TDS from both employers
- AIS/Form 26AS data

Check the pre-filled values match Form 16 Part A data.

### Step 3: Add Both Salaries Under "Salary Schedule"

In ITR-1 or ITR-2:
- Under Schedule S (Salary), there's a place to add multiple salary incomes
- Add Employer A's total salary (from their Form 16 Part B)
- Add Employer B's total salary (from their Form 16 Part B)
- Apply standard deduction only **once** (₹75K for AY 2026-27 under new regime; ₹50K old regime)

### Step 4: Add Both TDS Credits

Under the TDS section, add:
- TDS from Employer A (matching Form 16 Part A)
- TDS from Employer B (matching Form 16 Part B)

Both should be pre-filled from 26AS. Verify they match.

### Step 5: Check Net Tax Liability

The portal calculates total tax on combined income minus total TDS. If there's a shortfall:
- Pay the difference as **Self Assessment Tax** (Challan 280, Type 300)
- Add the payment in ITR under "Tax Payments" before submitting

### Step 6: Also Pay Advance Tax Interest

If TDS shortfall > ₹10K, you likely owe **Section 234B interest** (1%/month from April 1 of AY to ITR filing date). The portal calculates this automatically.

## What About Deductions?

Apply deductions **once** based on what you actually claimed:

| Deduction | How to Handle |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Standard deduction** | Only ₹75K total (not from each employer separately) |
| **Section 80C** | Total investments during the year (once, regardless of employers) |
| **HRA** | Claim based on actual rent paid and actual HRA received |
| **80D (health insurance)** | Premium paid during the year |

**The portal typically handles this correctly** when you enter each employer's Part B data — it won't double-count standard deduction. But verify.

## HRA With Job Change — Special Consideration

If you received HRA from both employers:
- Employer A's HRA may be exempt (based on A's salary + your rent during Jan–June)
- Employer B's HRA may be exempt (based on B's salary + your rent during July–March)

Calculate each employer's HRA exemption separately for the months they paid you. Total HRA exemption is the sum of both periods.

## What If You Didn't Get Form 16 from Previous Employer?

**Employers are legally required to issue Form 16 by June 15.**

If they don't:
1. Email/write to HR requesting Form 16 formally
2. Check your AIS and Form 26AS on incometax.gov.in — it shows TDS credit from all employers

You can file using:
- Your salary slips (to calculate gross income)
- Form 26AS (for TDS credits)
- Bank statements (for confirmation)

If TDS was deducted but Form 16 not issued, the credit is still valid in 26AS.

## Interest for Late/Short TDS — Section 234

The TDS shortfall from dual employers creates two issues:

1. **Section 234B**: If total advance tax paid (via TDS from both employers + any advance tax you paid yourself) was less than 90% of actual tax → 1% per month interest from April 1 of AY to ITR date

2. **Section 234C**: If TDS fell short of quarterly thresholds (15% by June 15, 45% by September 15, etc.) → 1% per month for each quarter

Both are auto-calculated by the portal. Add them to your payment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I changed jobs. Can I ask my new employer to adjust TDS from the old employer's income?

Yes — this is the correct approach. Submit Form 12B to the new employer with previous employer's income details. Your new employer will calculate TDS on the combined income for the remaining months. This avoids the shortfall at ITR time.

### I have two Form 16s. Which ITR form should I use?

Use **ITR-1** if your combined income is below ₹50L (salary + interest only). Use **ITR-2** if you have any capital gains. With just two Form 16s (no other income), ITR-1 is fine for most salaried job-changers.

### My previous employer deducted TDS but I never got the money — and they shut down. How do I handle it?

Check Form 26AS. If the TDS deposit shows in 26AS (deposited to government), your credit is valid and you can claim it in ITR even without Form 16. If the employer didn't deposit TDS after deducting (which is an employer's crime), file Form 26AS showing "nil" and report the issue to the tax department. You're not liable for the employer's failure to deposit.

### My combined income from two jobs is ₹30L (new regime). What's my estimated additional tax to pay?

New regime tax on ₹30L (₹23,25,000 after single standard deduction ₹75K):
- ₹0–₹4L: 0%
- ₹4L–₹8L: 5% = ₹20,000
- ₹8L–₹12L: 10% = ₹40,000
- ₹12L–₹16L: 15% = ₹60,000
- ₹16L–₹20L: 20% = ₹80,000
- ₹20L–₹23.25L: 25% = ₹81,250

Total tax: ~₹2,81,250. Subtract what both employers collectively deducted from TDS. The difference is what you pay as self-assessment tax.
