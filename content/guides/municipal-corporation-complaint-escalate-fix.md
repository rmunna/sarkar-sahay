# Municipal Corporation Complaint Not Resolved — How to Escalate

You filed a complaint with your municipal corporation — garbage not collected, road not repaired, water supply disrupted, drainage overflowing, or street lights not working. Weeks have passed. Nothing happened. Now what?

This guide gives you a clear escalation path — from online grievance portals to RTI applications, elected representatives, and consumer court — to get your municipal corporation to act.

## Common Municipal Complaints That Go Unresolved

Before diving into escalation steps, here are the most common municipal service failures that citizens face:

- **Garbage not collected** — irregular or no door-to-door collection, overflowing community bins
- **Road repair** — potholes, broken footpaths, unpaved streets
- **Water supply** — irregular supply, low pressure, contaminated water, no new connection
- **Drainage and sewage** — blocked drains, sewage overflow, open manholes
- **Street lights** — non-functional lights, no lights in new areas
- **Building permission delays** — pending approvals, no response on applications
- **Property tax disputes** — wrong assessment, excessive charges
- **Encroachment** — illegal construction on public land, blocked footpaths

If you've already complained to the local ward office or municipal helpline and got no resolution within **15–30 days**, it's time to escalate.

## Step 1: File on the Official Online Grievance Portal

Every major municipal corporation in India has an online complaint system. If you haven't used it yet, start here — online complaints create a traceable record.

### How to File

1. Visit your city's municipal grievance portal (see state-wise list below)
2. Register with your mobile number and Aadhaar/email
3. Select the **complaint category** (sanitation, roads, water, etc.)
4. Describe the issue clearly — include **ward number, street name, and landmark**
5. Upload photos or videos as evidence
6. Note down the **complaint/ticket number** — this is your proof

### Important Tips

- Be specific: "Garbage not collected from Ward 42, Street 7, near Government School since 15 Feb 2026" is better than "garbage problem"
- Always upload photos — complaints with evidence get faster attention
- Set a follow-up reminder for **7 days** after filing

## Step 2: Escalate to CPGRAMS (Central Government Portal)

If the municipal portal doesn't work, escalate to **CPGRAMS (Centralized Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System)** — a central government grievance portal that forwards complaints to the relevant department and tracks them.

### How to File on CPGRAMS

1. Visit [pgportal.gov.in](https://pgportal.gov.in)
2. Click **"Lodge Public Grievance"**
3. Register or login with your mobile number
4. Select **Ministry:** Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (for municipal issues)
5. Or select the **relevant state government department** if it's a state municipal body
6. Write a clear description with your previous complaint number, dates, and what happened
7. Upload supporting documents (previous complaint receipt, photos)
8. Submit and note the **CPGRAMS Registration Number**

### What Happens Next

- CPGRAMS forwards your complaint to the concerned department
- The department **must respond within 30 days**
- You can track status at [pgportal.gov.in](https://pgportal.gov.in)
- If the response is unsatisfactory, you can **request reopening** with reasons

> **Note:** CPGRAMS is very effective because it's monitored at the central level. Departments take it seriously.

## Step 4: File an RTI Application

If the municipality is not responding at all — no rejection, no action, just silence — the **Right to Information Act** is your strongest tool. An RTI forces a response.

### What to Ask in RTI

File an RTI application to the **Municipal Commissioner** asking:

1. What is the current status of my complaint (reference number: ___)?
2. Which officer is responsible for resolving this complaint?
3. What action has been taken so far?
4. What is the reason for the delay?
5. What is the expected date of resolution?

### How to File RTI — Step by Step

**Online (Central Government bodies):**
1. Visit [rtionline.gov.in](https://rtionline.gov.in)
2. Click "Submit Request"
3. Select the appropriate **Public Authority** (Municipal Corporation)
4. Write your questions clearly
5. Pay ₹10 fee online
6. Submit and save the registration number

**Offline (State/Municipal bodies):**
1. Write an application on plain paper addressed to the **Public Information Officer (PIO)** of the Municipal Corporation
2. Write: "Under the Right to Information Act 2005, I request the following information..."
3. List your questions
4. Attach a ₹10 postal order or court fee stamp (varies by state)
5. Send by registered post or submit in person at the municipal office
6. Keep the acknowledgment receipt

### RTI Timeline

- **PIO must respond within 30 days** of receiving the application
- If no response, file a **First Appeal** to the Appellate Authority (usually a senior officer in the same municipal body) within 30 days
- If still no response, file a **Second Appeal** with the State Information Commission

> **Pro tip:** The mere act of filing an RTI often triggers action. Officials don't want to be found non-compliant.

## Step 5: Approach Your Elected Representative

Your **elected corporator (ward member)** and **MLA** can be very effective in getting municipal issues resolved. They have direct influence over municipal administration.

### How to Approach

**Ward Corporator / Councillor:**
- Visit your ward councillor's office with a written complaint
- Bring photos and your previous complaint receipts
- Ask them to raise it in the **municipal council meeting**
- Corporators have allocated **ward development funds** that can be used for local infrastructure

**MLA (Member of Legislative Assembly):**
- If the corporator is unhelpful, approach your MLA
- Visit during their **weekly public meeting (jan sampark)**
- Write a formal letter describing the issue and municipal inaction
- MLAs can direct the District Magistrate to take action
- MLA fund (MLALAD) can be used for civic infrastructure

**MP (Member of Parliament):**
- For large-scale or systemic issues
- MPLAD funds can be used for civic infrastructure
- Write to your MP with full documentation

### Tips

- Always go with a **group of residents** — collective complaints get more attention
- Bring **signed petitions** from your locality
- Follow up in writing after every meeting

## Step 6: Consumer Court for Municipal Services

If a municipal corporation fails to provide basic services you're entitled to, you can approach the **Consumer Court** (Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission).

### When to Use Consumer Court

- Water connection denied or delayed without reason (see our [water connection rejection guide](/guide/water-connection-application-rejected-fix) for specific fixes)
- Sewage or drainage causing health hazard despite complaints
- Property tax overcharged and refund denied
- Any paid municipal service not delivered

### How to File

1. Visit [consumerhelpline.gov.in](https://consumerhelpline.gov.in) or the **e-Daakhil** portal at [edaakhil.nic.in](https://edaakhil.nic.in)
2. Register and file a complaint against the Municipal Corporation
3. Attach all evidence: complaint receipts, RTI responses, photos, dates
4. **Filing fee:** ₹0 for claims up to ₹5 lakh; ₹200 for ₹5–10 lakh; higher for bigger claims
5. You can file in person at the **District Consumer Forum** without a lawyer

### What You Can Claim

- **Compensation** for damages caused by municipal negligence
- **Direction** to the municipality to perform the service
- **Costs** of the legal proceedings

> Consumer courts have ruled against municipal corporations multiple times for service failure. Precedents are strong.

## Tips to Make Your Complaint More Effective

1. **Always file in writing** — verbal complaints have no trail. Use online portals where possible
2. **Include evidence** — photos, videos, dates, and location details strengthen your case
3. **Reference previous complaints** — mention ticket numbers and dates in every escalation
4. **Go as a group** — resident welfare associations (RWAs) carry more weight than individual complaints
5. **Use social media** — tag the municipal corporation's official Twitter/X handle with photos. Many cities have social media response teams
6. **Keep copies of everything** — every receipt, every complaint number, every response

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should I wait before escalating a municipal complaint?
Give the municipal corporation **15 days** for routine complaints (garbage, street lights) and **30 days** for infrastructure work (road repair, drainage). If no action within this time, escalate.

### Can I file a complaint against a municipal corporation on CPGRAMS?
Yes. CPGRAMS accepts complaints against both central and state government bodies, including municipal corporations. Select the appropriate state government department when filing.

### Is there a cost to file an RTI application?
Yes, ₹10 for the central government. For state-level bodies (like municipal corporations), the fee is ₹10 in most states but may vary. BPL cardholders are exempt from RTI fees.

### Can I go to consumer court against a municipal corporation?
Yes. Municipal corporations are considered **service providers** under the Consumer Protection Act 2019. If they fail to provide services you're entitled to or have paid for, you can file a consumer complaint.

### What if the municipal corporation says "no budget" for repairs?
This is not a valid excuse for denying basic services. File an RTI asking for the ward's budget allocation and expenditure details. Escalate to the District Magistrate through CPGRAMS or CM Helpline.

### Do I need a lawyer to file a consumer court complaint?
No. Consumer courts are designed for self-representation. You can file and argue your case yourself. The process is straightforward, especially for claims under ₹5 lakh.

### What is the difference between Nagar Nigam, Nagar Palika, and Nagar Panchayat?
They are all urban local bodies but serve different population sizes: **Nagar Nigam** (Municipal Corporation) for large cities, **Nagar Palika** (Municipality/Municipal Council) for medium towns, and **Nagar Panchayat** (Town Panchayat) for smaller towns. The complaint process is similar for all.

### Can my RWA file complaints on behalf of residents?
Yes. Registered Resident Welfare Associations can file complaints, RTIs, and even consumer court cases on behalf of their members. Collective complaints from RWAs often get faster response.
