# ESOP and RSU Taxation India 2026 — Two Tax Events, ITR Declaration, and Foreign Stock Guide

If you work for a company that gives you **ESOPs (Employee Stock Options)** or **RSUs (Restricted Stock Units)**, you face tax at two separate points:

1. **At vesting/exercise**: Taxed as **salary income** (perquisite)
2. **At sale**: Taxed as **capital gains**

This guide explains both events, how TDS works, how to declare in ITR, and the special rules for foreign company ESOPs.

## ESOP vs RSU — The Difference

| | **ESOP (Employee Stock Option)** | **RSU (Restricted Stock Unit)** |
|-|---------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| What you get | Right to BUY shares at a fixed (exercise/strike) price | Actual shares, given free |
| When you pay for shares | At exercise (you pay the strike price) | Never — shares given at vesting |
| Tax event 1 | At **exercise** (when you actually buy the shares) | At **vesting** (when shares are allotted) |
| Tax on event 1 | FMV − strike price = perquisite, taxed as salary | FMV at vesting = perquisite, taxed as salary |
| Tax event 2 | At **sale** (capital gains on sale price − FMV at exercise) | At **sale** (capital gains on sale price − FMV at vesting) |

## Tax Event 1 — Perquisite at Exercise/Vesting

### For ESOPs

When you exercise an ESOP (pay the strike price and get shares):

**Perquisite value** = Fair Market Value (FMV) on exercise date − Strike price paid

This perquisite is added to your salary income and taxed at your slab rate.

**Example**:
- Strike price: ₹100/share
- FMV on exercise date: ₹800/share
- Shares exercised: 1,000
- Perquisite = (₹800 − ₹100) × 1,000 = **₹7,00,000** added to salary

If you're in the 30% slab: Tax = ₹2,10,000 at exercise.

### For RSUs

When RSUs vest (shares are allotted to you at zero cost):

**Perquisite value** = FMV on vesting date × number of shares vested

**Example**:
- RSU vesting: 500 shares
- FMV on vesting date: ₹2,000/share
- Perquisite = 500 × ₹2,000 = **₹10,00,000** added to salary

Your employer deducts TDS on this ₹10 lakh as part of salary TDS (Form 16 will reflect it).

### FMV Determination

| Company Type | FMV on Exercise/Vesting Date |
|-------------|------------------------------|
| Listed Indian company | Closing market price (NSE/BSE) on that date |
| Unlisted Indian company | Value determined by a SEBI-registered merchant banker (Category I) |
| Listed foreign company (NYSE, NASDAQ, etc.) | Market price on foreign exchange, converted to INR using SBI TT rate on that date |

## Tax Event 2 — Capital Gains at Sale

When you sell the shares you received:

**Cost of acquisition for capital gains = FMV that was already taxed as perquisite** (not zero, not the strike price — the FMV on exercise/vesting date).

**Capital gain = Sale price − FMV at exercise/vesting**

### Holding Period and Capital Gains Rate

For **Indian listed company** shares:
| Holding Period | Type | Rate |
|----------------|------|------|
| > 12 months (held from exercise/vesting to sale) | LTCG | 12.5% above ₹1.25L exemption |
| ≤ 12 months | STCG | 20% |

For **foreign company** shares:
| Holding Period | Type | Rate |
|----------------|------|------|
| > 24 months | LTCG | 12.5% (no indexation) |
| ≤ 24 months | STCG | Slab rate |

**Example (Indian listed company)**:
- ESOP exercised March 2024; FMV = ₹800/share
- Sold April 2026 (>12 months from exercise) at ₹1,200/share
- Capital gain = ₹1,200 − ₹800 = ₹400/share → LTCG at 12.5%

## TDS by Employer

Your employer deducts TDS on the **perquisite value** as part of regular salary TDS:

- For Indian company ESOPs: TDS at your slab rate on the perquisite added to salary
- This appears in your **Form 16** under "Value of Perquisites"

**For listed company ESOPs of startups**: There's a special deferral — tax is deferred to the earlier of: 5 years from exercise, date of sale, or when you leave the company. This is specifically for DPIIT-recognized startups' employees.

## How to Declare ESOP/RSU in ITR

### Indian Company ESOPs

1. **Perquisite income**: Already included in Form 16 salary. No separate entry needed.
2. **Capital gains at sale**: Schedule CG (Capital Gains) in ITR-2 or ITR-3.
   - Short-term: Schedule CG → "STCG on securities (other than 111A)" or STCG 111A (if listed equity)
   - Long-term: Schedule CG → LTCG 112A (listed equity) or LTCG 112 (others)

### Foreign Company ESOPs/RSUs (US, UK, Singapore, etc.)

**Additional requirement**: You must also report **foreign assets** in **Schedule FA** (Foreign Assets) in your ITR.

In Schedule FA, report:
- **Country**: US (for US company stocks)
- **Name of entity**: Company name
- **Nature of interest**: Employee/beneficiary (ESOP/RSU)
- **Date of acquisition**: Vesting/exercise date
- **Total investment**: Cost basis (FMV on vesting/exercise, in INR)
- **Highest value during year**: Peak value
- **Closing value at year end**: FMV × shares as on March 31

**This is mandatory even if you sold the shares within the year.** Failure to declare foreign assets in Schedule FA can lead to ₹10 lakh penalty under the Black Money Act.

### Foreign Capital Gains Reporting

For gains from selling foreign company shares:
- Report in Schedule CG
- STCG (<24 months): Schedule OS or CG at slab rate
- LTCG (>24 months): 12.5% without indexation
- Also report in **Schedule FSI** (Foreign Source Income) and **Schedule TR** (Tax Relief) if tax was already withheld in the foreign country

## Common Errors to Avoid

| Error | Correct Approach |
|-------|-----------------|
| Declaring perquisite as capital gain (not salary) | Perquisite at exercise/vesting is salary income — shows in Form 16 |
| Using strike price as cost basis for capital gains | Cost basis = FMV at exercise/vesting (already taxed as salary) |
| Forgetting Schedule FA for foreign ESOPs | Always file Schedule FA if you hold foreign stocks |
| Not converting foreign FMV to INR | Use SBI TT buying rate on the transaction date |
| Ignoring vested but unsold RSUs in Schedule FA | Must report the value of vested unsold shares as foreign asset |

## Startup ESOP — Special Tax Deferral

If your employer is a **DPIIT-recognized startup** and the ESOP is from a startup, the perquisite tax is deferred:

- Tax is due at the **earliest of**:
  1. 5 years from exercise
  2. Date you sell the shares
  3. Date you leave the company

**What this means**: You exercise ESOPs in 2024 from a DPIIT startup — you don't pay salary tax immediately. If you sell in 2026, tax on the 2024 perquisite is due at that time.

This prevents a situation where the employee owes large tax but has illiquid shares.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I got RSUs from my US employer. My company withholds tax in the US at vesting. Do I pay tax again in India?

Yes — India taxes the RSU perquisite as salary income when you are resident in India. US withholding and Indian salary tax are independent. However, you can claim a **DTAA credit** for the US tax withheld. Declare in Schedule FSI (foreign income) and Schedule TR (tax relief). This avoids true double taxation — you get credit for what you paid in the US.

### My ESOPs vested but I haven't exercised them. Is there any tax?

No — unvested and unexercised options are not taxable. Tax event 1 (perquisite) only happens at **exercise** (for ESOPs) or **vesting** (for RSUs).

### I sold my ESOPs on the same day I exercised (same-day sale). How is tax calculated?

If you exercise and immediately sell: perquisite = FMV − strike price (taxed as salary); capital gain = sale price − FMV = likely zero or very small (since you sell at FMV on the same day). Sometimes slightly positive due to intraday movement — short-term capital gain at slab rate. Your company's broker typically handles the calculation.

### My company gave ESOPs in the company itself (unlisted Indian private company). How is FMV determined?

For unlisted Indian private companies, FMV at exercise must be determined by a **SEBI-registered Category I Merchant Banker**. The company typically arranges this valuation before the exercise event. Insist on getting this valuation certificate — you'll need it for ITR and to prove the FMV used for perquisite calculation.
