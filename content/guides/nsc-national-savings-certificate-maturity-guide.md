# NSC (National Savings Certificate) Maturity — How to Claim Your Money

**NSC (National Savings Certificate)** is a Post Office small savings scheme with a **5-year tenure** and **7.7% p.a. interest** (compounded annually, paid at maturity). If your NSC is maturing or has matured, here's how to claim the amount.

## NSC at a Glance — Current Rules

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Interest rate FY 2025-26** | **7.7% p.a.** (compounded annually) |
| **Tenure** | 5 years |
| **Minimum investment** | ₹1,000 (multiples of ₹100) |
| **Maximum investment** | No limit |
| **80C deduction** | Yes — investment qualifies for 80C deduction (old regime) |
| **Interest paid** | At maturity only (no annual payouts) |
| **TDS** | No TDS at post office on NSC interest |
| **Issued by** | Post offices across India |

## NSC Maturity Amount — What You Get Back

## How to Claim NSC at Maturity

### Step 1: Go to the Issuing Post Office

You can encash NSC at:
- **Any post office** (not just the one where it was issued) — post offices in the same state
- If issued at a different city: You may need to transfer first OR the receiving post office accepts with verification

Carry:
- **Original NSC certificate(s)** (physical documents)
- **Photo ID**: Aadhaar card, Voter ID, PAN card, Passport
- **PAN card**: Required if encashing more than ₹50,000 (for reporting)
- **Bank account passbook/details**: If you want amount credited to bank

### Step 2: Fill the Encashment Form

At the post office counter:
1. Request the **NSC encashment form**
2. Fill: Certificate number(s), amount, maturity date, your PAN, bank account details
3. Sign the back of the NSC certificate (discharge receipt)

### Step 3: Verify and Receive Payment

The post office:
- Verifies the certificate details
- Checks your ID
- Computes the maturity amount
- Pays cash (for amounts ≤ ₹20,000) or credits to your account (for larger amounts)

**Processing time**: Same day for routine cases. If post office needs to verify (old certificates, different city), may take 2-3 working days.

## Tax Treatment of NSC Interest

NSC interest has a unique tax treatment that confuses many investors:

**Each year's interest is technically taxable** — even though it's not actually paid to you until maturity. The IT rules treat NSC interest as "deemed reinvestment" year by year.

| Year | What Happens |
|------|-------------|
| Year 1 | Interest credited as "deemed reinvestment" — taxable as income, but ALSO qualifies for 80C deduction |
| Years 2–4 | Same — interest is income AND 80C deduction |
| Year 5 | Final year interest is income (NOT 80C deductible — since it's the maturity year) |

**Result**: For years 1-4, the 80C deduction on re-invested interest cancels out the tax on interest (they net to zero for most taxpayers). Only Year 5 interest has net taxable impact.

**How to report in ITR**:
1. Under "Income from Other Sources" → Add NSC interest for the year
2. Under Section 80C (old regime) → Deduct the NSC interest for Years 1-4 (since it's deemed reinvested)
3. Year 5: Only report interest as income, no 80C deduction

**TDS**: Post offices do NOT deduct TDS on NSC interest. You must declare it yourself in ITR.

## Can NSC Be Encashed Before 5 Years (Premature)?

Premature encashment of NSC is **generally not allowed**. Exceptions:
- **Death of the account holder**: Legal heir can claim with death certificate + succession certificate
- **Court order**
- **Forfeiture by pledgee** (if NSC was pledged as collateral)

NSC cannot be prematurely encashed for personal financial need — this is a key difference from FDs.

## NSC Can Be Pledged as Collateral

NSC certificates can be pledged (mortgaged) to take loans from:
- Nationalized banks
- Cooperative banks
- NBFCs (subject to lender policy)
- Gazetted officers (for specific purposes)

The post office endorses the pledge on the certificate. After the loan is repaid, the endorsement is removed and you can encash normally at maturity.

## NSC vs PPF vs Bank FD

| Feature | NSC | PPF | Bank FD |
|---------|-----|-----|---------|
| **Rate** | 7.7% | 7.1% | 6.5–7.5% |
| **Tenure** | 5 years | 15 years | 7 days – 10 years |
| **Interest payout** | Only at maturity | Annual (credited) | Quarterly/Annual |
| **80C deduction** | ✅ On investment + reinvested interest | ✅ On investment | ✅ Only on 5-year tax-saver FD |
| **Premature exit** | ❌ No (except death/court) | Limited | ✅ With penalty |
| **TDS** | ❌ None | ❌ None | ✅ 10% on interest > ₹40K |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I have old NSC certificates from 2018 at 7.6% rate. What's my maturity amount?

Interest rate is fixed at the time of purchase — your 2018 NSC earns 7.6% for its full 5 years regardless of what the current rate is. Maturity: ₹1,000 invested at 7.6% for 5 years = ₹1,444.

### I can't find the original NSC certificate. Can I still claim?

Visit the issuing post office with your ID and PAN. Request a duplicate certificate issuance (Form for lost/destroyed certificate). After due process, a duplicate is issued and you can then encash.

### My father had NSC certificates and passed away. How do we claim?

The legal heir/nominee must present: death certificate, original NSC, succession certificate (or legal heir certificate), their own ID proof. Post office processes the claim after verification.

### NSC interest wasn't declared for 4 years in my ITR. What to do?

You may face a notice for under-reporting. File revised/belated ITRs for the open years. The 80C deduction on years 1-4 interest would have offset much of the tax, so the actual demand may be small. Consult a CA.
