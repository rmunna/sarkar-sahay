# Rental Income Tax India 2026 — Complete Calculation Guide

If you own a property and rent it out, the rent you receive is taxed as **"Income from House Property"** under the Income Tax Act. Unlike business income, you don't get to deduct actual expenses — instead, a flat **30% standard deduction** is allowed, plus **home loan interest** under Section 24.

## Rental Income Tax — Quick Formula

```
Gross Annual Rent (GAR)
- Municipal taxes paid by you
= Net Annual Value (NAV)
- 30% Standard Deduction (on NAV)
- Home Loan Interest (Section 24b, up to ₹2L for self-occupied; unlimited for let-out)
= Taxable Income from House Property
```

This taxable amount is added to your other income (salary, etc.) and taxed at your slab rate.

## Step-by-Step Calculation

### Step 1: Gross Annual Rent (GAR)

Total rent received or receivable in the financial year.

**Example**: Monthly rent ₹25,000 × 12 = **₹3,00,000 GAR**

### Step 2: Deduct Municipal/Property Taxes

Only taxes you (the owner) have paid to the municipality are deductible. If tenant pays the taxes, no deduction.

If municipal tax paid = ₹12,000:
- **Net Annual Value (NAV)** = ₹3,00,000 − ₹12,000 = **₹2,88,000**

### Step 3: 30% Standard Deduction (Section 24a)

A flat 30% of NAV is allowed — no questions asked, no bills needed.

- 30% of ₹2,88,000 = **₹86,400**
- After standard deduction: ₹2,88,000 − ₹86,400 = **₹2,01,600**

### Step 4: Home Loan Interest (Section 24b)

If you have a home loan on the rented property:
- **No limit** on interest deduction for let-out property (unlike self-occupied where it's capped at ₹2L)
- Deduct entire home loan interest paid

If home loan interest = ₹1,50,000:
- After interest deduction: ₹2,01,600 − ₹1,50,000 = **₹51,600**

**Taxable "Income from House Property" = ₹51,600**

This ₹51,600 is added to your salary and taxed at your slab rate.

## Common Scenarios

### Scenario 1: One Rental Property, No Home Loan

- Monthly rent: ₹20,000; Annual = ₹2,40,000
- Municipal tax paid by you: ₹8,000
- NAV = ₹2,40,000 − ₹8,000 = ₹2,32,000
- 30% standard deduction: ₹69,600
- Taxable = ₹2,32,000 − ₹69,600 = **₹1,62,400**
- Tax (at 20% slab): ₹32,480

### Scenario 2: Rental Property With Home Loan

- Annual rent: ₹4,80,000
- Municipal tax: ₹15,000
- Home loan interest: ₹3,60,000 (high interest phase)
- NAV = ₹4,80,000 − ₹15,000 = ₹4,65,000
- 30% deduction: ₹1,39,500
- After standard deduction: ₹3,25,500
- Home loan interest: ₹3,60,000
- **Taxable income from house property = ₹3,25,500 − ₹3,60,000 = −₹34,500 (Loss)**

This loss can be set off against salary income (up to ₹2L cap — see below).

## Loss from House Property — Set-off Rules

| Year | Maximum Loss Set-off Against Salary/Other Income |
|------|------------------------------------------------|
| From let-out property with home loan | **Up to ₹2 lakh** (Budget 2017 introduced cap) |
| Remaining loss | Carry forward for 8 years (set off against future house property income only) |

**Important change**: Before 2017, the full interest was deductible from salary. Now the cap is ₹2L per year. Excess loss is carried forward.

## Deemed Rent for Vacant Properties

If you own more than one property and one is vacant:

| Properties Owned | Tax Treatment |
|----------------|--------------|
| First property: Self-occupied | No tax |
| Second property onwards: Vacant | Taxed on **Deemed Rent** (expected rent the property would fetch) |

**Deemed rent** = Higher of: (a) standard rent per municipal records, or (b) fair rent in that locality.

Exception (Budget 2019): Two self-occupied properties can be tax-free (no deemed rent). From third property onwards, deemed rent applies.

## TDS When You Receive Rent

If your tenant is an individual paying more than ₹50,000/month rent:
- Tenant must deduct **2% TDS** (Section 194IB) from your rent
- Once a year (annual or at vacating)
- You see this in your Form 26AS as TDS credit

**What you do**: Declare the full rent as income, claim the TDS credit in ITR.

## Which ITR Form to Use

| Situation | ITR Form |
|-----------|---------|
| Salaried + ONE rental property | **ITR-1** (Sahaj) — if income ≤ ₹50L |
| Salaried + TWO or more rental properties | **ITR-2** |
| Business income + rental income | **ITR-3** |
| Rental income only (no salary/business) | **ITR-2** typically |

## New vs Old Tax Regime for Rental Income

| Regime | Home Loan Interest Deduction (Section 24b) |
|--------|------------------------------------------|
| Old regime | ✅ Full deduction allowed (unlimited for let-out) |
| New regime | ❌ Not available (home loan interest deduction not allowed) |

Under new regime, only the 30% standard deduction from House Property income is available. If you have a large home loan on your rental property, old regime is typically better.

## Advance Tax on Rental Income

If your rental income (after all deductions) results in a tax liability > ₹10,000, you must pay advance tax quarterly.

Landlords earning rent from multiple properties should track:
- June 15: Pay 15% of estimated tax
- September 15: 45% cumulative
- December 15: 75%
- March 15: 100%

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My tenant has deposited a security/advance of ₹2 lakh. Is this taxable?

Security deposit is not rental income — it's a refundable amount. Not taxable when received. If the deposit is adjusted against rent or forfeited, that portion becomes taxable in the year it's adjusted/forfeited.

### I pay home loan EMI and rent this property but also rent another place to live. Can I claim both HRA and rental deduction?

Yes — you can simultaneously claim HRA (if employer provides and you pay rent for your residence) AND claim Section 24b interest deduction on the rented-out property. These are independent deductions.

### I have two properties — both rented out. How is tax calculated?

Calculate taxable income separately for each property using the same formula (GAR → municipal taxes → NAV → 30% deduction → home loan interest). Add both net figures to get total "Income from House Property." File ITR-2.

### My tenant hasn't paid rent for 3 months. Do I still pay tax on those months?

Unrealized rent is not taxable. You pay tax only on rent actually received. For the months you didn't receive rent, exclude from GAR. If you later receive arrears, they're taxable in the year received.
