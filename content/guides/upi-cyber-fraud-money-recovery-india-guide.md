# UPI Fraud Money Recovery India 2026 — Complete Action Guide

Speed is everything in UPI fraud recovery. Every hour of delay reduces recovery probability significantly. If you've been defrauded in the last 24 hours, start with Step 1 immediately — come back to read the full guide later.

---

## Immediate Actions (Do These First)

**If fraud happened in last 4 hours**:
1. **Call 1930** (National Cyber Crime Financial Helpline) — available 24/7
2. Give them transaction ID, amount, your bank account number, fraudster's UPI ID/bank details
3. They can freeze the fraudster's account before money is withdrawn

**If fraud happened today (within 24 hours)**:
1. Call 1930 — still possible to freeze
2. File complaint at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in) — online, 10 minutes

**If fraud happened more than 24 hours ago**:
1. File at cybercrime.gov.in
2. File FIR at cyber police station
3. Initiate bank dispute

## Common UPI Frauds in India

### 1. Fake Payment Screenshot Fraud (Seller Fraud)
Buyer sends you a "payment successful" screenshot (Photoshopped). You deliver goods; no money actually arrives.
**Recovery**: Harder — it's civil fraud. File police complaint; transaction never happened on bank's side.

### 2. UPI PIN / OTP Fraud
Fraudster calls saying "I'm from Google Pay/PhonePe/your bank" and asks for UPI PIN or OTP to "verify" or "fix an issue."
UPI PINs and OTPs should never be shared with anyone. Banks will never ask for them.

### 3. Collect Request Fraud (Receive Money Scam)
Fraudster sends a "Collect Request" via UPI. Victim thinks they're receiving money but enters PIN — which deducts money from their account, not deposits.
**Key**: Never enter your UPI PIN for "receiving" money. A PIN is only needed to SEND.

### 4. Screen Share Fraud
Fraudster asks you to download AnyDesk or TeamViewer for "technical support." They watch you enter OTP/PIN and transfer money.
**Never share screen** with anyone claiming to be bank/UPI support.

### 5. QR Code Fraud
"Scan this QR to receive your refund/prize money." Scanning a QR code authorizes a PAYMENT (not receipt). Never scan QR codes from unknown people.

### 6. SIM Swap Fraud
Fraudster obtains a duplicate SIM of your number, receives all OTPs, empties accounts.
**Signs**: Your phone loses signal completely. Check immediately with your carrier.

## Step-by-Step Recovery Process

### Step 1: Call 1930 Immediately

**1930** = National Cyber Crime Financial Helpline
- Available 24/7, all languages
- They connect with RBI's Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting & Management System (CFCFRMS)
- If you call within 4 hours, they can place a "hold" on the fraudster's account before the money is withdrawn
- Give: your name, UPI ID, fraudster UPI ID, transaction amount, date/time, brief description

**Success rate**: Reports suggest 30–40% recovery rates when reported within 4 hours. Drops significantly after 24 hours.

### Step 2: File Online Complaint at cybercrime.gov.in

1. Go to [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in) → "Report Financial Fraud"
2. Fill: your details, bank account number, transaction ID, amount, fraudster details
3. Upload: screenshots of transaction, fraudster's messages, any evidence
4. Get complaint number — **save this**

This generates an incident reference that police and banks use for follow-up.

### Step 3: Complain to Your Bank

**Via net banking/app**: Raise a "fraud dispute" or "chargeback request" within 3 days.
**Via phone banking**: Call bank's fraud helpline (listed on back of your card/debit card).
**In writing**: Walk in to your home branch and submit written complaint.

**Bank's obligation under RBI circular**:
- If you report fraud within 3 days: Zero liability (bank must refund)
- 4–7 days: You bear ₹5,000 or 10% (lower), rest refunded
- After 7 days: Refund at bank's discretion

These timeframes apply to unauthorized electronic transactions under RBI's 2017 circular on customer liability.

**Important**: Keep proof of all bank communications — when you first reported, who you spoke to, complaint reference number.

### Step 4: File FIR at Cyber Police Station

Visit your nearest cyber police station (every district now has one) and file an FIR:
- Under Section 420 IPC (Cheating) and IT Act Section 66D (fraud using computer resources)
- Bring: printed cybercrime.gov.in complaint, bank statement showing debit, all screenshots
- Demand a copy of the FIR (it's your right)

**Online FIR (in some states)**:
- Karnataka: cybercrime.kar.gov.in
- Maharashtra: mahapolice.gov.in
- Delhi: delhipolice.gov.in

### Step 5: Follow Up with NPCI (For UPI-Specific Disputes)

**NPCI** manages the UPI infrastructure. Banks submit dispute requests to NPCI who investigate with the beneficiary bank.

You can track UPI disputes via your bank, but escalating to NPCI directly:
- Email: [helpdesk@npci.org.in](mailto:helpdesk@npci.org.in)
- Include: transaction reference number, cybercrime complaint number, FIR number

## If Your Bank Refuses to Help

**RBI Banking Ombudsman**: If bank doesn't respond within 30 days or rejects your claim unfairly:
- File at [bankingombudsman.rbi.org.in](https://bankingombudsman.rbi.org.in)
- Online complaint, free, resolved typically in 30–60 days
- RBI can direct bank to compensate if they find the bank at fault

**Consumer Forum**: For amounts > ₹1 lakh where banks are negligent, file at [edaakhil.nic.in](https://edaakhil.nic.in) — District Consumer Forum.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I sent money voluntarily but was tricked. Can I recover?

If you were deceived (fake OLX seller, fake matrimonial site, fake investment scheme), it's fraud even if you sent money willingly. File cybercrime complaint. Recovery is harder for authorized-push-payment fraud (where you sent money voluntarily) than for unauthorized transactions, but reports still help.

### The fraudster said they're from my bank. What do I do?

File FIR specifically mentioning "impersonation of bank employee." This is a serious offense. Your bank has no liability if you shared OTP/PIN voluntarily — but some banks still assist based on goodwill.

### I got a job offer scam and paid advance fees. Is this recoverable?

These are typically online advance-fee frauds. File cybercrime complaint. Recovery is unlikely if money was transferred internationally or to mule accounts already. Important for police data collection. Keep all messages/emails from the scammer.

### GPay/PhonePe says the transaction was "authorized" and can't reverse it.

"Authorized" by UPI means you entered your PIN. The UPI app cannot reverse authorized transactions — this is handled by the bank dispute and NPCI process, not the app. Bank is the right escalation.
