# Personal Loan EMI Bounced — What Happens & How to Fix It

Missing a personal loan EMI is more common than you think — an auto-debit fails because your account didn't have enough balance, and suddenly you're dealing with penalty charges, angry bank calls, and a damaged credit score. This guide explains exactly what happens when your EMI bounces, what the real consequences are, and how to fix the situation quickly.

> **Disclaimer:** CitizenNest is an independent information platform and is not affiliated with any bank, NBFC, or government body. Always verify details with your lender and RBI guidelines.

## What Happens Immediately When Your EMI Bounces?

When your bank or NBFC tries to auto-debit your EMI and the account has insufficient balance, the following happens:

1. **ECS/NACH mandate fails** — The automated debit instruction (ECS or NACH) is returned unpaid by your bank.
2. **Bounce charges by your bank** — Your savings bank charges ₹250–₹750 for the dishonoured auto-debit (varies by bank).
3. **Penalty from lender** — The loan provider (separate from your bank) charges a late payment fee, typically ₹500–₹1,500 plus GST.
4. **Penal interest kicks in** — An additional 1%–2% per month (annualized 12%–24%) is charged on the overdue amount until you pay.
5. **SMS/email notification** — The lender sends you an overdue notice within 1–3 days.

## Impact on CIBIL/Credit Score

A bounced EMI can significantly damage your credit score. Here's what happens:

### Single EMI Bounce
- **Score drop:** 50–70 points if reported (a single bounce within 30 days, if resolved quickly, may not always be reported)
- **Reported as:** "Days Past Due (DPD)" — shows 1–30 days overdue
- **Stays on record:** Up to 36 months in your CIBIL report

### 30+ Days Overdue
- **Score drop:** 70–100 points
- **DPD marker:** Shows 31–60, 61–90, etc. depending on delay
- **Effect:** Severely impacts future loan/credit card applications

### 90+ Days Overdue
- **Account classified as:** SMA (Special Mention Account) → NPA (Non-Performing Asset)
- **Score drop:** 100–150+ points
- **Future lending:** Most banks will reject applications outright

> **Tip:** Check your credit score for free at [CIBIL](https://www.cibil.com/) to see the damage and track recovery.

## How to Pay Bounced EMI Immediately

Don't wait for the next auto-debit cycle. Pay the overdue EMI as fast as possible:

### Method 1: Lender's Mobile App / Website
1. Log in to your loan provider's app (e.g., HDFC Bank app, Bajaj Finserv app)
2. Go to **Loans** → **My Loans** → Select the personal loan
3. Look for **Pay Overdue** or **Pay EMI Now** option
4. Pay via UPI, net banking, or debit card
5. Save the payment confirmation receipt

### Method 2: NEFT/RTGS Transfer
1. Find the loan repayment account number from your loan statement or sanction letter
2. Add it as a beneficiary in your bank's net banking/app
3. Transfer the exact overdue amount (EMI + penalty, if shown)
4. Use your loan account number as the payment reference
5. Keep the transaction reference number

### Method 3: Visit Bank Branch
1. Visit the nearest branch of your lender
2. Carry your loan account number and ID proof
3. Pay via cheque or cash (cash may have limits)
4. Collect the payment receipt

### Method 4: Call Customer Care
1. Call your lender's helpline and request a payment link
2. Many lenders send a one-time payment link via SMS
3. Pay immediately through the link

> **Important:** After paying, confirm with the lender that your account is marked as "current" and the overdue status is cleared.

## Multiple EMI Bounces — What Happens Next

If you miss EMIs repeatedly, the consequences escalate:

### 1–2 EMIs Missed (30–60 Days)
- Repeated calls and SMS from the collections team
- Penalty charges accumulate
- CIBIL report shows increasing DPD

### 3 EMIs Missed (90 Days)
- Account classified as **NPA (Non-Performing Asset)**
- Bank may issue a formal **legal notice / demand notice**
- Your loan account is transferred to the recovery department
- Co-applicants and guarantors are also contacted

### 6+ EMIs Missed (180+ Days)
- Bank may initiate **legal proceedings** under relevant laws
- Account may be **written off** (but you still owe the money)
- Recovery agents may visit your home or workplace
- Bank may file a **civil suit** for recovery
- For secured loans: asset seizure proceedings may begin

### Recovery Agents — What They Can and Cannot Do

Recovery agents are bound by strict **RBI guidelines** (Circular dated April 24, 2008):

**They CANNOT:**
- Visit before 7:00 AM or after 7:00 PM
- Use abusive language, threats, or intimidation
- Harass your family members, neighbours, or colleagues
- Damage property or use physical force
- Call you repeatedly to harass
- Share your loan details with third parties

**They CAN:**
- Visit your registered address during permitted hours
- Call you on registered phone numbers
- Send written reminders and legal notices
- Negotiate a settlement or repayment plan

> **If harassed:** File a complaint on RBI's Sachet portal at [sachet.rbi.org.in](https://sachet.rbi.org.in/) or the CMS portal at [cms.rbi.org.in](https://cms.rbi.org.in/).

## RBI Guidelines on Fair Recovery Practices

The Reserve Bank of India has issued clear guidelines to protect borrowers:

1. **Agents must carry authorization** — Every recovery agent must have a valid authorization letter from the lender with their photo and details.
2. **No third-party disclosure** — Your default status cannot be shared with people not connected to the loan.
3. **Grievance redressal** — Banks must have a grievance mechanism for complaints against agents.
4. **Cooling period for calls** — Persistent calling is classified as harassment.
5. **Written communication** — All demands must be made in writing before recovery action.

If your bank violates these rules, you can:
- Complain to the bank's **Grievance Redressal Officer**
- Escalate to the **Banking Ombudsman** (now integrated into [RBI CMS portal](https://cms.rbi.org.in/))
- File an FIR if agents use threats or physical intimidation

## How to Restructure Your Loan if Facing Financial Difficulty

If you're struggling to pay EMIs, don't hide — talk to your lender. Banks prefer restructuring over default.

### Options Available

**1. EMI Restructuring**
- Request lower EMI by extending the loan tenure
- Example: ₹15,000/month EMI over 3 years → ₹10,000/month over 5 years
- Bank may charge a restructuring fee (typically 0.5%–1% of outstanding)

**2. One-Time Settlement (OTS)**
- Negotiate a lump-sum payment that's less than the total outstanding
- Typical OTS: 50%–80% of outstanding amount
- **Warning:** OTS is reported as "settled" on CIBIL — impacts score for 7 years
- Only consider this as a last resort

**3. Balance Transfer**
- Transfer your loan to another bank at a lower interest rate
- Possible only if you haven't already defaulted significantly
- See our [balance transfer guide](/guide/personal-loan-balance-transfer) for details

**4. Top-Up Loan**
- Some banks allow a top-up to clear overdue and restart fresh EMIs
- Works if your relationship with the bank is still reasonable

### How to Request Restructuring
1. Write a formal letter or email to the bank's loan department
2. Explain your financial difficulty (job loss, medical emergency, etc.)
3. Attach supporting documents (termination letter, medical bills, etc.)
4. Request specific relief — lower EMI, moratorium, tenure extension
5. Follow up within 7–10 working days

## EMI Moratorium / Holiday Options

A moratorium means temporary pause on EMI payments. Key points:

- **RBI COVID moratorium** (2020) was a one-time measure — not a permanent option
- Some banks offer **EMI holiday** as a feature for 1–3 months (interest continues to accrue)
- You must apply before missing EMIs — moratorium is not granted retroactively
- **Interest still accumulates** during the moratorium period, increasing your total repayment
- Check your loan agreement for any built-in moratorium/holiday clause

> **Tip:** Even during a moratorium, interest accrues. A 3-month pause on a ₹5 lakh loan at 14% p.a. adds roughly ₹17,500 to your total cost.

## SARFAESI Act — When Can Banks Seize Assets?

The **Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest (SARFAESI) Act, 2002** allows banks to seize assets **without court intervention**, but with important conditions:

### Does SARFAESI Apply to Personal Loans?
- **Unsecured personal loans:** SARFAESI does **NOT** apply (no collateral to seize)
- **Secured loans** (home loan, loan against property, car loan): SARFAESI applies if outstanding is above ₹1 lakh

### SARFAESI Process (for Secured Loans)
1. Account is classified as NPA (90 days overdue)
2. Bank issues **60-day notice** under Section 13(2)
3. Borrower can reply with objections within 60 days
4. If unresolved, bank can **take possession** of the secured asset
5. Bank publishes a public notice and conducts **auction**
6. Sale proceeds are used to recover the outstanding amount

### Your Rights Under SARFAESI
- You can file an appeal at the **Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT)** within 45 days of possession
- You must deposit 50% of the outstanding amount to stay the sale (reduced from earlier requirement by courts in some cases)
- Agricultural land **cannot** be seized under SARFAESI

> **For unsecured personal loans:** Banks can only recover through civil suits, arbitration, or Lok Adalat — they cannot seize your property directly.

## Important Tips

1. **Set up multiple reminders** — Keep 2–3 days' buffer of EMI amount in your auto-debit account before the due date
2. **Enable standing instruction alerts** — Most banks send a reminder 2–3 days before auto-debit
3. **Talk to your bank early** — If you know you'll miss an EMI, inform the bank proactively
4. **Don't ignore calls** — Cooperating with the bank leads to better outcomes than avoidance
5. **Document everything** — Save all payment receipts, emails, and communication with your lender

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Will one EMI bounce ruin my CIBIL score permanently?
**A:** No. A single bounce that you clear within 30 days may cause a 50–70 point drop, but your score recovers over 6–12 months of regular payments. The DPD entry stays on your report for 36 months but its impact reduces over time.

### Q: Can the bank send recovery agents for one missed EMI?
**A:** Generally, no. Banks use recovery agents only after prolonged default (usually 90+ days). For a single bounce, you'll receive calls and SMS reminders.

### Q: I paid the bounced EMI but my CIBIL still shows overdue — what do I do?
**A:** Banks report to CIBIL monthly. After paying, wait 30–45 days for the update. If it's still not corrected, raise a dispute on [cibil.com](https://www.cibil.com/) and contact your bank to update the records.

### Q: Can I go to jail for not paying a personal loan?
**A:** No. Loan default is a **civil matter**, not criminal. You cannot be arrested or jailed for not paying a personal loan. However, if you issued a cheque that bounced, you may face action under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act.

### Q: What is the difference between "settled" and "closed" on CIBIL?
**A:** "Closed" means you paid the full amount — no negative impact. "Settled" means you paid less than owed via one-time settlement (OTS) — this is a negative mark and stays on your report for 7 years, making future loans difficult.

### Q: Can the bank deduct money from my savings account for a missed EMI?
**A:** If your loan and savings account are with the **same bank**, they may exercise a "right of set-off" and deduct the overdue amount from your savings/FD. They must notify you, but it can happen. This is legal.

### Q: How long does it take to recover CIBIL score after EMI default?
**A:** With consistent, on-time payments: 6–12 months for a minor default (30 days), 12–24 months for major default (90+ days). The negative DPD entries remain on your report for 36 months.

### Q: Is EMI moratorium available in 2026?
**A:** The RBI COVID-era moratorium was a one-time measure (2020). As of 2026, there is no blanket moratorium. However, individual banks may offer EMI holiday or restructuring on a case-by-case basis. Contact your lender to check eligibility.
