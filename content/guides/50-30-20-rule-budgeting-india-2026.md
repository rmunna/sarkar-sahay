# 50-30-20 Budget Rule India 2026 — Practical Guide for Indian Salaries

The 50-30-20 rule is the simplest budgeting framework that works: put 50% on needs, 30% on wants, 20% on savings. Here's how to actually apply it to an Indian salary.

---

## What Is the 50-30-20 Rule?

Developed by US Senator Elizabeth Warren in her book "All Your Worth," the rule splits your **take-home pay** (not CTC) into three buckets:

| Bucket | % | What Goes Here |
|--------|---|----------------|
| **Needs** | 50% | Rent/EMI, groceries, utilities, transport, insurance premiums, minimum loan payments |
| **Wants** | 30% | Eating out, OTT subscriptions, shopping, travel, gym, entertainment |
| **Savings/Investments** | 20% | SIP, PPF, EPF top-up, emergency fund, down payment savings |

**Use take-home pay**, not CTC. EPF deducted from salary counts as savings — it's already in your 20% bucket before you see it.

## The Metro India Problem — Why 50% Isn't Enough for Needs

In Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi, Pune, Hyderabad — rent alone can be ₹20,000–₹50,000 for a decent 2BHK. Add groceries, transport, utilities, and 50% of ₹50K salary barely covers rent + food.

**Modified rule for metros**: **60-20-20**
- 60% Needs (high rent cities)
- 20% Wants
- 20% Savings

**Or**: If you have a home loan EMI:
- EMI is a "need" but it's also wealth building (principal repayment = forced savings)
- Some people count home loan EMI's principal portion as savings

**The point**: The percentages are guidelines, not rigid rules. The key principle: **savings should be non-negotiable, even if it means cutting wants first**.

## Where Indian Budgets Usually Break

### Problem 1: EMI Overload

When EMI (home loan + car loan + personal loan) exceeds 40% of take-home, there's no room for savings.

**Fix**: Financial advisors recommend keeping all EMIs below **40% of take-home** (FOIR). Don't take on new loans without checking this ratio.

### Problem 2: Lifestyle Inflation

₹50K salary → ₹1L salary → needs + wants both expand to consume the new income. Nothing extra goes to savings.

**Fix**: When you get an increment, increase savings proportionally before you increase lifestyle spending. Step-up SIP automates this.

### Problem 3: No Emergency Fund

Most Indians skip the emergency fund ("I can use credit card if needed"). Then a medical emergency or job loss forces them to break investments at the worst time.

**Fix**: Build 3–6 months of expenses in a liquid fund or high-yield savings account BEFORE investing in SIPs. See our [emergency fund guide](/guide/emergency-fund-how-much-where-to-keep).

## How to Track Your 50-30-20 in Practice

### Option 1: Envelope Method (Physical)

Three envelopes labeled Needs, Wants, Savings. Put cash in each at salary time. Spend only from respective envelope.

### Option 2: Separate Bank Accounts

1. **Primary account**: Salary credited here
2. **Savings account**: Auto-transfer 20% on salary day (standing instruction to SIP/PPF/RD)
3. **Spending account**: Transfer 80% for month's spending; sub-divide mentally

**Easiest implementation**: Set auto-debit for SIP, PPF, and emergency fund to execute on salary+1 day. Whatever's left is for spending.

### Option 3: Apps

- **Walnut** (India-specific): Tracks bank SMS, categorizes spends
- **CRED**: Tracks credit card spends, shows category breakdown
- **Fi Money**: Intelligent banking app with spend analytics
- **INDmoney**: Tracks all investments + expenses in one view
- **Paytm / PhonePe**: Month-end spend summary by category

## 20% Savings — Where to Put It?

Priority order for the savings bucket:

1. **Emergency fund first** (3–6 months expenses): Liquid fund or FD — do this before any market investment
2. **EPF/PF** (auto via employer): Already happening; counts toward your 20%
3. **Term insurance**: If you have dependents and no term plan — buy now (₹10K–₹15K/year for ₹1 crore cover)
4. **Health insurance**: If not covered by employer — buy a family floater
5. **ELSS SIP** (80C deduction): ₹12,500/month reaches the ₹1.5L 80C limit
6. **NPS**: Extra ₹50K deduction (80CCD(1B))
7. **Index fund SIP**: Nifty 50 or Nifty 500 for long-term wealth
8. **PPF**: For guaranteed debt component

**Don't invest in stock market** until steps 1–4 are done. No point getting 14% CAGR if you have to break SIPs during emergency.

## 50-30-20 Doesn't Work — When to Change the Ratio

The rule assumes a certain income level. For very low incomes (₹25K take-home), 50% of needs is aspirational — basic necessities alone may take 70–80%.

**Scale the rule to reality**:
- If needs take 70%: Save minimum 5–10% even if it means cutting wants to almost zero
- If needs take 50%: Follow the rule as designed
- If needs take 30%: Save 30–40% (tech professionals with lower cost of living, single, no EMI)

**The non-negotiable**: Save SOMETHING every month, even if it's ₹500. The habit matters more than the amount when you're starting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I include EPF deduction in my 50% or 20%?

Include EPF in your 20% savings bucket. Most budget calculators use take-home (after EPF deduction) — if you use CTC for the calculation, include EPF in savings. Either way, EPF is forced savings, not a want or need.

### My rent alone is 40% of take-home in Bengaluru. What do I do?

You're in a common situation. Options: Find a flatmate (split rent), move to cheaper area (save on commute cost too), or accept 60-20-20 temporarily. Prioritize reaching 20% savings as income grows — don't sacrifice savings completely for "nice" housing.

### I'm saving 30% but it's all in FD. Is that okay?

Better than not saving, but FDs at 7% barely beat inflation (6%) after tax. For money you won't need for 5+ years, equity MF SIPs grow faster. Move at least half your long-term savings to equity index funds.

### How do I count home loan EMI?

The principal portion of EMI = savings (reduces liability). The interest portion = need (cost of borrowing). Most budgets simplify and count the whole EMI as a need. For the savings tracker, count net worth improvement (outstanding loan reduced + equity in property).
