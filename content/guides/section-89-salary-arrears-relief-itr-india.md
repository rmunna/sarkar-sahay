# Section 89 Relief India 2026 — Tax Relief on Salary Arrears

You worked hard for 3 years, but your salary revision was approved late. Now you receive all 3 years of arrears in one shot this year — and suddenly your income is high enough to push you into a much higher tax bracket. Section 89 fixes this by spreading the tax impact across the actual years it belonged to.

## The Problem Section 89 Solves

Without Section 89:
- You receive ₹5L in salary arrears in FY 2025-26
- This pushes your current year income from ₹15L to ₹20L
- You're now in the 30% slab for a larger portion of income
- Tax is much higher than if you'd received ₹1.67L per year for 3 years

**With Section 89**: Tax is calculated "as if" the arrears were received in the years they belonged to — and the relief is the difference.

## Types of Receipts Eligible for Section 89 Relief

| Receipt Type | Eligible? |
|-------------|----------|
| Salary arrears (pay revision, increments paid late) | ✅ Yes |
| Advance salary (received in advance for future years) | ✅ Yes |
| Arrears of gratuity | ✅ Yes |
| Leave encashment in excess of exemption | ✅ Yes |
| Commuted pension arrears | ✅ Yes |
| Compensation on termination | ✅ Yes |
| Arrears of family pension | ✅ Yes |

## Section 89 Calculation — Step by Step

This is a 4-step calculation:

**Given**: You receive ₹3L in salary arrears for FY 2022-23 and FY 2023-24 (₹1.5L per year), paid in FY 2025-26.

### Step 1: Calculate Tax for Current Year WITH Arrears
Total income FY 2025-26 = Regular salary + Arrears = ₹14L + ₹3L = ₹17L
Calculate tax on ₹17L (new regime) = Tax A

### Step 2: Calculate Tax for Current Year WITHOUT Arrears
Total income FY 2025-26 = ₹14L only
Calculate tax on ₹14L (new regime) = Tax B

### Step 3: Calculate Tax for Past Years WITH Arrears
For FY 2022-23: Total income that year (₹10L) + arrear portion (₹1.5L) = ₹11.5L
Tax on ₹11.5L for FY 2022-23 using THAT year's slabs = Tax C1

For FY 2023-24: Income (₹12L) + arrear (₹1.5L) = ₹13.5L
Tax on ₹13.5L for FY 2023-24 using THAT year's slabs = Tax C2

### Step 4: Calculate Tax for Past Years WITHOUT Arrears
Tax on ₹10L (FY 2022-23 actual income, no arrears) = Tax D1
Tax on ₹12L (FY 2023-24 actual income, no arrears) = Tax D2

### Section 89 Relief Amount

**Relief = (Tax A − Tax B) − [(Tax C1 − Tax D1) + (Tax C2 − Tax D2)]**

If this is positive: You get tax relief.
If negative: No relief (you don't have to PAY more — relief is just zero).

The relief reduces your tax liability in the current year.

## Form 10E — Mandatory Before ITR Filing

**Critical**: Section 89 relief can be claimed **only if you file Form 10E** on the income tax portal **before** filing your ITR.

If you claim Section 89 in ITR but haven't filed Form 10E: You'll receive a demand notice and the relief will be disallowed.

### How to File Form 10E

1. Go to incometax.gov.in → Login
2. Go to e-File → Income Tax Forms → Form 10E
3. Select Assessment Year (e.g., 2026-27 for FY 2025-26 arrears)
4. Select the type of receipt (salary arrears, gratuity, etc.)
5. Fill in the calculation details (prior year incomes, arrear amounts)
6. The portal auto-calculates the relief
7. Submit the form
8. You'll get an acknowledgement — note the submission date

**Then file your ITR** and claim the Section 89 relief in the salary schedule.

## Using Form 16 for Past Year Income

For the Section 89 calculation, you need your actual income for the past years. Sources:
- Form 16 from employer for past years
- Previous ITR filed
- Salary slips

The IT portal's Form 10E has fields for each year's income — enter the correct amounts.

## Old vs New Regime and Section 89

Use the **same regime** for both current year and past year calculations. If you're in the new regime in FY 2025-26, use new regime slabs for all years in the Section 89 calculation (even though the new regime slabs may have been different in those years, or the new regime may not have existed in those years).

Practically: Use the regime you're filing under for the current year consistently.

## Common Errors

| Mistake | Consequence |
|---------|------------|
| Filing ITR without filing Form 10E first | Relief disallowed; demand notice |
| Using wrong year's tax slabs | Incorrect relief calculation |
| Claiming relief when tax in current year is already lower | No issue (relief = 0, no harm) |
| Including non-qualifying receipts (bonus, LTA, etc.) | These are taxable fully; Section 89 only for specific receipts |

## Salary Revision (Pay Commission Arrears) — Special Case

Government employees receiving **Pay Commission arrears** often have large lump-sum payments. Section 89 is specifically designed for this:

- Form 10E is mandatory
- Calculate year-by-year impact
- Relief is typically significant when several years' arrears are paid at once

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I received ₹2L as salary arrears (3 years' accumulated increment). My employer deducted TDS on the full amount. How do I claim Section 89?

File Form 10E before ITR. Calculate relief. Claim the relief in ITR. If the relief reduces your tax below what was TDS-deducted: You get a refund. The employer's TDS was calculated without Section 89 — the relief is claimed by you in ITR, not at the employer level.

### My company paid me a promotion bonus that includes arrears from 2 past years. Is this eligible for Section 89?

Depends on nature. If it's explicitly "arrears" (pay revision for past periods): Yes. If it's a discretionary bonus for current year: No. Performance bonuses are taxable fully in the year received — they're not "arrears of salary" for past service. Ask your employer for the breakdown.

### I forgot to file Form 10E but already filed my ITR claiming Section 89. What happens?

The IT Department will send a demand notice disallowing the relief (since Form 10E is mandatory). You have two options: (a) File Form 10E now + file a revised ITR, or (b) Pay the additional tax demand. The portal may allow you to file Form 10E and then revise your ITR — do this before the ITR revision deadline (December 31 of the AY).
