# Crypto Tax India 2026 — Complete Guide on Bitcoin, ETH & VDA Taxation

India introduced a specific tax framework for **VDAs (Virtual Digital Assets)** — which includes Bitcoin, Ethereum, altcoins, NFTs, and all cryptocurrencies — effective from **April 1, 2022 (FY 2022-23 onwards)**. The rules have not changed since then and continue to apply in 2026.

> **Quick summary**: 30% flat tax on all crypto gains. No losses can offset gains. 1% TDS by exchanges. Must declare in ITR even if gains are zero.

## Key Crypto Tax Rules at a Glance

| Rule | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Tax rate** | 30% flat (+ 4% cess = effective 31.2%) |
| **Applicable to** | Crypto profit, NFT sales, airdrops, staking rewards, mining income |
| **Deductions allowed** | ONLY cost of acquisition — nothing else |
| **No set-off** | VDA losses cannot offset VDA gains OR any other income |
| **No carry-forward** | VDA losses cannot be carried forward to next year |
| **TDS** | 1% on crypto exchange transactions above ₹50,000/year (₹10,000 for specified persons) |
| **Gift of crypto** | Taxable for recipient at 30% on fair market value |
| **ITR form** | Schedule VDA in ITR-2 or ITR-3 |

## What Counts as a VDA?

| Included | Not Included |
|----------|-------------|
| Bitcoin (BTC) | Foreign currencies |
| Ethereum (ETH) | Gold/Gold ETFs |
| All altcoins (Solana, XRP, etc.) | Stock market investments |
| NFTs | Mutual funds |
| Stablecoins (USDT, USDC in India) | Real estate |
| Tokens and DeFi yields | PPF, EPF |

## How Crypto Tax is Calculated

**Formula**:
`Tax = (Selling Price − Cost of Acquisition) × 30%`

No indexation. No LTCG/STCG distinction — all crypto is taxed at 30% regardless of holding period.

**Example**:
- Bought 1 BTC on January 2024 for ₹30,00,000
- Sold 1 BTC in March 2026 for ₹50,00,000
- Profit: ₹20,00,000
- **Tax: ₹20,00,000 × 30% = ₹6,00,000** (+ cess ₹24,000 = ₹6,24,000)

## What You CANNOT Deduct

Unlike stock market investments, you **cannot deduct**:
- Exchange fees/trading fees
- Transfer fees/gas fees
- Electricity cost (for miners — this is controversial, consult CA)
- Interest paid on crypto loans

**Only allowed deduction**: The original cost of purchasing the crypto (your buy price).

## Crypto Loss Rules — No Set-Off

**Important**: Crypto tax rules prohibit loss set-off in two ways:

1. **Cannot offset against other income**: ₹5 lakh crypto loss cannot reduce your ₹10 lakh salary income for tax purposes
2. **Cannot offset crypto losses between different cryptos**: Loss on Bitcoin cannot be set off against profit on Ethereum in the same year

**Implication**: If you made ₹1L profit on BTC and ₹2L loss on ETH, you still pay 30% on ₹1L BTC profit. The ₹2L ETH loss is "wasted" for tax purposes.

## 1% TDS on Crypto Transactions

From July 1, 2022, crypto exchanges deduct **1% TDS** (Tax Deducted at Source) on:
- Every crypto sale above threshold
- Threshold: ₹50,000/year for regular taxpayers; ₹10,000/year for specified persons (those who haven't filed ITR in past 3 years)

**The TDS is a credit** against your final tax liability. You can see it in your Form 26AS. When filing ITR, this TDS is credited against your total 30% tax due.

> Note: TDS is NOT a separate tax — it's an advance deduction toward your 30% liability. If your total tax is ₹1L and exchanges deducted ₹20,000 TDS, you pay ₹80,000 more at filing.

## Tax on Staking Rewards, Airdrops, Mining

| Income Type | When Taxed | Amount |
|-------------|------------|--------|
| **Staking rewards** | When received (fair market value at receipt) | 30% on value received |
| **Airdrops** | When received (fair market value) | 30% on value received |
| **Mining income** | When received (as business income — but VDA rules apply for tax rate) | 30% |
| **Crypto interest** | When received | 30% on interest amount |

**Important for staking/airdrops**: When you later sell the staked coins or airdrop tokens, your **cost of acquisition** is the value at which you were taxed when you received them (not zero).

## Gifted Crypto — Recipient Pays Tax

If someone gifts you crypto:
- If gift value > ₹50,000 in a year (and not from exempt relatives): You pay 30% tax on the fair market value at the time of receipt
- Exempt relatives: Parents, spouse, siblings, parents-in-law, etc. (same as Income Tax gift rules)
- If you later sell: Your cost of acquisition = price at which you were taxed on receipt (or donor's cost if exempted gift)

## How to File ITR for Crypto Income

### Step 1: Calculate Your Gains

Download your transaction history from each exchange (Coinbase, Binance, WazirX, Coinswitch, etc.) and calculate:
- Buy price of each coin
- Sell price
- Profit/Loss

### Step 2: Choose the Right ITR Form

- **ITR-2**: Salaried individuals with crypto income (no business income)
- **ITR-3**: Business owners, freelancers with crypto income
- **ITR-1**: NOT applicable if you have any VDA income

### Step 3: Fill Schedule VDA

In ITR-2 or ITR-3:
1. Go to **Schedule VDA**
2. For each crypto transaction (or aggregate per coin):
   - Date of acquisition
   - Date of transfer (sale)
   - Cost of acquisition
   - Sales consideration
   - Head of income: "Income from Other Sources" (not Capital Gains)
3. System calculates 30% tax automatically

### Step 4: Claim TDS Credit

In Schedule TDS / 26AS reconciliation, the exchange's 1% TDS deduction appears automatically. This is credited against your 30% tax.

## Crypto Tax Compliance — What Happens If You Don't Report

**Unreported crypto income** is detectable by the IT department through:
- Exchange reports (CBDT mandated exchanges to report)
- KYC data from exchanges matched with IT records
- Bank transaction patterns

Penalty for under-reporting:
- 50% additional tax on underreported income
- 200% penalty for misreported income (fraud)
- Income Tax department has sent notices to crypto investors

## Overseas Crypto Exchanges

If you use foreign exchanges (like Binance, Coinbase, Kraken) without an Indian account:
- Still taxable — Indian tax law applies to all income of Indian residents globally
- You must report in Schedule FA (Foreign Assets) if holdings > ₹7 lakh at any point
- No TDS by foreign exchange — you pay the entire tax at self-assessment time

## Frequently Asked Questions

### If I only converted Bitcoin to Ethereum (no INR withdrawal), is it taxable?

**Yes.** A crypto-to-crypto swap (BTC → ETH) is treated as a sale of BTC and purchase of ETH. You compute gains on the BTC sold at the market price at the time of conversion, even though no INR was received.

### I hold crypto but haven't sold it. Do I pay tax?

**No.** Tax is triggered on **sale/transfer** of the VDA, not on holding. Unrealized gains (paper profits) are not taxable. However, you must still declare holdings if they are above the Schedule FA threshold.

### My employer pays salary partly in crypto tokens. How is it taxed?

Crypto received as salary is taxable as **salary income** at your income slab rate (not 30% VDA rate) at the time of receipt. When you later sell the tokens, the difference from receipt price is taxed at 30%.

### Can I save crypto tax by putting it in my spouse's name?

No — income from assets transferred to spouse is **clubbed** back to you for tax purposes.

### Is there any threshold below which crypto tax is not applicable?

No minimum threshold. Even ₹100 profit from crypto is technically taxable at 30%. However, the ITR Schedule VDA requires reporting if total VDA transactions exceed ₹50,000 (approximate threshold per reporting guidelines).
