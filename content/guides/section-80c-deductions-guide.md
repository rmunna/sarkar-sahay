## What is Section 80C?

Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961 is the most popular tax-saving provision in India. It allows individual taxpayers and HUFs (Hindu Undivided Families) to claim deductions of up to **₹1,50,000 per financial year** from their gross total income by investing in specified instruments.

If you are in the highest tax bracket (30%), this deduction can save you up to **₹46,800 in taxes** (including 4% cess) every year.

**Key Points:**
- Maximum deduction limit: **₹1,50,000 per financial year**
- This limit is combined for Sections 80C, 80CCC, and 80CCD(1)
- Available under the **Old Tax Regime** only (not available under the New Tax Regime from FY 2023-24 onwards, unless you opt out)
- Both salaried and self-employed individuals can claim it

## Who Can Claim Section 80C Deductions?

- **Individual taxpayers** (resident and non-resident Indians)
- **Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs)**
- Companies, firms, and LLPs **cannot** claim 80C deductions

## All Eligible Investments and Expenses Under Section 80C

Here is the complete list of investments and payments that qualify for deduction under Section 80C:

### 1. Public Provident Fund (PPF)
- **Lock-in period:** 15 years
- **Interest rate:** 7.1% (government-set, revised quarterly)
- **Min/Max investment:** ₹500 to ₹1,50,000 per year
- **Tax on returns:** Fully exempt (EEE status)
- **Where to open:** Post office or any nationalised bank

### 2. Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS)
- **Lock-in period:** 3 years (shortest among 80C options)
- **Expected returns:** 10-15% (market-linked, not guaranteed)
- **Min investment:** ₹500 (via SIP)
- **Tax on returns:** LTCG above ₹1.25 lakh taxed at 12.5%
- **Where to invest:** Any mutual fund house or through brokers

### 3. Employee Provident Fund (EPF)
- **Lock-in period:** Until retirement (partial withdrawal allowed)
- **Interest rate:** 8.25% (for FY 2024-25)
- **Contribution:** 12% of basic salary (employee's share qualifies)
- **Tax on returns:** Exempt up to ₹2.5 lakh contribution per year

### 4. National Savings Certificate (NSC)
- **Lock-in period:** 5 years
- **Interest rate:** 7.7%
- **Min investment:** ₹1,000 (no maximum limit)
- **Tax on returns:** Interest is taxable but reinvested interest qualifies for 80C in subsequent years
- **Where to buy:** Post office

### 5. Life Insurance Premium (LIC and Others)
- **Eligible amount:** Premium paid for self, spouse, and children
- **Maximum eligible premium:** Up to 10% of sum assured (for policies issued after 1 April 2012)
- **Tax on maturity:** Exempt under Section 10(10D) if premium is within limits

### 6. Tax-Saving Fixed Deposits
- **Lock-in period:** 5 years
- **Interest rate:** 6.5-7.5% (varies by bank)
- **Tax on returns:** Interest is fully taxable
- **Where to open:** Any scheduled bank or post office

### 7. Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)
- **Lock-in period:** Until girl child turns 21
- **Interest rate:** 8.2%
- **Eligibility:** Only for girl child (max 2 accounts)
- **Tax on returns:** Fully exempt (EEE status)
- **Where to open:** Post office or authorised banks

### 8. Senior Citizens Savings Scheme (SCSS)
- **Lock-in period:** 5 years (extendable by 3 years)
- **Interest rate:** 8.2%
- **Eligibility:** Individuals aged 60+ (55+ for retired defence/government employees)
- **Max investment:** ₹30 lakh
- **Tax on returns:** Interest is taxable

### 9. Unit Linked Insurance Plans (ULIPs)
- **Lock-in period:** 5 years
- **Returns:** Market-linked
- **Tax on returns:** Exempt if annual premium ≤ ₹2.5 lakh

### 10. Home Loan Principal Repayment
- **Deduction on:** Principal portion of EMI
- **Condition:** Property should not be sold within 5 years of possession
- **Additional benefit:** Interest on home loan is deductible under Section 24(b)

### 11. Tuition Fees
- **Eligible for:** Full-time education of up to 2 children
- **Paid to:** Any university, college, or school in India
- **Not eligible:** Development fees, donations, coaching fees

### 12. Stamp Duty and Registration Charges
- **Deduction in:** The year of payment for purchase of residential property
- **One-time claim** in the year of property purchase

### 13. National Pension System (NPS) — Under 80CCD(1)
- **Limit:** Up to 10% of salary (included within ₹1.5 lakh limit)
- **Additional deduction:** ₹50,000 under Section 80CCD(1B) — over and above ₹1.5 lakh
- **Tax on returns:** Partially taxable at withdrawal

## How to Claim Section 80C Deductions — Step by Step

1. **Choose your investments** from the eligible list above based on your risk appetite and goals
2. **Invest before 31st March** of the financial year (e.g., 31 March 2026 for FY 2025-26)
3. **Collect proofs** — investment receipts, premium payment receipts, PPF passbook, home loan statements
4. **Submit proofs to employer** (for salaried individuals) — usually between January-March for TDS adjustment
5. **File your ITR** — Declare all 80C investments in Schedule VI-A of your income tax return
6. **Choose Old Tax Regime** — Section 80C is available only under the old regime. Compare both regimes before filing

## Tax Saving Strategies Under Section 80C

### Strategy 1: Start Early with SIP in ELSS
Invest ₹12,500 per month in ELSS via SIP. This covers the full ₹1.5 lakh limit, gives you the shortest lock-in (3 years), and potential for higher returns.

### Strategy 2: Combine EPF + PPF + ELSS
If your EPF contribution is around ₹50,000-₹60,000 per year, invest the remaining ₹90,000-₹1,00,000 across PPF (for safety) and ELSS (for growth).

### Strategy 3: Maximise Tax-Free Returns
For zero-tax returns, focus on PPF and SSY (if you have a daughter). Both enjoy EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) status — no tax at any stage.

### Strategy 4: Don't Forget Existing Payments
Before making new investments, count what you are already paying:
- EPF contribution (check salary slip)
- Home loan principal in EMI
- Children's school tuition fees
- Life insurance premiums

These may already cover a significant portion of your ₹1.5 lakh limit.

### Strategy 5: Use the Extra ₹50,000 NPS Benefit
Invest ₹50,000 in NPS under Section 80CCD(1B) for an additional deduction **over and above** the ₹1.5 lakh limit. This can save an extra ₹15,600 in taxes (at 30% bracket).

## Important Tips

1. **Don't invest only for tax saving** — choose instruments that match your financial goals
2. **Avoid last-minute rush** — plan your 80C investments at the start of the financial year (April)
3. **Keep all receipts and statements** — you will need them for ITR filing and potential scrutiny
4. **Old vs New regime** — if your total deductions (80C + 80D + HRA + others) exceed ₹3-4 lakh, old regime is usually better
5. **Joint investments** — PPF and SSY accounts are individual. Each family member can have separate accounts

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Q1. Can I claim Section 80C under the New Tax Regime?
**No.** Section 80C deductions are not available under the New Tax Regime (applicable from FY 2023-24 by default). You must opt for the Old Tax Regime to claim 80C benefits.

### Q2. What is the maximum limit under Section 80C?
The maximum deduction limit is **₹1,50,000 per financial year**. This is a combined limit for Sections 80C, 80CCC (pension fund contributions), and 80CCD(1) (NPS employee contribution).

### Q3. Can I claim both PPF and ELSS under 80C?
**Yes.** You can invest in multiple 80C-eligible instruments. The total combined deduction across all investments is capped at ₹1,50,000.

### Q4. Is the interest earned on PPF taxable?
**No.** PPF enjoys EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) status. The investment, the interest earned, and the maturity amount are all completely tax-free.

### Q5. What happens if I withdraw ELSS before 3 years?
You **cannot** withdraw ELSS before the 3-year lock-in period. Each SIP instalment has its own 3-year lock-in from the date of investment.

### Q6. Can I claim tuition fees for coaching classes?
**No.** Only tuition fees paid to a recognised university, college, or school in India for full-time education qualifies. Coaching classes, private tuitions, and donations are not eligible.

### Q7. Is Section 80C deduction available for NRIs?
**Yes.** NRIs can claim Section 80C deductions, but some instruments like PPF (new accounts), SSY, and NSC may not be available to NRIs for fresh investment.
