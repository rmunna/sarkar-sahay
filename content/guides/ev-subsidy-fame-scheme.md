# Electric Vehicle Subsidy India — FAME Scheme & State Incentives

India is pushing hard towards electric mobility. The government offers significant subsidies and incentives to make electric vehicles (EVs) affordable for common citizens. Whether you are planning to buy an electric scooter, car, or bus — or even set up an EV charging station — there are multiple schemes that can reduce your costs substantially.

This guide covers the central government's FAME scheme, the PM E-DRIVE scheme, state-level incentives, and how to make sure you get every rupee of subsidy you are entitled to.

> **Disclaimer:** CitizenNest is an independent informational website and is not affiliated with any government department or agency. Subsidy amounts and policies change frequently — always verify on official portals before making purchase decisions.

---

## What is the FAME Scheme?

FAME stands for **Faster Adoption and Manufacturing of Electric Vehicles**. It is the central government's flagship scheme to promote electric vehicles in India.

### FAME Timeline

- **FAME I (2015–2019):** Initial phase focused on demand incentives for EVs
- **FAME II (2019–2024):** Expanded with ₹10,000 crore budget, covering e-2W, e-3W, e-buses, and charging infrastructure
- **PM E-DRIVE (2024 onwards):** The successor scheme with ₹10,900 crore allocation, focusing on e-2W, e-3W, e-ambulances, e-trucks, and charging infrastructure

### How the Subsidy Works

The government provides a **demand incentive** (upfront subsidy) that reduces the ex-showroom price of the EV at the time of purchase. You do not need to apply separately — the subsidy is applied by the manufacturer/dealer, and they claim it from the government.

## Current Subsidy Amounts (PM E-DRIVE / FAME)

### Electric Two-Wheelers (E-2W)

- Subsidy of **₹5,000 per kWh** of battery capacity
- Maximum subsidy cap: **₹10,000** per vehicle
- Only for vehicles with ex-showroom price up to **₹5 lakh**

### Electric Three-Wheelers (E-3W)

- Subsidy varies by category (e-rickshaw, e-auto, e-cargo)
- Approximately **₹25,000–₹50,000** per vehicle depending on battery capacity

### Electric Four-Wheelers (E-4W)

- As of the current policy, **no direct central subsidy** for private electric cars
- However, significant **tax benefits** apply (see below)
- Some states offer their own subsidies for e-cars

### Electric Buses

- Subsidies provided to state transport undertakings for procurement of e-buses
- Not directly available to individual consumers

## State-Level EV Incentives

Many states offer additional subsidies and incentives on top of central schemes. Here are some key states:

### Delhi
- Additional subsidy of up to ₹30,000 for e-2W
- Up to ₹1.5 lakh for e-4W
- Road tax and registration fee waiver
- Scrapping incentive for replacing old petrol/diesel vehicles

### Maharashtra
- Up to ₹25,000 subsidy for e-2W
- Up to ₹2.5 lakh for e-4W (early bird incentive)
- Road tax exemption

### Gujarat
- Up to ₹20,000 for e-2W
- Up to ₹1.5 lakh for e-4W
- SGST reimbursement on EVs

### Tamil Nadu
- SGST reimbursement for EV buyers
- 100% road tax exemption
- Capital subsidy for EV manufacturing units

### Karnataka
- Road tax and registration fee exemption
- SGST reimbursement
- Incentives for EV charging infrastructure

### Rajasthan
- SGST reimbursement
- Road tax exemption for first 3 years
- Subsidy for e-2W and e-3W

**Note:** State policies change frequently. Check your state's EV policy document or transport department website for the latest incentives.

## Tax Benefits for EV Buyers

Beyond direct subsidies, EV buyers enjoy several tax advantages:

- **Lower GST:** EVs attract only **5% GST** compared to 28% + cess for petrol/diesel vehicles
- **Income tax deduction:** Under Section 80EEB, interest paid on loans taken to purchase EVs qualifies for a deduction of up to **₹1.5 lakh** (check current year applicability)
- **Road tax exemption:** Most states exempt EVs from road tax
- **Registration fee waiver:** Many states waive the registration fee for EVs
- **Green tax exemption:** EVs are exempt from the proposed green tax on older vehicles

## Eligibility

### For Vehicle Subsidy

- You must be an **Indian resident** purchasing an EV from an **authorised dealer**
- The vehicle must be on the **approved list** of models eligible for subsidy (listed on the FAME/E-DRIVE portal)
- The vehicle must meet **minimum technical specifications** (range, speed, battery capacity) set by the government
- **One subsidy per Aadhaar** for e-2W (to prevent misuse)

### For Charging Infrastructure Subsidy

- Available to individuals, companies, RWAs, and commercial establishments
- Must use **Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)** approved charging equipment
- Location must be accessible and meet DISCOM requirements

## Documents Required

### For Vehicle Purchase Subsidy

No separate documents needed — the dealer handles the subsidy claim. However, you will need:

- **Aadhaar card**
- **Valid driving licence** (for the vehicle category)
- **PAN card** (for tax benefit claims)
- **Bank account details**
- **Address proof**

### For Charging Infrastructure Subsidy

- Business registration documents (if applicable)
- Property ownership or lease documents
- Electrical load sanction from DISCOM
- BIS certification of charging equipment
- Bank account details

## Step-by-Step: How to Get EV Subsidy

### For Vehicle Purchase (Online + Offline)

#### Step 1: Check Eligible Models

Visit [e-amrit.niti.gov.in](https://e-amrit.niti.gov.in) or the FAME portal to see the list of vehicles eligible for central subsidy.

#### Step 2: Visit an Authorised Dealer

Go to the authorised dealership of your chosen EV brand. The dealer will show you the on-road price with the subsidy already deducted.

#### Step 3: Purchase the Vehicle

Complete the purchase. The ex-showroom price displayed will already reflect the central subsidy deduction. You pay the reduced price.

#### Step 4: Check State Subsidy

Ask the dealer about applicable state subsidies. In some states (like Delhi), you may need to apply separately through the state transport department portal.

#### Step 5: Claim State Subsidy (If Separate)

For states with separate application processes:

1. Visit the state transport or EV policy portal
2. Register with your vehicle details (registration number, chassis number)
3. Upload documents (invoice, RC, Aadhaar, bank details)
4. Subsidy is credited to your bank account within 30–60 days

#### Step 6: Claim Tax Benefits

When filing your income tax return, claim the Section 80EEB deduction on interest paid for the EV loan (if applicable).

### For Charging Station Subsidy

#### Step 1: Check FAME/RDSS Guidelines

Visit the heavy industries ministry portal for charging infrastructure subsidy guidelines.

#### Step 2: Apply Through Your DISCOM

Most charging station subsidies are routed through the local DISCOM. Apply with your project proposal and documents.

#### Step 3: Get Approval and Install

After approval, install the charging equipment as per specifications. Get it inspected by the DISCOM.

#### Step 4: Claim Subsidy

Submit the installation proof, invoices, and inspection certificate to claim the subsidy.

## Tips for EV Buyers

1. **Compare total cost of ownership** — EVs have lower running costs (₹1–1.5 per km vs ₹5–7 for petrol). Factor in fuel savings over 5–7 years.

2. **Check your state's policy** — state incentives can be even larger than central subsidies. Some states like Delhi offer very generous packages.

3. **Verify model eligibility** — not all EV models qualify for subsidies. Check the approved list before buying.

4. **Plan for charging** — ensure you have a charging point at home or nearby. Home charging with a 15A socket works for most e-2W and some e-4W.

5. **Consider battery warranty** — a good EV should offer at least 3 years or 50,000 km battery warranty. Premium models offer 8 years.

6. **Look at resale value** — the EV market is evolving fast. Popular brands with good service networks hold value better.

7. **Do not buy from unauthorised sources** — subsidy is only available through authorised dealers. Grey market purchases do not qualify.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### 1. Do I need to apply separately for the FAME/E-DRIVE subsidy?

No, the central subsidy is applied automatically at the dealership. The dealer deducts the subsidy amount from the ex-showroom price and claims it from the government. You pay the reduced price directly.

### 2. Can I get both central and state subsidies?

Yes, central and state subsidies can be availed simultaneously. They are independent schemes. For example, in Delhi, you can get both the PM E-DRIVE subsidy and the Delhi EV subsidy.

### 3. Is there a subsidy for electric cars?

The central government does not currently offer a direct purchase subsidy for private electric cars (e-4W). However, you benefit from lower GST (5%), road tax exemption, and income tax deduction on loan interest. Several states like Delhi and Maharashtra offer state-level subsidies for e-cars.

### 4. What if I sell my subsidised EV within a few years?

Some subsidy schemes have a **lock-in period** — you may need to keep the vehicle for a minimum period (usually 2–3 years). Selling before the lock-in period may require you to refund the subsidy. Check the specific scheme terms.

### 5. Are used/second-hand EVs eligible for subsidy?

No, subsidies are only available for new EVs purchased from authorised dealers. Used EVs do not qualify for any government subsidy.

### 6. Can a company or business buy EVs with subsidy?

The PM E-DRIVE scheme primarily targets individual buyers for e-2W. For fleet operators and businesses, separate provisions exist for e-buses and commercial e-3W. Check the scheme guidelines for your specific use case.

### 7. How do I find EV charging stations near me?

Use apps like **PlugShare**, **Tata Power EZ Charge**, or the **e-Amrit** portal to locate public EV charging stations across India. Most EV manufacturer apps also show nearby charging points.
