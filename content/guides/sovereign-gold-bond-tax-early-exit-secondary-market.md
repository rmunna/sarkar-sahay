# Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) Tax Guide 2026 — Interest, Early Exit, and Secondary Market Tax

Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) are one of India's most tax-efficient gold investments — but the tax treatment is different depending on **when and how you exit**. Here's the complete tax picture.

## The Four SGB Tax Scenarios

| Scenario | Tax Treatment |
|----------|--------------|
| 2.5% annual interest paid every 6 months | ✅ Taxable at your slab rate |
| Hold till 8-year maturity (RBI redemption) | ❌ Capital gains completely tax-free |
| Early redemption at Year 5, 6, or 7 (RBI window) | ❌ Capital gains completely tax-free |
| Sell on stock exchange (secondary market) — any time | ✅ LTCG 12.5% (if held >12 months); slab rate STCG (if <12 months) |

## SGB Interest — Taxable at Slab Rate

RBI pays 2.5% per annum on the initial issue price, credited to your bank account every 6 months.

**Example**:
- You invested ₹60,000 (100 grams of gold at ₹6,000/gram issue price)
- 2.5% annual interest = ₹1,500/year = ₹750 every 6 months

This ₹1,500 is taxable as **income from other sources** at your slab rate. It appears in your **AIS** (Annual Information Statement) and Form 26AS.

**Note**: There is NO TDS deducted on SGB interest. You pay tax on it when filing your annual ITR. Add it to Schedule OS (Interest from Savings/Other).

The interest is on the **original issue price** — not the current gold price. If gold price doubles, your 2.5% interest stays the same in absolute rupee terms.

## Tax at Maturity (Year 8) — Completely Tax-Free

If you hold SGB till the 8-year maturity and receive the redemption amount from RBI:
- **Capital gains = 0 tax** — Section 47(viic) of the Income Tax Act explicitly exempts this
- The full appreciation (whatever gold price increased over 8 years) is tax-free
- No TDS at redemption

**Example**:
- Bought SGB at ₹4,000/gram in 2018
- Gold price in 2026 at maturity: ₹8,000/gram
- Gain: ₹4,000/gram = 100% return
- Tax: ₹0

This is the primary tax advantage of SGB over physical gold (which faces LTCG 12.5% on sale) and gold ETFs (also LTCG 12.5%).

## Early Redemption (Year 5, 6, or 7) — Also Tax-Free

RBI allows early redemption of SGBs at the 5th, 6th, or 7th year anniversary coupon date. If you exit at these RBI-sanctioned early redemption windows:

**Capital gains from early redemption = Tax-free** (same as maturity)

This is confirmed in the original SGB scheme guidelines and the IT Act provisions for SGB redemptions.

**When early redemption dates open**: Typically 1-2 months before the coupon date. RBI announces the redemption price (based on average closing gold price of preceding week).

**How to access early redemption**:
- For demat-held SGBs: Through your broker (contact broker 2 weeks before the redemption date)
- For physical SGB certificates: At any RBI branch or issuing bank

## Selling on Secondary Market — Capital Gains Tax Applies

Many investors buy SGBs from stock exchanges (NSE/BSE) — especially now that fresh RBI issuances are paused. When you sell on the exchange (before maturity/early redemption windows), capital gains tax applies:

| Holding Period (from purchase on exchange) | Tax Rate |
|-------------------------------------------|---------|
| > 12 months (Long-term) | **12.5% LTCG** (Budget 2024 rate) |
| ≤ 12 months (Short-term) | **Slab rate STCG** |

**Note**: The 24-month LTCG period for gold (physical gold/gold ETFs) does NOT apply to SGBs. SGBs are treated like listed securities — the threshold is 12 months.

**Example**:
- Bought SGB on NSE in February 2025 at ₹7,500/gram
- Sold on NSE in March 2026 (13 months later) at ₹8,200/gram
- Gain: ₹700/gram → LTCG at 12.5%
- If 100 grams: LTCG = ₹70,000. Above ₹1.25L exemption threshold? No → ₹0 tax (within exemption)
- If gain was ₹1,80,000: Tax = (₹1,80,000 − ₹1,25,000) × 12.5% = ₹6,875

## Budget 2024 Change — SGB Secondary Market Tax

**Before Budget 2024**: Long-term capital gains on debt-category assets (which SGBs were sometimes classified under by courts) used the 24-month holding period and 20% with indexation.

**Budget 2024 clarification**: SGBs are treated as **listed securities** (similar to equity) with:
- 12-month holding period for LTCG
- 12.5% LTCG rate (same as equity)
- ₹1.25L annual exemption applies

This is favorable — the 12-month threshold is easier to achieve than 24 months, and 12.5% is lower than the earlier 20% without indexation that some were treating it as.

## Buying SGBs on Stock Exchange — What to Know

Since RBI paused fresh issuances in Feb 2024, secondary market buying on NSE/BSE is the only way:

- **Ticker format**: Search "SGB" or "SGOLD" on Zerodha/Groww — multiple series available
- **Face value**: Each unit = 1 gram of gold
- **Liquidity**: Volume can be low for older series — set limit orders, not market orders
- **Maturity dates vary**: Each series has different issue date → different maturity (8 years from issue)
- **Early redemption availability**: Varies by series

Buying on exchange = you're buying from another investor (not RBI directly). The maturity is still calculated from the original RBI issue date (not your purchase date), but tax on any exchange sale/purchase is based on your holding period.

## SGB and NRI Taxation

NRIs can hold SGBs (purchased when they were resident) but cannot buy new SGBs post becoming NRI. For SGBs held as NRI:
- Interest: Taxable in India at 30% TDS
- Maturity proceeds: Still tax-free under Section 47(viic) even for NRIs
- Secondary market sale: LTCG taxed as for NRI capital gains (12.5% + surcharge)

## How to Declare SGB in ITR

| Income | ITR Schedule |
|--------|-------------|
| SGB interest (2.5%) | Schedule OS — Interest from Others |
| Exchange sale STCG | Schedule CG — STCG on securities |
| Exchange sale LTCG | Schedule CG — LTCG 112A (listed securities) |
| Maturity/early redemption gain | NOT declared in ITR (exempt, no reporting required) |

**SGB in AIS**: The 2.5% interest appears in AIS. Exchange sale transactions appear in AIS under capital gains. Maturity/early redemption appears as "exempt capital gains" — check AIS for confirmation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I bought SGBs in 2019 and they mature in 2027. Should I hold till maturity or sell now on exchange?

Financial answer: If you sell now on exchange, you pay 12.5% LTCG on gains. If you wait till 2027 maturity, gains are tax-free. The tax saving at maturity vs selling today = capital gain × 12.5%. If you're expecting to exit within the next 1-2 years anyway, worth waiting for the early redemption window (Year 5 onwards) to get tax-free exit. If you need money urgently, sell on exchange now.

### I received SGB as a gift from my parents. What's my cost basis for capital gains?

If you sell on the stock exchange, your cost basis for capital gains = the value at which your parents acquired it (cost to the original owner). The holding period also includes parents' holding period. If they've held it for 12+ months, the gift recipient also gets LTCG treatment. For maturity held till 8 years from original issue date = still tax-free.

### How do I show maturity proceeds in ITR? My refund seems wrong.

Maturity proceeds from SGB (RBI redemption) are fully exempt — no entry needed in ITR. However, these amounts may appear in AIS. If they do, declare them in Schedule EXFINAL (Exempt income) to avoid a mismatch notice. Simply noting "SGB maturity — exempt u/s 47(viic)" clarifies for the IT Department.
