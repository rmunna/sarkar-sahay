# How to Buy a New Car in India 2026 — Complete Guide

Buying a car is the second-largest purchase for most Indian families. Dealers have training to maximize margins; knowing their playbook puts you in control. Here's how to buy right.

---

## Step 1: Understand the Actual Price — Ex-Showroom vs On-Road

The "ex-showroom" price advertised is never what you pay. Add:

| Component | Typical Amount |
|-----------|--------------|
| Ex-showroom price | Base (as advertised) |
| GST | 28% + cess (0–22% based on car type) |
| Road tax | 7–15% (varies by state; MH ~11%, Karnataka ~14%, UP ~7%) |
| Insurance (1st year) | ₹15,000–₹50,000 (depends on IDV) |
| Registration | ₹2,000–₹8,000 |
| TCS (on cars above ₹10L) | 1% of ex-showroom |
| Fastag | ₹500 |
| Number plate, handling | ₹1,000–₹3,000 |
| **On-road price** | Ex-showroom × 1.35–1.45 (approx) |

**Example**: Tata Nexon (mid variant) ex-showroom ₹12 lakh
- GST (28% + 1% cess) = ₹3.48L
- Road tax (Karnataka ~14%) = ₹1.68L
- Insurance = ₹20K
- Registration, handling = ₹10K
- TCS 1% = ₹12K
- **On-road = ₹17.08 lakh**

**Why this matters**: Compare cars on on-road price, not ex-showroom. A "cheaper" car may have higher road tax in your state.

## Step 2: GST on Cars — Which Rate Applies?

| Car Type | GST Rate | Cess | Total |
|----------|---------|------|-------|
| Small cars (<1,200cc, <4m, petrol) | 28% | 1% | 29% |
| Small cars (<1,500cc, <4m, diesel) | 28% | 3% | 31% |
| Mid-size cars (>4m, <1,500cc) | 28% | 15% | 43% |
| Large cars (>4m, >1,500cc) | 28% | 20% | 48% |
| EVs (electric vehicles) | 5% | 0% | 5% |
| Hybrid cars (mild hybrid) | 28% + 12–20% cess | | |

**EV advantage**: 5% GST vs 29–48% on petrol/diesel cars — massive price difference especially for larger vehicles.

## Step 3: TCS — Tax Collected at Source on Cars Above ₹10 Lakh

**Since October 2020**: Dealers must collect TCS @ **1% of ex-showroom price** for vehicles above ₹10L.

- This appears on your Form 26AS as advance tax
- You can claim full TCS credit in your ITR
- For salaried: Adjust TCS against your tax liability — reduces tax due at year end
- For zero/low-tax individuals: TCS is refunded via ITR

**TCS is NOT extra cost** — it's advance tax. You get it back. Don't let dealers mislead you.

## Step 5: Negotiation — How to Get the Best Deal

Dealers typically work with a margin of 3–7% on ex-showroom price. Here's how to extract maximum value:

### Timing — Go at Month-End

**Sales targets reset monthly.** Dealers push hardest to meet targets in the last 3–5 days of the month. Visit on the 28th–31st — dealer motivation to close is highest.

### Research Before Walking In

1. Check on-road price quotes from 2–3 dealers in your city (same model, same variant)
2. Join car owner forums (Team-BHP, Facebook groups for the car model) — current discount/offer information is shared there
3. Know the ex-showroom price exactly from manufacturer's website

### What to Negotiate

| Negotiable | What to Get |
|-----------|-------------|
| Cash discount | 1–5% of ex-showroom on popular models; more on slow-moving |
| Free accessories | Dashcam, seat covers, floor mats, reverse sensor — ₹15K–50K worth |
| Extended warranty | 1 extra year free (dealer-funded) |
| Free service package | 2–3 free services |
| Free insurance | First year insurance absorbed by dealer |
| Exchange bonus | Extra ₹10K–₹30K if you're exchanging an old car |

**Don't negotiate piecemeal**: Get total on-road offer in writing with all items, then compare with another dealer's offer.

### What NOT to Compromise On

- Mandatory accessories: Don't add dealer-added accessories you don't want. They inflate price while reducing overall discount room.
- Loan from specific NBFCs: Dealers earn commission from their "preferred" financiers. Compare independently.

## Step 6: Car Loan — Compare Independently

Don't use the dealer's in-house financing without comparing:

| Lender | Rate Range | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| HDFC Bank | 8.5–10.5% | Check relationship discount |
| SBI | 8.65–9.25% | Competitive for existing account holders |
| ICICI Bank | 8.7–10.0% | Quick processing |
| Kotak | 9.0–10.5% | Check pre-approved offers |
| Dealer NBFC (e.g., Maruti Finance) | 9.0–11.0% | Convenient but check rate |

**EMI calculation**: ₹10L loan, 7 years, 9% rate = ₹15,968/month (₹13.41L total payment including ₹3.41L interest)

**Loan-to-value**: Most banks finance 80–90% of on-road price (not ex-showroom).

**Avoid 0% finance schemes**: Usually involves giving up the cash discount. Calculate total cost: dealer's "0% finance" price vs independent loan with cash discount. Cash discount + independent loan often wins.

## Step 7: Car Insurance — Don't Just Accept Dealer's Quote

Mandatory at registration: Third-party (TP) insurance. Optional but recommended: Own Damage (OD) cover.

**Dealer quotes bundled insurance** and earns commission. Compare independently at policybazaar.com, acko.com, or directly at Bajaj Allianz, ICICI Lombard, etc.

**For a ₹12L car**: 
- Third-party only: ~₹3,000–5,000/year (fixed by IRDAI)
- Comprehensive (TP + OD): ~₹15,000–25,000/year
- Zero-dep add-on: Additional ₹3,000–6,000/year (worth it for new cars)

**Buy from insurer directly** if you can: Slightly cheaper, no dealer commission.

## Step 8: EV Considerations (2026)

**Why EVs are growing rapidly**:
- 5% GST (vs 28–48% on ICE vehicles)
- State subsidies: Delhi, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka have additional EV subsidies
- Lower running cost: ₹1.5–2/km vs ₹8–12/km for petrol at current prices
- Home charging: If you have a parking spot, home charging (AC charger) is cheapest

**EV concerns for India**:
- Charging network: Improving but still limited in tier-2/3 cities and highways
- Apartment charging: Complex if no dedicated parking
- Battery life: 8-year/1.6L km warranty from most manufacturers (Tata, MG, Hyundai)
- Range anxiety: Most EVs do 400–500km per charge — adequate for city use

**Most popular EVs in India (2026)**: Tata Nexon EV, Tata Punch EV, MG Windsor, Hyundai Creta EV, BYD Seal/Atto3.

## Step 9: Delivery Checklist

Before accepting the car at the showroom:

| Check | What to Look For |
|-------|----------------|
| VIN matches | VIN on dashboard + documents must match exactly |
| No scratches/dents | Walk around under daylight — not under showroom lighting |
| All features work | AC, windows, lights, horn, infotainment, ADAS features |
| Fuel level | Petrol: 1/4 tank minimum; EV: 80%+ charge |
| Documents | RC (or temp registration), insurance policy, warranty card, service booklet |
| Accessories | All promised/paid accessories installed |

**Don't rush**: Delivery day is your last leverage point. Any defect found after signature is a claim, not a free repair.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I pay cash or take a loan for a car?

If you can get home loan interest rates (deductible) vs car loan rate — car loan is not tax-deductible. **If you have liquid savings earning 7%+ and car loan rate is 9%+, pay cash** (net cost of loan = 2%+ opportunity cost). If money is locked in long-term instruments, take a short 3-year loan and close early.

### New car or pre-owned (second-hand)?

Pre-owned: 30–40% cheaper for 2–3-year-old cars with ~30,000 km. New: full warranty, latest features, no ownership history risk. For first car or budget above ₹12L (price-to-value is better for new), buy new. For tight budget or second car, 2-3 year pre-owned with certified used car from manufacturer is excellent value.

### Is there a GST refund if I export the car or move abroad?

No GST refund on personal vehicle purchase for individuals.

### What's the best month to buy a car in India?

**December–March**: Dealers clear inventory before new models/variants launch. Best discounts. Also, many offers end March 31 (financial year end). **Festival season (October–November)**: Manufacturers run schemes, but dealers are busy and less willing to negotiate.
