# Life Insurance Maturity Tax India 2026 — Section 10(10D) and ULIP Tax Rules

Not all life insurance payouts are tax-free. Understanding which payouts are exempt and which are taxable prevents surprises at ITR filing time.

## The General Rule: Section 10(10D)

Under Section 10(10D) of the Income Tax Act:

**All life insurance payouts (maturity + death benefits) are TAX-FREE** if these conditions are met:
1. The annual premium paid does NOT exceed 10% of the sum assured (for policies issued after April 1, 2012)
2. For older policies (before April 1, 2003): premium ≤ 20% of sum assured
3. For policies issued April 1, 2003 – March 31, 2012: premium ≤ 20% of sum assured

**If premium exceeds these limits**: Maturity proceeds become taxable as "Income from Other Sources."

## Applying the 10% Rule

**Policy example**: Sum Assured = ₹10 lakh, Annual Premium = ₹80,000
- Premium as % of sum assured: ₹80,000 / ₹10,00,000 = 8%
- 8% < 10% → **Tax-free** under Section 10(10D) ✅

**Policy example**: Sum Assured = ₹5 lakh, Annual Premium = ₹70,000
- Premium as % of sum assured: ₹70,000 / ₹5,00,000 = 14%
- 14% > 10% → **Maturity IS TAXABLE** ❌

The agent may show you impressive returns, but high-premium policies relative to sum assured create taxable maturities.

## Term Insurance — Always Tax-Free

**Death benefit** from a pure term insurance policy: Always tax-free under Section 10(10D), regardless of premium amount. The 10% rule only affects maturity proceeds.

For term insurance, there is no maturity payout — it pays only on death. So 10(10D) practically means term insurance death claims are always fully tax-free.

**Premium deductible under Section 80C**: Up to ₹1.5L in premiums paid for life insurance (for self, spouse, or children) is deductible under 80C. Available under old regime.

## Traditional Policies (Endowment, Money-Back) — Tax Treatment

**Endowment / whole life / money-back policies** issued on or after April 1, 2012:
- If annual premium ≤ 10% of sum assured: Maturity payout tax-free (Section 10(10D))
- If premium > 10% of sum assured: **Maturity payout taxable** — added to "Income from Other Sources" at your slab rate

**Survival benefit payouts** (periodic payouts from money-back policies during policy term):
- Tax-free under 10(10D) IF the underlying policy qualifies (premium ≤ 10%)

## ULIP (Unit Linked Insurance Plans) — Budget 2021 Change

ULIPs are investment-linked insurance plans. Budget 2021 created a separate rule for high-value ULIPs:

### Pre-Budget 2021 (Policies issued before February 1, 2021):
- Maturity: Tax-free under Section 10(10D) regardless of premium (if premium ≤ 10% of sum assured)
- Beloved by high-income individuals as tax-free investment vehicle

### Post-Budget 2021 (Policies issued on or after February 1, 2021):

**If annual ULIP premium aggregate exceeds ₹2.5 lakh in any year**:
- The maturity proceeds are **NOT tax-free** under 10(10D)
- Instead treated like equity MF — **LTCG at 10% (now 12.5%)** on gains above ₹1L/₹1.25L
- Since Budget 2024: LTCG at 12.5% above ₹1.25L LTCG

### If Multiple ULIPs:
The ₹2.5L threshold applies to the **aggregate annual premium across all ULIPs** held in a financial year. Even if each ULIP's premium is below ₹2.5L, the combination may cross the threshold.

**Example**:
- ULIP A annual premium: ₹1,80,000
- ULIP B annual premium: ₹1,00,000
- Total: ₹2,80,000 (exceeds ₹2.5L threshold)
- **Both ULIPs' maturity proceeds are taxable** (LTCG rate for the "taxable" portion)

### The ₹2.5L Threshold — Which ULIPs Are Covered?

When aggregate exceeds ₹2.5L, the **ULIPs are sorted by premium high-to-low**. The highest-premium ULIPs are treated as "qualifying" (exempt) up to ₹2.5L, and the remaining are "non-qualifying" (taxable).

This is complex — generally, if aggregate premium > ₹2.5L, treat all ULIP maturity as potentially taxable and consult a CA.

## High-Value Life Insurance — Budget 2023

**Budget 2023** added another rule for traditional (non-ULIP) life insurance policies:

From April 1, 2023 onwards (policies issued on or after):
- If **annual premium exceeds ₹5 lakh**, maturity is taxable (no 10(10D) exemption)
- Taxable as "Income from Other Sources" at slab rate (not LTCG)

**For policies issued before April 1, 2023**: Old rules apply (10% of sum assured test, regardless of ₹5L threshold).

## Summary of Taxability Rules (Simplified)

| Policy Type | Issued Before | Annual Premium | Maturity Tax Treatment |
|-------------|--------------|----------------|----------------------|
| Term insurance | Any date | Any | Death benefit always tax-free |
| Traditional (endowment/money-back) | April 1, 2012 | ≤ 10% of sum assured AND ≤ ₹5L | **Tax-free** |
| Traditional | April 1, 2023 onwards | > ₹5L/year | **Taxable (slab rate)** |
| ULIP | Before Feb 1, 2021 | Any | **Tax-free** (if premium ≤ 10% sum assured) |
| ULIP | Feb 1, 2021 onwards | Aggregate ≤ ₹2.5L | **Tax-free** |
| ULIP | Feb 1, 2021 onwards | Aggregate > ₹2.5L | **Taxable (LTCG 12.5%)** |

## Keyman Insurance — Different Tax Treatment

If a company buys "keyman insurance" on an employee's life: Premiums are deductible for the company, but maturity proceeds are taxable in the hands of the key employee.

## Section 80C and Insurance Premiums

Insurance premiums paid are deductible under Section 80C (up to ₹1.5L), but:
- If the policy's maturity is taxable (due to premium > 10% of sum assured), the 80C deduction is **also disallowed** for that year's premiums

You lose both the tax deduction AND the tax-free maturity — making high-premium policies doubly unfavorable.

## What to Do If Your Policy's Maturity Is Taxable

If you're holding an old policy where premiums > 10% of sum assured and maturity is taxable:
1. Include the maturity proceeds in "Income from Other Sources" in ITR
2. The **cost of the policy** (premiums paid, net of any bonuses received) becomes your cost basis
3. Only the gain (maturity value minus premiums paid) is taxable
4. No TDS is typically deducted by insurer on maturity (if tax-exempt), but for taxable policies, TDS at 1% may apply (check with your insurer)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I paid ₹1L premium per year on an endowment policy for 20 years. Sum assured is ₹5L. Is my maturity taxable?

Premium as % of sum assured: ₹1,00,000 / ₹5,00,000 = 20%. This exceeds the 10% limit (for policies after April 1, 2012). Your maturity is **taxable** at your slab rate. This is a common mistake in old traditional policies.

### I have a LIC Jeevan Saral policy. Is the maturity tax-free?

LIC Jeevan Saral is a notorious policy where the "sum assured" in marketing materials differed from the actual "sum assured" for 10(10D) calculation. The IT Department has clarified that the sum assured for 10(10D) is the actual guaranteed death benefit, not the maturity sum. Check your policy document for the death benefit amount and compare to your annual premium.

### Should I surrender my old ULIP and invest in equity MF instead?

Compare: ULIP charges (mortality, fund management, allocation) vs MF expense ratio. If ULIP was bought pre-2021 (tax-free maturity) and has another 5–10 years: Hold for the tax-free exit. If ULIP bought post-2021 with premium > ₹2.5L/year: Compare net-of-charge returns with equity MF LTCG (12.5%). Often MF wins even with tax.
