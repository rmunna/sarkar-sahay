# How to Write & Register a Will in India 2026

Most Indians don't write a Will thinking it's only for the elderly or wealthy. But if you have any assets — bank accounts, mutual funds, property, insurance — a Will is the most powerful and cheapest legal tool to ensure they go exactly where you want without family disputes or court delays.

---

## What Is a Will?

A Will (also called Testament) is a legal document declaring how you want your assets distributed after death. In India, it's governed by:
- **Indian Succession Act, 1925** (for Hindus, Sikhs, Jains, Parsis, Christians)
- **Muslim Personal Law** (for Muslims — Wasiyat, with restrictions: can Will only 1/3rd of estate to non-heirs)

A Will can cover: bank accounts, fixed deposits, mutual funds, shares, property, jewelry, vehicles, business interests, and personal belongings.

---

## Legal Requirements for a Valid Will in India

A Will is valid in India if it meets these conditions:

| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| **In writing** | Handwritten or typed — both valid |
| **Signed by testator** | You (the Will maker) must sign at the bottom, in the presence of witnesses |
| **Two witnesses** | Two adult witnesses must sign in your presence |
| **Witnesses cannot be beneficiaries** | If a beneficiary witnesses, their gift under the Will becomes void |
| **Sound mind** | Will must be made when you are of sound mind and free from coercion |
| **Age** | 18+ years to make a Will |

**Registration**: Legally optional. But registration makes Will practically uncontestable and admissible in all courts without proving authenticity.

**Stamp duty**: None. Will is exempt from stamp duty in India.

---

## Should You Register the Will?

| | Registered Will | Unregistered Will |
|--|----------------|------------------|
| Legal validity | Same (both valid) | Same |
| Court admissibility | Automatic | Must prove authenticity |
| Challenge-proof | Much harder to challenge | Can be contested more easily |
| Privacy | Filed at Sub-Registrar, public record | Private until your death |
| Cost | ₹200–₹1,000 + lawyer fee | Nil |
| Requirement | Optional | Not required |

**Recommendation**: Register your Will. The small cost and effort dramatically reduces the chance of disputes after your death.

## How to Write a Will — Format

A simple Will format (you can write in plain language):

---

**WILL OF [YOUR FULL NAME]**

I, [Full Name], son/daughter of [Father's Name], aged [Age] years, residing at [Full Address], being of sound mind and disposing memory, hereby revoke all my former Wills and codicils (if any) and declare this to be my Last Will and Testament.

**APPOINTMENT OF EXECUTOR**: I hereby appoint [Executor's Full Name], [Relationship], residing at [Address], as the Executor of this my Will. In the event of Executor's inability or unwillingness to act, I appoint [Alternate Executor] as alternate Executor.

**BEQUESTS**:

1. My Bank Account(s) at [Bank Name], Account Number [XXXX], Branch [Branch Name], with all monies therein, I bequeath to [Beneficiary Name], [Relationship], aged [Age] years, residing at [Address].

2. My mutual fund investments held in folio number(s) [XXXX] with [AMC Name] / in my Zerodha Demat Account [Account No.], I bequeath to [Beneficiary Name].

3. My property/flat at [Full Property Address], bearing [registration details], I bequeath to [Beneficiary Name].

4. All remaining assets not specifically mentioned above, I bequeath to [Residuary Beneficiary Name].

**GUARDIAN**: If any beneficiary named above is a minor at the time of my death, I appoint [Guardian Name] as their guardian for the purpose of this Will.

IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have signed this Will at [City] on this [Date] in the presence of the witnesses undersigned.

Signature: _______________
Name: [Your Full Name]
Date: [Date]

**WITNESSES**:

I. Signature: _______________ Name: _______________ Address: _______________
II. Signature: _______________ Name: _______________ Address: _______________

---

*Keep it simple and clear. Specific details (account numbers, property descriptions) prevent confusion.*

## How to Register a Will — Step by Step

### Step 1: Choose Office

Go to the Sub-Registrar's office in whose jurisdiction:
- Your residential address falls, OR
- Your property (if you're registering property bequest specifically) falls

### Step 2: Prepare Documents

| Document | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Original Will (one original + 2 copies) | Signed and witnessed |
| Photo ID of testator | Aadhaar / PAN / Passport |
| Photo ID of witnesses | Both must appear in person |
| Photo ID of executor | If possible; not mandatory |
| Passport photographs | 2–4 photos of testator |
| Registration fee | ₹200–₹1,000 (check state-specific rates) |

### Step 3: Visit Sub-Registrar

Visit with both witnesses (they must appear in person to verify their signatures). The testator (Will maker) must appear in person.

### Step 4: Registration Process

1. Present Will and documents to Sub-Registrar
2. Sub-Registrar reads/confirms document
3. Testator and witnesses sign in Sub-Registrar's presence
4. Sub-Registrar affixes certificate of registration
5. Will is recorded in the books of Sub-Registrar
6. You get registered Will copy with endorsement

**Privacy note**: Registered Wills become part of public records — anyone can theoretically access them with the document number. If privacy is a concern, some people register only after death (executor can register a Will after testator's death too, though more complex).

## What Happens If You Die Without a Will (Intestate)?

Property distribution follows the personal succession law:

**For Hindus (Hindu Succession Act)**:
- Class I heirs (equal share): Spouse + Sons + Daughters + Mother
- If no Class I heirs: Class II (Father, siblings, siblings' children, etc.)

**For Muslims (Muslim Personal Law)**:
- Fixed shares for specific relatives (spouse, parents, children) — cannot be altered by Will for >1/3rd estate

**For Christians/Parsis (Indian Succession Act)**:
- Spouse and children inherit proportionally

**Problems without Will**:
- All legal heirs must consent for any property transaction (one unhappy relative can block everything)
- Bank accounts need succession certificate / legal heir certificate
- Courts may need to get involved (months to years)

A Will with clear instructions to a named executor avoids all of this.

## Common Will Mistakes to Avoid

**Mistake 1: Making beneficiary a witness**
If your spouse witnesses your Will, and you leave everything to your spouse — the bequest to the spouse may be void. Witnesses must be neutral third parties.

**Mistake 2: Not updating Will after major life events**
Marriage, divorce, birth of children, death of beneficiaries — update your Will to reflect changes. Write a new Will (explicitly revoke old one) rather than crossing out sections.

**Mistake 3: Will conflicts with nomination**
Nominations in bank accounts, EPF, insurance take precedence for those specific assets — your Will cannot override nominations. Nominee for mutual funds holds assets as custodian (so legal heirs can still claim via court, but Will doesn't automatically override nomination for quick practical access).

**Mistake 4: Joint property cannot be Willed away independently**
If you co-own property with someone (50-50), you can only Will your 50% share — not the other person's share.

**Mistake 5: Not naming an executor**
Without an executor named, courts appoint an administrator (time-consuming). Name a trusted person (lawyer, family member, trusted friend) as executor.

## Can a Will Be Challenged?

Yes — a Will can be challenged in court on grounds of:
- Unsound mind (dementia, illness, coercion at time of Will)
- Forgery
- Undue influence
- Improper execution (missing witness signatures)
- Fraud

**How to minimize challenge risk**:
- Get Will registered
- Have a doctor's certificate dated around Will date if there are concerns about mental capacity
- Use a lawyer to draft (clear, unambiguous language)
- Ensure witnesses are not beneficiaries
- Keep a copy with the executor

## Codicil — Updating Will Without Rewriting

A codicil is an amendment to an existing Will. Format: "I, [Name], declare this Codicil dated [Date] to my Will dated [Original Will Date], and hereby amend Clause X to read..." — signed and witnessed same as original Will.

Can be registered separately at Sub-Registrar.

**Simpler option**: Write a completely new Will (explicitly state "I revoke all previous Wills") — avoids confusion from multiple codicils.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I write a Will on plain paper without a lawyer?

Yes — a handwritten or typed Will on plain paper is legally valid if signed by you and two witnesses. No lawyer, no stamp paper, no registration required (though registration is recommended).

### What if my mother died without a Will and left bank FDs?

You need a legal heir certificate (from District Magistrate) or succession certificate (from court) to claim bank FDs without a Will. Process takes 30–90 days. See: [Legal Heir Certificate Guide →](/guide/legal-heir-certificate-apply-online)

### Can NRIs write a Will for Indian property?

Yes — NRIs can write a Will in India (or abroad with proper attestation) for Indian assets. An NRI Will executed abroad needs to be notarized and apostilled (or attested by Indian Embassy) before use in India.

### Can I exclude a child from my Will?

Technically yes — you can leave property to whoever you choose. However, a completely excluded child may challenge the Will claiming "undue influence" or "lack of sound mind." A Will lawyer can help you draft language that reduces challenge risk if excluding someone.
