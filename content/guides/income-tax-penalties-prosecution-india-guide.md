# Income Tax Penalties India 2026 — Complete Guide to All Penalties and When They Apply

Income tax compliance failures attract different levels of consequences — from minor fees for late filing to criminal prosecution for tax evasion. Understanding which penalty applies when helps you take the right corrective action.

## Level 1: Late Filing Fee (Section 234F)

The most common: if you file ITR after July 31 (the due date for non-audited individuals):

| Filing Timeline | Income | Fee |
|----------------|--------|-----|
| August 1 – December 31 | Above ₹5 lakh | ₹5,000 |
| August 1 – December 31 | ₹5 lakh or below | ₹1,000 |
| January 1 onwards (belated ITR) | Any | Same as above |
| Did not file at all | — | Same fee + interest |

**Section 234F fee is payable when you file** — it's added to your tax liability. Unlike other penalties, it's relatively small and not subject to negotiation.

**Note**: If income is below the basic exemption limit (₹2.5L old regime), no fee is charged even for late filing.

## Level 2: Interest on Late Tax Payment

### Section 234A — Interest for Not Filing ITR
- 1% per month on unpaid tax
- From July 31 to date of filing

### Section 234B — Interest for Non-Payment of Advance Tax
- 1% per month on shortfall in advance tax
- Applicable if advance tax paid < 90% of total tax liability

### Section 234C — Deferral of Advance Tax Installments
- 1% per month on shortfall in each installment (June 15, Sept 15, Dec 15, March 15)

These are interest charges, not penalties — they're compensatory. No discretion in waiver; they're calculated automatically.

## Level 3: Penalties for Incorrect / Incomplete Returns

### Section 271(1)(b) — Failure to Respond to Notice
- ₹10,000 per default
- If you don't respond to a notice (143(2), 133(6), etc.)
- Each non-response is a separate default

### Section 271A — Non-Maintenance of Books of Account
- If your business requires books but you don't maintain them
- ₹25,000 penalty

### Section 271B — Non-Audit of Accounts (When Mandatory)
- Business with turnover > ₹1 crore (cash business) or ₹10 crore (non-cash) must be audited under Section 44AB
- Non-compliance: 0.5% of turnover, minimum ₹1.5 lakh, maximum ₹1.5 lakh (Budget 2021 reduced cap)

## Level 4: Underreporting and Misreporting of Income

### Section 270A — Underreported Income

**Underreported income** = income returned is less than income per assessment order.

| Type | Penalty |
|------|---------|
| **Underreporting** (simple mistake, genuinely misunderstood) | 50% of tax on underreported income |
| **Misreporting** (false documentation, suppression of evidence, fraud) | **200% of tax on misreported income** |

**Underreporting examples**:
- Forgot to include FD interest
- SFT data mismatch
- Minor capital gains not declared
- HRA exemption slightly over-claimed

**Misreporting examples**:
- Falsified receipts for deductions
- Fake business expenses
- Claiming deduction for non-existent investment
- Suppressing rental income with fake documents

## Level 5: Concealment Penalty (Section 271(1)(c))

The older provision (before 270A): Penalty for concealment of income or furnishing inaccurate particulars.
- **100%–300% of tax evaded** (discretion with assessing officer)
- Applied for cases filed before AY 2017-18 or under legacy assessments

For newer assessments, Section 270A (above) applies.

**Key difference**: Underreporting = you disclosed less than actual (mistake or deliberate). Concealment = you actively hid income (deliberate). Misreporting under 270A is closest to concealment.

## Level 6: Prosecution (Criminal Action)

For serious, wilful cases, the IT Department can initiate **criminal prosecution** under the Income Tax Act:

| Offence | Section | Punishment |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Wilful failure to file ITR | 276CC | 3 months to 2 years (+ fine) |
| Wilful failure to pay tax | 276B | 3 months to 7 years (+ fine) |
| Wilful concealment of income | 276C(1) | 6 months to 7 years (+ fine) |
| False statement in return | 277 | 6 months to 7 years (+ fine) |
| Abetting false return | 278 | 6 months to 7 years (+ fine) |

**For amounts above ₹25 lakh**: Minimum imprisonment is 6 months (cannot be less).
**For amounts below ₹25 lakh**: Minimum imprisonment is 3 months.

**Prosecution is rare for honest mistakes**. It's initiated by senior CBDT officials for deliberate, wilful, and significant tax evasion.

## Immunity Through Compounding

**Compounding**: Paying compounding fees to avoid prosecution. If prosecution has been initiated, the taxpayer can pay compounding fees (percentage of tax evaded) and the prosecution is dropped.

Compounding fees: Generally 100%–300% of tax evaded + late fees + interest. Expensive but avoids criminal record.

## Penalty vs Interest — Key Distinction

| Type | Nature | Can Be Waived? |
|------|--------|---------------|
| Section 234F late fee | Statutory fee | No (automatic) |
| Section 234A/B/C interest | Interest (compensatory) | No (automatic) |
| Section 270A underreporting penalty | Penalty | Yes (by Commissioner on reasonable cause) |
| Section 271(1)(c) concealment | Penalty | Yes (by Tribunal/Court) |
| Prosecution | Criminal | Via compounding |

## How to Avoid Most Penalties

1. **File ITR by July 31** → Avoids 234F fee and 234A interest
2. **Pay advance tax on time** → Avoids 234B/C interest
3. **Respond to notices** → Avoids Section 271(1)(b) ₹10K penalty
4. **Declare all income** → Avoids 270A underreporting penalty
5. **Maintain proper books** (if required) → Avoids 271A/271B
6. **Never forge documents** → Avoids 270A misreporting (200% penalty) and prosecution

## If You Received a Penalty Notice

If the IT Department issues a show-cause notice before imposing penalty (which they must):
1. Respond within the given time (usually 30 days)
2. Explain why the apparent discrepancy happened (genuine mistake, calculation error, source of income explanation)
3. Provide supporting documents
4. If penalty is unjust: Waiver application to Commissioner for "reasonable cause"

**Reasonable cause examples**: Natural calamity, serious illness, death in family, reliance on incorrect professional advice. These can lead to penalty waiver.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I forgot to file ITR for FY 2022-23. What's the penalty now (2026)?

The window to file belated ITR for FY 2022-23 (AY 2023-24) closed on December 31, 2023. However, you can file an **Updated ITR (Section 139(8A))** within 4 years (up to AY 2027-28 for FY 2022-23 with Budget 2025's extension). Updated ITR attracts additional tax of 25–50% on incremental tax due — not a penalty but an additional tax. This is better than facing a notice later.

### My ITR was filed on time but I underreported ₹50,000 due to a genuine error. Will I face a penalty?

If the underreported amount is discovered in assessment, a 50% penalty under Section 270A can apply. However, if it's a genuine mistake (not misreporting), you can file a revised ITR (if within December 31 of the AY) to correct it — avoiding the assessment scenario entirely. Pay the correct tax + interest (234A/B if any).

### My friend received a prosecution notice. Will he go to jail?

Prosecution doesn't mean immediate jail — it initiates criminal proceedings. At court, the accused has the opportunity to defend, pay compounding fees, or the matter may take years. Most prosecution cases either result in compounding (payment) or lengthy court battles. Very few result in actual imprisonment, and those are for large-scale, deliberate evasion with clear evidence.
