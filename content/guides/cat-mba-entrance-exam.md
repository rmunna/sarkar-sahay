# CAT Exam — Complete MBA Entrance Exam Guide

The Common Admission Test (CAT) is India's most prestigious MBA entrance exam. Every year, lakhs of candidates appear for CAT hoping to secure a seat in one of the IIMs or other top B-schools. This guide covers everything you need to know about CAT 2026 — from eligibility and exam pattern to preparation tips and the IIM selection process.

---

## What is CAT?

CAT (Common Admission Test) is a national-level management entrance exam conducted by the Indian Institutes of Management (IIMs) on a rotational basis. It is the gateway to MBA/PGDM programmes at all 21 IIMs and over 1,200 other B-schools across India.

**Key facts about CAT:**

- **Conducted by:** IIMs (rotational basis — a different IIM each year)
- **Frequency:** Once a year (usually in November or December)
- **Mode:** Computer-Based Test (CBT)
- **Duration:** 2 hours (120 minutes)
- **Sections:** 3 (VARC, DILR, QA)
- **Total questions:** 66
- **Score used by:** All 21 IIMs + 1,200+ B-schools

CAT scores are valid for one year from the date of result declaration.

---

## Eligibility Criteria

To appear for CAT 2026, candidates must meet these requirements:

- **Educational qualification:** Bachelor's degree with at least **50% marks** (or equivalent CGPA) from a recognised university. For SC, ST, and PwD candidates, the minimum is **45%**.
- **Final year students:** Candidates in their final year of graduation can also apply, provided they complete the degree before the admission deadline.
- **Age limit:** There is **no upper age limit** for CAT.
- **Work experience:** Not mandatory, but it helps during the IIM selection process.
- **Number of attempts:** There is **no limit** on the number of attempts.

> **Note:** Each IIM may have additional eligibility criteria for their specific programmes. Always check individual IIM websites.

## Exam Pattern

CAT 2026 is expected to follow the same pattern as recent years:

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Mode** | Computer-Based Test (CBT) |
| **Duration** | 2 hours (120 minutes) |
| **Sections** | 3 |
| **Total Questions** | 66 |
| **Question Types** | MCQ + Non-MCQ (TITA) |
| **Marking** | +3 for correct, -1 for wrong (MCQ only) |
| **Sectional Time** | 40 minutes per section |

### The Three Sections

1. **Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension (VARC)** — 24 questions
2. **Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning (DILR)** — 20 questions
3. **Quantitative Ability (QA)** — 22 questions

**Important:** You cannot switch between sections. Each section must be completed within its 40-minute time limit.

### Scoring

- **MCQ questions:** +3 for correct answer, -1 for wrong answer
- **Non-MCQ (TITA) questions:** +3 for correct answer, no negative marking
- TITA stands for "Type In The Answer" — you type the answer instead of choosing from options

## Syllabus

### VARC (Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension)

- Reading Comprehension passages (main focus — 3-4 passages)
- Para Jumbles (sentence rearrangement)
- Para Summary
- Odd Sentence Out
- Sentence Completion
- Vocabulary-based questions

### DILR (Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning)

- Tables, Bar Graphs, Pie Charts, Line Graphs
- Data Sufficiency
- Seating Arrangement
- Blood Relations
- Syllogisms
- Puzzles and Games
- Set-based questions (data sets with 4-5 questions each)

### QA (Quantitative Ability)

- Arithmetic (Percentages, Profit & Loss, Time & Work, Ratios)
- Algebra (Equations, Inequalities, Functions)
- Geometry and Mensuration
- Number Systems
- Modern Maths (Permutations, Combinations, Probability)
- Trigonometry (basic)

## How to Apply for CAT 2026

Follow these steps to register for CAT:

1. **Visit the official website:** [iimcat.ac.in](https://iimcat.ac.in)
2. **Register** with a valid email ID and mobile number
3. **Fill the application form** with personal, academic, and work experience details
4. **Upload documents** — photograph, signature, and category certificate (if applicable)
5. **Select test cities** (you can choose up to 6 preferences)
6. **Pay the application fee:**
   - General/OBC: ₹2,400 (approx.)
   - SC/ST/PwD: ₹1,200 (approx.)
7. **Download the admit card** when released (usually 2-3 weeks before the exam)

## Preparation Strategy

### 6-Month Plan (Starting May-June)

**Months 1-2: Build Foundations**
- Learn all basic concepts for QA (arithmetic, algebra, geometry)
- Start reading newspapers and editorials daily for VARC
- Solve basic DILR sets from standard books

**Months 3-4: Practice and Strengthen**
- Solve topic-wise questions from previous year papers
- Take sectional mock tests (1-2 per week)
- Focus on weak areas identified from mocks
- Practice RC passages daily (aim for 2-3 passages)

**Months 5-6: Mock Tests and Revision**
- Take full-length mock tests (2-3 per week)
- Analyse every mock thoroughly — check where you lost time
- Revise formulas, shortcuts, and vocabulary
- Practice time management within 40-minute sections

### Section-Wise Tips

**VARC:**
- Read editorials from The Hindu, Indian Express, or LiveMint daily
- Focus on RC — it carries the most marks
- For para jumbles, look for opening and closing sentences first

**DILR:**
- Practice set-based questions — this section is about cracking the right sets
- Learn to identify easy vs difficult sets quickly
- Time management is critical — skip sets you cannot crack in 10 minutes

**QA:**
- Master arithmetic basics first — these questions are scoring
- Learn shortcuts for calculations (Vedic maths helps)
- Practice previous year CAT questions — patterns repeat

### Recommended Resources

- **Books:** Arun Sharma (QA & DILR), Nishit Sinha, Word Power Made Easy (vocabulary)
- **Online:** IMS, TIME, Career Launcher, Unacademy (mock tests and courses)
- **Free mocks:** IMS SimCAT, CL MockCAT, BYJU's free mocks
- **Previous papers:** Available on iimcat.ac.in after each exam cycle

## IIM Selection Process

Getting a good CAT score is just the first step. IIMs use a multi-stage selection process:

### Stage 1: Shortlisting for WAT/PI

Each IIM has its own shortlisting criteria based on:
- **CAT score** (highest weightage)
- **Class 10 and 12 marks**
- **Graduation marks**
- **Work experience**
- **Diversity factors** (academic, gender, etc.)

### Stage 2: WAT & PI (Written Ability Test & Personal Interview)

- **WAT:** Write an essay on a given topic (15-30 minutes)
- **PI:** Personal interview with a panel of 2-3 professors
- Topics can range from current affairs to your work experience to "why MBA?"

### Stage 3: Final Selection

Final admission offers are based on a composite score:
- CAT percentile (30-50% weightage typically)
- WAT & PI performance (30-40%)
- Academic profile (10-20%)
- Work experience and diversity (5-10%)

> **Each IIM has different weightages.** Check the official admission policy of each IIM you're targeting.

## Important Tips

1. **Start early** — 6 months of focused preparation is ideal. Don't wait for the notification to begin.

2. **Mock tests are non-negotiable** — Take at least 30-40 full-length mocks before the exam. Analysis after each mock is more important than taking the next one.

3. **Don't ignore any section** — IIMs have sectional cutoffs. Even if your overall score is high, low sectional scores can disqualify you.

4. **Accuracy over attempts** — It's better to attempt 40 questions with 85% accuracy than 55 questions with 60% accuracy. Negative marking is real.

5. **Read daily** — For VARC, there is no shortcut. Daily reading of quality content (editorials, magazines, non-fiction) builds comprehension over months.

6. **Health matters** — CAT preparation is a marathon. Sleep well, exercise, and don't burn out in the last month.

7. **Keep documents ready** — Ensure your Class 10, 12, and graduation marksheets are accessible. You'll need them during registration and later for interviews.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### 1. How many times can I appear for CAT?

There is no limit on the number of attempts. You can appear for CAT every year as long as you meet the eligibility criteria.

### 2. Is CAT only for IIMs?

No. Over 1,200 B-schools across India accept CAT scores for admission to their MBA/PGDM programmes, including FMS Delhi, MDI Gurgaon, SPJIMR Mumbai, IITs, and many more.

### 3. Can final year students apply for CAT?

Yes. Students in the final year of their bachelor's degree can apply and appear for CAT. They must complete their degree before the admission deadline set by the respective IIM.

### 4. What is a good CAT score?

A score of **99+ percentile** is needed for the top 3 IIMs (A, B, C). For newer IIMs and other good B-schools, **90-97 percentile** is competitive. Even an **80+ percentile** opens doors to many decent MBA colleges.

### 5. Is coaching necessary for CAT preparation?

Not mandatory. Many toppers have prepared through self-study using books, online resources, and mock tests. However, coaching can provide structure and guidance, especially if you're a first-time aspirant.

### 6. What is the CAT exam fee?

The fee is approximately ₹2,400 for General/OBC candidates and ₹1,200 for SC/ST/PwD candidates. The exact amount is confirmed when registration opens.

### 7. Can I use a calculator in CAT?

No physical calculator is allowed. However, an **on-screen calculator** is provided during the computer-based test.
