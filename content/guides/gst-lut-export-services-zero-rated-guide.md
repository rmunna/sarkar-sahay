# GST LUT for Export of Services India 2026 — Zero-Rated Supply Guide

If you're a **freelancer, IT professional, or company** providing services to clients **outside India** (software development, consulting, design, content writing, etc.), those services are **exports** under GST — and exports are "zero-rated," meaning **no GST is charged**.

But to export without collecting/paying GST, you must file an **LUT (Letter of Undertaking)** on the GST portal each financial year. Without an LUT, you'd have to pay 18% IGST on exports and then claim a refund — far more complex.

## Export of Services Under GST — The Basics

A service qualifies as "export of services" if:
1. Supplier is in India
2. Recipient (client) is **outside India**
3. Place of supply is outside India
4. Payment received in **convertible foreign exchange** (USD, EUR, GBP, SGD, etc.)
5. Supplier and recipient are not establishments of the same company

Most international freelancing/IT work qualifies.

**Tax rate**: **0% (Zero-rated)** — you do NOT charge GST to your foreign client.

## LUT vs IGST Route — Two Options

When exporting services (zero-rated), you have two choices:

| Option | What You Do | Complexity |
|--------|------------|-----------|
| **LUT Route** | File LUT online; export without paying GST; claim ITC refund on inputs | ✅ Simple — recommended |
| **IGST Route** | Pay 18% IGST on exports; claim full refund later | ❌ Complex — cash flow hit, refund delays |

Almost everyone chooses the **LUT route**. The IGST route is rarely used.

## Who Can File LUT?

Any **GST-registered** person who:
- Exports goods or services (or intends to)
- Has NOT been prosecuted for GST evasion of ₹2.5 crore or more in the last 5 years

If you have a GST registration (even as a sole proprietor/freelancer), you can file LUT.

**If you're below GST threshold (₹20L/year, ₹10L for special category states)**:
- You're not required to register for GST
- You don't need to worry about LUT
- You can still invoice foreign clients without GST (since exports are naturally zero-rated even for unregistered persons at the supply level)

## How to File LUT Online — Step by Step

**Where**: gst.gov.in → Services → User Services → Furnish Letter of Undertaking (LUT)

**Steps**:
1. Log in to gst.gov.in with your GST credentials
2. Go to: Services → User Services → Furnish Letter of Undertaking (LUT)
3. Select Financial Year (must file for each FY separately)
4. Fill in the form:
   - Name and address of organization
   - GSTIN
   - Witnesses (two witnesses with name, occupation, address)
   - Self-declaration that you'll export goods/services and comply with rules
5. Submit using EVC (OTP) or DSC
6. Download the LUT acknowledgement (keep for records)

**Cost**: Completely free. No fees.

**When to file**: File before April 1 (start of financial year) or at least before your first export invoice of the year.

## How to Invoice Foreign Clients After LUT

Once LUT is filed, your export invoice for foreign clients:
- Shows "Zero Rated Supply under GST — LUT filed" or "Export of Services — Zero Rated"
- **No GST amount on the invoice** (you don't charge 0% IGST — you simply don't charge it)
- Invoice in USD/EUR/etc. (or INR equivalent at the exchange rate)
- Currency: Any convertible foreign exchange

**Sample invoice note**: "Supply of services to [Client Name, USA]. Zero-rated supply under GST. LUT filed for FY 2025-26. GSTIN: XXXXXXXXXX."

## ITC Refund on Inputs — Export Benefit

As an exporter, you can claim **refund of Input Tax Credit (ITC)** that you accumulated on your business purchases (because your output is zero-rated, you have no GST outflow to offset ITC against).

**Example**:
- You bought a laptop for ₹1,00,000 + 18% GST = ₹18,000 GST paid
- Your services are zero-rated (no output GST)
- ITC of ₹18,000 is stuck in your GSTIN — you can claim it as refund

Refund claim: File on gst.gov.in → Refunds → Application for Refund → Select "Excess ITC" or "Export without IGST" category.

Processing time: 15-30 working days in most cases.

## LUT Renewal — Annual Requirement

LUT must be filed for **each financial year** (April 1 to March 31). If you forget to renew:
- You can file it later in the year and it applies retroactively for the whole year (as long as exports haven't been invoiced improperly)
- Practically: File it before your first export invoice of the new financial year

## What Happens If You Export Without LUT?

If you exported services without LUT and didn't pay IGST either:
- Technically, you must pay IGST on those exports
- You can file IGST and then claim refund
- Or file a belated LUT with application to the jurisdictional GST officer explaining the situation

The GST department generally takes a reasonable view for genuine first-time errors — but don't make it a habit.

## RBI Compliance for Foreign Payments — FIRA

When you receive foreign payments for services:
- Your bank will issue a **FIRA (Foreign Inward Remittance Advice)** — keep this for GST and income tax records
- Export proceeds must be received within 9 months of invoice date (RBI rule)
- Report foreign receipts in your GST returns under "zero-rated supply"

## GST Return Filing for Exporters

Even as a zero-rated exporter, you must file:
- **GSTR-1**: Monthly/quarterly (show exports in Table 6A — "Zero Rated Supply")
- **GSTR-3B**: Monthly (show zero-rated supplies under "Zero Rated and Exports")
- **GSTR-9**: Annual return (if applicable)

Exporters with only zero-rated supplies may qualify for quarterly GSTR-1 and monthly GSTR-3B if turnover < ₹1.5Cr.

## Practically — IT Freelancers and Small Exporters

**Scenario**: You're a freelance developer earning ₹30L/year from a US client (paid via Payoneer/Wise/wire).

- GST registration: Required above ₹20L (₹30L > threshold)
- Export: Services to US client → zero-rated
- LUT: File at gst.gov.in before invoicing
- Invoice: In USD; no GST charged to client
- GSTR-1: Show the export in Table 6A each month/quarter
- GSTR-3B: Show zero-rated supply
- ITC: Claim refund on GST paid on your software subscriptions, laptop, internet, phone, office expenses

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I'm a freelancer earning ₹15L from US clients. Do I need GST registration and LUT?

₹15L is below the ₹20L GST registration threshold for services. You don't need to register for GST. You don't need an LUT. You can invoice your US client without any GST compliance. (Income tax returns are separate — still required.)

### My client pays in USD but I invoice from an Indian entity. Is it still zero-rated?

Yes — the key conditions for export of services (supplier in India, recipient outside India, payment in foreign exchange) are all met. It's zero-rated regardless of whether you invoice from a sole proprietorship, partnership, or company.

### I filed LUT for FY 2025-26. Do I need to file again for FY 2026-27?

Yes — LUT must be renewed each financial year. File for FY 2026-27 before April 1, 2026 (or before your first export invoice after April 1).

### I received USD from a foreign client but it was deposited in INR to my account (bank converted). Is it still valid export?

If you receive foreign currency which the bank converts to INR at the time of receipt, it's still valid as the original payment was in foreign exchange. Get a FIRA from your bank confirming foreign inward remittance — this is your proof.
