# Invest for Child's Education India 2026 — How Much to Save and Where

Education costs in India are growing faster than inflation. Starting when your child is born (or young) gives compound interest time to do the heavy lifting. Here's what you actually need and how to get there.

---

## How Much Will Education Cost? (The Honest Numbers)

Education inflation in India runs at **10–12% per year** for private colleges — double the general inflation rate.

| Course | Cost Today (2026) | Cost in 15 Years (at 10% inflation) |
|--------|------------------|------------------------------------|
| Engineering (private) | ₹8–15L total | **₹33–62L** |
| Medicine (MBBS private) | ₹60–80L total | **₹2.5–3.3 crore** |
| MBA (IIM-tier) | ₹25–35L | **₹1–1.45 crore** |
| CA + articleship | ₹3–5L | **₹12–20L** |
| B.Sc / BA (private) | ₹4–8L | **₹16–33L** |
| Abroad (US/UK MS) | ₹40–80L | **₹1.6–3.3 crore** |

**Bottom line**: Plan for ₹25–50L for a good private engineering degree, ₹1 crore+ for medicine or IIM-level MBA.

## Best Investment Options for Child Education

### Option 1: Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) — For Girl Child Only

**Who**: Girl children below 10 years
**Interest rate**: 8.2% per annum (guaranteed, government-backed)
**Tax**: Completely tax-free — EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt). Investment qualifies for 80C. Interest tax-free. Maturity tax-free.
**Lock-in**: Until girl turns 21 (partial withdrawal allowed at 18 for education: up to 50% of balance)
**Maximum**: ₹1.5L/year
**Return at maturity**: ~₹37.3L if ₹1.5L/year for 15 years at 8.2%

**Best feature**: Government-backed, completely tax-free. Better than PPF (7.1%) for girl children.

**Where to open**: Any Post Office, SBI, or major bank. Minimum ₹250/year. Account in girl's name with parent/guardian. [See our SSY guide](/guide/sukanya-samriddhi-account-open)

### Option 2: Equity Mutual Fund SIP (ELSS or Index Fund)

**Expected return**: 12–15% CAGR over 15 years
**Tax**: LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25L exemption
**Liquidity**: Can redeem anytime (index funds) or after 3-year lock-in (ELSS)
**80C benefit**: Only ELSS qualifies; index funds don't

**For education corpus: Use a large-cap or flexi-cap index fund SIP**. Switch to debt fund 2–3 years before you need the money (to protect corpus from market volatility).

**Example**: ₹5,000/month SIP at 10% step-up for 15 years → ₹52.6L pre-tax. After LTCG on gains: approximately ₹46–48L in hand.

### Option 3: PPF (Public Provident Fund)

**Interest rate**: 7.1% (currently)
**Tax**: Completely tax-free (EEE)
**Lock-in**: 15 years (partial withdrawal from year 7)
**Maximum**: ₹1.5L/year
**80C benefit**: Yes

**Better than FD**, but lower return than equity. Use PPF for the safe/guaranteed portion of the education corpus.

**Open PPF in child's name**: You can open a PPF account in your minor child's name. The 80C deduction is in your (parent's) name. You can contribute to both your PPF and child's PPF, but combined limit is ₹1.5L/year for 80C.

### Option 4: National Savings Certificate / 5-Year Bank FD

**Interest rate**: 7.7% (NSC), 6.5–7.5% (bank FD)
**Tax**: Taxable at slab rate (TDS on FD)
**Lock-in**: 5 years (renew for 10 years for education goal)

Less efficient due to tax. Only use if you're in 0–5% tax bracket.

## Investing in Minor Child's Name — Tax Rules

**Clubbing of income**: If you invest in your child's name, income earned is **clubbed with your income** and taxed in your hands. This applies until the child turns 18.

**Exceptions**:
- Income earned by minor from their own skill, talent, or activity (not investment income)
- Income of a minor suffering from disability under Section 80U

**Exemption**: ₹1,500 per minor child's income is exempt under Section 10(32) — but this is tiny.

**Practical implication**: Don't put money in child's name in an FD or savings account to avoid tax — the interest is clubbed with your income anyway. Use SSY (where clubbing doesn't affect you because returns are tax-free) or start an MF folio in your own name tagged to child's education goal.

**After child turns 18**: Income is in their name. Good time to transfer equity MF to child's demat (ITR filing in child's name begins).

## Minor Demat Account and MF Folio

### Can a Minor Have a Demat Account?

Yes — a minor can have a demat account and mutual fund folio with a parent/guardian as account operator.

**How**: Open as guardian account at any broker (Zerodha, HDFC Securities, etc.). Child's PAN required (apply PAN for minor; free, and easy via NSDL online). Guardian operates until child turns 18, after which account must be converted to major (new KYC required).

### Should You Open MF Folio in Child's Name?

For equity MF: Income is clubbed with parent's income until 18. But at redeeming for education (when child is 17-18), the LTCG applies at parent's rate. Consider keeping the MF in your own name and earmarking it mentally for education — simpler and same tax treatment.

## Recommended Strategy by Age of Child

### Child Age 0–5 (15+ years to education)

- **SSY** (if girl): Max ₹1.5L/year. This alone can give ₹37L+ tax-free
- **Equity index fund SIP**: ₹5,000–₹10,000/month with 10% annual step-up. 15 years gives strong corpus
- **ELSS SIP**: If you want 80C deduction; 3-year lock-in per installment

**Aggressive equity (80–90%)** — you have time to ride out volatility

### Child Age 5–10 (8–13 years to education)

- Continue SIPs; increase via step-up
- Add PPF contribution for guaranteed component (open child's PPF account)
- Review corpus progress annually

**60–70% equity, 30–40% debt**

### Child Age 13–15 (3–5 years to education)

- Start shifting equity to debt gradually (1–2 years before needed)
- Don't make large fresh equity investments — market timing risk increases
- FD laddering for the corpus you'll need in 3–5 years

**40–50% equity, 50–60% debt**

### Child Age 16–18 (1–2 years to education)

- Majority in liquid/ultra-short duration debt fund
- Keep 3–6 months' fees in a high-interest savings account
- Equity SIP can continue for any surplus beyond the education corpus

## Child LIC Policy vs Mutual Fund — the Honest Comparison

Many insurance agents sell child endowment / money-back / ULIP policies for education. Here's the comparison:

| Factor | Child LIC Endowment | Equity MF SIP |
|--------|---------------------|---------------|
| Return | 4–5.5% (money-back, endowment) | 12–15% (historical CAGR) |
| Lock-in | 15–20 years | None (ELSS: 3 years) |
| Surrender penalty | Heavy (25–35%) | Zero (index funds) |
| Flexibility | Low | High |
| Insurance | Yes (pure term is better) | No (buy separate term plan) |

**The verdict**: Child LIC endowment policies have poor returns and high lock-in. Buy a term insurance separately for the parent (cheap) and invest the rest in equity MF + SSY. You'll end up with 2–3x more corpus.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much should I save for my 2-year-old's engineering education?

Engineering at a private college in India will cost approximately ₹30–60L in 16 years (at 10% education inflation). To accumulate ₹40L in 16 years at 12% CAGR: SIP of about ₹5,500/month with no step-up, or ₹3,000/month with 10% annual step-up. Start SSY (if girl child) for ₹1.5L/year alongside.

### Can I withdraw from SSY for education before maturity?

Yes — up to 50% of SSY balance can be withdrawn after the girl turns 18 (for higher education). Full maturity is at 21 years. For a 18-year education goal, you can use the 50% partial withdrawal for college fees.

### Should I open PPF in child's name or my name?

Either works — but combined PPF contribution (yours + child's) is limited to ₹1.5L/year for 80C. If you already max out your PPF, open child's PPF for the force-saving aspect (even if 80C limit is reached, the 7.1% tax-free return is still good).

### My child is 12. Is it too late to start investing for education?

Not at all — you have 6 years. ₹10,000/month SIP for 6 years at 12% CAGR = ₹9.9L. Step up 10% annually: ~₹12.5L. Add FD and liquid fund: you can accumulate ₹15–20L. May not cover private engineering fees fully, but significantly reduces the loan burden.
