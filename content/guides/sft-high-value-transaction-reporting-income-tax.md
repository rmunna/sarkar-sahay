# SFT: High-Value Transactions the Income Tax Department Already Knows About

Ever wonder how the Income Tax Department sends notices for undeclared income even when you never told them about it? The answer is **SFT — Statement of Financial Transactions** under **Section 285BA** of the Income Tax Act.

Banks, mutual fund houses, registrars, and other institutions are legally required to report high-value transactions to the IT Department every year. This data appears in your **AIS (Annual Information Statement)** — visible when you log in to incometax.gov.in.

## What Is SFT?

SFT is a mandatory annual report submitted by specified entities to the Income Tax Department. Every entity that processes high-value transactions must file Form 61A by May 31 after the financial year end.

These reports are aggregated into your AIS, making your financial footprint visible to the department even without a tax audit.

## SFT-Covered Transactions — Complete List

### Banks and Post Offices

| Transaction | Threshold | Reported By |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Cash deposit in savings account | > ₹10 lakh in a year (aggregate across accounts) | Bank |
| Cash deposit in current/FD/RD | > ₹10 lakh in a year | Bank |
| Cash withdrawal from account | > ₹50 lakh in a year | Bank |
| Cash purchase of bank drafts/pay orders | > ₹10 lakh in a year | Bank |
| Cash purchase of pre-paid instruments (forex cards, gift cards) | > ₹10 lakh | Bank |

### Credit Cards

| Transaction | Threshold | Reported By |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Credit card bill payment via cash | > ₹1 lakh in a single payment | Bank |
| Credit card bill payment overall (any mode) | > ₹10 lakh in a year | Bank |

### Shares and Mutual Funds

| Transaction | Threshold | Reported By |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Purchase of shares (listed and unlisted) | > ₹10 lakh in a year from a single person | Company/Broker |
| Purchase of mutual fund units | > ₹10 lakh in a year | MF house / RTA |
| Purchase of bonds / debentures | > ₹10 lakh in a year | Issuing entity |
| Buyback of shares | > ₹10 lakh in a year | Company |
| Purchase of RBI bonds | > ₹10 lakh | Banks |

### Property

| Transaction | Threshold | Reported By |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Purchase or sale of immovable property | > ₹30 lakh | Sub-registrar |
| Receipt of cash for sale of property | Any amount above ₹30L | Sub-registrar |

### Foreign Transactions

| Transaction | Threshold | Reported By |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Foreign currency purchases (cash) | > ₹10 lakh | Authorized dealers |
| Remittances sent abroad (LRS) | > ₹10 lakh | Banks |

## How This Data Reaches You: AIS

Every SFT filed about you appears in your **AIS (Annual Information Statement)** at incometax.gov.in → e-File → Income Tax Returns → View AIS.

Your AIS shows:
- **SFT Information**: All high-value transactions reported about you
- **TDS/TCS**: What was deducted/collected on your income
- **Interest income**: FD interest, savings account interest
- **Capital gains**: From broker/registrar reports
- **Foreign remittances**

**The IT Department AI compares your AIS entries against your filed ITR**. If large transactions appear in AIS but your declared income doesn't support them, you get a notice.

## What Happens After SFT Is Reported

### Step 1: You receive a compliance notice

The IT Department issues notices under different sections:
- **Section 133(6)**: Non-intrusive inquiry — "explain these transactions"
- **Section 143(2)**: Scrutiny assessment
- **Section 148**: Reassessment if income escaped
- Or simply an **Annual Information Statement email** asking you to review and confirm/deny

### Step 2: You respond via e-Proceedings

Log in to incometax.gov.in → Pending Actions → e-Proceedings. For each SFT transaction you can:
- **Confirm**: Yes, this was me, here's the source
- **Deny/Dispute**: This transaction isn't mine (then rectification process begins)
- **Partially confirm**: Some entries are mine, others aren't

### Step 3: Provide source of funds

If you deposited ₹15 lakh cash, the department wants to know where it came from:
- Agricultural income (attach Form J / agricultural land documents)
- Sale of property (attach sale deed)
- Gift from relative (gift deed + bank transfer proof)
- Prior savings / withdrawal from another account
- Business income already declared in ITR

## Common SFT Notices and How to Handle Them

### "₹X cash deposited in your bank account — explain source"

**What to do**:
1. Identify the date and source of each deposit
2. Gather supporting documents (withdrawal slips from another account, sale proceeds, gift documentation)
3. Respond via e-Proceedings within the time given (usually 30 days)

Agricultural income from small farmers is a common genuine source — attach mandi receipts, Kisan Credit Card records, or agricultural income declarations from state government.

### "High-value credit card payment — explain income source"

**What to do**:
1. Your ITR income should be consistent with high credit card spending
2. If it's from savings/investments sold, show those records
3. If income was genuinely declared but seems mismatched, explain with documentation

### "Property purchase worth ₹X not consistent with declared income"

**What to do**:
1. Source of funds: home loan sanction letter + bank transfer
2. Down payment source: salary savings, FD redemption, gift from parents
3. IT Dept is checking if undeclared cash was used — if it was loan+legitimate savings, documentation is straightforward

## Pro Tips to Avoid SFT Notices

1. **Declare all income in ITR** — if you have agricultural income, declare it (even if tax-free). SFT entries showing cash deposits inconsistent with zero-income ITR trigger notices.

2. **Don't accumulate cash** — breaking large amounts into smaller sub-₹10L deposits doesn't help. Banks aggregate deposits within a year and still report.

3. **Keep investment trails** — if you invest ₹12 lakh in mutual funds, ensure this matches declared income or show existing savings as source.

4. **Review your AIS every year before filing ITR** — incometax.gov.in → AIS lets you see what the department already knows before you file. File consistently with AIS data.

5. **Dispute wrong AIS entries** — if a transaction in your AIS doesn't belong to you (misquoted PAN, bank error), dispute it in AIS online before filing ITR. Filing consistently with disputed entries protects you.

## SFT Threshold Is Aggregate, Not Per Transaction

**Common misconception**: "I deposited ₹9 lakh three times — no single deposit crossed ₹10L so no SFT."

**Reality**: Banks aggregate ALL deposits in a financial year. ₹9L + ₹9L + ₹9L = ₹27L aggregate — reported as SFT with ₹27L total deposit.

The threshold is an annual aggregate per bank, not per transaction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I received ₹11 lakh as gift from my parents and deposited it. Will I get a notice?

Gifts from parents are tax-exempt. But the deposit will appear in AIS as an SFT entry. If you get a notice, respond with gift documentation: parent's bank statement showing transfer + gift deed or declaration letter. The notice doesn't mean you've done anything wrong — it's a verification request.

### My FD matured for ₹12 lakh. I deposited it in savings. Will IT Dept count this twice?

Yes — both the original FD (possibly an old SFT entry) and the new savings deposit appear. When responding to a notice, show the paper trail: FD maturity receipt → savings deposit. Same money, different account. This is a simple explanation.

### My business has large cash transactions regularly. How do I protect myself?

Maintain a proper cash book and day-to-day records of all receipts and payments. Every cash deposit should map to a business transaction in your books. File ITR with business income consistent with cash flows. Businesses with regular large cash transactions should consider a CA for compliance.

### I didn't file ITR because my income was below taxable limit. But I had a large FD. Now what?

If your FD interest + other income was genuinely below the taxable limit, file a belated ITR now (if within December 31 of the assessment year) or request condonation. Show that income was below taxable limit. Having a large FD isn't itself a problem — earning interest above the exemption limit without paying tax is.
