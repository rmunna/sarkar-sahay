# Property Registration Document Rejected: How to Fix

Property registration in India involves submitting documents to the Sub-Registrar's office for legal transfer of ownership. Document rejection at this stage can delay your property purchase by weeks or months, create legal complications, and cause financial stress. This guide covers all common rejection reasons and how to resolve them.

## Common Rejection Reasons

When the Sub-Registrar rejects your property registration documents, the reasons typically include:

- **"Insufficient stamp duty paid"** — stamp duty amount is less than required
- **"Document not properly executed"** — missing signatures, witnesses, or notarization
- **"Encumbrance on property"** — existing loans, liens, or legal disputes on the property
- **"Title defect"** — seller doesn't have clear ownership or chain of title is broken
- **"Mismatch in property details"** — survey number, plot details, or area doesn't match records
- **"Identity documents insufficient"** — missing or expired ID/address proof of parties
- **"Photographs not affixed"** — passport photos of buyer/seller not attached
- **"Power of Attorney defective"** — if transaction is through POA, the POA is incomplete or unregistered
- **"Agricultural land restriction"** — non-agriculturist attempting to buy agricultural land
- **"Municipal NOC/conversion order missing"** — required clearances not obtained

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### Step 1: Understand the Exact Rejection Reason

1. Collect the **rejection memo/order** from the Sub-Registrar's office
2. Read the specific objections mentioned
3. Note the **deadline** for resubmission (if mentioned)
4. Some states provide rejection details online — check your state's registration portal

### Step 2: Fix Stamp Duty Issues

Stamp duty problems are the most common cause of rejection:

**If stamp duty is insufficient:**
1. Calculate the correct stamp duty using your state's **stamp duty calculator** (available on state registration websites)
2. Stamp duty is based on the **higher of**: the actual sale consideration OR the government guideline/circle rate
3. Pay the **deficit amount** — you'll need to purchase additional stamp paper or pay e-stamp
4. Some states charge a **penalty** for deficit stamp duty (usually 2% per month)
5. Resubmit the document with the additional stamp duty receipt

**Common stamp duty mistakes:**
- Using old circle rates instead of current rates
- Not including GST/other charges in the consideration
- Applying wrong category (residential vs. commercial vs. agricultural)
- Forgetting women's concession or first-time buyer discounts in applicable states

### Step 3: Fix Document Execution Errors

If the sale deed or registration document has execution issues:

1. **Missing signatures:** Get all parties (buyer, seller, witnesses) to sign on every page and at designated places
2. **Witness requirements:** Most states require **2 witnesses** with valid ID proof. Witnesses must be present at the Sub-Registrar's office
3. **Photos:** Affix recent **passport-size photographs** of buyer and seller on the document (usually 2 each)
4. **Thumb impressions:** If any party is illiterate, thumb impression with identification by a known person is required
5. **Notarization:** While not always required, some Sub-Registrars require documents to be notarized before registration

### Step 4: Clear Encumbrance Issues

If the property has encumbrances (loans, liens, legal disputes):

1. Obtain an **Encumbrance Certificate (EC)** from the Sub-Registrar's office for the last 13–30 years (varies by state)
2. If the property has an **existing loan:**
   - The seller must obtain a **No Objection Certificate (NOC)** from the lender
   - Or the loan must be fully repaid and the **mortgage release deed** registered
3. If there's a **legal dispute:**
   - The dispute must be resolved in court before registration can proceed
   - Alternatively, obtain a court order permitting the sale
4. If there's a **revenue lien:**
   - Clear pending property taxes and obtain a **tax clearance certificate** from the municipal corporation

### Step 5: Resolve Title Defects

Title issues require careful attention:

1. Get a **title search report** from a property lawyer — this traces the chain of ownership
2. Common title defects:
   - **Broken chain of title** — missing link in ownership transfers (get the missing transfer deed registered)
   - **Undivided property** — property not partitioned among co-owners (get a partition deed first)
   - **Unauthorized seller** — seller doesn't have legal right to sell (verify ownership documents)
   - **Pending succession** — previous owner deceased without succession certificate/will probate
3. Consult a **property lawyer** for complex title issues — don't attempt to fix these without legal guidance

### Step 6: Correct Property Detail Mismatches

If property details don't match revenue or municipal records:

1. **Survey number errors:** Get a certified copy of the latest land records from the Tahsildar's office
2. **Area mismatch:** Commission a **fresh survey** from the District Survey Office
3. **Plot details:** Obtain the **approved layout plan** from the planning authority
4. **Address discrepancy:** Get a certificate from the municipal corporation confirming the correct address/property identification
5. Update the sale deed with correct details and resubmit

### Step 7: Obtain Required NOCs and Clearances

Depending on your state and property type:

- **Agricultural land:** Obtain **permission under Section 63** (varies by state) or land conversion order
- **Municipal NOC:** Required in many states for properties within municipal limits
- **Society NOC:** For flats in cooperative housing societies
- **Builder NOC:** For resale of under-construction or newly completed flats
- **Income Tax clearance:** Required in some cases for properties above certain value
- **Family consent:** Some states require consent of family members for ancestral property

## When to Contact Authorities

Contact if:
- The Sub-Registrar's objections seem unreasonable or arbitrary
- Your resubmission deadline is approaching and you can't resolve the issues
- The Sub-Registrar demands unofficial payments
- You need clarification on specific objections

**Contact Options:**
- **District Registrar (DR):** The Sub-Registrar's superior — you can appeal rejection to the DR
- **Inspector General of Registration (IGR):** State-level authority for registration matters
- **State registration portal:** Most states have grievance mechanisms on their registration websites
- **Revenue court:** For property record disputes
- **Consumer forum:** If a builder is causing registration issues
- **Anti-corruption helpline:** If unofficial payments are demanded

## Prevention Tips

1. **Hire a property lawyer** to review all documents before registration — the legal fee is small compared to rejection costs
2. **Get a title search done** before finalizing the property deal — at least 13 years of title history
3. **Check encumbrance certificate** before paying any advance to the seller
4. **Calculate stamp duty correctly** using the official state calculator — don't rely on broker estimates
5. **Ensure all NOCs are ready** before your registration appointment date
6. **Verify property details** against revenue records — survey number, area, boundaries
7. **Keep original documents** of all previous transactions in the chain of title
8. **Visit the Sub-Registrar's office** before the registration date to confirm document requirements
9. **Carry extra copies** of all documents and photographs on registration day
10. **Avoid buying disputed properties** — always check court records for any pending litigation

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a Sub-Registrar refuse to register a document?

Yes. Under the Registration Act, 1908, the Sub-Registrar can refuse registration if the document doesn't meet legal requirements — insufficient stamp duty, missing signatures, identity not established, or property details not matching records. The refusal must be in writing with reasons.

### What is the appeal process if my registration is rejected?

You can appeal to the **District Registrar** within 30 days of rejection (timeline varies by state). If the District Registrar also rejects, you can approach the **civil court**. Consult a property lawyer for appeals.

### How long does property registration take after resubmission?

Resubmission processing typically takes **7–15 working days** depending on the state and complexity of the correction. Some states offer same-day registration if all documents are in order.

### What is an Encumbrance Certificate and why is it important?

An Encumbrance Certificate (EC) is a document that shows all registered transactions on a property for a specified period. It reveals existing loans, mortgages, liens, or other claims. An EC with no encumbrances means the property has a clear title for the period checked.

### Can I register property without physical presence at the Sub-Registrar?

In most states, physical presence of buyer and seller (or their authorized Power of Attorney holders) is mandatory at the Sub-Registrar's office during registration. Some states have started **video conferencing-based registration** for NRIs and in special circumstances.

### What happens if I don't register my property?

Unregistered property documents have no legal validity for transfer of ownership under the Registration Act. You won't get legal title to the property, can't get a bank loan against it, and can't resell it legally. Always ensure registration is completed.
