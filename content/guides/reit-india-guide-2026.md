# REIT India 2026 — Complete Investment Guide

REITs let you invest in commercial real estate (office parks, malls, warehouses) with as little as ₹200–500 and earn regular rental income as dividends. You don't need crores to own a piece of Embassy Tech Park or Nexus Malls — you buy listed REIT units on NSE/BSE.

---

## What is a REIT?

**REIT = Real Estate Investment Trust**

A REIT owns income-generating real estate properties (offices, malls, warehouses) and:
1. Collects rental income from tenants
2. Must distribute minimum 90% of net distributable cash flow as dividends
3. Trades on the stock exchange like a share

**Analogy**: A REIT is like a mutual fund that owns physical real estate. You own units, the fund owns buildings, you get proportional rental income as dividends.

## REIT Distributions — How You Receive Income

REITs distribute quarterly (every 3 months) or semi-annually. The distribution has 3 components:

| Component | Tax Treatment |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Dividend** | Taxable at your slab rate (added to income) |
| **Interest income passed through** | Taxable at your slab rate |
| **Return of capital (amortization)** | Tax-free but reduces your cost basis |

**Typical split**: 50–70% of REIT distribution is return of capital (tax-free); 30–50% is taxable income.

**For a 30% tax bracket investor**: After-tax yield is lower than headline yield. A 6% headline yield may be ~4–4.5% after tax, depending on the split.

## Who Should Invest in REITs?

**REITs are suitable for**:
- Investors who want regular income (like dividend investors) from commercial real estate
- Those who want real estate exposure without dealing with tenants, loans, registration
- Retirees or near-retirees seeking 5–7% yield with some inflation protection
- Diversification beyond stocks and bonds

**REITs may not be ideal for**:
- Long-term wealth building with maximum growth (equity mutual funds do better over 15+ years)
- Pure capital appreciation plays (stocks beat REITs for growth)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I invest in REITs via SIP?

Not directly (REITs aren't mutual funds). But you can buy REIT units regularly every month — effectively creating your own SIP. Many brokers allow recurring orders.

### REIT vs NCD (Non-Convertible Debenture) — which gives better yield?

NCDs give 9–11% fixed interest, fully taxable. REITs give 5–7% yield (partly tax-free). At 30% tax bracket: NCD after-tax ~6.3–7.7%; REIT after-tax ~4–5%. NCDs beat REITs on pure yield. But REITs have capital appreciation potential and inflation-linked rent increases — NCDs don't.

### Are REIT dividends guaranteed?

No — distributions depend on rental income collected. If tenants vacate or default, distributions can reduce. However, Grade A office REITs (Embassy, Mindspace) have mostly blue-chip tenants (Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, Microsoft, Infosys) with long leases.
