# How to Revise Your ITR (Section 139(5)) — Fix Mistakes Before December 31

If you noticed a mistake in your filed ITR — wrong income figure, a deduction you forgot to claim, wrong bank account, incorrect schedule — you can fix it by filing a **Revised Return** under Section 139(5). You can revise an ITR multiple times as long as it's within the deadline.

> **Good news**: There is NO penalty for revising an ITR. You can revise as many times as needed before the deadline.

## Deadline to Revise ITR

| Original Filing | Deadline to Revise |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Filed before July 31 (on time) | December 31 of the Assessment Year |
| Filed as belated return (after July 31) | December 31 of the Assessment Year (same deadline) |

**Example**: For FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27), the revision deadline is **December 31, 2026**.

> Even a belated ITR can be revised — the revision deadline is the same December 31 regardless of when you originally filed.

## What You Can Fix in a Revised Return

| Item | Can Be Revised? |
|------|----------------|
| Income figures (salary, capital gains, etc.) | ✅ Yes |
| Missed deductions (80C, 80D, HRA) | ✅ Yes |
| Wrong ITR form | ✅ Yes (need to file correct form) |
| Wrong bank account for refund | ✅ Yes |
| Wrong TDS credit figures | ✅ Yes |
| Missing income sources | ✅ Yes |
| Wrong address or contact details | ✅ Yes |
| E-verification issue | ✅ Yes (file new revision + re-verify) |
| Adding income after getting AIS mismatch | ✅ Yes |

## Step-by-Step: How to File Revised ITR Online

### Step 1: Login to Income Tax Portal

1. Go to **[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/)**
2. Login with PAN + password

### Step 2: Start a New ITR for the Same Assessment Year

1. Click **"e-File"** → **"Income Tax Returns"** → **"File Income Tax Return"**
2. Select the **same Assessment Year** as the original (e.g., AY 2026-27 for FY 2025-26)
3. Select your ITR form (same as before, or correct form if you were using the wrong one)

### Step 3: Select "Revised Return" Filing Type

The portal will ask the filing type:
- Original Return
- **Revised Return** ← Select this
- Belated Return
- Revised Belated Return

When you select "Revised Return," the portal asks for:
- **Original return's acknowledgement number** (15-digit number from your original ITR-V)
- **Date of filing** the original return

> Your original acknowledgement number is in the ITR-V PDF you received by email, or visible at incometax.gov.in → "View Filed Returns."

### Step 4: Make Your Corrections

The revised return starts with a pre-fill from your original data (in many cases). Make the specific corrections:
- Add/remove income
- Change deduction amounts
- Update bank account
- Correct TDS figures

### Step 5: Pay Additional Tax (If Applicable)

If your revision increases your tax liability (e.g., you forgot to include some income):
1. Calculate the additional tax + interest (Section 234A if applicable)
2. Pay via E-Pay Tax → Self-Assessment Tax (300)
3. Add the challan details in the ITR before submitting

### Step 6: Submit and E-Verify

Submit the revised ITR and e-verify within **30 days**:
- Aadhaar OTP (fastest)
- Net banking
- Bank EVC

The revised ITR replaces your original — the original is superseded.

## Common Scenarios When to Revise

### You Got a 143(1) Demand Notice

If you disagree with a 143(1) demand because you missed a deduction or income was wrongly computed:
1. First: File Rectification (for errors in records — wrong TDS shown)
2. Second: File Revised ITR (if you need to change the income/deductions you declared)

Both tools exist — use the right one:
- **Rectification**: For tax computation errors, TDS credit mismatches
- **Revised ITR**: For changes to income, deductions, schedules

### You Got an AIS Mismatch Notice

AIS shows income you didn't declare in your ITR:
1. Check if the income is valid (not a duplicate or non-taxable item)
2. If valid income was missed: File Revised ITR including the correct income
3. Pay additional tax + 234A interest on the new amount

### You Forgot to Claim 80C Deduction

If you filed and forgot ₹1.5L PPF investment:
1. File Revised ITR adding the 80C deduction
2. Your tax liability decreases → If tax paid > now-lower liability, you get a refund

### Wrong Bank Account Entered

Your refund is going to a wrong account:
1. File Revised ITR correcting the bank account details
2. Make sure the correct account is pre-validated in your IT portal profile first

## How Many Times Can You Revise?

There is **no limit** on how many times you can revise. Each revision replaces the previous one. The December 31 deadline applies to each revision — you can do 10 revisions all before December 31.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I filed my ITR but realized I used ITR-1 when I should have used ITR-2 (I have capital gains). Can I fix this?

Yes — file a Revised Return using the correct ITR-2 form. The original ITR-1 is superseded. This must be done before December 31.

### I filed a revised ITR but it went through without me noticing one more error. Can I revise again?

Yes — file another Revised ITR. Enter the acknowledgement number of the most recent revised return (not the original). The latest revision is always the operative one.

### My original ITR gave me a refund. I now realized I missed income. If I revise, will I owe tax?

Yes — if your revised return shows higher income, your tax liability increases. You may owe the difference (plus interest from the original due date). Pay the additional tax via self-assessment tax and mention the challan in the revised ITR.

### I filed the original after July 31 (belated return). Can I still revise?

Yes — a belated return can be revised under the same December 31 deadline. The fact that you filed late doesn't affect your ability to revise.
