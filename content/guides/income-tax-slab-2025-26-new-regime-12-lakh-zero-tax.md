# Income Tax Slab 2025-26 — ₹12 Lakh Zero Tax, New Regime Calculator

Budget 2025 (presented February 1, 2025) delivered the biggest income tax cut in years. Under the new tax regime, income up to ₹12 lakh is now effectively zero-tax. Here's how the slabs work and whether you should switch from old regime.

---

## New Tax Regime Slabs — AY 2026-27 (FY 2025-26)

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|------------|---------|
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | Nil |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

**Key feature**: Section 87A rebate of ₹60,000 wipes out all tax for income up to ₹12 lakh (tax liability before rebate = ₹60,000; rebate = ₹60,000; tax payable = **₹0**).

**For salaried employees**: Add ₹75,000 standard deduction → **income up to ₹12.75 lakh CTC = zero tax** under new regime.

---

## Why ₹12 Lakh Is Zero Tax — The Math

For someone with ₹12 lakh income (new regime, AY 2026-27):

| Slab | Calculation | Tax |
|------|-------------|-----|
| ₹0–₹4L | Nil | ₹0 |
| ₹4L–₹8L | 5% of ₹4L | ₹20,000 |
| ₹8L–₹12L | 10% of ₹4L | ₹40,000 |
| **Total before rebate** | | **₹60,000** |
| Section 87A rebate | -(₹60,000) | -(₹60,000) |
| **Tax payable** | | **₹0** |
| Add: 4% health + education cess | 4% of ₹0 | ₹0 |
| **Final tax** | | **₹0** |

**Note**: The ₹12 lakh zero-tax is a cliff. At ₹12,00,001 income, you pay tax on the full amount above ₹12L (₹15% on ₹1). The rebate is an all-or-nothing benefit — you either get it (income ≤₹12L) or you don't.

## Tax for Salaried Employees (With Standard Deduction)

Salaried employees get ₹75,000 standard deduction in the new regime (raised from ₹50,000 in Budget 2025):

| Gross Salary | Standard Deduction | Taxable Income | Tax (New Regime) |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|------------------|
| ₹12,00,000 | ₹75,000 | ₹11,25,000 | **₹0** (87A rebate) |
| ₹12,75,000 | ₹75,000 | ₹12,00,000 | **₹0** (87A rebate) |
| ₹13,00,000 | ₹75,000 | ₹12,25,000 | ₹3,750 |
| ₹15,00,000 | ₹75,000 | ₹14,25,000 | ₹33,750 + cess |
| ₹20,00,000 | ₹75,000 | ₹19,25,000 | ₹1,06,250 + cess |
| ₹25,00,000 | ₹75,000 | ₹24,25,000 | ₹1,95,000 + cess |
| ₹30,00,000 | ₹75,000 | ₹29,25,000 | ₹3,37,500 + cess |

*These are approximate. Add 4% cess on tax amount for final liability.*

## Old Tax Regime Slabs — AY 2026-27 (Unchanged)

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|------------|---------|
| Up to ₹2,50,000 | Nil |
| ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹10,00,000 | 30% |

**Old regime 87A rebate**: Tax on income up to ₹5 lakh is rebated (max rebate ₹12,500). So income ≤₹5L = zero tax in old regime.

**Old regime preserves**: 80C (₹1.5L), HRA, LTA, 80D, home loan interest (Section 24), NPS extra deduction (80CCD 1B), etc.

## Old vs New Regime — Which Is Better for You?

The answer depends on your total deductions:

### Quick Rule of Thumb

**Choose New Regime if**: Your total deductions (80C + HRA + 80D + home loan + NPS etc.) are less than the "break-even deduction" for your income.

**Choose Old Regime if**: Your deductions are higher than the break-even.

### Break-Even Deduction (Approximate) by Income Level

| Income | Break-Even Deduction | Common Scenario |
|--------|---------------------|-----------------|
| ₹10L | ~₹1.25L | New regime usually better |
| ₹12L | ~₹1.75L | New regime better for most |
| ₹15L | ~₹2.25L | Old regime better if EPF+80C+HRA > ₹2.25L |
| ₹20L | ~₹3.75L | Old if home loan + 80C + HRA = ₹4L+ |
| ₹30L | ~₹5.5L | Old regime better if deductions are high |

**Detailed calculation**: You need to do the actual math for your specific situation. A tax calculator (ClearTax, TaxBuddy) does this in 5 minutes.

### When Old Regime Is BETTER

You likely save more in old regime if you have:
- **Home loan interest**: Section 24 deduction of ₹2L/year (self-occupied)
- **High HRA**: Especially if rent is ₹25,000+/month in metro
- **Full 80C**: EPF + ELSS + PPF = ₹1.5L
- **80D**: Health insurance for family = ₹25–₹50K
- **NPS**: Additional ₹50K deduction under 80CCD(1B)

Total = ₹2L (home loan) + ₹1.5L (80C) + ₹50K (80D) + ₹50K (NPS) = **₹4.5L deductions**

At ₹15L income: Old regime tax = ₹15L – ₹4.5L – ₹50K standard deduction = ₹10L taxable → ~₹1.17L tax
New regime tax at ₹14.25L taxable (after standard deduction) → ~₹1.79L tax

Old regime saves ~₹62,000 in this scenario.

### When New Regime Is BETTER

- Renting in tier-2 city with low rent (HRA exemption is small)
- No home loan
- Invests in instruments not under 80C (stocks, international funds)
- Employer doesn't offer NPS
- Income ≤ ₹12.75L (salary) — zero tax anyway

## Surcharge on High Income

| Income | Surcharge Rate |
|--------|---------------|
| ₹50L – ₹1 Cr | 10% |
| ₹1 Cr – ₹2 Cr | 15% |
| ₹2 Cr – ₹5 Cr | 25% |
| Above ₹5 Cr | 37% (old regime) / 25% (new regime — capped) |

New regime caps surcharge at 25% for all income (better for very high earners). Old regime surcharge can hit 37% above ₹5 Cr.

**With surcharge + cess**: Effective marginal tax rate at ₹5 Cr+ income = old regime ~42.74%, new regime ~39%.

## Marginal Relief — Avoid the Tax Cliff

The 87A rebate cliff at ₹12 lakh creates a "notch" — earning ₹1 more can cost ₹60,000 in tax. Marginal relief prevents this:

**How marginal relief works**: If your income is ₹12,05,000 (₹5,000 above the ₹12L threshold), your tax is limited to the amount by which income exceeds ₹12L.

Tax without marginal relief: ₹60,750 (normal calculation)
But income exceeded threshold by only ₹5,000
So maximum tax = ₹5,000 (you can't pay more tax than the extra income you earned)

**Practical**: Between ₹12L and ~₹12.75L income, marginal relief applies and your actual tax is less than the slab calculation suggests. Ask your CA or use a detailed tax calculator for this band.

## How to Switch Tax Regime

**For salaried employees**:
- Inform employer at start of financial year (April) — employer deducts TDS accordingly
- Can switch ONCE per year (either at start of year through employer, or at ITR filing time if you haven't informed employer)
- New regime is now the **DEFAULT** — if you don't inform employer, TDS is deducted under new regime

**For business/professionals**:
- Once opted into old regime: can switch to new regime only once, then cannot go back to old regime (one-time switch rule)
- Salaried employees have more flexibility to switch year-to-year

**For self-employed/freelancers with presumptive taxation (44AD/44ADA)**:
- New regime is available; some 44ADA professionals find new regime beneficial

## Quick Tax Calculator — New Regime FY 2025-26

Formula for salaried employee net tax:

1. Gross salary − ₹75,000 (standard deduction) = Net income
2. Calculate tax on net income using new regime slabs
3. If net income ≤ ₹12,00,000: Apply Section 87A rebate (full tax offset)
4. Add 4% health + education cess on remaining tax
5. Result = final tax liability

**Example — ₹18L gross salary**:
1. ₹18L − ₹75K = ₹17.25L taxable
2. Tax: ₹0 + ₹20K + ₹40K + ₹60K + ₹25K = ₹1,45,000 (₹4L×0% + ₹4L×5% + ₹4L×10% + ₹4L×15% + ₹1.25L×20%)
3. No 87A (income > ₹12L)
4. Add 4% cess: ₹1,45,000 × 1.04 = **₹1,50,800**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I earn ₹12.5L. Do I pay zero tax under new regime?

Yes — with ₹75K standard deduction, your taxable income = ₹11.75L → below ₹12L → 87A rebate applies → zero tax.

### What about special incomes like capital gains at ₹12L income?

Special-rate incomes (LTCG, STCG, dividends above ₹10 lakh) may add to total income and affect 87A rebate eligibility. If you have ₹10L salary income + ₹2L LTCG = ₹12L → the LTCG is taxed separately at 12.5%; 87A rebate applies only on normal income (not LTCG). Consult your CA for mixed-income situations.

### Is the new regime also the default for ITR filing?

Yes — new regime is the default for both TDS by employers and ITR filing from AY 2026-27 onwards. If you want old regime, you must actively opt for it.

### Can I change regime in the middle of the year?

No — regime choice is for the full financial year. You can change it at the start of the next financial year.
