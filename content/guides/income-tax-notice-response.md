## What is an Income Tax Notice?

An income tax notice is an official communication from the Income Tax Department regarding your tax filing, assessment, or compliance. Receiving a notice doesn't always mean something is wrong — many notices are routine and require simple responses.

Common reasons for receiving notices include:
- **Discrepancies** between your ITR and Form 26AS/AIS
- **Non-filing** of income tax return
- **Defective return** with errors or missing information
- **Scrutiny assessment** for detailed verification
- **Demand notice** for outstanding tax dues
- **Refund adjustment** against pending demands

All income tax notices are now delivered electronically to your registered email and the [Income Tax e-filing portal](https://www.incometax.gov.in).

## Types of Income Tax Notices

### Section 143(1) — Intimation Notice
The most common notice. It's an auto-generated intimation after your ITR is processed, showing:
- Tax as computed by the department
- Any mismatch with your declared income or TDS
- Refund due or additional tax payable

**Action needed:** If the computation matches, no action required. If there's a mismatch, file a rectification under Section 154.

### Section 139(9) — Defective Return Notice
Issued when your ITR has errors or missing information, such as:
- Incorrect ITR form used
- Missing schedules or disclosures
- Mismatch between income and deductions

**Action needed:** Correct the defect and file a revised return within **15 days** (extendable on request).

### Section 142(1) — Inquiry Notice
Sent when the department needs more information before completing your assessment:
- Request for additional documents
- Specific queries about income or deductions
- Books of accounts

**Action needed:** Submit the requested information within the specified timeline.

### Section 143(2) — Scrutiny Notice
A serious notice indicating your return has been selected for detailed scrutiny. This means an assessing officer will examine your return closely.

**Action needed:** Prepare all supporting documents. You may want to engage a Chartered Accountant. Respond within the given timeline — usually 3-6 months.

### Section 148 — Reassessment Notice
Issued when the department believes income has escaped assessment. Can be issued within specific time limits:
- **3 years** from end of assessment year for regular cases
- **10 years** for cases involving income escaping assessment above ₹50 lakh

**Action needed:** File a return in response to the notice. Seek professional help as this is serious.

### Section 245 — Refund Adjustment Notice
Sent when the department wants to adjust your pending refund against outstanding tax demands from previous years.

**Action needed:** If you agree, no action needed. If you disagree, respond with reasons within 30 days.

### Section 156 — Demand Notice
Issued when tax, interest, or penalty is payable after assessment.

**Action needed:** Pay the demand or file an appeal if you disagree.

### Section 131(1A) — Search/Seizure
Issued in cases of suspected tax evasion for search and seizure operations.

## How to Check and View Income Tax Notices

### Step 1: Log in to IT Portal
Visit [Income Tax e-filing portal](https://www.incometax.gov.in) and log in with your PAN and password.

### Step 2: Go to Pending Actions
Navigate to **Pending Actions** → **Worklist** or **e-Proceedings**.

### Step 3: View Notice
Click on the relevant notice to view details, download the notice, and find the response submission link.

### Step 4: Check DIN
Every valid notice has a **Document Identification Number (DIN)**. Verify it on the [CBDT DIN verification portal](https://www.incometax.gov.in) to confirm authenticity.

## How to Respond to Income Tax Notices

### General Response Process
1. **Read carefully** — Understand what section the notice is under and what's being asked
2. **Verify authenticity** — Check DIN and confirm it's on the IT portal
3. **Gather documents** — Collect relevant supporting documents
4. **Draft response** — Prepare a clear, factual response
5. **Submit online** — Use the e-Proceedings facility on the IT portal
6. **Keep records** — Save acknowledgement of your response

### Responding to Section 143(1) Mismatch
1. Log in to IT portal
2. Go to **e-File** → **Income Tax Returns** → **View Filed Returns**
3. Check the intimation order details
4. If there's a genuine mismatch, file a **rectification** under Section 154
5. If the mismatch is due to TDS credit, verify Form 26AS

### Responding to Section 139(9) — Defective Return
1. Go to **e-File** → **Income Tax Returns** → **e-File in response to notice u/s 139(9)**
2. Correct the defects in the return
3. Submit the revised return within 15 days
4. If you need more time, apply for extension through the portal

### Responding to Scrutiny (Section 143(2))
1. Go to **Pending Actions** → **e-Proceedings**
2. View the queries raised by the assessing officer
3. Upload supporting documents for each query
4. Submit your response within the given timeline
5. Attend virtual/physical hearings if scheduled

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Ignoring the notice** — This is the worst mistake; it can lead to ex-parte assessment
- **Missing deadlines** — Respond within the specified time; extensions are available but not guaranteed
- **Providing incomplete information** — Answer every query raised, with supporting documents
- **Not verifying Form 26AS** — Many mismatches arise from TDS credit issues; see our [TDS guide](/guide/tds-return-filing-guide)
- **Panicking unnecessarily** — Most notices are routine; read calmly and respond factually

## When to Hire a Professional

Consider engaging a Chartered Accountant or tax professional for:
- **Scrutiny notices** (Section 143(2)) — These require detailed documentation
- **Reassessment notices** (Section 148) — Complex legal issues involved
- **Demand notices** with large amounts
- **Any notice you don't understand** — Better safe than sorry
- **Appeal proceedings** — If you disagree with the assessment order

## Important Tips

1. **Never ignore** an income tax notice — timely response is crucial to avoid adverse orders
2. **Verify DIN** on every notice to confirm it's genuine and not a scam
3. **Respond online** through the e-Proceedings facility — physical responses are no longer accepted for most cases
4. **Maintain records** of all documents submitted and acknowledgements received
5. **File ITR on time** to avoid most routine notices — see our [ITR filing guide](/guide/income-tax-return-file-online)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is receiving an income tax notice a serious issue?
Not always. Many notices like Section 143(1) intimations are routine and auto-generated. However, scrutiny and reassessment notices require careful handling. Read the notice carefully to understand its nature.

### What is the time limit to respond to an income tax notice?
It varies by notice type. Section 139(9) defective return notices give 15 days. Scrutiny notices typically give 3-4 weeks per query. The specific deadline is mentioned in the notice itself.

### Can I respond to income tax notices myself without a CA?
Yes, for simple notices like Section 143(1) mismatches or Section 139(9) defective returns, you can respond yourself. For scrutiny and reassessment notices, professional help is recommended.

### What happens if I don't respond to an income tax notice?
The assessing officer may pass an **ex-parte assessment order** based on available information, which could result in higher tax demand. Penalties may also be imposed for non-compliance.

### How do I know if an income tax notice is genuine?
Check for the Document Identification Number (DIN) on the notice and verify it on the Income Tax portal. Genuine notices also appear in the **Pending Actions** section of your e-filing account.

### Can I file an appeal against an income tax order?
Yes, you can file an appeal with the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) within **30 days** of receiving the assessment order. Further appeals can go to ITAT, High Court, and Supreme Court.
