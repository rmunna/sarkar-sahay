# Insurance Complaint? File with Ombudsman: Step-by-Step Guide

Had your insurance claim rejected unfairly? Insurance company not responding? You can file a complaint with the **Insurance Ombudsman** — a free, independent dispute resolution mechanism set up by the Government of India.

> **Disclaimer:** CitizenNest is an independent informational platform and is not affiliated with IRDAI or the Council for Insurance Ombudsmen.

---

## What Is the Insurance Ombudsman?

The Insurance Ombudsman is an **independent authority** established under the Insurance Ombudsman Rules, 2017 (replacing the 1998 rules). Key facts:

- **Purpose:** Resolve insurance complaints quickly and affordably
- **Cost:** Completely **free** for policyholders
- **Jurisdiction:** Complaints up to **₹50 lakh** (value of claim including expenses)
- **Binding:** Ombudsman's award is binding on the insurer; the policyholder can accept or reject it
- **Centres:** **17 Ombudsman centres** across India

---

## When Can You Approach the Ombudsman?

You can file a complaint **only after** first complaining to the insurance company directly. You must approach the Ombudsman if:

1. Your insurer **rejected your claim** and you disagree
2. Your insurer **hasn't responded** within 30 days of your complaint
3. You're **dissatisfied with the insurer's resolution**
4. There's a **dispute on premium, coverage, terms, or policy conditions**

### Eligible Complaint Types
- Claim rejection or partial settlement
- Delay in claim settlement
- Non-issuance of policy after premium payment
- Disputes on premium charged
- Policy terms misrepresentation by agent
- Any grievance related to life, general, or health insurance policies

### Not Eligible
- Complaints already pending in court or consumer forum
- Complaints beyond **₹50 lakh**
- Complaints older than **1 year** from the insurer's final response (or 1 year from the date of complaint to the insurer if no response was received)

## Step-by-Step: How to File a Complaint

### Step 1: Complain to the Insurance Company First
1. File a written complaint with the insurance company's **Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO)**
2. Use the insurer's **online grievance portal** or email
3. **Wait 30 days** for a response
4. If unsatisfied or no response — proceed to Step 2

### Step 2: Register on IGMS Portal
1. Visit the **IRDAI Grievance Management System (IGMS)**: [igms.irda.gov.in](https://igms.irda.gov.in/)
2. Click **"Register Complaint"**
3. Create an account with your **email and mobile number**
4. Log in to the portal

### Step 3: File Complaint on IGMS
1. Select complaint type: **"Against Insurance Company"**
2. Enter: policy number, insurer name, complaint details
3. Upload supporting documents (listed below)
4. Submit — you receive a **complaint registration number**
5. IGMS forwards your complaint to the insurer for resolution

> **Note:** IGMS is the first-level escalation. If the insurer still doesn't resolve it within 15 days on IGMS, proceed to the Ombudsman.

### Step 4: Approach the Insurance Ombudsman
1. Visit [cioins.co.in](https://www.cioins.co.in/) — Council for Insurance Ombudsmen
2. Click **"Complaint Registration"** → **"Online"**
3. Fill in details: your name, policy details, insurer, nature of complaint
4. Upload: IGMS complaint reference, insurer's rejection letter, supporting documents
5. Submit the complaint — you receive an acknowledgement number
6. The Ombudsman's office schedules a **hearing** (online or in-person)

### Step 5: Hearing & Resolution
1. You may be called for a **personal hearing** at the Ombudsman centre
2. The Ombudsman reviews evidence from both parties
3. **Recommendation** (mutual settlement) is made within **30 days**
4. If no settlement, the Ombudsman passes an **Award** within **3 months** of the complaint
5. The award is **binding on the insurer** — they must comply within 30 days

## Documents Required

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Policy document** | Proof of insurance coverage |
| **Claim form submitted** | Evidence of claim filing |
| **Insurer's rejection/response letter** | Shows grounds for dispute |
| **IGMS complaint reference** | Proof that you first escalated through IGMS |
| **Supporting evidence** | Medical reports, bills, FIR, photographs — depending on claim type |
| **Communication records** | Emails, letters exchanged with insurer |
| **Identity proof** | Aadhaar / PAN card |

## IRDAI Escalation (If Ombudsman Doesn't Help)

If you're still unsatisfied after the Ombudsman process:

1. **IRDAI Integrated Grievance Management System** — file at [igms.irda.gov.in](https://igms.irda.gov.in/)
2. **IRDAI Toll-Free Number:** 155255 (or 1800-4254-732)
3. **Consumer Court** — file a case under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 at:
   - **District Forum** (claims up to ₹1 crore)
   - **State Commission** (₹1 crore – ₹10 crore)
   - **National Commission** (above ₹10 crore)

## Important Tips

1. **Always complain to the insurer first** — the Ombudsman requires proof that you tried to resolve it directly
2. **Keep all communication in writing** — emails and letters serve as evidence
3. **File within the time limit** — you must approach the Ombudsman within 1 year of the insurer's response
4. **The process is free** — you don't need a lawyer; the Ombudsman process is designed for individuals
5. **Be prepared with documents** — organized evidence significantly improves your chances

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Q1: Is there any fee to file a complaint with the Insurance Ombudsman?
**No.** The entire process is completely **free of charge**. You don't need to hire a lawyer either.

### Q2: Can I file an Ombudsman complaint online?
**Yes.** You can file online at [cioins.co.in](https://www.cioins.co.in/). You can also send a physical complaint letter to the relevant Ombudsman centre.

### Q3: What is the maximum claim amount the Ombudsman can handle?
The Ombudsman can handle complaints where the claim value (including expenses) is up to **₹50 lakh**.

### Q4: Is the Ombudsman's decision final?
The Ombudsman's **award is binding on the insurer**. The policyholder can choose to accept or reject it. If rejected, you can still approach the Consumer Court.

### Q5: Can I approach the Ombudsman for health insurance complaints?
**Yes.** The Ombudsman handles complaints related to **all types of insurance** — life, general (motor, property), and health insurance.

### Q6: How long does the Ombudsman process take?
Typically, the recommendation is made within **30 days** and the award within **3 months**. Including the initial insurer complaint, the full process may take **4–6 months**.

### Q7: What if my insurance company doesn't comply with the Ombudsman's award?
Non-compliance is a serious regulatory violation. You can report it to **IRDAI** directly. The insurer faces regulatory action for non-compliance.
