# Insurance Complaint? File with Ombudsman: Step-by-Step

If your insurance claim has been rejected, delayed, or partially paid, and the insurance company hasn't resolved your complaint, you can approach the **Insurance Ombudsman**. The Ombudsman is an independent authority that resolves insurance disputes free of cost. This guide explains the complete process — from filing on the IGMS portal to the Ombudsman hearing.

## What is the Insurance Ombudsman?

The Insurance Ombudsman is a quasi-judicial authority established under the **Insurance Ombudsman Rules, 2017** (replaced the earlier 1998 rules). Key features:

- **Free of cost** — No fees for filing a complaint
- **Quick resolution** — Ombudsman must pass an award within 3 months
- **Binding on insurer** — The Ombudsman's award is binding on the insurance company (up to ₹50 lakh)
- **Not binding on complainant** — You can reject the award and approach Consumer Forum or court
- **17 centres** across India, each with territorial jurisdiction

---

## When Can You Approach the Ombudsman?

You can file a complaint with the Ombudsman if:

1. You have **already complained to the insurance company** and:
   - The company rejected your complaint, OR
   - You did not receive a response within **30 days**, OR
   - You are not satisfied with the response
2. The complaint value is **up to ₹50 lakh** (including expenses claimed)
3. The complaint is filed **within 1 year** of the insurer's final response (or within 1 year of the 30-day deadline)
4. The matter is **not pending** in any court, Consumer Forum, or arbitration

### Types of Complaints Handled

- Claim rejection (life, health, motor, property)
- Delay in claim settlement
- Dispute on claim amount
- Non-issuance of policy after premium payment
- Premium dispute
- Policy terms and conditions dispute
- Any grievance related to insurance policy servicing

---

## Step 1: First Complain to the Insurance Company

Before approaching the Ombudsman, you MUST first complain to the insurer:

1. **Write to the Grievance/Customer Service department** of your insurance company
2. Use the **IRDAI IGMS Portal** to register your complaint:
   - Visit [igms.irda.gov.in](https://igms.irda.gov.in/)
   - Register/login → File complaint against insurer
   - Upload supporting documents
   - Note down the complaint/token number
3. **Wait 30 days** for the insurer's response
4. If unresolved after 30 days (or unsatisfactory response), proceed to the Ombudsman

## Step 3: File Complaint with the Ombudsman — Online

### Online Filing (Recommended)

1. Visit the Council for Insurance Ombudsmen website: [cioins.co.in](https://cioins.co.in/)
2. Click on "Complaint Registration" or "Online Complaint"
3. Fill in the complaint form:
   - Your personal details (name, address, contact)
   - Insurance company name and policy number
   - Type of complaint (claim rejection, delay, etc.)
   - Brief facts of the complaint
   - Relief sought (amount or action requested)
4. Upload supporting documents:
   - Copy of the insurance policy
   - Claim correspondence with insurer
   - IGMS complaint copy and insurer's response
   - Any supporting evidence (medical reports, FIR, photos, etc.)
5. Submit the complaint
6. Note down the complaint reference number

### Offline Filing (By Post)

1. Write a complaint letter addressed to the Insurance Ombudsman of your jurisdiction
2. Include:
   - Your name, address, policy number
   - Insurance company details
   - Facts of the complaint
   - Copies of all relevant documents
   - IGMS complaint details and insurer's response
3. Send by registered post to the Ombudsman office address (available on [cioins.co.in](https://cioins.co.in/))

## Step 4: What Happens After Filing

### Mediation (Recommendation)

1. The Ombudsman first attempts **mediation** between you and the insurer
2. If both parties agree to a settlement, a **Recommendation** is issued
3. The Recommendation must be accepted by the insurer within 15 days
4. If you accept it, the matter is closed

### Award (If Mediation Fails)

1. If mediation fails, the Ombudsman conducts a **hearing**
2. You may be called for an in-person or virtual hearing
3. The Ombudsman passes an **Award** within **3 months** of receiving all documents
4. The Award is **binding on the insurer** for amounts up to ₹50 lakh
5. The insurer must comply within **30 days** of the Award
6. If you are not satisfied with the Award, you can approach the Consumer Forum or civil court

---

## Documents Required

- Copy of the insurance policy
- Claim form and all claim documents submitted to insurer
- Insurer's rejection/response letter
- IGMS complaint registration proof
- All correspondence with the insurer (emails, letters)
- Supporting evidence (medical reports, hospital bills, FIR, photographs, death certificate — as applicable)
- Aadhaar/ID proof
- Bank account details (for claim payment)

---

## Important Tips

1. **Always file on IGMS first** — The Ombudsman will check if you approached the insurer first
2. **File within 1 year** — Complaints must be filed within 1 year of the insurer's response or the 30-day deadline
3. **Keep all documents** — Maintain copies of every communication with the insurer
4. **Be specific** — Clearly state the relief amount and reason in your complaint
5. **No lawyer needed** — The Ombudsman process is designed for individuals; you represent yourself

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Is there any fee for filing a complaint with the Insurance Ombudsman?
No. The entire process is completely free of cost. You do not need to pay any fee or hire a lawyer.

### Q2. Can I directly approach the Ombudsman without complaining to the insurer first?
No. You must first complain to the insurance company (directly or through IGMS) and wait for 30 days. Only after that can you approach the Ombudsman.

### Q3. What is the maximum claim amount the Ombudsman can handle?
The Ombudsman can handle complaints with claim value up to **₹50 lakh** (including expenses).

### Q4. How long does the Ombudsman take to resolve a complaint?
The Ombudsman is required to pass a Recommendation within 1 month or an Award within 3 months of receiving complete documents. In practice, most cases are resolved within 2-4 months.

### Q5. Can I approach the Ombudsman for health insurance complaints?
Yes. The Ombudsman handles complaints related to all types of insurance — life, health, motor, property, travel, and more.

### Q6. What if the insurance company does not comply with the Ombudsman's Award?
The Award is legally binding on the insurer. If the company does not comply within 30 days, you can approach the Consumer Forum or IRDAI for enforcement.

### Q7. Can I still go to Consumer Court after the Ombudsman's decision?
Yes. The Ombudsman's Award is binding on the insurer but not on you. If you are not satisfied, you can approach the Consumer Disputes Redressal Forum or civil court.
