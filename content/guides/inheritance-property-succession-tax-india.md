# Inheritance and Property Succession Tax India 2026 — What's Taxable and What's Not

**India has no inheritance tax** (estate duty was abolished in 1985). When someone passes away and leaves property to their legal heirs — through a will or intestate succession — the heirs pay **zero tax on the inheritance itself**.

However, when heirs **sell inherited assets later**, capital gains tax applies. The rules for cost basis, holding period, and tax calculation for inherited assets are specific and important.

## The Core Rule — Inheritance Itself Is Tax-Free

| Situation | Tax at Inheritance |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Inheriting a house from parents | ❌ Zero tax |
| Inheriting shares from grandparents | ❌ Zero tax |
| Inheriting bank FD from spouse | ❌ Zero tax |
| Inheriting jewellery through a will | ❌ Zero tax |
| Receiving insurance payout as nominee | ❌ Zero tax |
| Inheriting cash/bank balance | ❌ Zero tax |

This is explicitly covered in income tax law — gifts received through inheritance or Will are exempt under Section 56(2)(x).

**Also**: Any income earned from the inherited asset AFTER the inheritance date is taxable in the heir's hands — but the inheritance event itself is tax-free.

## Selling Inherited Property — Capital Gains Tax

When you sell an asset you inherited, capital gains tax applies on the **profit** (sale price minus cost basis). The key questions are:

1. What is the cost basis?
2. What is the holding period (short-term or long-term)?

### Cost Basis for Inherited Property

The cost basis is the **original price paid by the deceased** — not the market value at the time of inheritance.

**Exception (very important)**: If the asset was acquired before **April 1, 2001**, you may use the **Fair Market Value (FMV) as on April 1, 2001** as the cost basis. This prevents unfair taxation of very old assets where records may be unavailable and pre-2001 price appreciation was significant.

**For assets acquired after April 1, 2001**: Use the original purchase price by the deceased.

### Holding Period for Inherited Assets

The holding period for the heir includes the period the deceased held the asset.

**Example**:
- Father bought flat in January 2018
- Father passed away in June 2023; you inherited the flat
- You sold in March 2026

Holding period = January 2018 to March 2026 = 8+ years → Long-term. Tax: LTCG at 12.5%.

**Your own holding period (June 2023 onwards)** is NOT used. The deceased's holding period is tacked on.

### Capital Gains Tax Rates on Inherited Assets Sold

| Asset Type | Long-Term Threshold | LTCG Rate | STCG Rate |
|------------|--------------------|-----------|----|
| Property (land/building) | 24 months | 12.5% (no indexation) | Slab rate |
| Listed equity shares / equity MF | 12 months | 12.5% above ₹1.25L | 20% |
| Jewellery, gold, art | 24 months | 12.5% (no indexation) | Slab rate |
| Unlisted shares | 24 months | 12.5% | Slab rate |
| Debt MF | 36 months (or same as property) | Slab rate | Slab rate |

**Note**: Budget 2024 changed property LTCG to 12.5% without indexation (previously 20% with indexation). For properties bought before July 23, 2024, a grandfathering option may apply — compare 12.5% without indexation vs 20% with indexation and choose the lower one.

## Specific Asset Inheritance Rules

### Inherited House Property

- **At inheritance**: No tax
- **Rent from inherited house**: Taxable in your hands (Income from House Property) from the date of inheritance
- **When you sell**: LTCG/STCG based on above rules; cost = original cost to deceased
- **Exemptions on sale**: You can claim Section 54/54EC/54F exemptions on capital gains (same as if you owned the property yourself)

### Inherited Bank FD / Savings

- **At inheritance**: No tax — you simply become the account holder
- **Interest from the date of inheritance**: Fully taxable in your hands
- **Interest before death (accrued but not paid)**: This may be reported in the deceased's final ITR or in the estate

### Inherited Shares and Mutual Funds

- **At inheritance**: No tax
- **Dividends after inheritance**: Taxable in your hands from the date of inheritance
- **When you sell**: Capital gains (holding period from deceased's purchase date; cost = deceased's purchase price)
- **Indexed SIP lots**: For mutual fund SIPs, each SIP installment has its own cost and date — the entire history is transferred to you

### Inherited Jewellery / Gold

- **At inheritance**: No tax
- **When you sell**: LTCG at 12.5% (if held total 24+ months including by deceased) or STCG at slab rate
- **FMV on April 1, 2001** rule applies if jewellery is very old (pre-2001)

## Section 54 Exemption on Inherited Property Sale

When you sell inherited property and make capital gains, you can claim LTCG exemption the same way any other property seller can:

- **Section 54**: Invest LTCG in another residential property within 2 years (purchase) or 3 years (construction)
- **Section 54EC**: Invest in specified bonds (NHAI, REC) within 6 months — up to ₹50L
- **Section 54F**: If selling a non-residential asset (shares, gold, etc.) — invest net sale proceeds in residential property

These exemptions apply equally to inherited property.

## Ancestral Property vs Self-Acquired Property

In Indian personal law (Hindu Succession Act), property has two categories:

| Property Type | Who Owns It |
|--------------|-------------|
| **Ancestral property** | All coparceners jointly (sons, daughters by birth) — co-parceners have birth-right |
| **Self-acquired property** | Individual alone; distributed per Will or succession laws |

For tax purposes, the distinction doesn't change the capital gains rules significantly — cost basis and holding period rules work the same way. However, for HUF income and partition rights, the distinction matters more.

## Family Settlement — Caution

Sometimes families distribute property through informal "family settlements" without legal documentation. This can create tax problems:

- An undocumented transfer may not qualify as "inheritance" — could be treated as a gift or sale
- If treated as a **gift** (not under Will/inheritance): Gift from relatives (parents, siblings) is still exempt
- If treated as a **sale**: Capital gains tax applies to the transferor

Always document family property distribution via proper legal instruments (registered Will, registered partition deed, or registered family settlement).

## Probate of Will — When Required

In India, probate (court-ordered validation of Will) is required only in certain jurisdictions:
- **Mandatory probate**: Wills executed in Mumbai, Calcutta, Chennai (or properties in these cities)
- **Other states**: Probate is optional but recommended for large estates

For income tax purposes, a registered Will (even without probate) is usually sufficient to prove inheritance. For bank accounts, nominee + probate/legal heir certificate is required.

## Deceased Person's Final ITR

After someone passes away, the legal representative (heir, executor) must file a final ITR for the deceased person:
- For income earned from April 1 of that year to the date of death
- File before the normal ITR deadline (July 31)
- Report all income: salary, rental income, FD interest, capital gains

Any tax due is paid from the estate before distribution to heirs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My father left me ₹50L in a bank account. Do I pay income tax on it?

No — inherited money from a deceased parent is tax-free. You simply become the beneficiary. The ₹50L doesn't add to your income. However, going forward, any interest earned on those ₹50L in your account is YOUR income and taxable.

### My mother died without a Will (intestate). How is property distributed? Is it taxed differently?

Intestate succession is governed by personal law (Hindu Succession Act for Hindus). The property is distributed among Class I heirs (spouse, children, mother) equally. Tax treatment is the same as Will-based inheritance — still tax-free at inheritance.

### I inherited a flat worth ₹1.5 crore. The registration is in my name now. When I sell, what's my cost?

Cost basis = what your parent originally paid for the flat (original purchase price to the deceased). If bought in 1995 for ₹8 lakhs: your cost is ₹8L. But if FMV on April 1, 2001 was ₹25L, you can use ₹25L as cost basis instead. Capital gain = ₹1.5Cr − ₹25L = ₹1.25Cr. LTCG tax at 12.5% (no indexation under Budget 2024 rules).

### My uncle (not a direct relative) died and left me shares worth ₹2 lakh. Taxable?

Inheritance from a Will is always tax-free, regardless of relationship (relatives or non-relatives). Section 56(2)(x) explicitly exempts inheritance/Will-received assets. No tax at inheritance. Dividends on those shares going forward are taxable in your hands.
