# Building Completion Certificate: How to Apply Online

A Building Completion Certificate (CC) is an official document issued by the local municipal authority confirming that a building has been constructed according to the sanctioned building plan and complies with all applicable building bye-laws and regulations. Without a CC, a building is technically considered incomplete and unauthorised for occupation.

## What is a Building Completion Certificate?

A Completion Certificate certifies that:

- The building has been constructed as per the approved building plan
- Construction complies with local building bye-laws, fire safety norms, and structural requirements
- All mandatory clearances (environmental, fire, structural) have been obtained
- The building is ready for the issuance of an [Occupancy Certificate](/guide/occupancy-certificate-apply)

**Note:** The CC is different from an Occupancy Certificate (OC). The CC confirms construction compliance, while the OC permits people to legally occupy the building.

---

## When is a Completion Certificate Required?

You need a Building Completion Certificate in these situations:

1. **After completing construction** of any residential, commercial, or industrial building
2. **Before applying for an Occupancy Certificate** — CC is a prerequisite for OC
3. **Property registration and sale** — buyers and banks require CC for home loans
4. **Utility connections** — permanent water, electricity, and sewage connections often need CC
5. **Property tax assessment** — municipal corporations use CC for tax records
6. **Insurance purposes** — building insurance may require valid CC

---

## Who Can Apply?

- The **building owner** or **developer/builder** who holds the sanctioned building plan
- An **authorised architect or licensed engineer** on behalf of the owner
- **Housing societies** for multi-storey buildings

---

## Documents Required

### Mandatory Documents

- Sanctioned/approved building plan (original)
- Approved layout plan
- Building permit/commencement certificate (original)
- Architect or structural engineer's completion certificate (Form-B or equivalent)
- Photographs of the completed building (all sides)
- Property ownership proof (sale deed, title deed)
- Property tax paid receipts (up to date)
- NOC from Fire Department
- Structural stability certificate from licensed structural engineer
- Land use certificate / conversion order (if applicable)

### Additional Documents (May Vary by Municipality)

- NOC from Airport Authority of India (if near airport zone)
- Environmental clearance (for large projects, via [Parivesh portal](https://parivesh.nic.in/))
- Lift/escalator inspection certificate (for multi-storey buildings)
- Rainwater harvesting compliance certificate
- Solar panel installation certificate (mandatory in some states)
- NOC from Pollution Control Board (for commercial/industrial)
- Plinth level verification report
- Soil testing report

## Step-by-Step Process: How to Apply Online

The exact online portal varies by city/municipality. Below is the general process followed by most municipal corporations:

### Online Process

1. **Visit the municipal corporation portal** of your city (e.g., BBMP for Bengaluru, MCGM for Mumbai, MCD for Delhi)
2. **Register/Login** with your credentials — use your building permit number or property ID
3. **Navigate to "Building Plan" or "Completion Certificate"** section
4. **Fill the application form** — enter building details, permit number, plot details, and construction specifics
5. **Upload documents** — scan and upload all required documents listed above
6. **Attach architect's completion certificate** — the licensed architect must certify that construction matches the sanctioned plan
7. **Pay the application fee** online via net banking, UPI, or debit/credit card
8. **Submit the application** — note down the application/reference number
9. **Site inspection** — municipal officials will schedule a site visit to verify construction
10. **Receive CC** — if inspection is satisfactory, CC is issued (digitally or physically)

### Offline Process

1. **Obtain the application form** from the municipal corporation office or download from their website
2. **Fill the form** and attach all required documents (self-attested photocopies + originals for verification)
3. **Get architect certification** — your licensed architect must sign the completion certificate form
4. **Submit at the municipal office** along with the prescribed fee
5. **Site inspection** is scheduled by the municipal engineer
6. **Collect CC** after successful verification

## Architect's Completion Certificate

A critical requirement is the **Architect's or Engineer's Completion Certificate**. This is a declaration by the licensed architect or structural engineer who supervised the construction, stating:

- Construction has been completed as per the sanctioned building plan
- No deviations exist from the approved plan
- The building is structurally sound and safe for occupation
- All building bye-laws and regulations have been followed

**Important:** The architect must be registered with the Council of Architecture (COA) and hold a valid licence from the local municipal body.

## What Happens If There Are Deviations?

If the municipal inspector finds deviations from the sanctioned plan:

- **Minor deviations** — you may be asked to pay a compounding fee and regularise the deviation
- **Major deviations** — the CC application will be rejected; you must either demolish the unauthorised construction or apply for a revised building plan
- **Penalties** — unauthorised construction can attract penalties ranging from ₹5,000 to several lakhs depending on the extent of deviation and local bye-laws

---

## Important Tips

1. **Get the architect involved early** — ensure your architect supervises construction to avoid deviations from the sanctioned plan
2. **Keep all NOCs ready** — fire, structural, environmental clearances take time; apply for them during construction itself
3. **Pay property tax before applying** — pending property tax will delay your CC
4. **Check state-specific requirements** — some states mandate rainwater harvesting, solar panels, or earthquake-resistant design certification
5. **Track your application online** — most portals allow status tracking; follow up if inspection is delayed beyond 30 days

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. What is the difference between a Completion Certificate and an Occupancy Certificate?

A Completion Certificate (CC) confirms that construction is complete and matches the approved building plan. An Occupancy Certificate (OC) is issued after the CC and permits people to legally move into and occupy the building. Read our detailed guide on [Occupancy Certificate](/guide/occupancy-certificate-apply).

### Q2. Can I get a home loan without a Completion Certificate?

Banks generally do not provide home loans for properties without a valid CC and OC. Under-construction properties may get loans based on the builder's approvals, but final disbursement usually requires CC/OC.

### Q3. Is a Completion Certificate mandatory for all buildings?

Yes, any construction done after obtaining a building permit must get a CC upon completion. This applies to residential, commercial, and industrial buildings. Small renovations or interior changes may not require a CC — check with your local municipal body.

### Q4. What happens if I occupy a building without a CC?

Occupying a building without CC/OC is illegal. The municipal corporation can issue notices, impose fines, disconnect utility services, or in extreme cases, order demolition. You may also face difficulties selling the property or getting insurance.

### Q5. How long is a Completion Certificate valid?

A Completion Certificate does not expire. Once issued, it remains valid permanently as a record that the building was constructed per approved plans at that point in time.

### Q6. Can I apply for CC if the builder has not applied?

Yes, if the builder fails to obtain a CC, individual flat owners or the housing society can apply directly to the municipal corporation. You will need the original sanctioned plan and building permit documents.

### Q7. What is the penalty for construction without a building permit?

Construction without a building permit is illegal and can attract heavy penalties, demolition orders, and even criminal prosecution under local municipal laws. Always obtain the building permit before starting construction.
