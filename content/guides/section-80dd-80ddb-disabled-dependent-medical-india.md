# Section 80DD and 80DDB India 2026 — Medical Tax Relief for Disabled Dependents and Serious Illness

Two often-overlooked deductions provide significant tax relief for families dealing with disability or serious illness:
- **Section 80DD**: For taxpayers who have a **disabled dependent** family member
- **Section 80DDB**: For taxpayers who are spending on treatment of **specific serious diseases** (self or dependent)

Both are available only under the **old tax regime**.

## Section 80DD — Disabled Dependent

### Who Can Claim

The taxpayer (individual or HUF) must be **maintaining a disabled dependent** — a family member with a disability who is dependent on the taxpayer for maintenance.

**Eligible dependents** (must be dependent on the taxpayer):
- Spouse
- Children (son/daughter)
- Parents
- Siblings (brothers/sisters)

The dependent must **not have claimed deduction under Section 80U** (which is for the disabled person themselves). 80DD is for the family member who supports them.

### Disability Condition (Must Be Certified)

The dependent must have a disability as per the Persons with Disabilities Act or National Trust Act:
- Blindness, low vision
- Leprosy (cured)
- Hearing impairment
- Locomotor disability
- Mental retardation
- Mental illness
- Autism, cerebral palsy, multiple disabilities

**Severity matters for the deduction amount**:

| Type | Disability Level | Deduction |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| Normal disability | 40%–79% disability | **₹75,000** |
| Severe disability | 80% or more disability | **₹1,25,000** |

### What Expenses Are Covered

Section 80DD covers:
1. Medical treatment, nursing, training, rehabilitation expenses for the disabled dependent
2. Amount paid as premium for insurance policy for maintenance of the disabled dependent (annuity/lump sum for dependent after taxpayer's death)

**Fixed deduction**: You get ₹75,000 or ₹1,25,000 regardless of actual spending — even if you spent only ₹20,000, you still get ₹75,000 deduction.

### Documents Required for 80DD

- **Certificate from a medical authority** in a Government hospital certifying the disability and its severity
- Form 10-IA (downloadable from IT portal) — needs to be filled by a specialist doctor and submitted along with ITR

## Section 80DDB — Specific Serious Diseases

### Who Can Claim

Individual or HUF taxpayer spending on treatment of:
- **Themselves** (the taxpayer)
- **Dependent** (spouse, children, parents, siblings)

### Prescribed Diseases (List)

Section 80DDB applies only to these specific diseases:

| Category | Diseases |
|----------|---------|
| Neurological diseases | Dementia, Dystonia musculorum deformans, Motor neuron disease, Ataxia, Chorea, Hemiballismus, Aphasia, Parkinson's disease |
| Cancer | Malignant cancers |
| AIDS | Full blown AIDS |
| Chronic Renal Failure | Including dialysis expenses |
| Haematological disorders | Haemophilia, Thalassaemia |

**NOT covered by 80DDB**: Diabetes, heart disease, hypertension, TB, kidney stones — only the specific diseases listed above.

### Deduction Limits (After Reimbursement)

The deduction is the ACTUAL amount spent on treatment, subject to these maximums:

| Patient Age | Maximum Deduction |
|------------|------------------|
| Below 60 years | **₹40,000** |
| 60 years or above (senior citizen) | **₹1,00,000** |

**Minus insurance reimbursement**: If any amount was reimbursed by health insurance or employer, that is reduced from the deduction claim.

**Example**: Cancer treatment cost ₹1,50,000. Insurance reimbursed ₹80,000. Actual out-of-pocket: ₹70,000. Patient is 65 years old.
- Maximum for senior citizen: ₹1,00,000
- Actual out-of-pocket: ₹70,000
- Deduction: ₹70,000 (lower of limit and actual out-of-pocket)

### Documents Required for 80DDB

- Prescription from a **specialist doctor** (oncologist for cancer, neurologist for neurological, nephrologist for renal failure, etc.) in a Government hospital
- Form 10-IA filled by the specialist

**Specialist hospital note**: Before 2015, a Government hospital certificate was mandatory. Budget 2015 allowed private hospital specialist certificates too — but many interpretations require Government hospital specialists. To be safe, get the certificate from a Government hospital specialist.

## Section 80U — Deduction for SELF-Disability

If the **taxpayer themselves** (not a dependent) has a disability:
- Use **Section 80U** (not 80DD)
- Same deduction amounts: ₹75,000 (40%–79% disability) or ₹1,25,000 (80%+ severe disability)
- Same documents: Disability certificate from Government medical authority + Form 10-IA

You cannot claim both 80DD (for dependent) and 80U (for self) from the same disability. They serve different scenarios.

## Interaction With Section 80D (Health Insurance)

Section 80D (health insurance premium) is separate from 80DD and 80DDB:
- 80D: Deduction for insurance premiums paid
- 80DD: Deduction for disabled dependent care (includes insurance for dependent's welfare)
- 80DDB: Deduction for specific disease treatment expenses

All three can be claimed simultaneously (within their respective limits), as they cover different aspects of medical expenditure.

## Claim Process in ITR

In ITR (Schedule VIA under Chapter VI-A deductions):
- Section 80DD: Enter amount (₹75,000 or ₹1,25,000 depending on severity)
- Section 80DDB: Enter actual expenses (net of reimbursement), up to limit
- Section 80U: If self-disabled, claim here

Upload or retain the disability certificate and specialist prescription — keep for potential scrutiny (7 years).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My father has Parkinson's disease (65 years old) and I paid ₹90,000 for his treatment. What's my deduction?

Parkinson's disease is a qualifying disease under Section 80DDB. Father is 65 (senior citizen). Maximum deduction: ₹1,00,000. Actual expenses: ₹90,000. Deduction = ₹90,000 (lower of maximum and actual). If any amount was reimbursed by insurance, subtract that from ₹90,000.

### My child has autism (certified 45% disability). Can I claim 80DD?

Yes — autism is covered under the Persons with Disabilities Act. 45% disability = "normal disability" category → ₹75,000 deduction under Section 80DD. Get the disability certificate from a Government medical authority.

### My spouse has cancer. I'm paying for chemotherapy. Is there a deduction for the premium of health insurance I bought for this?

Two deductions:
1. **Section 80D**: Premium paid for your spouse's health insurance (up to ₹25,000 if spouse below 60 / ₹50,000 if 60+)
2. **Section 80DDB**: Chemotherapy expenses up to ₹40,000 (if spouse below 60) or ₹1,00,000 (if 60+), minus insurance reimbursement

Both can be claimed in the same year.
