# Income Tax Scrutiny Notice Section 143(2) — How to Respond

A **Notice under Section 143(2)** means the Income Tax Department has selected your ITR for **detailed scrutiny** (also called selective/limited assessment). This is significantly more serious than a 143(1) intimation — a human Assessing Officer (AO) will examine your income, deductions, and claims.

> **Act immediately**: Ignoring a 143(2) notice results in an **ex-parte assessment** (the AO decides without your input), which almost always results in a large tax demand. Respond within the deadline.

## 143(2) Scrutiny vs 143(1) Intimation — Key Difference

| Aspect | 143(1) Intimation | 143(2) Scrutiny Notice |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| **Who sends it** | Computer (CPC Bengaluru) | Human Assessing Officer |
| **What it means** | Automatic processing done | Your ITR selected for human review |
| **Severity** | Routine — almost everyone gets it | Selective — only some ITRs selected |
| **Action required** | Minimal (only if demand) | Immediate response mandatory |
| **What AO can do** | Limited adjustments | Full examination of income and claims |
| **Outcome** | Usually no big change | Can result in large tax demand |

## Why Was Your ITR Selected for Scrutiny?

CBDT (Central Board of Direct Taxes) selects ITRs using risk parameters. Common triggers:

| Trigger | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **High-value transactions** | Large cash deposits, property sales, stock gains |
| **Income mismatch** | AIS shows income not matching ITR |
| **Large deductions** | 80C/80D/HRA claims that seem high relative to income |
| **Mismatch with TDS** | TDS amounts differ from what you claimed |
| **First-time high-income filer** | Sudden income spike vs prior years |
| **Specific sectors or schemes** | CBDT sometimes targets specific business types |
| **Third-party information** | Banks, property registrar, GST data flagged you |
| **Random selection** | CBDT allocates some % of returns for random scrutiny |

## Deadline to Respond

A 143(2) notice must be issued **within 3 months** of the end of the assessment year. You then have a **window stated in the notice** (typically 30 days) to respond.

**Do not miss the response deadline.** Request an extension from the AO in writing if you need more time.

## Types of Scrutiny

### Limited Scrutiny (CASS — Computer Aided Scrutiny Selection)

- Most 143(2) notices are limited scrutiny
- Examines **specific issues** mentioned in the notice (e.g., "mismatch in rental income," "high cash deposits")
- You need to provide documents only for those specific points

### Complete Scrutiny

- Rare — usually for high-value cases, fraud suspicion, or AO's specific request
- AO examines your entire return in detail
- Requires comprehensive documentation

## What Happens During Scrutiny

### Step 1: Notice Received → Respond

Acknowledge receipt in e-Proceedings and provide initial response within deadline.

### Step 2: AO Issues Questionnaire

AO sends specific questions about items in your ITR. Examples:
- "Provide source of cash deposit of ₹15 lakh in SBI account in November"
- "Provide documentary evidence of rental income declared at ₹4.5 lakh"
- "Provide details of LTCG claim of ₹8 lakh"

### Step 3: You Provide Documents

Upload documents via e-Proceedings:
- Bank statements
- Sale deeds, rent agreements
- Investment proofs
- Business books of accounts
- Tax audit report (if applicable)

### Step 4: AO Assessment Order

AO either:
- **Accepts your return as filed**: Assessment order with no change
- **Makes additions/disallowances**: Issues a Draft Assessment Order showing proposed additions, gives you a chance to respond
- After your response: **Final Assessment Order** issued

### Step 5: If Demand Raised → Appeal or Pay

If AO raises a demand in the final assessment order, you can:
- Pay it (within 30 days via Section 156 demand notice)
- Appeal to CIT(A) within 30 days

## How to Respond to 143(2) Notice Online

1. Login to **[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/)**
2. Go to **"Pending Actions"** → **"e-Proceedings"**
3. Find your 143(2) notice (identified by Assessment Year)
4. Click → **Submit Response**
5. Upload your documents (PDF format, max size per upload)
6. Submit — you receive an acknowledgement

> All communication during scrutiny happens through e-Proceedings on the income tax portal. Face-to-face meetings with AO are now rare (post-faceless assessment).

## Faceless Assessment — What It Means

From August 2020, India switched to **Faceless Assessment Scheme** for most taxpayers. This means:
- You don't need to visit the income tax office
- AO assigned to your case may be in a different city
- All communication is through the e-Proceedings portal
- **No in-person contact** — reduces harassment and corruption

Exception: Some high-value/complex cases may still be called for in-person meeting.

## Documents to Prepare (Common)

| What AO May Ask | Documents |
|-----------------|----------|
| Bank deposit explanations | Bank statements, FD receipts, gift deeds, loan documents |
| Cash income/deposits | Business invoices, cash register records |
| Property sale | Sale deed, stamp duty receipt, cost of acquisition documents |
| HRA claim | Rent agreement, landlord PAN, rent receipts |
| Business income | GST returns, books of accounts, balance sheet, P&L |
| Investments | Purchase slips, demat statements, MF statements |

## When to Hire a Chartered Accountant

**Hire a CA or tax consultant if**:
- The scrutiny involves business income or complex transactions
- The potential demand is large (> ₹1 lakh)
- You have multiple investments, properties, or sources of income
- AO is questioning claims you're not sure how to substantiate
- Faceless scrutiny has asked for books of accounts

**DIY may work if**:
- The query is about a single, specific, simple item (e.g., "explain salary income")
- You have all documents readily available
- The potential demand is small

## Timeline of a Scrutiny Assessment

| Stage | Deadline |
|-------|---------|
| 143(2) notice must be issued by department | Within 3 months of the end of Assessment Year |
| Assessment (final order) to be completed by AO | **18 months** from end of Assessment Year (older years: 12 months) |
| Appeal to CIT(A) after order | Within 30 days of assessment order |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I got a 143(2) notice for AY 2023-24. Do I need to produce all records from that year?

Only produce records relevant to the specific queries in the notice. For limited scrutiny, if the notice mentions only "rental income," you primarily need rent-related documents. If the AO expands scope, they must issue a fresh questionnaire.

### Can I be penalized just for being selected for scrutiny?

No — being selected for scrutiny is not a penalty or allegation. If the AO finds unreported income or false claims during scrutiny, then penalties under Section 271 apply. But selection itself is neutral.

### My CA has retired and I don't have old records. What to do?

Contact the AO via e-Proceedings explaining the situation. For very old documents, you can:
- Get bank statements re-issued from the bank
- Request copies of property documents from the registrar
- Ask for extension of time to gather documents

### I haven't responded to the notice for 40 days (missed the 30-day deadline). What to do?

Write to the AO immediately via e-Proceedings explaining the delay and requesting an extension. If the AO issues an ex-parte assessment order, you can still appeal within 30 days of that order.
