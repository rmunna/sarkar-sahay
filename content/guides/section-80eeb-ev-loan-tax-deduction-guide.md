# Section 80EEB — ₹1.5 Lakh Tax Deduction on Electric Vehicle Loan Interest

If you took a loan to buy an **electric vehicle (EV)** — car, two-wheeler, or any electric vehicle — and the loan was sanctioned between **April 1, 2019 and March 31, 2023**, you can claim up to **₹1.5 lakh per year** deduction on the interest paid under **Section 80EEB**.

This deduction applies for the **entire loan tenure** — every year until the loan is repaid, as long as the original sanction was within the eligible window.

## Section 80EEB — Eligibility Conditions

| Condition | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Loan sanction date** | April 1, 2019 to March 31, 2023 |
| **Loan from** | Banks, NBFCs (RBI-registered), or other notified financial institutions |
| **Vehicle type** | Any electric vehicle (car, two-wheeler, three-wheeler) with battery-operated motor |
| **Taxpayer type** | Individuals only (not HUF, companies, or firms) |
| **Tax regime** | Old tax regime only |

**Important**: The deduction is for **interest only** — not EMI principal. EMI = principal + interest; only the interest portion qualifies for 80EEB.

## Maximum Deduction

**₹1,50,000 per year** on interest paid on the EV loan.

If your annual EV loan interest is ₹80,000 (lower than the cap), you deduct ₹80,000 — not the full ₹1.5L. The deduction is limited to actual interest paid, subject to the ₹1.5L maximum.

## Which Vehicles Qualify?

Under the Motor Vehicles Act, an "electric vehicle" is defined as a vehicle propelled purely by an electric motor. This includes:
- Electric cars (Tata Nexon EV, MG ZS EV, Hyundai IONIQ, etc.)
- Electric two-wheelers (Ola S1, Ather, TVS iQube, Bajaj Chetak, Hero Vida, etc.)
- Electric three-wheelers / e-rickshaws
- Electric buses (not for individual taxpayers)

**Hybrid vehicles** (petrol + electric): These are NOT fully electric — they do not qualify for 80EEB.

| Annual EV Loan Interest | Deduction | Tax Saving (30% slab) | Tax Saving (20% slab) |
|------------------------|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| ₹80,000 | ₹80,000 | ₹24,000 | ₹16,000 |

**Practical example**:
- EV car loan: ₹10 lakh at 9% for 5 years
- Year 1 interest: ~₹85,000
- 80EEB deduction: ₹85,000 (within ₹1.5L cap)
- Tax saving at 30% slab: ₹25,500

## Old Regime Only

Section 80EEB is a Chapter VI-A deduction. Under the **new tax regime** (Section 115BAC), all Chapter VI-A deductions are unavailable — including 80EEB.

If you want to claim 80EEB, you must file under the **old tax regime** and calculate whether the combined deductions (80EEB + 80C + 80D + 24b etc.) make the old regime better than the new regime's lower tax rates.

For someone paying large EMIs on both a home loan and an EV loan, old regime is often significantly better.

## How to Claim in ITR

1. Get a **loan interest certificate** from your bank/NBFC (typically issued annually)
2. File using **ITR-1** (if only salary + one house property) or **ITR-2/3**
3. Go to **Schedule VIA** (Chapter VI-A deductions)
4. Enter amount under **Section 80EEB**
5. Maximum entry: ₹1,50,000 (system caps at this automatically in most portals)

## Section 80EEB for Business Use

This deduction is **for individuals only**. If you use an EV for business:
- You cannot claim 80EEB (which is a personal income tax deduction)
- Instead, you can claim the **interest as a business expense** against business income (under Section 37)
- Depreciation on the EV is also available as a business expense

If the EV is used partly for business and partly for personal use, you may need to split the interest between 80EEB (personal portion) and business expense deduction.

## Loan Must Be from Approved Lender

The loan must be from:
- Scheduled commercial banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, etc.)
- Cooperative banks
- RBI-registered NBFCs (Bajaj Finance, Tata Capital, etc.)
- Any other notified financial institution

**Dealer financing**: If the EV dealership arranged financing through a registered NBFC, it qualifies. Verify the NBFC is RBI-registered.

**Informal loans from relatives or friends**: Do NOT qualify for 80EEB, even if interest is genuinely paid.

## Expired for New Loans but Still Active for Existing Ones

The March 31, 2023 deadline was for **new loan sanctions**. If you took a 5-year EV loan in January 2023, you can claim 80EEB deduction every year until the loan is repaid (i.e., until January 2028).

If you're buying an EV today (2026) with a new loan, the loan does NOT qualify for 80EEB — the window has closed for new loans.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I bought an electric scooter in 2021 with a loan from Bajaj Finance. Can I claim 80EEB?

Yes — Bajaj Finance is an RBI-registered NBFC, the loan was sanctioned within the April 2019 – March 2023 window, and electric scooters are eligible vehicles. Claim the interest paid in the year in your ITR under 80EEB.

### I have an EV loan but I'm under the new tax regime. Can I switch back to old regime?

Salaried employees (who don't have business income) can switch between old and new regime every year. If 80EEB + other deductions make old regime more beneficial, choose old regime when filing your ITR for that year. Make sure to inform your employer before April (for TDS planning) — but final choice is made when filing ITR.

### My loan is jointly held with my spouse. Can both claim 80EEB?

Each individual co-borrower can claim 80EEB to the extent of their interest share. If you each pay 50% of the EMI interest, you can each claim 50% deduction (subject to ₹1.5L cap per person each). Get a loan interest certificate showing the split if it's a joint loan.

### The government announced 80EEB extension to 2025 — is that true?

The Budget 2022 extended the original March 2022 deadline to March 2023. There has been no further extension announced for new loan sanctions. Existing loan holders (sanctioned before March 2023) can continue claiming. Watch Budget 2024/2025 announcements for any further extensions.
