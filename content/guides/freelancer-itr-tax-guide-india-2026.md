# Freelancer Tax & ITR Filing India 2026 — Complete Guide

Freelancers, consultants, and independent professionals are treated differently from salaried employees in India's tax law. The good news: Section 44ADA presumptive taxation drastically simplifies tax filing. The bad news: you're responsible for advance tax, GST, and self-managed compliance that employers handle for salaried workers.

---

## Which ITR Form Should Freelancers File?

| Your Situation | Form |
|---------------|------|
| Freelancer/consultant under Section 44ADA (presumptive) | **ITR-4 (Sugam)** |
| Freelancer with actual books of accounts (not presumptive) | **ITR-3** |
| Freelancer who also has capital gains (mutual funds, stocks) | **ITR-3** |
| Salaried + freelancing income combined | **ITR-3** |

**Most freelancers use ITR-4** under the presumptive scheme — it's simpler and requires no detailed P&L.

---

## Section 44ADA — The Freelancer's Tax Superpower

Section 44ADA allows **presumptive taxation for professionals**:

**Who qualifies**: Doctors, lawyers, architects, engineers, accountants, technical consultants, interior designers, and ANY profession recognized by the CBDT.

**How it works**:
- Declare **50% of gross receipts as profit** — no expense tracking, no books of accounts needed
- Pay tax on that 50% profit at applicable slab rates

**Gross receipts limit (FY 2025-26)**:
- Up to ₹75 lakh: Can use 44ADA
- Up to ₹3.75 crore: Can use 44ADA if 95%+ receipts are digital/banking channels

**Example**:
- Gross freelancing receipts: ₹30L
- 44ADA profit: 50% = ₹15L
- Tax (new regime): On ₹15L as per slab

**You can declare more than 50% profit** if you want — there's no rule against it. But you cannot declare less than 50% under 44ADA.

## Tax Slabs on Freelancer Income (FY 2025-26)

**New Tax Regime (Default)**:

| Income Slab | Rate |
|-------------|------|
| Up to ₹4L | 0% |
| ₹4L–₹8L | 5% |
| ₹8L–₹12L | 10% |
| ₹12L–₹16L | 15% |
| ₹16L–₹20L | 20% |
| ₹20L–₹24L | 25% |
| Above ₹24L | 30% |

**Rebate Section 87A**: If total income ≤ ₹12L, tax = ₹0 (rebate wipes it out). For salaried + freelancer, the combined income applies.

**Old regime**: Still optable, but allows deductions (80C, 80D, home loan interest, etc.). Compare both regimes to see which is better for you.

## GST for Freelancers

### When is GST Registration Mandatory?

| Situation | Threshold |
|-----------|----------|
| Providing services in India | ₹20 lakh/year (₹10 lakh for hill/NE states) |
| Exporting services to foreign clients | Mandatory regardless of amount (for LUT filing) |

**Key exception**: Services exported outside India (paid in foreign currency) can be zero-rated under GST — you don't charge GST to foreign clients, and you file a **Letter of Undertaking (LUT)** to export without paying GST.

### GST Rates on Freelancer Services

| Service | GST Rate |
|---------|---------|
| Software development, IT services | 18% |
| Design, content writing, consulting | 18% |
| Professional services (legal, CA, doctor) | 18% |
| Export of services (foreign client pays in forex) | 0% (zero-rated with LUT) |

### GST Registration Process

1. Go to [gst.gov.in](https://www.gst.gov.in) → New Registration → Part A (enter name, PAN, mobile, email) → OTP verify → Part B (documents upload)
2. Documents: PAN, Aadhaar, bank account, address proof, photograph
3. Gets you GSTIN within 7 working days
4. File GSTR-1 (sales returns) monthly/quarterly + GSTR-3B (payment return) monthly

### GST on Foreign Clients (Export Rule)

If you invoice a US/UK/Singapore company and receive payment in foreign currency:
- **Declare as export of services**
- Charge 0% GST (not 18%)
- File LUT (Letter of Undertaking) annually at GST portal under the "LUT" tab — free, instant
- No GST collected from foreign client; Input Tax Credit refund possible on your purchases

**LUT filing is annual** — file it at the start of each financial year (April) to export zero-rated throughout the year.

## Advance Tax — Freelancers Must Pay This

Unlike salaried employees (where TDS covers most tax), freelancers must pay advance tax quarterly:

| Installment | Due Date | % of Total Tax |
|-------------|----------|---------------|
| 1st | June 15 | 15% |
| 2nd | September 15 | 45% (cumulative) |
| 3rd | December 15 | 75% (cumulative) |
| 4th | March 15 | 100% (cumulative) |

**How to calculate advance tax**:
1. Estimate annual income for the year
2. Calculate tax on that income (minus expected TDS from clients)
3. Pay the percentage by each due date via incometax.gov.in → e-Pay Tax → Advance Tax (Challan 280)

**Penalty for not paying**: 1% per month interest under Section 234B and 234C.

## TDS on Freelance Payments — Section 194J

Companies deducting TDS from your payment:
- **Section 194J**: TDS at **10%** on professional fees (consulting, technical services, legal fees)
- **Section 194C**: TDS at **2%** on contractor payments (work contracts, printing, transport)

**Form 26AS**: All TDS deducted by your clients appears in your Form 26AS. You claim this as advance tax paid when filing ITR.

**For ITR filing**: Tax liability = Total tax on income − TDS deducted − Advance tax paid. If TDS + advance tax > liability, you get a refund.

## How to File ITR-4 as a Freelancer

### Step 1: Collect Your Income Details

- Total receipts from all clients (check all invoices + Form 26AS for TDS deductions)
- Foreign income (from foreign clients, if any) — show in forex + INR equivalent at RBI rate

### Step 2: Calculate Presumptive Income

Under 44ADA: Profit = 50% of gross receipts (or more if you choose).

### Step 3: Go to incometax.gov.in

Login → e-File → Income Tax Returns → File Income Tax Return → Select FY → Select ITR-4.

### Step 4: Fill ITR-4 Sections

- **Personal information**: Name, PAN, address
- **Income details**: Gross receipts → 44ADA profit figure
- **TDS credits**: Pre-filled from Form 26AS — verify all entries
- **Taxes paid**: Advance tax paid (Challan 280 entries)
- **Tax calculation**: System calculates based on new/old regime selection

### Step 5: e-Verify

After filing, verify via Aadhaar OTP (easiest), net banking, or EVC. Non-verified return is invalid.

## Multiple Clients — Practical Tax Tips

### 1. Separate Bank Account for Freelance Income

Keep a separate current/savings account for all client payments. Makes income calculation and bank statement preparation far cleaner during ITR filing.

### 2. Invoice in INR for Domestic, Invoice in USD/GBP for Foreign

For foreign clients: Invoice in forex, receive in an exporter's account or regular savings account. Show as export of services in GSTR-1 for zero-rated GST.

### 3. Lower Deduction Certificate (LDC) if TDS is High

If clients deduct 10% TDS but your actual tax rate is lower (e.g., you're at 5% slab), you can get a **Lower Deduction Certificate** from your income tax officer (Form 13 at incometax.gov.in) — allows clients to deduct TDS at a lower rate, improving your cash flow.

### 4. PF / EPF for Self-Employed

Self-employed have no EPF. Alternative: **NPS (National Pension System)** via Tier 1 — tax deduction under 80CCD(1B): ₹50K extra deduction in old regime. Voluntary option.

## Switching Between 44ADA and Regular Books

| Regime | Condition |
|--------|----------|
| 44ADA (presumptive) | Gross receipts ≤ ₹75L; declare 50% profit |
| Regular books (ITR-3) | If receipts > ₹75L, or you want to declare actual profit < 50% |

**Important**: If you opt for 44ADA, you must use it for 5 consecutive years. If you switch to regular taxation (e.g., gross > ₹75L), you cannot go back to 44ADA for 5 years.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I'm a software freelancer for US clients. Do I pay GST?

If you're providing software services to foreign companies and receive payment in foreign currency: zero-rated GST (0% on exports). File LUT at gst.gov.in at start of April each year. No GST collected from client, no GST liability in India.

### My annual income from freelancing is ₹8 lakh. Do I have any tax?

Under 44ADA: 50% of ₹8L = ₹4L profit. Under new tax regime: income up to ₹4L has 0% tax. Tax = nil. Even in old regime with 80C deduction of ₹1.5L, you'd be at minimal tax.

### I'm salaried and do some freelancing on the side. Which ITR form?

If using 44ADA for the freelancing income and salary income: ITR-4 accommodates both. If you have capital gains too: use ITR-3.

### Do I need a CA to file ITR as a freelancer?

Under 44ADA, filing is straightforward enough for self-filing at incometax.gov.in. A CA is useful if: you have complex foreign income, significant TDS mismatch, or want to compare old vs new regime properly. Not mandatory.

### My client paid me but didn't deduct TDS. Is that my problem?

No — TDS deduction is the client's obligation. You still owe income tax on the income regardless. But you can't be penalized for a client's TDS non-deduction. Pay advance tax to cover it.
