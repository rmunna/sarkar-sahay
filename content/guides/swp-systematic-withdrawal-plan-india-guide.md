# SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan) India 2026 — Complete Guide

SWP is the mirror image of SIP: instead of investing every month, you *withdraw* a fixed amount every month from your mutual fund corpus. It's how retirees create a monthly salary from a lump sum — more tax-efficient and better return-adjusted than an FD or annuity for most situations.

---

## What is SWP?

A Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) lets you set a fixed monthly/quarterly withdrawal from any mutual fund. The AMC redeems just enough units to give you that amount, deposited directly to your bank account.

| Feature | SWP |
|---------|-----|
| Investment | One-time lump sum (corpus) |
| Withdrawal | Fixed amount, chosen frequency |
| Units redeemed | Only as many as needed to cover the withdrawal |
| Remaining corpus | Stays invested, continues to grow |
| Tax | Only on the *gain* portion of each withdrawal (not principal) |

## SWP Calculator — How Long Does Your Corpus Last?

The math: if your corpus grows at X% while you withdraw Y%, the corpus survives indefinitely. If withdrawal rate > growth rate, corpus decays.

**Safe Withdrawal Rate for India**: 6–8% of corpus per year (to be conservative given inflation).

| Corpus | Monthly Withdrawal | Assumed CAGR | How Long It Lasts |
|--------|-------------------|--------------|-------------------|
| ₹25 lakh | ₹15,000 | 10% | 30+ years |
| ₹50 lakh | ₹25,000 | 10% | Forever (grows) |
| ₹50 lakh | ₹40,000 | 10% | ~22 years |
| ₹1 crore | ₹50,000 | 10% | Forever (grows) |
| ₹1 crore | ₹80,000 | 10% | ~22 years |
| ₹2 crore | ₹1,00,000 | 10% | Forever |

**Rule of thumb**: Monthly SWP should be ≤ 0.6% of corpus per month (7.2% per year) to leave room for inflation adjustment and market volatility.

## SWP vs FD: Which Is Better for Retirement Income?

| Feature | FD Interest | SWP (Equity/Hybrid Fund) |
|---------|-------------|--------------------------|
| Rate | 6.5–7.5% p.a. | 10–12% CAGR (long-term) |
| Tax | Taxable as income at slab rate | Only *gain* portion taxed at LTCG rate (12.5%) |
| For 30% bracket (₹50L) | Pay ~30% tax on ₹3.5L interest = ₹1.05L tax/year | Pay ~12.5% on gain portion only — much less |
| Inflation protection | None (fixed interest) | Equity grows with economy |
| Capital growth | Zero — you get interest, capital doesn't grow | Remaining corpus continues compounding |
| TDS | Yes, 10% TDS on FD interest above ₹40K/year | None (you file LTCG in ITR yourself) |
| Flexibility | Breaking FD = penalty | Redeem anytime (no penalty beyond exit load) |

**Conclusion for retirees in higher tax brackets**: SWP from a Balanced Advantage or Equity Savings fund is significantly more tax-efficient than FD interest income. For those in the 5–10% slab, FD is simpler and still valid.

## Tax on SWP Withdrawals

**Each SWP withdrawal has two components**:
1. **Principal return** — your original investment coming back → NOT taxable
2. **Capital gain** — profit on units redeemed → taxable

**For equity funds (>65% equity)**:
- Units held >12 months: LTCG @ 12.5% (above ₹1.25L cumulative gains per year)
- Units held <12 months: STCG @ 20%

**Practical example**: You invest ₹50L in a balanced advantage fund. After 2 years, you start SWP of ₹30,000/month. Each month, the fund redeems units. Say each ₹30K withdrawal = ₹24K principal returned + ₹6K gain. You owe LTCG tax only on ₹6K × 12 = ₹72K/year — well below ₹1.25L exemption limit. Tax = zero.

**FIFO method**: Mutual funds use First In First Out (FIFO) — oldest units redeemed first. If you held those units for 12+ months, withdrawals are taxed at LTCG rates (lower).

## Best Funds for SWP — Which Category to Use

| Fund Category | Why Use for SWP | Risk |
|--------------|-----------------|------|
| **Balanced Advantage Fund** (BAF) | Auto-adjusts equity/debt ratio; smoother returns; less volatility than pure equity | Medium |
| **Equity Savings Fund** | 65%+ equity (LTCG treatment) but lower volatility than flexi-cap | Low-Medium |
| **Conservative Hybrid Fund** | 25–40% equity + debt; stable; good for very conservative retirees | Low |
| **Nifty 50 Index Fund** | Best long-term returns; higher short-term volatility | Medium-High |
| **Short Duration Debt Fund** | For corpus parked while doing SWP; very stable | Very Low |

**Recommended SWP structure for retirees**:
- Keep 2 years of SWP amount in liquid/short-duration debt fund (buffer for market falls)
- Keep rest in balanced advantage or equity fund (growth engine)
- SWP from debt fund; rebalance annually from equity to debt

## How to Set Up SWP — Step by Step

### On Groww

1. Open Groww → Portfolio → select the fund
2. Tap "Withdraw" → Choose "SWP (Systematic Withdrawal)"
3. Set amount, date (1st to 28th), frequency (monthly/quarterly)
4. Enter bank account (pre-linked)
5. Confirm via OTP

**Groww SWP minimum**: ₹500/month (varies by fund; check before setting up).

### On Zerodha Coin

1. Open Coin → Portfolio → tap the fund
2. Go to "Withdraw" → "SWP"
3. Set amount, start date, frequency
4. Link bank account if not already done
5. Submit — SWP starts from chosen date

**Zerodha SWP**: Supported for most direct funds. Coin processes redemptions at end-of-day NAV.

### On MF Central (mfcentral.com)

MF Central is the industry-standard portal managed by CAMS + KFintech. Works for all AMCs:

1. Login at mfcentral.com using PAN + OTP
2. Go to Transactions → SWP / Systematic Withdrawal
3. Choose fund and folio
4. Set amount, start date, frequency
5. Confirm — units auto-redeemed each period

### Directly with AMC

Every AMC (SBI MF, HDFC AMC, ICICI Prudential, Mirae, etc.) allows SWP directly on their app/website. If you have a large corpus with one AMC, their app is cleanest.

## SWP for Different Life Stages

### Retiree (60+, ₹1 Cr corpus)

- Keep ₹10L in liquid fund (2 years' SWP buffer)
- ₹90L in Balanced Advantage Fund
- SWP: ₹40,000/month from liquid fund
- Every December: transfer ₹4.8L from BAF to liquid fund (refill buffer)

### Pre-retirement (55–60, building corpus)

- Don't start SWP yet — let corpus compound
- Shift from pure equity → balanced advantage 3 years before retirement

### Wealth drawdown (accumulated large corpus, 40–55)

- If financial independence achieved with ₹3–5 Cr corpus
- SWP of ₹1–1.5L/month from equity/BAF funds
- 4% rule: ₹3Cr corpus → ₹12L/year (₹1L/month) with high survival probability

## Common SWP Mistakes to Avoid

**Mistake 1: Starting SWP in a pure equity fund during market peak**
Small-cap/mid-cap funds can fall 40% in a downturn. If SWP is running during a crash, you're selling at the bottom and depleting corpus fast.
*Fix*: Use Balanced Advantage or Equity Savings fund, which have built-in downside protection.

**Mistake 2: SWP amount too high relative to corpus**
If withdrawing 12% of corpus annually but fund returns 10%, corpus erodes by 2% per year — plus inflation erodes purchasing power.
*Fix*: Keep SWP below 7% of corpus per year.

**Mistake 3: Not accounting for exit load**
Most equity funds have 1% exit load if units are sold within 12 months. If you start SWP within 12 months of lump sum investment, early withdrawals attract exit load.
*Fix*: Wait 12 months after investing before starting SWP. Or split investment across 12 months before starting SWP.

**Mistake 4: Keeping everything in one fund**
Market falls affect all equity funds simultaneously. If 100% in one equity fund and SWP is running, you're forced to redeem at low NAV.
*Fix*: Maintain 1–2 years of SWP amount in liquid/short-duration debt as buffer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I pause or change my SWP amount?

Yes — SWP can be paused, modified, or stopped anytime through your fund platform. No penalty for changing frequency or amount.

### Is SWP income taxed as salary?

No — SWP redemptions are treated as capital gains, not income. Only the *gain portion* (not the full withdrawal) is taxed as LTCG or STCG depending on holding period.

### What if my fund NAV falls during SWP?

More units are redeemed to give you the same amount (since NAV is lower). This depletes your unit count faster during market downturns — which is why having a debt buffer is important.

### Can NRIs use SWP?

Yes — NRIs can have SWP from NRE or NRO folios. Tax deduction at source (TDS) applies to NRI redemptions — 20% (or DTAA rate). NRIs should check repatriation rules for NRO funds.

### What is the minimum SWP amount?

Varies by fund. Most equity funds: ₹500–₹1,000 minimum per withdrawal. Check the fund's SID (Scheme Information Document) for exact minimum.
