# JEE Advanced Exam Guide — IIT Entrance Exam

JEE Advanced is the gateway to India's most prestigious engineering institutions — the Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs). Every year, lakhs of students dream of cracking this exam. This guide covers everything you need to know about JEE Advanced — eligibility, pattern, syllabus, preparation tips and the counselling process.

---

## What is JEE Advanced?

JEE Advanced (Joint Entrance Examination Advanced) is a national-level entrance exam conducted for admission to **23 IITs** and other premier institutes like **IISc Bangalore**, **IISER**, and **IIST**. It is organised by one of the seven zonal IITs on a rotational basis.

**Key facts:**

- Only students who qualify **JEE Main** can appear for JEE Advanced
- It is a **computer-based test (CBT)** held in two papers (Paper 1 and Paper 2)
- Both papers are **compulsory**
- Approximately **2.5 lakh** top JEE Main qualifiers are eligible each year

JEE Advanced is widely regarded as one of the toughest undergraduate entrance exams in the world.

---

## Eligibility for JEE Advanced

To appear for JEE Advanced, you must satisfy **all** of the following criteria:

### 1. JEE Main Qualification
You must be among the **top 2,50,000 candidates** (across all categories) in JEE Main 2026.

### 2. Age Limit
- Candidates should have been born on or after **1st October 2001** (general category)
- **5-year relaxation** for SC, ST and PwD candidates

### 3. Number of Attempts
- A candidate can attempt JEE Advanced a **maximum of 2 times** in two consecutive years
- You must have first appeared for Class 12 (or equivalent) in 2025 or 2026

### 4. Class 12 Qualification
- Must have passed Class 12 (or equivalent) in **2025 or 2026** with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics
- Must meet the **minimum marks** requirement set by the respective IIT (typically 75% aggregate or top 20 percentile)

### 5. No Prior Admission to IIT
- Candidates who have previously accepted a seat at any IIT are **not eligible** (with some exceptions for seat upgrade)

> **Note:** Category-wise relaxations apply for SC, ST, OBC-NCL and PwD candidates. Always check the [official JEE Advanced website](https://jeeadv.ac.in) for the latest criteria.

## JEE Advanced Exam Pattern

JEE Advanced consists of **two papers**, both compulsory:

| Feature | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Duration** | 3 hours | 3 hours |
| **Subjects** | Physics, Chemistry, Maths | Physics, Chemistry, Maths |
| **Mode** | Computer-based (CBT) | Computer-based (CBT) |
| **Question Types** | MCQ (single/multiple correct), Integer type, Matching type | MCQ (single/multiple correct), Integer type, Matching type |

### Marking Scheme
- **Single correct MCQs:** +3 for correct, −1 for wrong
- **Multiple correct MCQs:** Partial marking applies — +4 for all correct, partial credit for partially correct, −2 for wrong
- **Integer/Numerical type:** +4 for correct, 0 for wrong (no negative marking)

> The exact pattern may vary slightly each year. The organising IIT releases the detailed pattern in the official information brochure.

**Total marks** are typically around **360** (180 per paper), but this can change.

## JEE Advanced Syllabus

The syllabus broadly covers **Class 11 and 12 Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics** as per the NCERT curriculum, with additional depth:

### Physics
- Mechanics (kinematics, laws of motion, work-energy, rotational motion)
- Electrostatics and current electricity
- Magnetism and electromagnetic induction
- Optics (ray and wave optics)
- Modern physics (atomic structure, nuclear physics, photoelectric effect)
- Thermodynamics and kinetic theory
- Waves and oscillations

### Chemistry
- **Physical Chemistry:** Mole concept, thermodynamics, equilibrium, electrochemistry, kinetics
- **Organic Chemistry:** General organic chemistry, hydrocarbons, functional groups, biomolecules
- **Inorganic Chemistry:** Periodic table, coordination compounds, metallurgy, s/p/d/f block elements

### Mathematics
- Algebra (complex numbers, quadratic equations, sequences, permutations)
- Calculus (limits, differentiation, integration, differential equations)
- Coordinate geometry (straight lines, circles, conics)
- Trigonometry
- Vectors and 3D geometry
- Probability and statistics

> Download the detailed syllabus from [jeeadv.ac.in](https://jeeadv.ac.in).

## How to Apply for JEE Advanced

**Step 1:** Qualify JEE Main 2026 and be in the top 2,50,000 rank list.

**Step 2:** Register on the [JEE Advanced official portal](https://jeeadv.ac.in) during the registration window (usually in April–May).

**Step 3:** Fill in personal, academic and category details.

**Step 4:** Upload required documents — photograph, signature, category certificate (if applicable).

**Step 5:** Pay the application fee online.
- General/OBC: ₹2,800 (approx.)
- SC/ST/PwD: ₹1,400 (approx.)
- Female candidates (all categories): ₹1,400 (approx.)

**Step 6:** Download your admit card from the portal before the exam date.

> **Exam date:** JEE Advanced is expected in **June 2026**. Check official notifications for confirmed dates.

## Preparation Strategy for JEE Advanced

### 1. Master NCERT First
Start with NCERT textbooks for Class 11 and 12. They form the foundation, especially for Chemistry.

### 2. Use Standard Reference Books
- **Physics:** H.C. Verma (Concepts of Physics), Irodov for advanced problems
- **Chemistry:** O.P. Tandon (Physical), Morrison & Boyd (Organic), J.D. Lee (Inorganic)
- **Mathematics:** R.D. Sharma, S.L. Loney (Trigonometry), I.A. Maron (Calculus)

### 3. Solve Previous Year Papers
JEE Advanced questions often repeat concepts. Solving **10–15 years of past papers** is the single best strategy.

### 4. Take Mock Tests Regularly
Simulate real exam conditions — 3-hour papers, no breaks. Analyse mistakes after every test.

### 5. Focus on Conceptual Clarity
JEE Advanced tests deep understanding, not rote learning. Focus on **why** a concept works, not just the formula.

### 6. Time Management
- Allocate fixed hours daily to each subject
- Practice finishing sections within time limits
- Don't spend too long on any single question during the exam

### 7. Revision is Key
Keep short notes and formula sheets. Revise weekly. The last month before the exam should be **mostly revision and mock tests**.

## IIT Counselling — JoSAA Process

After JEE Advanced results, seat allocation to IITs happens through **JoSAA (Joint Seat Allocation Authority)**.

### How JoSAA Works:
1. **Registration:** Register on [josaa.nic.in](https://josaa.nic.in) after results
2. **Choice filling:** Select your preferred IIT + branch combinations (you can fill up to hundreds of choices)
3. **Seat allocation:** JoSAA runs **multiple rounds** (usually 5–6) of seat allotment based on rank, category and preferences
4. **Accept/Freeze/Float:**
   - **Accept:** Take the seat, done
   - **Float:** Accept current seat but stay in the system for upgrades in later rounds
   - **Freeze:** Accept current seat and exit the process
5. **Document verification:** Report to the allotted institute with original documents
6. **Fee payment:** Pay the seat acceptance fee to confirm admission

### Participating Institutes:
- 23 IITs
- 32 NITs
- IISERs, IIST and other GFTIs

> **Tip:** Fill as many choices as possible. Use the "Float" option wisely to get a better branch in later rounds.

## Important Tips for JEE Advanced

1. **Start early** — Begin serious preparation from Class 11 itself. JEE Advanced covers Class 11 topics heavily.

2. **Don't ignore any subject** — All three subjects carry equal weight. A weak subject can pull your rank down significantly.

3. **Practise numerical/integer-type questions** — These carry no negative marking and can boost your score.

4. **Join a test series** — Platforms like Allen, FIITJEE, and Vedantu offer excellent mock test series that simulate the actual exam.

5. **Stay healthy** — Maintain a proper sleep schedule, exercise regularly, and eat well. Burnout is real.

6. **Read questions carefully** — JEE Advanced is known for tricky wording. Misreading a question can cost precious marks.

7. **Know the marking scheme** — Understand partial marking for multiple-correct questions. Sometimes attempting partially is better than skipping.

8. **Keep official dates bookmarked** — Missing the registration window means losing a year. Set reminders.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Q1: Can I appear for JEE Advanced without clearing JEE Main?
**No.** You must qualify JEE Main and be in the top 2,50,000 candidates to be eligible for JEE Advanced.

### Q2: How many times can I attempt JEE Advanced?
You can attempt JEE Advanced a **maximum of 2 times** in consecutive years.

### Q3: Is JEE Advanced harder than JEE Main?
**Yes.** JEE Advanced tests deeper conceptual understanding and has more complex question formats like multiple-correct and integer-type questions.

### Q4: What rank do I need in JEE Advanced to get into a top IIT?
This varies by branch and category. For popular branches like **Computer Science at IIT Bombay**, you typically need a rank under **100**. Less popular branches at newer IITs may admit ranks up to **8,000–10,000**.

### Q5: Is there any reservation in JEE Advanced?
**Yes.** Reservation is applicable as per Government of India norms — SC (15%), ST (7.5%), OBC-NCL (27%), EWS (10%) and PwD (5% horizontal).

### Q6: Can I get admission to NITs through JEE Advanced?
NIT admissions are through **JEE Main** ranks, not JEE Advanced. However, JoSAA counselling covers both IITs and NITs in a single process.

### Q7: What is the exam fee for JEE Advanced?
Approximately ₹2,800 for general/OBC candidates and ₹1,400 for SC/ST/PwD/female candidates. Fees are updated annually.
