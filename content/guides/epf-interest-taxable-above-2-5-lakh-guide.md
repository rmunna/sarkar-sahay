# EPF Interest Taxability India 2026 — Budget 2021 Changes, ₹2.5L Threshold, and ITR Declaration

EPF (Employee Provident Fund) had always been a triple-exempt (EEE) investment — contributions deductible under 80C, interest earned tax-free, and maturity tax-free. **Budget 2021 changed this** for high earners, making interest on contributions above ₹2.5 lakh taxable.

Here's what changed, who it affects, and how to declare in ITR.

## The Budget 2021 Change — What Exactly Changed

**Before Budget 2021 (up to FY 2020-21)**:
- All EPF interest: Tax-free (regardless of contribution amount)

**After Budget 2021 (from FY 2021-22 onwards)**:
- Interest on employee EPF contribution up to **₹2.5 lakh**: Still tax-free
- Interest on employee EPF contribution **above ₹2.5 lakh**: **Taxable at slab rate**
- For **government employees**: Higher threshold of **₹5 lakh** (because government employees don't get employer PF contribution in the same way)

**Important**: This is on the **employee's own contribution** only (not employer's contribution). The employer's 12% contribution has separate tax rules.

## Who Is Affected?

The ₹2.5 lakh annual threshold in employee EPF contribution means:

| Monthly Employee PF Contribution | Annual Total | Taxable? |
|----------------------------------|--------------|---------|
| ₹20,833/month or below | ₹2,50,000 or below | ❌ Not taxable |
| Above ₹20,833/month | Above ₹2,50,000 | ✅ Taxable (on interest for excess portion) |

**Monthly PF contribution = 12% of Basic salary**. So ₹20,833/month employee PF = ₹1,73,608/month basic salary.

This primarily affects **high earners** with basic salary above ~₹1.7 lakh per month. For most salaried employees, EPF interest remains tax-free.

## How the Taxable Interest Is Calculated

EPFO maintains two sub-accounts in your EPF:
1. **Taxable sub-account**: Tracks interest on contributions above ₹2.5L threshold
2. **Non-taxable sub-account**: Tracks interest on contributions up to ₹2.5L

You don't do this calculation — EPFO does it. The interest split is reflected in your EPFO passbook and the Form 26AS/AIS from FY 2023-24 onwards.

**Simplified example**:
- Employee monthly PF contribution: ₹30,000
- Annual employee PF: ₹3,60,000
- Threshold: ₹2,50,000
- Excess: ₹1,10,000 (contribution above threshold)
- Interest rate: 8.15% (FY 2024-25)
- Taxable interest = 8.15% × ₹1,10,000 = **₹8,965** (taxable at slab rate)

Note: The taxable portion accumulates over years. By Year 5 of being above the threshold, the taxable sub-account is larger.

## Employer Contribution Taxability — Separate Rule

**Budget 2020** (earlier change): Employer contributions above certain limits also became taxable:

| Component | Annual Employer Contribution | Taxability |
|-----------|------------------------------|-----------|
| Employer EPF (12% of Basic) | Up to ₹7.5L combined (EPF + NPS + Gratuity) | Tax-free |
| Employer EPF (excess) | Above ₹7.5L aggregate with NPS+Gratuity | Taxable as perquisite |
| Interest on taxable employer PF | On excess above ₹7.5L | Also taxable |

This affects employees where employer PF + NPS + gratuity contributions combined exceed ₹7.5L/year — typically very senior executives.

## Voluntary PF (VPF) — Same Rules Apply

If you contribute to **Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF)** (extra contribution above mandatory 12%), VPF is also counted as employee contribution:

- EPF (12%) + VPF contribution combined = total employee contribution
- If total > ₹2.5L: interest on excess is taxable

Many employees use VPF for tax-free returns. The Budget 2021 change now limits this for high earners.

## EPF Withdrawals — Still Tax-Free After 5 Years

The Budget 2021 change affects **interest income** during accumulation, not the withdrawal rules:

| Withdrawal Situation | Tax Treatment |
|---------------------|--------------|
| EPF withdrawal after 5 years of continuous service | Fully tax-free (EEE still applies for accumulated corpus) |
| EPF withdrawal before 5 years | Taxable (employer contribution + interest portion) |
| EPF at maturity/retirement | Non-taxable sub-account: tax-free; Taxable sub-account: 10% TDS by EPFO |

**The taxable sub-account at maturity**: EPFO deducts 10% TDS when you withdraw from the taxable sub-account at retirement/exit. You may file ITR to claim refund if actual slab rate is lower (5%) or to pay the difference if higher (20%, 30%).

## How to Declare EPF Taxable Interest in ITR

From FY 2023-24, the taxable EPF interest appears in your **AIS (Annual Information Statement)** under "Interest on PF" — automatically populated.

In your ITR:
1. Taxable EPF interest → **Schedule OS (Income from Other Sources)**
2. Under "Interest income" — add it along with FD interest and savings bank interest
3. It's taxed at your slab rate (no special rate)

If EPFO deducted 10% TDS on the taxable interest (at maturity/withdrawal), it appears in Form 26AS and you claim TDS credit in ITR.

## Is EPF Still a Good Investment?

Despite the Budget 2021 change, EPF remains highly tax-efficient for most employees:

- **Employee contribution up to ₹2.5L**: Still fully EEE (deductible under 80C, interest tax-free, maturity tax-free)
- **Return**: 8.15% (FY 2024-25) — higher than most risk-free instruments
- **For those above ₹2.5L threshold**: Interest on excess is taxable, but 8.15% pre-tax is still competitive vs FDs at lower post-tax returns

The change mainly affects those earning >₹1.7L/month basic who were putting everything into VPF for tax-free returns. For most salaried employees, EPF is unaffected.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My basic salary is ₹80,000/month and employer puts 12% into EPF. Am I affected?

Employee PF contribution = 12% × ₹80,000 = ₹9,600/month = ₹1,15,200/year. This is well below ₹2.5L. You are NOT affected by the Budget 2021 change. All your EPF interest remains tax-free.

### I contribute extra VPF of ₹20,000/month. When is my total over ₹2.5L?

Your EPF = 12% of basic. Add VPF. If combined > ₹20,833/month, your annual contribution crosses ₹2.5L.
Example: Basic = ₹1,50,000/month → EPF = ₹18,000 + VPF ₹20,000 = ₹38,000/month = ₹4,56,000/year → Excess = ₹2,06,000. Interest on that excess is taxable.

### My company shows EPF interest in my Form 16. Is that the taxable part?

If your Form 16 shows EPF interest in "Gross Salary" or "Perquisites" or "Income from Other Sources" section, your employer has already computed and added the taxable EPF interest to your salary. If it's not in Form 16, check your AIS on the IT portal — EPFO reports the taxable interest separately from FY 2023-24.

### Is EPF maturity amount at retirement (age 58) now taxable because of this rule?

No — the maturity corpus is still tax-free if you've completed 5 years of service. The Budget 2021 rule only makes the **annual interest credited** to the taxable sub-account an income in that year. The corpus in the taxable sub-account itself was created from after-tax contributions plus taxable interest — so at withdrawal, EPFO applies 10% TDS but there's no separate maturity tax as such.
