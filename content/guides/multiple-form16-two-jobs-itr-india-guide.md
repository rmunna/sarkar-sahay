# Multiple Form 16 / Two Jobs India 2026 — How to File ITR Correctly

If you changed jobs during the financial year or had two jobs simultaneously, you'll receive Form 16 from each employer. Filing only one is under-reporting income — the Income Tax Department can see total TDS deducted across employers in Form 26AS and will issue a mismatch notice. Here's the correct way.

---

## Why You Have Two Form 16s

**Scenario 1: Job change during the year**
Left Employer A in November, joined Employer B in December. Both deducted some TDS. Both issue Form 16 for the salary they paid you.

**Scenario 2: Two simultaneous jobs (moonlighting)**
Primary job + freelance/consulting with a company that issues salary. Both employers issue Form 16. Note: if the second "job" is freelancing where you invoice, that income goes in ITR under business income, not salary — only if there's a formal employment contract and TDS deducted under Section 192.

**Scenario 3: Internship + job**
Short stint with a company that deducted TDS + main job. Two Form 16s.

In all cases: **you must combine all salary income and declare total in ITR**.

---

## The Risk of Filing Only One Form 16

The Income Tax Department cross-checks:
- **Form 26AS**: Shows ALL TDS deducted on your PAN by every employer
- **AIS (Annual Information Statement)**: Shows all income/transactions on your PAN

If your ITR shows ₹8L salary (one employer) but Form 26AS shows TDS from two employers suggesting ₹14L total salary → **Section 143(1)(a) mismatch notice** is auto-generated.

**Resolution** if you ignore it: Demand notice for unpaid tax + 12% interest per annum + possible penalty.

**Easy fix**: File correctly with both Form 16s from the start.

## How to Combine Two Form 16s — Step by Step

### Step 1: Collect Both Form 16s

Each Form 16 has two parts:
- **Part A**: TDS details (from employer, generated via TRACES)
- **Part B**: Salary breakup (gross salary, allowances, deductions)

Verify Part A of both Form 16s against your Form 26AS (available on incometax.gov.in → AIS/26AS).

### Step 2: Calculate Total Taxable Salary

Add the taxable salary from each employer's Form 16 (Part B, Row "Income from Salary").

**Example**:
| Employer | Gross Salary | Allowances Exempt | Taxable Salary |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|----------------|
| Employer A (April–October) | ₹7,00,000 | HRA ₹1,20,000 | ₹5,80,000 |
| Employer B (November–March) | ₹5,00,000 | HRA ₹75,000 | ₹4,25,000 |
| **Total** | **₹12,00,000** | **₹1,95,000** | **₹10,05,000** |

### Step 3: Total TDS (Both Employers)

Add TDS amounts from both Form 16 Part A.

**Common issue**: Each employer calculates TDS assuming their salary is your only salary. Employer A may have deducted TDS at a lower rate not knowing you'll earn more at Employer B.

Result: Total TDS may be **less than what you actually owe** on combined income. You'll need to pay the difference as self-assessment tax before filing ITR.

### Step 4: Apply Deductions Once (Not Twice)

Deductions like 80C (₹1.5L), 80D, HRA are per-person, not per-employer. If Employer A gave you HRA exemption and Employer B also gave HRA exemption, you need to verify the total is not double-counted.

**HRA exemption**: Can claim for the months HRA was actually received. If you received HRA from both employers in overlapping months, you can only claim for one employer's HRA per month (typically the higher one).

**80C**: Both employers may have considered your 80C investments (EPF + ELSS + PPF etc.) when computing TDS. In your ITR, enter 80C once — total eligible investments, capped at ₹1.5L.

**Standard deduction**: ₹50,000 once per year (not per employer). Some employers may give full ₹50K deduction individually — correct this in ITR.

### Step 5: Enter in ITR

**In ITR-1 (Sahaj) or ITR-2**:
- Under "Salary Income": Enter the **combined total taxable salary** from all employers
- In "TDS": Under Schedule TDS1, enter each employer's TDS separately (Employer A's TAN + TDS amount, then Employer B's TAN + TDS amount)
- The system adds them automatically

## Step-by-Step on Income Tax Portal (incometax.gov.in)

1. Login → e-File → Income Tax Returns → File Now → AY 2026-27
2. Select ITR-1 (if only salary + savings income) or ITR-2 (if you have capital gains)
3. Under "Income Sources" → "Salary": If pre-filled, verify both employers appear
4. If only one is pre-filled, click "Add Salary" to manually add the second
5. Enter gross salary, exempt allowances, and net taxable salary from each Form 16
6. Under TDS: Verify both employers' TDS appear (cross-check with Form 26AS)
7. Enter deductions (80C, 80D, etc.) — once, correctly totaled
8. Review tax liability — pay self-assessment tax if any balance tax is due
9. Submit and e-verify

## What If Employer B Didn't Know About Your Employer A Income?

This is the most common issue. Employer B calculated TDS assuming their ₹5L salary is your only income. They gave you full standard deduction, full 80C, and computed TDS at 0% or low rate.

**Result**: You owe more tax than was deducted. Calculate the difference and pay Self-Assessment Tax (SAX) before filing ITR.

**How to inform new employer** (to prevent under-deduction): Submit Form 12B to your new employer within 30 days of joining. This form declares your previous employer's income, helping the new employer correctly calculate TDS for the rest of the year.

**Most employees don't do this** — which is why they get a tax payment surprise at ITR filing time.

## Tax Computation Example (FY 2025-26, New Regime)

**Combined income**: ₹10,05,000 (as above)
**Standard deduction**: ₹75,000 (enhanced limit for FY 25-26)
**Net taxable income**: ₹9,30,000

**New Regime Tax (FY 2025-26)**:
- ₹0–₹4L: 0%
- ₹4L–₹8L: 5% = ₹20,000
- ₹8L–₹9,30,000: 10% = ₹13,000
- **Total tax**: ₹33,000
- Add 4% health + education cess: ₹1,320
- **Total liability**: ₹34,320

**TDS deducted** (example): Employer A ₹15,000 + Employer B ₹5,000 = ₹20,000

**Self-assessment tax to pay**: ₹34,320 − ₹20,000 = **₹14,320** (pay before filing ITR)

## Moonlighting — Second Job at Night

If you have a full-time job AND a second employment contract with another company (both deducting TDS under Section 192) — same treatment as job change. Both Form 16s must be combined.

**Key risk**: If your primary employer's moonlighting policy prohibits it and they see dual employment (which they can check via Form 26AS or new employer IT data), it's an HR issue, not an income tax issue. The IT Department doesn't care about moonlighting — they just want the tax.

For moonlighting via freelance invoices (not employment): Income goes under "Business/Professional Income" in ITR-3/4, not salary. Different tax treatment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My new employer already included previous employer's salary in my Form 16. Do I still need both?

If Employer B included Employer A's salary in their Form 16 (common practice — you submit Form 12B and they account for it), you may only have one comprehensive Form 16. Verify your Form 26AS to confirm both TDS amounts appear. File with the combined number from Employer B's Form 16.

### What if I can't get Form 16 from old employer?

Use Form 26AS + AIS from incometax.gov.in — it shows TDS deducted by each employer. Compute salary from your payslips and bank credits. Form 16 is helpful but not mandatory if you can calculate from payslips.

### I got a notice after filing with only one Form 16. What to do?

File a revised ITR before December 31st (AY deadline) including both employers' income. Pay any additional tax + interest. The notice typically gives 15–30 days to respond. Respond by filing revised return and providing the AY computation.
