# EMI Default or Loan Overdue? What Happens & How to Fix It (2026)

Missing an EMI is stressful — but it's not the end. Whether you missed one EMI or several, there are clear paths to fix the situation. Acting immediately and proactively always gives better outcomes than ignoring it.

This guide covers what happens when you miss an EMI, the timeline of consequences, and exactly what you can do at each stage.

---

## What Happens When You Miss an EMI — Timeline

| Days Overdue | What Happens |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Day 1–30** | Bounce charge (₹300–₹1,000) + late payment penalty. Reminder call/SMS from bank |
| **30 days** | Bank reports to credit bureaus (CIBIL, Experian) — credit score drops 50–100 points |
| **30–60 days** | "Special Mention Account" (SMA-0) tag. Recovery calls increase. Interest + penalty accruing |
| **60–90 days** | SMA-1 tag. Bank sends formal notice. Collection agents may visit |
| **90 days** | Loan classified as **NPA (Non-Performing Asset)**. Serious credit damage |
| **180+ days** | Bank may initiate legal action. For secured loans: SARFAESI notice (property seizure) |
| **360+ days** | Legal proceedings, arbitration, court case. Asset auction for secured loans |

---

## How Much EMI Default Damages Your Credit Score

| Number of Missed EMIs | Approximate CIBIL Impact |
|----------------------|-------------------------|
| 1 EMI (paid within 30 days) | -30 to -50 points |
| 1 EMI (paid after 30 days) | -80 to -100 points |
| 3 EMIs (NPA status) | -150 to -200 points |
| Written off / settled | -200 to -300 points (stays 7 years) |

A credit score drop affects: future loan eligibility, interest rates offered, credit card limits, insurance premiums (some insurers check CIBIL).

## Step 1: Immediate Actions If You've Missed EMI

### 1a. Pay the Overdue Amount ASAP

The longer you wait, the more interest + penalty accumulates and the worse the credit damage.

- **Bounce charge:** Already applied — pay when paying overdue EMI
- **Penal interest:** 2%–3% per month on overdue amount (check your loan agreement)
- Pay via: Net banking → Loan Repayment → Overdue Payment OR NEFT to loan account

### 1b. Call Your Bank/NBFC Immediately

Don't wait for the bank to call you. Proactive communication shows good faith.

**What to say:** "I have missed [X] EMIs due to [reason — job loss/medical/business downturn]. I want to discuss options to regularize my account."

Banks prefer to restructure loans over going through legal recovery. Call before they call you.

---

## Step 2: Negotiate With Your Bank — Options Available

### Option 1: Moratorium (EMI Pause)

- Bank can grant a moratorium (pause) of **1–6 months** on EMI
- **Interest continues to accrue during moratorium** — no free lunch
- Available for: Medical emergency, job loss, natural disaster
- **How to request:** Branch visit with written application + supporting documents (medical bills, layoff letter)

### Option 2: Loan Restructuring

Restructuring changes your loan terms to make EMIs more manageable:

| Restructuring Option | What Changes | Impact |
|---------------------|-------------|--------|
| **Tenure extension** | EMI reduces, loan period increases | Recommended if cash flow is tight |
| **Interest rate reduction** | Rate reduced temporarily | Bank's discretion |
| **Principal holiday** | Only interest paid for some months | Rare — for severe hardship |
| **Balance transfer** | Move to new lender with lower rate | Good if credit score still ok |

**How to request:** Visit branch → Meet branch manager → Submit written request with income documents → Bank evaluates in 5–7 working days.

### Option 3: One-Time Settlement (OTS)

If you genuinely cannot repay the full amount:
- Bank agrees to settle at a discounted amount
- **Impact:** Settled loans show "Settled" on CIBIL (not "Closed") — significant negative mark
- Only use as last resort
- Negotiate: Some banks settle at 40%–80% of outstanding principal (waiving accumulated interest + penalties)

## Step 3: What If Bank Has Sent a Legal Notice?

### SARFAESI Notice (for Secured Loans — Home Loan, Car Loan)

If you receive a notice under **SARFAESI Act 2002** (Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets):

1. **Don't ignore it** — you have a right to respond
2. **60-day response window** — you have 60 days to reply to the notice
3. **Repay full overdue** within this window → SARFAESI proceeding stops
4. **File DRT (Debt Recovery Tribunal) objection** if you dispute the amount
5. **Consult a lawyer** — SARFAESI proceedings can be challenged at DRT

### Legal Notice (Unsecured Loans — Personal Loan, Credit Card)

1. You have 30 days to respond
2. Option 1: Pay the overdue + negotiate settlement
3. Option 2: Appear before court if case is filed
4. Default judgment (if you don't appear): Bank gets decree → salary attachment, asset attachment possible

## Step 4: Dealing With Recovery Agents

**Your rights under RBI guidelines:**

| Your Right | What It Means |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Harassment is illegal** | Agents cannot threaten, abuse, or disturb between 7 PM – 7 AM |
| **No workplace visits** | Agents cannot visit your office without consent |
| **No third-party disclosure** | Agent cannot disclose your loan default to neighbors, relatives, employer |
| **Written communication** | Request all communication in writing |
| **File complaint** | If harassed: file complaint with bank's grievance cell + RBI at cms.rbi.org.in |

**If an agent harasses you:**
1. Note: name, phone number, company name
2. File complaint with your bank's nodal officer (email/grievance portal)
3. File complaint with RBI Banking Ombudsman at [cms.rbi.org.in](https://cms.rbi.org.in)
4. File complaint with police under IPC 506 (criminal intimidation) if threatening

## CIBIL Score Recovery After Default

After you clear the default:

| Timeline | What Happens |
|----------|-------------|
| Default cleared | CIBIL reflects repayment. Score improves gradually |
| 12–18 months | Score recovers 50–100 points with consistent payments |
| 24–36 months | Score can return to near-pre-default level |
| 7 years | Default record removed from credit history |

**Speed up recovery:**
1. Pay all existing EMIs on time (most important)
2. Keep credit card utilization below 30%
3. Don't apply for multiple loans simultaneously
4. Get a secured credit card (against FD) to build positive history

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will one missed EMI destroy my credit score permanently?**
No — one missed EMI reported to CIBIL reduces score by 30–100 points. Once you repay and maintain regular payments for 12–24 months, the score recovers substantially. CIBIL records are maintained for 7 years, but older records weigh less.

**Can I get a new loan after EMI default?**
Difficult with low credit score. Options: secured loan (against FD or gold), loan from NBFC (more lenient), or wait 1–2 years and rebuild score before reapplying.

**The bank called me but I don't have money — should I call them back?**
Yes — always call back. Explain your situation honestly. Banks have frameworks for distressed borrowers. Avoiding calls leads to faster escalation to legal recovery teams.

**Can the bank seize my house immediately after default?**
No — for home loans, SARFAESI process requires 60-day notice + additional steps. You have multiple opportunities to respond and repay. Seizure happens only after a lengthy legal process.

**Can EMI default affect my family members?**
Only if they are co-borrowers or guarantors. Your individual default does not affect your spouse's, parents', or children's credit scores unless they co-signed the loan.
