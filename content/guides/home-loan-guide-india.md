# Home Loan in India – Complete Guide to Interest Rates, Eligibility & Process

Buying a home is one of the biggest financial decisions in life. A home loan makes it possible by financing up to 80–90% of the property value. This guide covers everything you need to know — from comparing interest rates to understanding tax benefits and the step-by-step application process.

---

## What is a Home Loan?

A home loan is a secured loan provided by banks and housing finance companies (HFCs) to purchase, construct, or renovate a residential property. Key features:

- **Loan type:** Secured (property is the collateral)
- **Loan-to-Value (LTV):** Up to 75–90% depending on loan amount
- **Interest rate range:** 8.25% – 12% per annum
- **Tenure:** 5 to 30 years
- **Tax benefits:** Under Section 80C (principal) and Section 24(b) (interest)
- **Government subsidy:** PMAY (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana) for eligible buyers

---

## Types of Home Loans

| Type | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Home purchase loan** | Buy a new or resale property |
| **Home construction loan** | Build a house on owned land |
| **Home improvement loan** | Renovation or repair |
| **Home extension loan** | Add rooms/floors to existing property |
| **Plot loan** | Purchase land for construction |
| **Balance transfer** | Transfer existing loan to a lower-rate lender |
| **Top-up loan** | Additional loan on existing home loan |

## Who is Eligible?

### Salaried Individuals
- Age: 21–65 years (at loan maturity)
- Minimum income: ₹25,000/month (varies by lender)
- Minimum work experience: 2–3 years (1 year with current employer)
- Good CIBIL score: 700+ preferred (750+ for best rates)

### Self-Employed
- Age: 21–70 years (at loan maturity)
- Business vintage: 3+ years
- ITR for last 2–3 years
- CIBIL score: 700+

### LTV Ratio (RBI Guidelines)
| Loan Amount | Maximum LTV |
|-------------|-------------|
| Up to ₹30 lakh | 90% |
| ₹30 lakh – ₹75 lakh | 80% |
| Above ₹75 lakh | 75% |

## Documents Required

### Salaried Applicants
- **Identity proof:** Aadhaar, PAN card (mandatory)
- **Address proof:** Aadhaar, passport, utility bill
- **Income proof:** Salary slips (3 months), Form 16, bank statements (6 months)
- **Employment proof:** Offer letter, appointment letter
- **Property documents:** Sale agreement, title deed, approved building plan, encumbrance certificate
- **Photographs** (passport-size)

### Self-Employed Applicants
- Identity and address proof (same as above)
- **Business proof:** Registration certificate, GST registration
- **Income proof:** ITR for 3 years, profit & loss statement, balance sheet
- **Bank statements** (12 months — business and personal)
- Property documents (same as above)

## Step-by-Step Application Process

1. **Check eligibility** — use online eligibility calculators on bank websites
2. **Compare lenders** — shortlist 2–3 based on interest rate, fees, and service
3. **Get pre-approved** — apply for in-principle approval before finalizing property
4. **Select property** — ensure it's approved by the lender and RERA-registered
5. **Submit application** — fill form online or at branch with all documents
6. **Property valuation** — bank sends a valuer to assess the property
7. **Legal verification** — bank's legal team checks title and documents
8. **Loan sanction** — sanction letter issued with terms and conditions
9. **Sign agreement** — execute the loan agreement and mortgage deed
10. **Disbursal** — loan amount disbursed (in parts for under-construction property)

## Tax Benefits on Home Loan

### Section 24(b) — Interest Deduction
- **Self-occupied property:** Up to ₹2 lakh/year on interest paid
- **Let-out property:** Entire interest is deductible (no limit)
- Available from the year construction is completed

### Section 80C — Principal Repayment
- Up to ₹1.5 lakh/year on principal repaid
- Includes stamp duty and registration charges (in the year of purchase)

### Section 80EEA — Additional Interest Deduction
- Additional ₹1.5 lakh for affordable housing (stamp duty value ≤ ₹45 lakh)
- Loan sanctioned between 1 April 2019 and 31 March 2022
- No other residential property owned on date of loan sanction

- **Disbursal:** 2–5 working days after sanction

## Important Tips

1. **Improve your CIBIL score** to 750+ before applying — it gets you the best rates
2. **Choose floating rate** over fixed — floating rates are linked to repo rate and are typically lower
3. **Negotiate processing fees** — many banks waive or reduce fees, especially during festive offers
4. **Don't max out tenure** — a 20-year loan costs significantly less in interest than a 30-year loan
5. **Prepay when possible** — even ₹1–2 lakh extra per year can save lakhs in interest and years of EMI

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the minimum CIBIL score for a home loan?
Most banks require a minimum score of 650, but a score of 750+ will get you the best interest rates and faster approval. Below 650, your application may be rejected or offered at higher rates.

### Can I get a home loan for a resale property?
Yes, home loans are available for resale/second-hand properties. The bank will verify the property's title, age, and condition. Older properties (20+ years) may get lower LTV ratios.

### Is it better to take a joint home loan?
Yes, a joint loan (with spouse or parent) increases your eligibility and may give you a lower interest rate. Women co-applicants get 0.05% rate concession from many lenders. Additionally, both applicants can claim tax benefits separately.

### Can I transfer my home loan to another bank?
Yes, home loan balance transfer is allowed and can save money if another bank offers a lower rate. You'll need to pay processing fees and may need to get the property re-valued.

### What is the difference between fixed and floating rate?
Fixed rate stays the same throughout the tenure (rare in India, typically higher). Floating rate changes based on the lender's benchmark rate (repo-linked). Most home loans in India are floating rate.

### Can NRIs get home loans in India?
Yes, most banks offer home loans to NRIs for purchasing property in India. Additional documents like overseas employment proof, NRE/NRO statements, and passport/visa copies are required.
