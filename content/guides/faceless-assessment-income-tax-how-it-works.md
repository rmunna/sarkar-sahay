# Faceless Assessment Income Tax India 2026 — Complete Guide to E-Assessment Scheme

**Faceless Assessment** (officially the "Faceless Assessment Scheme") was launched in 2020 as a major reform. Before this, a taxpayer selected for scrutiny had to visit the local Income Tax office and deal with the assigned officer in person.

Under faceless assessment:
- Your case is assigned to an officer in a **different city** (you don't know which)
- All communication happens **online through e-Proceedings** on incometax.gov.in
- You NEVER meet the officer in person
- The officer can't meet you either — reducing corruption and harassment

## What Is "Assessment" in Income Tax?

An "assessment" is when the IT Department formally examines your ITR in detail. Most ITRs are processed under Section 143(1) (computerized, automatic) without human scrutiny.

Cases selected for detailed scrutiny go to "Assessment" — this is when you get a Section 143(2) notice, and the faceless assessment scheme covers how that scrutiny is conducted.

## Who Gets Selected for Faceless Assessment?

CBDT (Central Board of Direct Taxes) uses a risk-based, AI-driven selection system. Cases are selected if:

| Risk Factor | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| High-value cash transactions (SFT mismatch) | Large cash deposits not matching income |
| Capital gains not matching broker data | Gains in AIS not declared in ITR |
| Significant unexplained income | Large bank credits unexplained |
| Refund claims significantly higher than typical | Excessive deduction claims |
| Income below sector average | Professionals with unusually low declared income |
| Non-filing despite income indicators | High SFT data but no ITR |

Selection is now done by a national **Risk Assessment Centre** — your local jurisdiction officer doesn't choose whether to scrutinize you.

## How the Faceless Assessment Process Works

### Step 1: Section 143(2) Notice

You receive a notice under Section 143(2) via email (to your registered email on incometax.gov.in) and in your IT portal under Pending Actions → e-Proceedings.

**Time limit**: Section 143(2) must be issued within 3 months of the end of the year in which ITR was filed. (If ITR filed July 2025 for FY 2024-25, notice must come by June 30, 2026.)

### Step 2: Questionnaire Under Section 142(1)

A questionnaire is issued asking for:
- Explanation of specific transactions
- Supporting documents for deductions claimed
- Bank statements for specific periods
- Contract notes for capital gains
- Proof of business expenses

### Step 3: You Respond Online via e-Proceedings

Log in to incometax.gov.in → Pending Actions → e-Proceedings → Open the notice → Submit response.

You can:
- Upload documents (PDFs, up to 50MB per submission)
- Provide written explanations
- Request extensions if needed (with reason)
- Ask for a hearing (video conference — see below)

**Time to respond**: Usually 30 days from notice date. Extensions may be given with reason.

### Step 4: Assessment Unit Review

A team (not your local officer) reviews your response at the National Assessment Centre. They may:
- Accept your explanation → case closed
- Ask more questions → another round of notices
- Prepare a draft assessment order

### Step 5: Draft Assessment Order with Additions

If the assessing officer finds unreported income or disallows deductions, they prepare a **Draft Assessment Order** showing proposed additions to income.

You get a chance to **object to the draft** before the final order is passed (Section 144C opportunity).

### Step 6: Final Assessment Order (Section 143(3))

After considering your objections, a final assessment order is passed:
- **No addition**: Case closed; your ITR is accepted as filed
- **Addition**: Extra tax demand; you can pay or appeal

## Your Rights in Faceless Assessment

| Right | What It Means |
|-------|--------------|
| Personal hearing via video conference | If you ask in writing, you're entitled to a video call with the assessment unit |
| Voir the draft order | You see proposed additions before the final order |
| Objection opportunity | Formally object to draft order before finalization |
| Appeal to CIT(A) | After final order, you can appeal to Commissioner (Appeals) |
| Further appeal to ITAT | If CIT(A) doesn't give relief, appeal to Income Tax Appellate Tribunal |

**Important**: The word "faceless" means no physical meetings — NOT that you have no rights. You have the same rights as in traditional assessment, just exercised online.

## Faceless vs Traditional Assessment

| Feature | Faceless | Traditional (pre-2020) |
|---------|----------|----------------------|
| Meeting officer | ❌ Not allowed | ✅ In-person visits |
| Officer location | Different city (anonymous) | Local jurisdiction |
| Communication | e-Proceedings only | In-person + written |
| Corruption potential | Much lower | Higher |
| Process time | Theoretically faster | Often prolonged |
| Physical document submission | Not required (upload scans) | Sometimes required |

## How to Respond Effectively to Faceless Assessment

### Tip 1: Respond promptly

Extensions are given but delays can be viewed unfavorably. Respond within the deadline or request an extension with specific reason.

### Tip 2: Upload high-quality scans

Assessors review documents digitally. Ensure:
- Documents are clearly legible
- PDFs are not password-protected
- File sizes are manageable (compress large PDFs)

### Tip 3: Be specific and factual

When providing written explanations:
- Don't be vague ("amounts are from savings")
- Be specific: "₹8,50,000 credited on June 15, 2025 is proceeds from sale of flat at [address] — sale deed attached as Exhibit A"

### Tip 4: Organize documents by question

If the questionnaire has 5 questions, organize your upload clearly labeled by question number. Don't dump all documents at once.

### Tip 5: Request video conference if complex

For complex cases (multiple years, large additions proposed), request a video conference. This gives you a chance to verbally explain nuances the documents may not fully capture.

### Tip 6: Engage a CA for scrutiny cases

Faceless assessment responses are legal proceedings — errors in your response can be used against you. A chartered accountant or tax advocate can draft appropriate responses and know which arguments are legally strong.

## Common Reasons Cases Are Selected — And Typical Responses

| Common Query | What You Need |
|-------------|--------------|
| Unexplained cash credit in bank | Bank statement tracing source: withdrawal from another account, family transfer, business receipt already in ITR |
| Capital gains mismatch | Contract notes + P&L statement from broker + AIS reconciliation |
| HRA exemption questioned | Rent agreement + 12 months bank transfers + landlord PAN |
| Large charitable donation | Form 10BE from NGO + bank transfer proof |
| Business expense disallowed | Invoices + payments + business purpose explanation |
| Property purchase inconsistent with income | Home loan sanction + down payment source documentation |

## Faceless Appeal — After Assessment Order

If you receive an assessment order with additions and want to appeal:
1. File **Form 35** within 30 days → Faceless appeal to CIT(A)
2. Pay **20% mandatory pre-deposit** of disputed tax demand
3. Submit grounds of appeal specifically

The **Faceless Appeal Scheme** operates similarly — the CIT(A) officer is also randomly assigned and all communication is online.

## Penalties in Faceless Assessment

| Situation | Penalty |
|-----------|---------|
| Concealment of income | 200% of tax on concealed amount |
| Furnishing inaccurate particulars | 50–200% of tax |
| Failure to respond to notice | ₹10,000 per default (Section 271(1)(b)) |
| Non-maintenance of books (business) | ₹25,000 to ₹1,50,000 |

Penalties can be waived if there's a reasonable cause — explain circumstances honestly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I got a 143(2) notice but I'm very small — salary of ₹8L, no business. Why me?

You may have been selected due to an AIS entry mismatch (e.g., a bank flagged a large credit, or your employer reported different figures) or a random quality check. Respond with honest documentation. Small salaried taxpayers who respond accurately typically get cases closed quickly.

### My CA is not available and the deadline is in 3 days. What do I do?

Log in to e-Proceedings and file a request for extension with a clear reason ("CA/tax representative unavailable; require 15 additional days"). Extensions are often granted. Don't miss the deadline without requesting an extension — that triggers a non-response penalty.

### I'm NRI living in the US. Can I respond to a faceless assessment without coming to India?

Yes — that's the whole point of faceless assessment. You respond entirely online from wherever you are. Use a Power of Attorney (POA) document to authorize someone in India (a CA or trusted family member) to appear on your behalf if needed.
