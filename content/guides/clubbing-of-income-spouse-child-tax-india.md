# Clubbing of Income India 2026 — When Your Spouse's or Child's Income Is Added to Your Return

A common tax planning idea is: "Put money in wife's name — her income will be taxed at a lower rate." In India, the Income Tax Act specifically prevents this through **"clubbing of income"** rules under Section 64.

If you transfer assets to your spouse or minor children, any income from those assets is **added back to your taxable income** — not taxed in their hands.

## The Core Rule — Section 64(1)(iv): Transfer to Spouse

**Rule**: If you transfer any asset to your spouse (not for adequate consideration), the income from that asset is clubbed to **your** income.

| Situation | Clubbing |
|-----------|---------|
| You give ₹10L to wife; she puts it in FD | FD interest taxed in **your** hands |
| You gift shares to wife; she gets dividend | Dividend taxed in **your** hands |
| You gift property to wife; she rents it | Rental income taxed in **your** hands |
| You gift ₹10L to wife; she runs a business | **Clubbing does NOT apply** to business income from gifts |

**Exception**: If the transfer is for "adequate consideration" (fair market value), clubbing doesn't apply. Also, if the transferor and transferee are no longer spouses at the time income arises (divorce), clubbing stops.

## Section 64(1)(iv): Transfer to Son's Wife (Daughter-in-Law)

The same rule applies to your daughter-in-law:
- Transfer assets to your son's wife (without adequate consideration)
- Income from those assets → clubbed to **your** income (not husband's/son's)

## Section 64(1)(vi): Transfer to Son's Wife by Husband

If a husband (your son) transfers assets to his wife (your daughter-in-law), the income is clubbed to the **son's** income. This is the normal case between spouses.

## What Happens to Capital Gains — Important Nuance

When you transfer an asset (say, shares) to your spouse and the spouse **sells it at a gain**:

1. The **capital gain at the time of transfer** (if any) is taxed in **your** hands
2. The **subsequent capital gain** after the spouse acquired it is:
   - If sold soon after = taxed in **your** hands (clubbing continues)
   - Clubbing applies on income "arising from assets transferred" — including capital appreciation

But the specific rule: If your spouse uses the **income** (interest, dividend) to invest further, the **income on that income** is taxed in their hands (not clubbed back to you). "Clubbing of income on clubbed income" doesn't apply — only first-generation income is clubbed.

## Minor Children — Section 64(1A)

Income earned by your **minor child** (below 18) is also clubbed to your income — specifically to the parent with the higher income.

| Income Type | Clubbing |
|-------------|---------|
| Interest from FD/savings in child's name | ✅ Clubbed to higher-income parent |
| Dividend from child's shares | ✅ Clubbed |
| Rental income from child's property | ✅ Clubbed |
| Income from child's **own skill/work/talent** | ❌ NOT clubbed (a child actor's earnings, music prodigy's fees) |

**Exemption threshold**: ₹1,500 per child per year is exempt. If the child has two children, ₹3,000 total is exempt before clubbing. Only income above ₹1,500/child is clubbed.

**Which parent?** Clubbed to the parent with **higher gross total income** (before clubbing). If parents have equal income, the father's income is used by default.

## When a Minor Turns 18

Once the child turns 18, they are no longer a minor. Their income is no longer clubbed — it's their own income from that birthday onwards.

## Transfers That AVOID Clubbing

| Transfer Type | Clubbing? |
|---------------|----------|
| Gift to **major** child (18+) | ❌ No clubbing — adult child's own income |
| Gift to **parents** | ❌ No clubbing rule for parents |
| Gift to **siblings** | ❌ No clubbing rule for siblings |
| Transfer for **adequate consideration** | ❌ Clubbing doesn't apply |
| Inheritance / Will | ❌ Inherited assets → recipient's income (no clubbing) |
| Loan to spouse (with interest) | ❌ If spouse borrows at market rate, income not clubbed |

## The SB Interest Exception — Pin Money

What if you give your spouse a small amount for regular expenses (pocket money/household)? If the spouse saves those funds and earns interest:
- If it's a reasonable "pin money" amount for expenses (not investment), courts have sometimes not applied strict clubbing
- But for significant amounts, the strict Section 64 rule applies

## Practical Strategy — How to Invest in Spouse's Name Without Clubbing

**Strategy 1**: Give assets to adult children (18+). No clubbing.

**Strategy 2**: Give money to parents. No clubbing on parents' income.

**Strategy 3**: For business income. If the spouse uses gifted money to run their own business, the business income is NOT clubbed (business income generated through personal skill/effort of the spouse is excluded from Section 64 clubbing).

**Strategy 4**: HUF. Transfer assets to an HUF (Hindu Undivided Family), where both you and your spouse are members. Income is taxed as HUF income (with separate ₹2.5L basic exemption).

**Strategy 5**: PPF/NPS in spouse's name. PPF and NPS accounts are individual tax-free savings instruments. Contributions to a spouse's PPF are eligible for 80C deduction (if spouse's PPF is yours to claim), and there's no clubbing of PPF interest (it's tax-free anyway). But NPS maturity annuity income would be clubbed if funded by you.

## Reporting Clubbed Income in ITR

When you file your ITR and have clubbed income from spouse/child:
1. In your ITR-2/ITR-3, include the clubbed income in the appropriate schedule:
   - Interest: Schedule OS (Other Sources)
   - Capital gains: Schedule CG
   - Rental: Schedule HP (House Property)
2. Write "Clubbing u/s 64(1)(iv)" or similar note for self-reference
3. The spouse/child should **not** declare this income in their own ITR (it's declared only in your ITR)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My wife is not earning. I put ₹20L in her FD. FD interest ₹1,40,000. I'm in 30% slab, she has zero income. Will all of it be taxed at 30%?

Yes — under Section 64(1)(iv), FD interest is clubbed to your income (since you gave the funds). Even though she has no other income and a ₹20L FD in her name sounds like a good idea, the ₹1,40,000 interest is added to your income and taxed at your 30% rate.

**Alternative**: Give the money as a loan at market interest rate (document it with a loan agreement). She invests the borrowed money — income is hers. The interest she pays you is income in your hands.

### I bought shares in my wife's name from my savings. When she sells, is capital gain in her hands?

The capital gain is clubbed to you (Section 64). The assets were transferred without adequate consideration, so any income (including capital gains) from those assets is taxed in your hands.

### My wife earned ₹5L through her own freelancing. Is that clubbed?

No — income earned through her own skill, labor, and personal effort is NOT clubbed. Section 64 only applies to income from assets you transferred to her. Her own freelancing income is independently her income and taxed in her hands.

### My minor daughter won a music competition and got ₹1 lakh prize. Clubbed?

No — income from a minor child's own talent or skill is exempt from clubbing (Section 64(1A) proviso). This includes prize money from talent shows, sports, music, etc. She's taxed on this (via her PAN) as it's above ₹1,500.
