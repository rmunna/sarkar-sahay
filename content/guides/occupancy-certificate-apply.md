# Occupancy Certificate: How to Apply & Documents Needed

An Occupancy Certificate (OC) is a legal document issued by the local municipal authority that certifies a building is safe for habitation and has been constructed in accordance with the approved building plan. Without an OC, occupying a building is technically illegal and can lead to penalties, utility disconnection, and legal complications.

## What is an Occupancy Certificate?

An Occupancy Certificate confirms that:

- The building has been completed as per the sanctioned plan
- It complies with all safety norms (fire, structural, environmental)
- Essential services (water supply, drainage, lifts) are functional
- The building is fit and safe for people to move in

Under the [Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA)](https://rera.gov.in/), builders are legally required to obtain an OC before handing over possession to buyers.

---

## OC vs CC: What is the Difference?

| Aspect | Completion Certificate (CC) | Occupancy Certificate (OC) |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Confirms construction is complete per plan | Permits legal occupation of the building |
| **When issued** | After construction completion | After CC is obtained |
| **Issued by** | Municipal corporation | Municipal corporation |
| **Verifies** | Construction compliance | Safety and habitability |
| **Required for** | OC application, property records | Moving in, utility connections, sale |

In simple terms: CC says "building is built correctly," OC says "building is safe to live in." Read more about the [Completion Certificate process](/guide/completion-certificate-apply).

## Who is Responsible for Getting the OC?

### Builder's Responsibility

- Under **RERA (Section 14)**, the builder/promoter must obtain the OC before offering possession to buyers
- Failure to provide OC is a violation of RERA and buyers can file a [RERA complaint](/guide/rera-complaint-file)
- The builder must apply for OC at their own cost

### Individual Homeowner

- If you have built your own house on your own plot, **you** must apply for the OC
- For independent houses, the owner applies after obtaining the [Completion Certificate](/guide/completion-certificate-apply)

### Housing Society

- If the builder has not obtained OC for an older building, the housing society can apply collectively

## Documents Required

### For Builder/Developer Projects

- Completion Certificate (CC) — original
- Sanctioned building plan (original)
- Building permit / commencement certificate
- NOC from Fire Department
- Structural stability certificate
- Lift safety certificate (for multi-storey)
- Water supply and drainage completion certificate
- Environmental clearance (if applicable)
- Photographs of completed building
- Property tax paid receipts

### For Individual House Owners

- Completion Certificate from municipal authority
- Sanctioned building plan
- Building permit
- Architect/engineer's completion certificate
- Property ownership documents (sale deed, title deed)
- NOC from Fire Department (for buildings above G+1)
- Property tax receipts
- Photographs of the completed house

## Step-by-Step Process: How to Apply for OC Online

### Online Process

1. **Visit your municipal corporation's online portal** (e.g., MCD Online for Delhi, BBMP Sakala for Bengaluru)
2. **Login** using your building permit number or property registration ID
3. **Select "Occupancy Certificate"** from the building services section
4. **Fill the application form** — enter building details, CC number, and construction completion date
5. **Upload all required documents** — scanned copies of CC, NOCs, structural certificate, etc.
6. **Pay the OC fee** online through net banking, UPI, or card payment
7. **Submit the application** and save the reference number
8. **Municipal inspection** — officials will visit the site to verify safety compliance
9. **OC issuance** — if inspection is satisfactory, OC is issued digitally or as a physical certificate

### Offline Process

1. **Collect application form** from the municipal corporation office
2. **Attach all documents** (self-attested copies + originals for verification)
3. **Submit at the building plan section** of your municipal office with the prescribed fee
4. **Site inspection** is conducted by municipal officials
5. **Collect OC** after successful inspection

## Penalty for Not Having an Occupancy Certificate

Living in or selling a property without a valid OC carries serious consequences:

- **Utility disconnection** — municipal authorities can cut water and electricity supply
- **Fines and penalties** — range from ₹10,000 to several lakhs depending on the city and building size
- **Property sale issues** — buyers and banks will refuse transactions without OC
- **Home loan rejection** — banks require OC for loan approval and disbursement
- **Legal liability** — in case of accidents, the owner/builder faces criminal liability
- **RERA violation** — buyers can file complaints against builders who hand over possession without OC; penalty up to 10% of project cost

## Important Tips

1. **Demand OC from your builder** before taking possession — it is your legal right under RERA
2. **Never pay full amount without OC** — retain at least 5-10% payment until OC is provided
3. **Check CC before OC** — ensure the Completion Certificate has been obtained first
4. **Verify OC authenticity** — cross-check the OC number on the municipal corporation's online portal
5. **Apply for OC promptly** — some municipalities impose penalties for delayed OC application after construction completion

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. Can I move into a flat without an Occupancy Certificate?

Technically, no. Occupying a building without OC is illegal and the municipal authority can take action including fines, utility disconnection, and eviction notices. Always insist on OC before moving in.

### Q2. What if my builder refuses to provide OC?

You can file a complaint under RERA against the builder. You can also approach the municipal corporation directly or file a complaint in consumer court. See our guide on [filing a RERA complaint](/guide/rera-complaint-file).

### Q3. Is OC required for resale of a flat?

Yes. Banks require OC for processing home loans, and buyers are legally advised to verify OC before purchasing any property. Properties without OC sell at significant discounts and carry legal risks.

### Q4. Can a housing society apply for OC if the builder hasn't?

Yes. If the builder has defaulted, the housing society can apply to the municipal corporation for OC by submitting the required documents and the sanctioned building plan.

### Q5. Is partial OC valid?

Yes, some municipal corporations issue partial or phase-wise OC for large projects where some towers/wings are complete while others are under construction. Verify that your specific wing/tower is covered.

### Q6. What is the difference between OC and Part OC?

A full OC covers the entire building/project. A Part OC (or Phase OC) covers only specific completed portions. Ensure your flat/unit is included in the Part OC before taking possession.

### Q7. Does OC expire?

No. An Occupancy Certificate does not have an expiry date. Once issued, it remains valid permanently. However, major structural modifications require a fresh building permit and fresh OC.
