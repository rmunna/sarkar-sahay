# Budget 2025 Income Tax Changes — New Regime Revised, ₹12L Zero Tax Threshold

Union Budget 2025, presented on **February 1, 2025**, brought significant income tax relief for middle-class taxpayers. The most impactful change: the new tax regime was restructured so that individuals with income up to **₹12 lakh** pay **zero income tax** (through revised slabs + Section 87A rebate).

## New Tax Regime Slabs — FY 2025-26 (Effective from April 1, 2025)

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|-------------|---------|
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | 0% |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

**Plus 4% Health and Education Cess on tax amount.**

**Surcharge** for high incomes:
- 10% surcharge on income ₹50L – ₹1Cr
- 15% surcharge on ₹1Cr – ₹2Cr
- 25% surcharge on ₹2Cr – ₹5Cr
- 37% surcharge on above ₹5Cr

## Why Is Tax Zero Up to ₹12 Lakh?

Budget 2025 also raised the Section 87A rebate to ₹60,000 (up from ₹25,000 earlier) for the new regime:

| Income | Tax Before Rebate | 87A Rebate | Net Tax |
|--------|-------------------|-----------|---------|
| ₹8,00,000 | ₹20,000 (0 on ₹4L + 5% on ₹4L) | ₹20,000 | **₹0** |
| ₹10,00,000 | ₹40,000 | ₹40,000 | **₹0** |
| ₹12,00,000 | ₹60,000 | ₹60,000 | **₹0** |
| ₹12,00,001 | ₹60,001+ | ₹0 (above threshold) | **₹60,001+** (cliff!) |

**Important**: The ₹12L threshold includes the 87A rebate wiping out tax completely. If income is ₹12,00,001, you lose the rebate and owe the full tax amount — the cliff effect is significant.

## For Salaried Employees — Effective Zero-Tax Income

With standard deduction of ₹75,000:
- Gross salary: ₹12,75,000
- Minus standard deduction: ₹75,000
- **Taxable income: ₹12,00,000**
- Tax on ₹12L: ₹60,000
- 87A rebate: ₹60,000
- **Net tax: ₹0**

So salaried employees earning up to **₹12.75 lakh gross salary** pay zero tax under the new regime in FY 2025-26.

## Old Tax Regime — No Changes in Budget 2025

Budget 2025 made **no changes to the old tax regime** slabs. Old regime slabs remain:

| Income Slab | Rate |
|-------------|------|
| Up to ₹2,50,000 | 0% |
| ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹10,00,000 | 30% |

87A rebate under old regime: Still ₹12,500 (income up to ₹5L).

Old regime deductions (80C, 80D, 24b, HRA, etc.) remain unchanged.

## New Regime vs Old Regime After Budget 2025

| Annual Income | New Regime Tax (FY 2025-26) | Old Regime Tax (with full deductions) | Winner |
|--------------|---------------------------|---------------------------------------|--------|
| ₹8L | ₹0 (87A rebate) | ~₹0 (with 80C + standard deduction) | Tie |
| ₹12L | ₹0 (87A rebate) | ~₹52,000 (with 80C/HRA/24b) | **New Regime** |
| ₹15L | ₹45,000 + cess | ~₹75,000 + cess (without deductions) | **New Regime** |
| ₹15L (with full 80C+80D+HRA+NPS+24b) | ₹45,000 + cess | ~₹10,000–₹20,000 + cess | Old Regime may win for heavy deductors |
| ₹20L | ₹1,00,000 + cess | Depends on deductions | Case by case |
| ₹30L+ | Higher slabs matter | Old regime with large HRA/24b/80CCD | Compare individually |

**Key insight after Budget 2025**: For most taxpayers below ₹15L with limited deductions, the new regime is now clearly better. The advantage of old regime is now only for those with very large deductions (big home loan interest, large HRA, significant NPS contributions).

## Other Budget 2025 Tax Highlights

### TCS on Foreign Remittance — Raised Threshold
- No TCS on overseas remittances up to ₹10 lakh (raised from ₹7 lakh)
- Tour packages: TCS threshold also raised to ₹10 lakh

### Section 80CCD(2) — NPS Employer Contribution Cap Raised
- Private sector employees: Employer NPS deduction raised to 14% of salary (from 10%)
- This aligns private sector with government employees (who already had 14%)

### Updated Return Window Extended
- Section 139(8A) updated return: Now allowed for 4 years from end of relevant AY (extended from 2 years)
- Useful if you realize you missed income in earlier years — can file updated return and pay tax without major penalties

### TDS Rate Rationalization
- Various TDS rates harmonized across sections
- Senior citizens: Higher TDS-free threshold for FD interest raised to ₹1 lakh

## How to Calculate Your Tax Under New Regime FY 2025-26

**Formula**:
1. Start with gross total income (salary + all other income)
2. Subtract standard deduction (₹75,000 for salaried)
3. Apply the new slab rates (table above)
4. Apply 87A rebate (if income ≤ ₹12L, rebate = full tax amount)
5. Add 4% cess
6. Add surcharge if income > ₹50L

**Example 1**: Gross salary ₹10L, no other income
- Taxable: ₹10L − ₹75K = ₹9,25,000
- Tax: 0 + ₹20,000 (5% on ₹4L–₹8L) + ₹12,500 (10% on ₹8L–₹9.25L) = ₹32,500
- 87A rebate: ₹32,500 (since ₹9.25L < ₹12L threshold)
- **Net tax: ₹0**

**Example 2**: Gross salary ₹18L
- Taxable: ₹18L − ₹75K = ₹17,25,000
- Tax: 0 + ₹20,000 (5% on ₹4L–₹8L) + ₹40,000 (10% on ₹8L–₹12L) + ₹75,000 (15% on ₹12L–₹17.25L) = ₹1,35,000
- 87A rebate: ₹0 (income above ₹12L)
- Cess: ₹5,400
- **Net tax: ₹1,40,400**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I was in old regime last year. Should I switch to new regime now?

For FY 2025-26 (filing by July 31, 2026), calculate under both regimes:
- New regime: Use Budget 2025 slabs above
- Old regime: Use previous slabs with your actual deductions

The breakeven point shifted significantly after Budget 2025. For most people earning ₹8L–₹15L without massive deductions, new regime now wins. Salaried employees can switch regime annually when filing ITR.

### My income is ₹12 lakh. I pay zero tax now. What about next year if I get a raise to ₹12.5 lakh?

Your taxable income (₹12.5L − ₹75K standard deduction = ₹11.75L) is below ₹12L — still zero tax with 87A rebate. The cliff starts when taxable income (after standard deduction) exceeds ₹12L — i.e., gross salary above ₹12.75L for salaried.
