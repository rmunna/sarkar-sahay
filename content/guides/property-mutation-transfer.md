## What is Property Mutation?

Property mutation (also called **Dakhil Kharij** or **name transfer**) is the process of updating the ownership records in the revenue/municipal records after a property changes hands. It does NOT transfer legal ownership — that happens through the sale deed, gift deed, or succession. Mutation updates the **government revenue records** to reflect the new owner for:

- **Property tax assessment** — Municipal records for tax billing
- **Revenue records** — Tehsil/panchayat land records
- **Government records** — For future transactions

Mutation is essential because without it, you may face issues with property tax billing, further sale, obtaining building permissions, and proving ownership.

## When is Mutation Required?

- **After purchasing property** (sale deed registered)
- **After inheriting property** (through will or succession)
- **After receiving property as gift** (gift deed registered)
- **After property partition** (partition deed executed)
- **After court order** (transfer by decree)
- **After acquiring property** through government acquisition or allotment

## Types of Mutation

### 1. Sale Mutation
After registering a sale deed at the Sub-Registrar's office.

### 2. Inheritance/Succession Mutation
After the death of the property owner — requires legal heir certificate or succession certificate. See our [legal heir certificate guide](/guide/legal-heir-certificate-apply).

### 3. Gift Mutation
After registering a gift deed.

### 4. Partition Mutation
After registering a partition deed among co-owners.

## Documents Required

### For Sale-Based Mutation
- **Registered sale deed** (original and copy)
- **Previous owner's property documents** (previous mutation, tax receipts)
- **Identity proof** — Aadhaar, PAN — see our [Aadhaar guide](/guide/aadhaar-card-apply-online)
- **Property tax paid receipts** (up to date)
- **Encumbrance certificate** (from Sub-Registrar's office)
- **Application form** (available online or at tehsil office)
- **Stamp duty receipt** and registration receipt
- **NOC** from housing society (if applicable) — see our [NOC guide](/guide/noc-certificate-apply)

### For Inheritance-Based Mutation
All of the above plus:
- **Death certificate** of the previous owner
- **Legal heir certificate** or succession certificate
- **Will** (if applicable) — registered will or probated will
- **Affidavit** from all legal heirs consenting to the mutation
- **Family tree** declaration

## How to Apply for Property Mutation Online

### General Process (Available in Most States)
1. Visit your state's revenue or municipal portal
2. Register/login with your mobile and Aadhaar
3. Select **"Property Mutation"** or **"Dakhil Kharij"**
4. Enter property details — khasra number, plot number, survey number
5. Select mutation type (sale, inheritance, gift, etc.)
6. Upload scanned documents
7. Pay the mutation fee online
8. Submit application and note the reference number
9. Track status online

### For Municipal Property (Urban)
For properties within municipal/corporation limits:
1. Visit the municipal corporation's online portal
2. Apply for **property mutation/transfer**
3. Upload sale deed, ID proof, and tax receipts
4. Municipal assessor may inspect the property
5. Mutation reflected in property tax records

## How to Apply Offline

### Step 1: Visit Tehsil Office (Rural) or Municipal Office (Urban)
Go to the local tehsildar/revenue office or municipal corporation office.

### Step 2: Submit Application
Fill the prescribed mutation form and attach:
- All required documents (originals + copies)
- Application fee stamp/receipt

### Step 3: Verification
- The revenue officer or municipal assessor verifies the documents
- **Public notice** may be published inviting objections (15-30 days)
- If no objections, the mutation is approved

### Step 4: Field Inspection
In some cases, a spot inspection (mouza/field verification) may be conducted.

### Step 5: Mutation Order
- The tehsildar/municipal officer passes the mutation order
- Revenue records are updated with the new owner's name
- You receive a mutation certificate/extract

## How to Check Mutation Status

1. Visit your state's land records portal
2. Enter your application number or property details
3. Check current status — Pending, Under Verification, Approved, Rejected
4. Download the mutation certificate/extract after approval

## Common Issues and Solutions

### Mutation Rejected — Multiple Claimants
- Submit clear documentation proving ownership chain
- Get affidavits from all parties accepting the transfer
- Court order may be needed in dispute cases

### Old Records Not Available
- Apply for certified copies of old records from the Sub-Registrar
- Use encumbrance certificate to establish ownership chain
- Older records may be available at the district collectorate

### Delay in Processing
- File RTI application asking for status — see our [RTI guide](/guide/how-to-file-rti-online)
- Approach the Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM) for escalation
- Many states have time-bound service guarantees

## Important Tips

1. **Apply for mutation immediately** after property registration — Don't delay as it becomes harder with time
2. **Get encumbrance certificate** before applying — It shows the property is free from legal claims
3. **Pay property tax** up to date — Outstanding tax can delay or block mutation
4. **Keep all original documents safe** — Sale deed, mutation certificate, and tax receipts form your ownership chain
5. **For inherited property**, get the legal heir certificate first — without it, inheritance mutation cannot proceed

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is property mutation proof of ownership?
No, mutation is NOT proof of ownership. It only updates revenue/tax records. The **registered sale deed** (or succession certificate/will) is the primary proof of ownership. Mutation supports but doesn't replace it.

### What if the seller doesn't cooperate for mutation?
In most cases, the buyer can apply for mutation independently with the registered sale deed. The seller's presence is not mandatory — the sale deed itself is sufficient proof of transfer.

### Is there a time limit for applying for mutation?
While there's no strict legal time limit, it's advisable to apply **within 3-6 months** of property registration. Delay can lead to complications, especially for inheritance cases.

### Can mutation be challenged?
Yes, any interested party can file an objection during the public notice period. After mutation is approved, it can be challenged before the appellate authority (SDM or Commissioner) or in court.

### Is mutation required for flat/apartment purchases?
Yes, mutation in the **municipal records** is needed for property tax assessment in the new owner's name. Additionally, society transfer (in case of cooperative housing) is also required.

### What is Dakhil Kharij?
Dakhil Kharij is the Hindi term for property mutation, commonly used in Bihar, Jharkhand, and parts of UP. It literally means "inclusion-exclusion" — including the new owner and excluding the old one from revenue records.
