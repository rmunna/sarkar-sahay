# Consumer Court Guide – How to File a Consumer Complaint in India

If you've been cheated by a seller, received defective goods, or experienced deficient services, you have the right to seek justice through India's consumer courts. The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 makes it easy to file complaints — even online. This guide covers everything from jurisdiction to filing to appeal.

---

## What is a Consumer Court?

Consumer courts (also called Consumer Dispute Redressal Commissions) are quasi-judicial bodies set up under the Consumer Protection Act to resolve disputes between consumers and businesses. They provide:

- **Quick resolution** — faster than regular courts
- **Affordable justice** — minimal fees
- **No lawyer needed** — you can represent yourself
- **Compensation** — for defective goods, deficient services, or unfair trade practices

---

## Types of Consumer Courts

| Court | Jurisdiction (Claim Value) |
|---|---|
| **District Consumer Commission** | Up to ₹1 crore |
| **State Consumer Commission** | ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore |
| **National Consumer Commission (NCDRC)** | Above ₹10 crore |

---

## Who Can File a Consumer Complaint?

- Any **consumer** who has purchased goods or availed services for personal use
- A **recognized consumer association** on behalf of consumers
- **Central or State Government** on behalf of consumers
- **Legal heirs** of a deceased consumer
- One or more consumers with the **same interest** (class action)

**Note:** Purchase for resale or commercial purposes is generally not covered (with some exceptions for self-employment).

---

## Grounds for Filing a Complaint

You can file a complaint for:

- **Defective goods** — products with manufacturing defects, not as described
- **Deficient services** — poor quality, delayed, or incomplete services
- **Unfair trade practices** — misleading ads, false claims, hidden charges
- **Overcharging** — charging more than the MRP or agreed price
- **Restricted trade practices** — tie-in sales, refusing after-sales service
- **Product liability** — harm caused by a defective product

---

## Documents Required

- **Proof of purchase:** Bill, invoice, receipt, or online order confirmation
- **Product/service details:** Description, model number, warranty card
- **Communication records:** Emails, messages, complaint letters with the seller
- **Evidence of defect/deficiency:** Photos, videos, expert reports
- **ID proof:** Aadhaar, PAN, or any valid ID
- **Bank statements:** If claiming financial loss
- **Medical reports:** If claiming injury from defective product

## Step-by-Step: File Consumer Complaint Online (e-Daakhil)

1. Visit [e-Daakhil](https://edaakhil.nic.in/)
2. Click **"Register"** and create an account
3. **Login** with your credentials
4. Click **"File New Case"**
5. Select the **type of commission** (District/State/National) based on your claim value
6. Select your **state and district**
7. Fill in the **complainant details** (your information)
8. Fill in the **opposite party details** (seller/company)
9. Write the **complaint details:**
   - Facts of the case
   - Relief sought (refund, replacement, compensation)
   - Amount of compensation claimed
10. **Upload documents:**
    - Purchase proof
    - Communication records
    - Evidence of defect/deficiency
    - Affidavit (signed statement of facts)
11. **Pay the court fee** online (based on claim value — see table below)
12. **Submit** the complaint
13. Note your **Case Number** for tracking

---

## Step-by-Step: File Consumer Complaint Offline

1. Draft a **complaint** on plain paper (or typed)
2. Include:
   - Name and address of complainant and opposite party
   - Facts of the case in chronological order
   - Documents relied upon
   - Relief sought
3. Attach an **affidavit** (sworn statement on stamp paper)
4. Make **3-4 copies** of the complaint and all documents
5. Pay the **court fee** via demand draft
6. Submit at the relevant **Consumer Commission** office
7. You can send by **registered post** if the commission is far away

## Timeline

- **Filing to admission:** 21 days
- **Opposite party response:** 30 days (extendable by 15 days)
- **Total resolution target:** 3-5 months for straightforward cases
- **Appeals:** Must be filed within 30-45 days of the order

---

## Appeal Process

| Level | Appeal To | Time Limit |
|---|---|---|
| District Commission order | State Consumer Commission | 45 days |
| State Commission order | National Consumer Commission (NCDRC) | 30 days |
| NCDRC order | Supreme Court of India | 30 days |

## Consumer Helpline

Before going to court, try the **National Consumer Helpline:**

- **Phone:** 1800-11-4000 or 14404 (toll-free)
- **Website:** [consumerhelpline.gov.in](https://consumerhelpline.gov.in/)
- **WhatsApp:** 8800001915
- **Available:** Monday to Saturday, 9:30 AM to 5:30 PM
- They mediate between you and the company and resolve many complaints without court

---

## Important Tips

1. **Try the National Consumer Helpline first** — many cases are resolved through mediation without court
2. **File within 2 years** of the cause of action — the limitation period is 2 years
3. **Keep all original documents** safe — submit copies to the court
4. **Be specific about compensation** — mention exact amounts for refund, damages, and mental agony
5. **No lawyer needed** — you can argue your own case, saving costs
6. **e-Daakhil** allows fully online filing — you don't need to visit the court physically for filing

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**Q1. Can I file a consumer complaint without a lawyer?**
Yes. Consumer courts are designed for easy access. You can file and argue your own case without a lawyer.

**Q2. What is the maximum compensation I can claim?**
There is no fixed maximum. You can claim the value of the defective product/service, plus additional compensation for mental agony, loss of time, and legal expenses.

**Q3. Can I file a complaint against online sellers (Amazon, Flipkart)?**
Yes. E-commerce platforms and sellers on them are covered under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and the E-Commerce Rules.

**Q4. What if I don't have a bill or receipt?**
You can use bank/UPI transaction records, delivery receipts, emails, or any other proof of purchase.

**Q5. Can I file a consumer complaint from another city?**
Yes. You can file in the district where the opposite party resides/operates OR where you reside. e-Daakhil makes remote filing easy.

**Q6. What happens if the company doesn't respond to the complaint?**
The court can pass an ex-parte order (one-sided judgment) in your favor if the opposite party fails to respond within the given time.

**Q7. How long does a consumer case take?**
Simple cases may be resolved in 3-5 months. Complex cases can take 1-2 years. The Act mandates resolution within 150 days of admission.
