# NPS How to Open Account India 2026 — Complete Guide

NPS (National Pension System) is India's government-backed retirement savings scheme. It's one of the best tax-saving options available — especially the additional ₹50,000 deduction under Section 80CCD(1B) that goes beyond the ₹1.5L limit of 80C.

## NPS Tier 1 vs Tier 2 — Key Difference

| Feature | Tier 1 | Tier 2 |
|---------|--------|--------|
| **Purpose** | Primary retirement account (mandatory for NPS) | Supplementary savings (voluntary) |
| **Lock-in** | Locked until age 60 (with limited early withdrawals) | **No lock-in** — withdraw anytime |
| **Tax benefit** | ✅ Yes (80C, 80CCD(1B), 80CCD(2)) | ❌ No (no tax deduction) |
| **Minimum opening contribution** | ₹500 | ₹250 |
| **Minimum annual contribution** | ₹1,000 | None |
| **Minimum per contribution** | ₹500 | ₹250 |
| **Exit at 60** | 60% lump sum (tax-free) + 40% annuity (taxable) | Withdraw everything anytime |

You **must open Tier 1** to open Tier 2. Tier 1 is the core NPS account; Tier 2 is optional.

## NPS Tax Benefits — Three Layers

### Layer 1: Section 80C (within the ₹1.5L limit)
- Section 80CCD(1): Employee/self-employed NPS contribution
- Up to 10% of salary (basic + DA) for salaried employees; up to 20% of gross income for self-employed
- But counted within the ₹1.5L overall 80C limit

### Layer 2: Section 80CCD(1B) — Extra ₹50,000
- Additional ₹50,000 NPS contribution over and above 80C
- Completely separate from ₹1.5L 80C limit
- **Effective total**: ₹2L own contribution (₹1.5L 80C + ₹50K 80CCD(1B))
- **Old regime only**

### Layer 3: Section 80CCD(2) — Employer NPS (Best Deal)
- Employer's NPS contribution: Up to **14% of basic salary** (Budget 2025 raised from 10% for private sector)
- This is OUTSIDE the ₹1.5L 80C limit AND outside the ₹50K 80CCD(1B) limit
- No cap from employee's perspective (employer can contribute 14% → no tax)
- Available in BOTH old and new regime

**If your employer offers NPS contribution**: This is the best tax-saving mechanism — ask HR to route part of your salary as employer NPS contribution. It reduces your taxable salary with no additional out-of-pocket cost.

## Who Should Open NPS?

| Profile | Recommendation |
|---------|---------------|
| Private sector employee with long career ahead (25-45 years old) | **Yes** — 80CCD(1B) ₹50K extra deduction is very useful |
| Self-employed professional | **Yes** — both 80CCD(1) and 80CCD(1B) available |
| Anyone who already maxes 80C and wants MORE tax saving | **Yes** — ₹50K additional via 80CCD(1B) |
| Government employees | **Already covered** — they're mandatory NPS subscribers since 2004 |
| Senior citizens (60+) | Not useful — NPS is designed for accumulation before 60 |
| Short career ahead (5 years to retirement) | Evaluate — lump sum at 60 and annuity route; may not be optimal |

## How to Open NPS Online (eNPS)

### Method 1: eNPS Portal (NSDL)

1. Go to enps.nsdl.com
2. Click "Register" → Select "Individual"
3. Choose **Aadhaar-based online registration** (fastest — uses Aadhaar OTP)
4. Fill: name, DOB, address, bank account details, nomination
5. Choose fund manager (pension fund): SBI Pension, HDFC Pension, ICICI Pru Pension, UTI Retirement Solutions, Kotak Pension, Aditya Birla Pension, Axis Pension, Max Life Pension
6. Choose investment option:
   - **Active Choice**: You decide allocation to Equity (E), Corporate Bonds (C), Government Securities (G), and Alternative Investments (A). Max 75% equity allowed.
   - **Auto Choice**: Age-based auto rebalancing (LC75/LC50/LC25 — higher number = more equity when young)
7. Make first contribution (minimum ₹500)
8. **PRAN (Permanent Retirement Account Number)** is generated immediately

### Method 2: Through Bank

Most major banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Kotak, Axis, etc.) have Point of Presence (POP) service for NPS:
- Visit bank branch with KYC documents
- Fill NPS enrollment form
- Bank opens the account and provides PRAN
- Slightly slower than online; useful if you want in-person assistance

### Required Documents for NPS

- PAN card
- Aadhaar card (for online verification)
- Bank account details (for contributions and redemption)
- Passport-size photo
- Signature scan (for offline)

## NPS Contributions

**For salaried employees**: Ask employer to deduct NPS contribution from salary. The employer does a direct debit. Alternatively, you can contribute directly via eNPS portal and claim deduction in ITR.

**For self-employed**: Contribute directly via eNPS or bank's NPS account at any time.

**Monthly SIP**: You can set up auto-debit from bank account for regular NPS contributions.

**Maximum per year**: No upper limit on contributions (though tax benefit is capped as above).

## NPS Withdrawal Rules

| Scenario | What You Get | Tax Treatment |
|----------|-------------|--------------|
| At 60 years (maturity) | 60% lump sum + must annuity 40% | 60% lump sum: Tax-free. Annuity income: Taxable at slab |
| Premature (before 60, after 3 years) | 20% lump sum + must annuity 80% | Similar; annuity taxable |
| Death | Nominee gets 100% | Tax-free for nominee |
| Partial withdrawal | Allowed after 3 years, up to 25% for specific reasons (education, medical, house purchase) | Tax-free |

**Annuity = pension**: 40% of your corpus (at maturity) must purchase an annuity from a Life Insurance Company. This annuity pays monthly pension for life — but this monthly pension is taxable as salary income.

## NPS vs PPF — Quick Comparison

| | NPS | PPF |
|-|-----|-----|
| Lock-in | Until 60 years | 15 years (extendable) |
| Tax on maturity | Partly taxable (annuity) | Fully tax-free |
| Returns | Market-linked (equity/debt mix) | Fixed 7.1% p.a. |
| Flexibility | Some partial withdrawals | Limited partial withdrawals |
| 80CCD(1B) benefit | Yes — extra ₹50K | No — within 80C only |
| Best for | Long-horizon retirement (high-equity NPS = 12–15% potential) | Conservative savers |

**Complementary approach**: PPF for guaranteed component + NPS for market-linked growth + ELSS for additional equity exposure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I already put ₹1.5L in PPF for 80C. Should I also open NPS for the ₹50K 80CCD(1B) benefit?

**Yes, definitely** — if you're in the 20–30% tax bracket. The ₹50,000 80CCD(1B) deduction saves ₹10,000–₹15,000 per year in tax. NPS equity fund returns are competitive (15%+ in good years). The only cost: lock-in until 60 and 40% annuity requirement at maturity. Worth it for most people below 50 with stable income.

### My employer gives 10% of basic as NPS contribution. Is this the 80CCD(2) benefit?

Yes — employer NPS up to 14% of basic (Budget 2025 raised limit for private sector) is deductible under 80CCD(2). This is in addition to your own contributions. It's a salary restructuring benefit — effectively ₹X of your salary goes as employer NPS instead of taxable salary.

### Can I choose 100% equity in NPS?

No — maximum equity allocation in NPS is **75%** (for investors below 50 years; it reduces to 50% after 50 years under Auto Choice). Active Choice allows up to 75% equity (E), rest in C (corporate bonds) and G (government securities).
