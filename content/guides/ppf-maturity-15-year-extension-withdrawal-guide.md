# PPF Maturity After 15 Years — Withdraw, Extend, or Partial Withdrawal

**PPF (Public Provident Fund)** is the government's most popular long-term savings instrument — 7.1% tax-free interest, 80C deduction, and a 15-year lock-in. When your PPF turns 15, you have three choices: withdraw everything, extend with contributions, or extend without contributions.

## PPF Maturity — The Basics

- PPF matures **15 years from the end of the financial year in which the first deposit was made**
- If you opened in July 2010 (FY 2010-11), maturity is **March 31, 2026** (end of FY 2025-26)
- The 15-year period starts from the **end of the financial year** of opening, not the exact date

**Example**:
- Opened: November 2009 (FY 2009-10)
- Maturity: End of FY 2024-25 = **March 31, 2025**

## Option 1: Withdraw Full Balance at Maturity

**Process**:
1. Visit the **bank / post office** where PPF is held
2. Submit **Form C** (Closure Form) within 1 year of maturity (i.e., by March 31 next year for March 31 maturity accounts)
3. Carry: Original PPF passbook + photo ID
4. Amount credited to your savings account within 5-7 working days

**Tax on maturity**: PPF maturity amount is **completely tax-free** (principal + interest) — EEE tax status.

> If you don't claim or extend within 1 year of maturity, the account continues to earn interest but at **Post Office Savings Account rate (4%)** instead of PPF rate (7.1%).

## Option 2: Extend with Contributions (5-Year Block)

You can extend PPF in **5-year blocks** and continue depositing.

**Rules**:
- Submit **Form H** within **1 year** of maturity date
- You continue contributing ₹500–₹1,50,000 per year
- Interest rate remains the same (current PPF rate)
- You can make **one partial withdrawal per year** during the extension (up to 60% of balance at start of extension period)
- Extension is renewable again in another 5-year block, indefinitely

**Why extend with contributions?**
- 80C deduction continues on contributions
- Interest is tax-free
- Higher corpus accumulation — ideal if you don't need the money

**Process**:
1. Visit bank/post office holding your PPF
2. Submit Form H (Extension of Account) + passbook
3. No online option for PPF extension at most banks — in-person required
4. Account continues seamlessly after form submission

## Option 3: Extend Without Contributions (Passive Extension)

If you don't submit Form H within 1 year, OR if you submit a "silent extension" request, your account extends **without further contributions** — but continues earning PPF interest (7.1%).

**Rules**:
- No further deposits accepted
- One partial withdrawal per year allowed (any amount, no cap)
- Interest continues to be tax-free
- No 80C deduction (no contributions)
- This mode continues indefinitely until you close the account

**When to choose this**: If you don't need 80C deductions but want the 7.1% tax-free interest on your existing corpus.

## PPF Partial Withdrawal Rules

Partial withdrawal is allowed from the **7th financial year** of the account, irrespective of maturity.

| Withdrawal Timing | Maximum Allowed |
|------------------|----------------|
| 7th year onwards (before maturity) | 50% of balance at end of **4th year** OR 50% of previous year-end balance, whichever is **lower** |
| During extension with contributions | 60% of balance at start of each 5-year extension block |
| During passive extension | Any amount, once per year |

**One withdrawal per financial year** allowed (before maturity).

**Process for partial withdrawal**:
1. Visit your PPF bank/post office
2. Submit **Form C** (same form as closure, but for partial)
3. State the amount
4. Credited to savings account within 5 working days

## PPF Loan Facility (Before 7th Year)

Before the 7th year (when partial withdrawal opens), you can take a **loan against PPF**:
- From 3rd year to 6th year
- Loan amount: Up to 25% of balance at end of 2nd preceding year
- Interest rate: PPF rate + 1% (currently 8.1%)
- Repayment within 36 months

## PPF Interest Rate History

| Period | PPF Rate |
|--------|---------|
| April 2020 – now | **7.1% p.a.** |
| July 2019 – March 2020 | 7.9% |
| April 2017 – June 2019 | 7.6%–7.8% |
| Earlier years | 8%–8.7% |

Interest is compounded annually and credited on March 31 each year, calculated on the **lowest balance between 5th and end of month**.

## Tax Benefits

| Aspect | Tax Treatment |
|--------|-------------|
| Annual deposit | Deductible under 80C (old regime, up to ₹1.5L) |
| Interest earned | **Tax-free** each year |
| Maturity amount | **Tax-free** |
| Partial withdrawal | **Tax-free** |
| Loan against PPF | Not taxable (it's a loan, not income) |

PPF is one of the few investments with complete EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) status.

## Online PPF Operations

Most banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, etc.) allow:
- Viewing PPF balance and passbook online
- Making deposits online (bank transfer to PPF account)
- Downloading annual interest statement

**What still requires in-person visit**:
- Account opening
- Extension (Form H)
- Closure
- Partial withdrawal
- Loan against PPF

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My PPF matured on March 31, 2025 and I didn't do anything. What happens?

Your account automatically enters **passive extension mode** — it continues earning PPF rate interest (not savings account rate) for the first year after maturity. You have until March 31, 2026 to submit Form H (for active extension) or Form C (for closure). After 1 year of no action, interest drops to savings account rate.

### Can I withdraw part of my PPF at maturity and keep the rest invested?

Yes — you can do a partial withdrawal at maturity (submit Form C for partial amount) and leave the rest in passive extension mode. The remaining balance continues to earn 7.1% tax-free.

### My PPF is at SBI but I moved cities. Can I transfer?

Yes — PPF accounts can be transferred between post offices and authorized banks. Visit your current bank/post office to initiate transfer. The new branch gets the account within 30-45 days.

### If I extend for another 5 years, can I contribute more than ₹1.5 lakh?

No — ₹1,50,000 per year is the maximum contribution limit for PPF regardless of extension. This limit is per PAN/account, not per financial year.

### Is there a penalty for not claiming PPF within 1 year of maturity?

No penalty — but the interest rate drops after 1 year of maturity without action (from PPF rate to savings account rate). There's no fine, but you lose the high-interest-rate window.
