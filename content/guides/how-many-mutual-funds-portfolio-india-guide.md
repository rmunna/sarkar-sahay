# How Many Mutual Funds Should You Have India 2026

Most investors have too many funds. A portfolio of 15 mutual funds sounds diversified but it's just owning the same top 100 Indian stocks multiple times. Here's what actually works.

---

## The Problem: Over-Diversification

**Investor A**: 12 large-cap and flexi-cap funds
**Investor B**: 1 Nifty 50 index fund

After 10 years, Investor B almost certainly outperforms Investor A.

Why? **Portfolio overlap**. Reliance Industries, TCS, HDFC Bank, and Infosys appear in the top 5 holdings of every large-cap fund. Holding 12 large-cap funds means owning Reliance 12 times over — the positions are correlated, not diversified.

More funds:
- No additional diversification after 5–6 different categories
- Higher combined expense ratio (each fund has its own)
- More complexity (tracking 15 XIRR numbers, 15 LTCG calculations)
- Harder to rebalance and review

---

## The Optimal Number: 3–5 Funds

**For most salaried investors in India, 3–5 funds covering different categories is ideal**:

### The Simple 3-Fund Portfolio (Index-Based)

| Fund | Category | % |
|------|----------|---|
| Nifty 50 Index Fund | Large-cap equity | 60% |
| Nifty Midcap 150 Index Fund | Mid-cap equity | 20% |
| Short Duration Debt / Liquid Fund | Debt | 20% |

**Why it works**: Zero overlap between large-cap and mid-cap indexes. Debt provides stability. Total portfolio covers ~650 Indian companies. Cost: 0.05–0.20% expense ratio.

### The 4-Fund Portfolio (Adding Flexi-Cap)

| Fund | Category | % |

| Nifty 50 Index Fund | Large-cap | 40% |
| Flexi-Cap Active Fund | Across market cap | 30% |
| Nifty Midcap 150 Index | Mid-cap | 10% |

## Check Your Portfolio Overlap

**How to check**:

1. **ValueResearch Online** (valueresearchonline.com → Portfolio → Portfolio Overlap)
2. **Morningstar India** (morningstar.in → Portfolio Tools)
3. **AMFI / Groww Fund Comparison**: Compare top holdings of two funds

**Warning signs of high overlap**:
- Two large-cap or two flexi-cap active funds → likely 70–80% overlap
- Any combination of large-cap funds → all hold Reliance, TCS, HDFC Bank in top 5
- "Diversified" portfolio with 8 funds but all equity → still 70%+ overlap in large-caps

## Signs Your Portfolio Needs Consolidation

You have too many funds if:
- You can't remember why you bought half of them
- You have 3+ large-cap or flexi-cap active funds
- Any two funds have >60% portfolio overlap
- Your total SIP across 10 funds is ₹5K/month (₹500 per fund — too small to track)
- XIRR for your whole portfolio is LOWER than Nifty 50 index (you're paying more in expenses + active manager underperformance to earn less)

---

## How to Consolidate (Simplify Your Portfolio)

**Steps**:
1. List all funds with current value
2. Check overlap between similar funds
3. Decide which to keep (keep index fund over active in large-cap; keep better-performing active in mid/small)
4. Sell underperforming/overlapping funds (mind LTCG and exit load — sell funds older than 1 year to minimize tax)
5. Reinvest proceeds in the funds you're keeping

**Exit load check**: Most equity funds have 1% exit load if sold within 12 months. Wait for units to complete 12 months before selling.

**LTCG**: On equity funds, LTCG above ₹1.25L = 12.5% tax. Consolidating a large portfolio may trigger LTCG — plan across financial years if possible.

---

## The Counter-Argument: When More Funds Are Okay

More than 5 funds makes sense if:
- You have different investment goals with different timelines (education corpus = debt-heavy, retirement = equity-heavy — these can be in separate funds/folios)
- You're investing in fundamentally different categories (domestic equity + international equity + gold + debt = 4 different asset classes, not overlapping)
- You're managing portfolio for multiple family members (spouse, child's education) — separate folios are fine

The problem is NOT having many funds for many purposes. The problem is having many funds for the SAME purpose (5 large-cap funds all meant for "long-term wealth creation").

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I have 15 mutual funds. Should I immediately sell all but 5?

Don't sell immediately — there may be exit loads or LTCG tax. Instead:
1. Stop SIPs in the funds you want to eliminate
2. Start SIPs in the funds you want to keep
3. Wait for old funds' units to turn 12 months old (no exit load, LTCG treatment)
4. Sell old fund units systematically, checking LTCG implications

### Which is better: one fund or diversified SIPs?

One well-chosen index fund (Nifty 50) beats most 10-fund diversified portfolios after 10 years net of expenses. Simplicity outperforms complexity in investing.

### Should I have separate fund for different goals?

Yes — but separate accounts/folios, not separate "types" of funds. For a 10-year goal: equity index fund. For a 3-year goal: short-duration debt or balanced advantage fund. Goal-based investing is good; duplicating within the same goal bucket is not.

### Nifty 50 vs Nifty 500 — which is better for the single-fund approach?

Nifty 500 includes mid and small caps (400 more companies beyond Nifty 50). Slightly higher volatility but better long-term representation of Indian economy. Nifty 500 index funds from SBI, UTI, and Motilal are good choices.
