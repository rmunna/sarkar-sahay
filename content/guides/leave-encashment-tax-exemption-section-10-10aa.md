# Leave Encashment Tax Exemption India 2026 — Section 10(10AA)

**Leave encashment** means receiving cash from your employer in exchange for accumulated, unutilized earned leave. The tax treatment depends on *when* you receive it (retirement vs during service) and *who* you work for (government vs private sector).

> **Quick answer**: Leave encashment at retirement/resignation — private employees exempt up to **₹25 lakh** (increased in Budget 2023 from ₹3 lakh). Government employees: **fully exempt**. During employment: **fully taxable**.

## Tax Rules at a Glance

| Scenario | Tax Treatment |
|----------|--------------|
| **Central/State Govt employee** — leave encashment at retirement/superannuation | ✅ **Fully exempt** (no limit) |
| **Private sector employee** — leave encashment at retirement/superannuation | ✅ **Exempt up to ₹25 lakh** (Budget 2023) |
| **Private sector employee** — leave encashment on resignation/termination | ✅ **Exempt up to ₹25 lakh** |
| **Leave encashment received during service** (while still employed) | ❌ **Fully taxable** at slab rate |
| **Leave encashment at death** (paid to legal heir) | ✅ **Fully exempt** (no limit, any employer) |

## Budget 2023 Change — ₹25 Lakh Limit

Prior to Budget 2023, the exemption limit for private sector employees was only **₹3 lakh** — a limit set in 2002. Budget 2023 (effective FY 2023-24) raised this to **₹25 lakh**.

| Period | Private Sector Leave Encashment Exemption |
|--------|------------------------------------------|
| Before FY 2023-24 | ₹3,00,000 |
| FY 2023-24 onwards | **₹25,00,000** |

**Note**: The ₹25 lakh is the **total lifetime limit** — not per year. If you received some leave encashment at one employer and some at another, the total exempt amount across all employers is ₹25 lakh.

## How Exemption is Calculated (Private Sector)

The actual exempt amount for private sector employees at retirement is the **lowest of**:
1. Actual leave encashment received
2. Average salary of last 10 months × unutilized earned leave (capped at 30 days per year of service)
3. ₹25,00,000 (maximum limit)

**Average salary** = (Basic + Dearness Allowance) averaged over last 10 months before retirement

**Example**:
- Employee retires after 25 years of service
- Average monthly salary (Basic + DA) last 10 months: ₹80,000
- Unutilized earned leave: 300 days (25 years × 30 days – taken leave)
- Capped leave for calculation: 25 years × 30 days = 750 days (but actual 300 days used)
- Leave encashment received from employer: ₹7,20,000 (300 days × ₹80,000/26 days per month)

## Leave Encashment During Service — Always Taxable

If you are still employed and receive payment for accumulated leave (many companies allow periodic leave encashment), this is treated as **salary income** and is fully taxable at your income tax slab rate. No exemption under 10(10AA) applies.

The exemption under Section 10(10AA) is available **only** when you cease employment (retirement, superannuation, resignation, termination).

## Leave Encashment at Death

If an employee dies while in service, the leave encashment paid to their **legal heir or nominee** is **fully exempt** — no limit, no slab calculation. This applies to both government and private sector employees.

## How to Report Leave Encashment in ITR

### If Fully Exempt

1. In ITR: Schedule EI (Exempt Income)
2. Enter the amount under "Leave encashment under Section 10(10AA)"
3. No tax deducted, no tax payable on this amount

### If Partially Taxable (Amount > ₹25 Lakh)

1. Exempt portion (₹25L): Report in Schedule EI
2. Taxable portion (amount above ₹25L): Report in Schedule S (Salary Income)
3. Your employer may reflect this in Form 16 with the split

### If Encashment During Service

- Reported in Form 16 as part of your salary
- Already included in your gross salary figure in ITR
- No separate action needed

## TDS on Leave Encashment

**Employers must deduct TDS** on leave encashment paid to private sector employees if the amount exceeds the applicable threshold. Your employer calculates the exempt portion and deducts TDS only on the taxable part.

If TDS has been incorrectly deducted (e.g., on a fully exempt amount), you can claim a refund while filing ITR.

## Leave Encashment vs Gratuity — Key Difference

| Aspect | Leave Encashment | Gratuity |
|--------|-----------------|---------|
| **What it is** | Payment for unused earned leave | Ex-gratia payment for 5+ years of service |
| **Who pays** | Employer | Employer |
| **Govt employees** | Fully exempt | Fully exempt |
| **Private employees** | Exempt up to ₹25 lakh | Exempt up to ₹20 lakh (under Gratuity Act) |
| **When taxable** | During service encashment | Amount above ₹20 lakh or not under Gratuity Act |
| **ITR section** | 10(10AA) | 10(10) |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I received ₹10 lakh leave encashment when I resigned from a private company at age 40. Is it fully exempt?

Yes — resignation triggers the 10(10AA) exemption. Since ₹10 lakh < ₹25 lakh lifetime limit, it is fully exempt, provided it satisfies the lowest-of-three formula (usually it does unless the calculation gives a lower figure).

### I worked at two companies. First gave ₹15L encashment, second gave ₹20L. How much is exempt?

The lifetime cap is ₹25 lakh. First employer: ₹15L is exempt. Second employer: only ₹10L is exempt (to reach ₹25L total); the remaining ₹10L is taxable salary. Keep track of exemptions claimed at previous employers.

### My employer deducted TDS on the full leave encashment amount saying nothing is exempt. Is this correct?

No — private sector employees are entitled to the ₹25 lakh exemption. If your employer deducted TDS on the exempt portion, claim a refund when filing ITR. Report the exempt portion under Schedule EI.

### Does the ₹25L limit cover leave encashment from multiple stints at the same company?

The limit is per-employee lifetime across all employers. If you've claimed exemption earlier at a previous company (and reported it in ITR), the remaining headroom is ₹25L minus what was previously exempt.
