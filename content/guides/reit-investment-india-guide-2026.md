# REIT Investment India 2026 — Complete Beginner's Guide

A **REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust)** lets you invest in commercial real estate (offices, malls, warehouses) without buying an actual property. You buy units on the stock exchange just like shares, get regular "distributions" (income), and benefit from property value appreciation.

India has four listed REITs as of 2026:
1. **Embassy Office Parks REIT** (embassy offices in Bengaluru, NCR)
2. **Mindspace Business Parks REIT** (tech parks in Hyderabad, Mumbai)
3. **Brookfield India Real Estate Trust** (grade-A offices in NCR, Kolkata, Mumbai)
4. **Nexus Select Trust** (retail malls across India)

## How REITs Work

1. REIT acquires and manages commercial properties
2. Collects rent from tenants (IT companies, banks, retailers)
3. Must distribute **at least 90% of net distributable cash flows** to unit holders every 6 months (SEBI requirement)
4. You receive regular distributions (similar to dividends) + NAV appreciation

## REIT vs Buying Real Estate Directly

| Factor | REIT | Direct Property |
|--------|------|----------------|
| **Minimum investment** | ₹300–₹500 (1 unit) | ₹30L–₹5Cr+ |
| **Liquidity** | High (sell on exchange anytime) | Low (months to sell) |
| **Rental income** | Distribution paid to you | You manage tenant |
| **Management hassle** | None (professional management) | High (tenant issues, maintenance) |
| **Diversification** | Multiple Grade-A properties | Usually 1 property |
| **Transparency** | Quarterly disclosures, audited | None |
| **Entry/exit** | Click to buy/sell | Stamp duty + registration cost |
| **Leverage** | REIT uses some debt | Your EMI is your leverage |

## How to Invest in REITs

### Step 1: Need a Demat + Trading Account
Same demat account you use for stocks/MFs. Any broker (Zerodha, Groww, Upstox, Angel) works.

### Step 2: Search on NSE/BSE
- **Embassy REIT**: Ticker EMBASSY
- **Mindspace REIT**: Ticker MINDSPACE
- **Brookfield REIT**: Ticker BIRET
- **Nexus Select Trust**: Ticker NEXUS

### Step 3: Buy Units
- Minimum lot size: 1 unit (SEBI reduced lot size to make REITs accessible)
- Current prices: ₹300–₹500 per unit approximately (varies)
- Buy any number of units you want

## REIT Returns — Distribution Yield + Appreciation

REIT returns have two components:
1. **Distribution yield**: Cash distributions paid to you (like rental income)
2. **Capital appreciation**: Unit price appreciation as property values rise

**Distribution yields** (indicative, as of early 2026):
- Embassy REIT: ~7–8% annualized yield
- Mindspace REIT: ~7–8.5%
- Brookfield REIT: ~8–9%
- Nexus Select Trust: ~7.5–8%

Distribution yields are higher than typical equity MF dividend yields because REITs must distribute 90% of income.

**Total returns (historical)**: REITs listed since 2019–2022. Embassy REIT (longest track record) has delivered ~8–12% total return annually including distributions.

## REIT Tax Treatment — Complex but Important

REIT distributions have three components with different tax treatment:

| Distribution Component | Tax Treatment |
|----------------------|--------------|
| **Interest income** (from REIT's loans to SPVs) | Taxable at your slab rate |
| **Dividend** (from SPV below 100% ownership) | Taxable at slab rate |
| **Capital repayment** (return of investment) | NOT taxable (reduces cost basis) |
| **Capital gains on selling units** | LTCG (>36 months) at 20%; STCG (<36 months) at slab rate |

**Each distribution notice from the REIT specifies what % is each component.** You pay tax accordingly in the year of distribution.

**TDS on distributions**: REIT deducts TDS at 10% on interest and dividend portions of distributions. TDS appears in Form 26AS — you get credit at ITR filing.

**Key tax point**: The **interest component** is the largest component of most REIT distributions and is taxed at your slab rate. If you're in the 30% slab, approximately 20–25% of your REIT distribution goes to tax.

## Who Should Invest in REITs?

REITs are suitable for:
- Investors who want **real estate exposure** without the hassle of buying property
- Retirees or near-retirees who want **regular income** (distributions every 6 months)
- Portfolio diversification beyond equity and debt
- People in lower tax brackets (10–20%) where the slab-rate distributions are more tax-efficient

**REITs may not be ideal for**:
- High-income investors (30% bracket) where slab-rate distributions are heavily taxed
- Young investors with long horizon who should prioritize equity MF for higher expected returns
- Short-term investors (REIT is 3+ year play for capital gains benefits)

## Risks of REIT Investing

| Risk | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Vacancy risk** | If tenants vacate, distributions fall |
| **Interest rate risk** | Rising rates may hurt REIT prices (REITs use debt) |
| **Refinancing risk** | If REITs can't refinance debt cheaply, distributions fall |
| **Sector risk** | Office REITs affected by WFH trends; mall REITs by e-commerce |
| **Price risk** | Unit price can fall like any listed security |

Embassy REIT had COVID-impact challenges in 2020–21 when office occupancy fell. Distributions were maintained but NAV fell significantly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I'm a salaried person with ₹20L income. Is REIT worth it?

At 30% bracket, your REIT distributions are taxed at 30%. On 8% distribution yield, effective post-tax yield = ~5.6%. A small finance bank FD at 8% post-tax (after 30% tax) = ~5.6% too. REITs win only on: (1) capital appreciation, (2) diversification, (3) liquidity advantage over property. Worth a 5–10% allocation of your portfolio.

### Can I buy REITs through mutual funds?

Yes — some diversified equity funds hold REIT units. Alternatively, there are specific "Real Estate Funds" that invest in REITs. But direct REIT investment gives you full transparency on distributions and tax components.

### Are InvITs the same as REITs?

No — InvITs (Infrastructure Investment Trusts) are similar in structure but invest in infrastructure projects (toll roads, power transmission, pipelines). InvITs (IRB InvIT, PowerGrid InvIT, India Grid Trust) are more infrastructure-focused vs REITs' real estate focus. Both have similar distribution structures and tax treatment.
