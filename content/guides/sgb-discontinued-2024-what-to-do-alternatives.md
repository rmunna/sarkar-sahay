# SGB Discontinued 2024 — What Happens to Your Existing SGBs and What's Next

The Government of India **stopped issuing new Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) tranches** in the Union Budget 2024-25 (presented July 2024). This ends a 9-year programme that started with Series I in November 2015.

Here's what this means for existing SGB investors and what to do if you wanted to buy SGBs.

## Why Was SGB Discontinued?

The official reason: the government cited **high cost of borrowing** via SGBs. Each SGB series required the government to pay:
1. **2.5% annual interest** to investors
2. **Gold price appreciation** (investors get higher of issue price or current gold price at redemption)

With gold prices rising significantly, the cost to the government ballooned. Alternative gold investment products (Gold ETFs managed by RBI, digital gold) were seen as sufficient.

## What Happens to Existing SGBs?

**Nothing changes for existing SGB holders**:
- Your SGBs continue to earn **2.5% per year** interest (paid semi-annually)
- SGBs will be **redeemed at maturity** (8 years from issue date) at current gold prices
- **Premature exit** after 5 years on coupon payment dates remains available
- **Trading on exchange**: Your SGBs can be sold on NSE/BSE through your Demat account

## How to Redeem Your SGB at Maturity

When your SGB matures:

**If held in Demat account (via broker)**:
1. Redemption is **automatic** — money is credited to your bank account linked to Demat
2. No action needed from your end
3. Confirmation comes from your broker/depository

**If held as RBI Bonds Certificate (paper)**:
1. Contact your bank (where you bought the SGB) 30 days before maturity
2. Submit the certificate for redemption
3. Bank processes and credits redemption amount to your account

**Amount you receive**: Current gold price × number of grams × 58 (since 1 unit = 1 gram gold × 58)

## Premature Redemption of SGB (After 5 Years)

You can exit SGB before the 8-year maturity, starting from the **5th year anniversary** on each semi-annual interest payment date:

1. Contact your bank or broker **15 days before** the next coupon date
2. Submit premature redemption request
3. **Price**: Current gold price at redemption date (not guaranteed profit)
4. Amount credited within 7 working days of coupon date

> Note: If gold price is lower than what you paid, premature redemption can result in a loss. The 8-year full tenure protects against this in most cases.

## Can You Still Buy SGB in 2025-2026?

**New tranches**: The government has not announced any new SGB issuance. As of mid-2026, no new series have been issued since 2024.

**Secondary market**: You can still buy existing SGBs from other investors via the stock exchange:
1. **Zerodha/Groww/HDFC Securities/others**: Search for SGB on NSE under "SGBMAR28" or similar codes
2. SGBs trade at market price (may be at premium/discount to RBI issue price)
3. **Tax treatment differs for secondary market purchases**: Capital gains on buying/selling SGB on exchange are taxed normally (not the same tax benefits as primary issuance)

## Taxation of SGB — Important for Existing Holders

| Scenario | Tax Treatment |
|----------|--------------|
| Interest (2.5%/year) | Taxable as "Income from Other Sources" at your income slab |
| Redemption at maturity (8 years, RBI) | **Capital Gains TAX FREE** (if held for 8 years and redeemed via RBI) |
| Premature redemption via RBI (after 5 years) | Indexed Long-Term Capital Gains (20% with indexation) |
| Sold on stock exchange | Short/Long-term capital gains based on holding period |

## Best Alternatives to SGB in 2025

Now that new SGB tranches aren't available, here are the best alternatives:

### 1. Gold ETFs (Best Alternative)
- **Examples**: Nippon India Gold ETF, SBI Gold ETF, HDFC Gold ETF
- **Traded on**: NSE/BSE via your Demat account
- **Returns**: Track gold price (no 2.5% interest like SGB)
- **Tax**: Same LTCG/STCG as stocks
- **Minimum**: 1 unit (~₹60-70 for most ETFs)
- **Liquidity**: High — can sell any trading day

### 2. Gold Mutual Funds
- **Examples**: Nippon India Gold Fund, SBI Gold Fund
- **For**: People without Demat accounts
- **Invest**: Via SIP (₹500/month)
- **Tax**: Same as Gold ETF (LTCG after 3 years: 20% with indexation)

### 3. Multi-Asset Allocation Funds
- **Invest in**: Mix of equity, debt, and gold
- **Gold allocation**: Typically 10–25%
- **Best for**: Diversified investors

### 4. Digital Gold (Paytm, Google Pay)
- **Purchase**: Fractions as small as ₹1
- **Storage**: Guaranteed by third-party vault
- **Not regulated by SEBI/RBI**: Higher counterparty risk than ETF
- **Best for**: Very small amounts; not recommended as primary gold investment

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will the government issue new SGBs in the future?

No official announcement has been made. Market participants expect new tranches are unlikely in the near term given the cost structure. Watch RBI announcements or the annual Union Budget for any reversal.

### I bought SGB in 2020 and want to sell now. How?

You have two options:
1. **Secondary market**: Sell via Zerodha/Groww — search for your SGB ISIN code in the search bar and sell like a stock. You'll get current market price.
2. **Premature redemption via RBI**: Wait until 5 years from issue date (2025 if you bought in 2020), then submit request to issuing bank on coupon date.

### My bank says the SGB can be redeemed but I haven't received money after maturity. What to do?

Contact your bank with: SGB certificate/ISIN, bank account details, Aadhaar/PAN. Maturity processing can take up to 30 working days. If the bank delays beyond 60 days, file a complaint at bankingombudsman.rbi.org.in.

### If gold prices fall at my SGB maturity, will I still get the original investment back?

No — SGB is redeemed at **current gold price**, not guaranteed at original price. If gold falls, you could receive less than invested. However, the 2.5% annual interest partially offsets price risk. Over 8 years, gold has historically been higher than entry price for most SGB series.
