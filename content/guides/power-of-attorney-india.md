# Power of Attorney in India – Types, Registration & Process

A **Power of Attorney (POA)** is a legal document that authorises one person (the "agent" or "attorney") to act on behalf of another person (the "principal") in legal, financial, or property matters. POA is governed by the **Powers of Attorney Act, 1882** in India and is widely used when a person is unable to be physically present to handle legal or financial transactions.

This guide explains the types of POA, the registration process, documents required, stamp duty, revocation procedure, and special rules for NRIs.

---

## What Is a Power of Attorney?

A Power of Attorney is a written authorisation that allows the appointed agent to:

- Buy, sell, or manage property on behalf of the principal
- Operate bank accounts or handle financial transactions
- Represent the principal in court or government offices
- Sign documents, file taxes, or manage business affairs

The principal must be of **sound mind** and **at least 18 years of age** at the time of executing the POA. A POA does not transfer ownership — it only grants authority to act.

---

## Types of Power of Attorney

### 1. General Power of Attorney (GPA)

A **General Power of Attorney** grants broad authority to the agent to handle multiple types of transactions on behalf of the principal. It is commonly used when someone needs to manage all affairs — property, banking, legal — while the principal is away.

**Key features:**
- Covers multiple actions and decisions
- Remains valid until revoked or the principal becomes incapacitated
- Often used by NRIs who need someone in India to manage their affairs

### 2. Special Power of Attorney (SPA)

A **Special Power of Attorney** grants limited authority for a specific task or transaction. Once that task is completed, the POA automatically becomes invalid.

**Key features:**
- Limited to a single transaction (e.g., selling a specific property)
- Automatically expires upon completion of the specified task
- More secure than GPA as scope is restricted

### 3. Durable Power of Attorney

A **Durable Power of Attorney** remains valid even if the principal becomes mentally incapacitated. This is particularly useful for elderly individuals or those anticipating health issues.

**Key features:**
- Explicitly states it survives incapacity of the principal
- Must include specific "durable" language in the document
- Useful for long-term care and estate planning

> **Note:** In India, a regular POA (non-durable) automatically becomes void if the principal becomes mentally unsound, as per the Indian Contract Act, 1872 (Section 201).

## Registration Process for Power of Attorney

### Is Registration Mandatory?

Under the **Registration Act, 1908 (Section 33)**, a POA that authorises the agent to execute documents related to **immovable property** (which themselves require registration) must be registered or authenticated. For other POA types, registration is optional but highly recommended for legal validity.

The **Supreme Court of India** in *Suraj Lamp & Industries Pvt. Ltd. vs State of Haryana (2012)* ruled that property transfers through GPA (without a registered sale deed) are **not legally valid**. A POA alone cannot transfer property ownership.

### Step-by-Step Registration Process

1. **Draft the POA** — Prepare the POA document on appropriate stamp paper. Mention the principal, agent, scope of authority, and duration clearly. You can use a lawyer or legal services portal for drafting.

2. **Purchase stamp paper** — Buy non-judicial stamp paper of the value prescribed by your state (see stamp duty section below).

3. **Visit the Sub-Registrar Office (SRO)** — Both the principal and agent (or their representatives) must visit the SRO under whose jurisdiction the principal resides.

4. **Carry required documents** — Bring all originals and photocopies (listed below).

5. **Witness signatures** — Two witnesses must be present and sign the document at the SRO.

6. **Biometric verification** — Most states now require Aadhaar-based biometric verification at the SRO.

7. **Pay registration fee** — Pay the applicable registration fee (varies by state, typically ₹100–₹1,000).

8. **Collect registered POA** — The registered document is returned after processing (usually 2–7 working days).

## Stamp Duty for Power of Attorney

Stamp duty varies significantly across states. Below are indicative rates:

| State | GPA (Property-related) | SPA |
|---|---|---|
| Delhi | ₹100 (if for family) / Market-linked for others | ₹100 |
| Maharashtra | 5% of property value (if authorising sale) / ₹500 otherwise | ₹100–₹500 |
| Karnataka | ₹200–₹500 | ₹200 |
| Uttar Pradesh | ₹10–₹100 | ₹10 |
| Tamil Nadu | ₹100 (non-property) / Higher for property transactions | ₹100 |

> **Important:** Stamp duty rates change periodically. Always verify current rates at your state's registration department website or the Sub-Registrar Office.

## How to Revoke a Power of Attorney

A principal can revoke a POA at any time (unless it is an irrevocable POA coupled with interest). The revocation process:

1. **Execute a Revocation Deed** — Draft a deed of revocation on stamp paper, mentioning the original POA details.

2. **Register the Revocation** — Get the revocation deed registered at the same SRO where the original POA was registered.

3. **Notify the Agent** — Send a written notice to the agent informing them of the revocation. Keep proof of delivery.

4. **Notify Relevant Parties** — Inform banks, property registry offices, and other institutions where the POA was being used.

5. **Publish a Public Notice** — It is advisable (and sometimes required) to publish a notice of revocation in a local newspaper.

**A POA automatically terminates when:**
- The principal dies
- The principal becomes mentally unsound (unless it's a durable POA)
- The specified task is completed (for SPA)
- The agent dies or becomes incapacitated
- The principal revokes it

## Power of Attorney for NRIs

NRIs frequently use POA to manage property and financial matters in India. Special rules apply:

### Execution of POA Abroad

1. **Draft the POA** — Can be drafted in India or abroad.

2. **Notarisation** — Get the POA notarised by a Notary Public in the country of residence.

3. **Apostille / Attestation** — 
   - For **Hague Convention countries**: Get an Apostille certificate from the designated authority.
   - For **non-Hague countries**: Get the POA attested by the **Indian Embassy or Consulate**.

4. **Adjudication in India** — Once the POA reaches India, it must be adjudicated (stamped) within **3 months** of arrival. Submit it to the Collector of Stamps or the Sub-Registrar for adjudication and payment of stamp duty.

5. **Registration** — Register the POA at the appropriate SRO in India if it involves immovable property.

### Key Points for NRI POA
- POA executed outside India is valid in India only after proper attestation and adjudication
- The 3-month window for adjudication starts from the date the document arrives in India
- NRIs should use Special POA (SPA) for specific transactions to limit risk
- Some banks require the POA holder to provide a fresh POA for banking operations

## Important Tips

- **Use SPA over GPA** — Limit the scope of authority to reduce misuse risk. GPA grants broad powers that can be exploited.
- **Always register the POA** — Unregistered POAs may not be accepted by banks, registrars, or courts.
- **POA does not equal ownership** — A GPA cannot be used to "sell" property. A registered sale deed is always required for property transfer.
- **Include an expiry date** — Specify a validity period to prevent indefinite use.
- **Keep certified copies** — Maintain multiple attested copies for use at different offices.
- **Verify agent's credentials** — Choose a trusted family member or professional. The agent has a fiduciary duty to act in the principal's interest.
- **Consult a lawyer** — POA laws have state-specific nuances. Legal advice ensures the document is valid and enforceable.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### 1. Can a Power of Attorney holder sell property in India?

A POA holder can execute a sale deed on behalf of the principal **only if** the POA specifically authorises the sale of that property. However, the sale deed must be separately registered. A GPA-based property sale without a registered sale deed is **not valid** as per the Supreme Court ruling in *Suraj Lamp (2012)*.

### 2. Is a notarised POA valid without registration?

A notarised but unregistered POA may be accepted for some purposes (bank operations, minor legal tasks), but it will **not be accepted** by the Sub-Registrar for property transactions involving immovable property. Registration is strongly recommended.

### 3. How long is a Power of Attorney valid?

A POA remains valid until it is revoked by the principal, the principal dies, or the principal becomes mentally incapacitated (unless it's a durable POA). An SPA expires automatically once the specified task is completed. You can also set an explicit expiry date in the document.

### 4. Can a POA be executed on plain paper?

No. A POA must be executed on **non-judicial stamp paper** of the value prescribed by the respective state. A POA on plain paper is not legally enforceable. Stamp duty must be paid as per the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 and the relevant state amendment.

### 5. What is the difference between GPA and SPA?

A **General Power of Attorney (GPA)** grants broad authority to handle multiple types of transactions, while a **Special Power of Attorney (SPA)** is limited to a specific task or transaction. SPA is more secure and recommended for one-time transactions like selling a specific property.

### 6. Can an NRI give POA to someone in India remotely?

Yes. An NRI can execute a POA abroad, get it notarised and apostilled (or attested by the Indian Embassy), send it to India, and get it adjudicated and registered within 3 months of its arrival in India. Physical presence of the NRI in India is not required.

### 7. Can a Power of Attorney be revoked?

Yes. The principal can revoke a POA at any time by executing and registering a deed of revocation. The agent and all relevant institutions must be notified. It is also advisable to publish a public notice in a newspaper.
