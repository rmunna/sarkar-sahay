# Asset Allocation & Portfolio Rebalancing India 2026 — Practical Guide

"Don't put all eggs in one basket" is the simplest statement of asset allocation. But the specifics matter enormously — how much in equity, debt, gold? When to rebalance? What changes with age? Here's a systematic approach for Indian investors.

---

## Why Asset Allocation Matters More Than Stock Picking

Studies consistently show that **asset allocation determines 90%+ of portfolio returns** over long periods. Picking the right large-cap fund vs another large-cap fund matters less than deciding how much to put in equities at all.

**Example**: Investor A — 100% equity. Investor B — 60% equity, 40% debt.
- In bull market (2021): A returns 30%, B returns 20%
- In bear market (2022): A falls -20%, B falls -10%
- 10-year total return: A might edge ahead, but B sleeps better and sticks to the plan

**B will outperform A in practice** because B didn't panic-sell in 2022.

## The 100-Minus-Age Rule (Simplified Starting Point)

A classic starting formula:
- **Equity %** = 100 − Your Age
- **Debt %** = Age
- Optional: Take 5–10% from equity and put into gold

**Examples**:

| Age | Equity | Debt | Gold |
|-----|--------|------|------|
| 25 | 70–75% | 15–20% | 5–10% |
| 35 | 60–65% | 25–30% | 5–10% |
| 45 | 50–55% | 35–40% | 5–10% |
| 55 | 40–45% | 45–50% | 5–10% |
| 65 | 25–30% | 60–65% | 5–10% |

**This is a starting point, not a rigid rule.** Adjust based on:
- Risk tolerance (can you sleep during -30% market fall?)
- Income stability (government job vs startup = very different risk capacity)
- Other assets (own a house = real estate exposure already high; reduce real estate allocation)
- Goals and timelines

## When to Rebalance — Triggers and Frequency

**Two rebalancing triggers**:

### 1. Time-Based (Annual)
- Every year in April (after new financial year begins, before April SIP investments)
- Review your actual allocation vs target
- Bring each class back to target %

### 2. Threshold-Based (5% Drift Rule)
- If any class drifts more than 5% from target: rebalance immediately
- Example: Target equity 60%, current 68% (market ran up) → sell equity, buy debt

**Why both?** Annual review is disciplined maintenance; threshold triggers handle extreme market moves.

---

## How to Rebalance — Practical Steps

Say your target is 60% equity / 30% debt / 10% gold, but current is 70% equity / 20% debt / 10% gold (market rallied):

**Option A**: Sell equity, buy debt
- Sell ₹X from index fund → buy FD/liquid fund
- Tax implication: LTCG on equity gains (if held >12 months) at 12.5%

**Option B**: New money into underweight class
- Direct all new investments into debt until rebalanced
- No tax event — preferred if you have ongoing SIPs or fresh capital
- Slower to rebalance but tax-efficient

**Option C**: STP (Systematic Transfer Plan)
- Move money from equity MF to debt MF monthly via STP
- Tax-efficient (spread gains over time)
- Takes 3–6 months to complete rebalance

---

## Tax Implications of Rebalancing

| Rebalancing Action | Tax Event |
|------------------|-----------|
| Selling equity MF held >12 months | LTCG 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25L |
| Selling equity MF held <12 months | STCG 20% on gains |
| Selling debt MF | Gains as per slab rate |
| Selling SGB | If held 8 years: tax-free on maturity; if sold early in secondary market: LTCG 20% |

**Tax-efficient rebalancing**: 
1. Use new money to rebalance before selling
2. If selling, prioritize selling assets where gain is under ₹1.25L (LTCG exemption)
3. Use STP to spread gains across financial years

## Common Asset Allocation Mistakes in India

| Mistake | Problem |
|---------|---------|
| 100% in bank FD | Inflation erodes returns over 20 years |
| 100% in equity | Panic-selling during 2020/2022-type crashes locks in losses |
| Physical gold >10% | Not productive asset; storage/security issue; GST on making charges |
| Neglecting debt (only equity + FD) | No intermediate buffer; FD is not the same as proper debt allocation |
| Never reviewing allocation | Starts at 60:40 in 2015, by 2025 has drifted to 85:15 without knowing |
| Treating EPF/PPF as "not an investment" | These ARE your debt allocation — count them |

## Special Situations — Allocation Adjustments

### Planning a Home Loan in 2–3 Years

Reduce equity allocation to 40% and increase debt/liquid. A 30% market fall when you need the down payment = crisis.

### After Receiving Large Windfall (Inheritance, Bonus, ESOP vesting)

Don't lump sum into equity at market high. Use:
1. 30% immediately (market may be fairly valued)
2. 70% via STP over 12 months (equity MF to debt MF monthly transfer)

### Divorce/Life Change

Rebuild financial plan — liability structure may change (alimony, new household expenses). Adjust risk accordingly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I count EPF/PPF in my debt allocation?

Yes — absolutely. Many Indians have large EPF balances and think they have no "debt investment." EPF IS your debt. If EPF is already ₹20L and your target debt is 30% of ₹50L portfolio (₹15L), you're overweight debt — increase equity.

### Gold has done very well recently. Should I increase to 20%?

Past performance isn't a reason to increase allocation. Gold's role is as a hedge/diversifier, not a return driver. 10% is typically adequate. Going above 15% for non-specific reasons means chasing recent returns.

### Is real estate counted in my portfolio allocation?

If you have investment property (not the home you live in), count its estimated value in your total portfolio and categorize as real estate/alternative. Many middle-class Indians are heavily overweight real estate — buying a ₹1Cr investment flat when your liquid portfolio is ₹20L means your allocation is 83% real estate.
