# Work From Home Tax Benefits India 2026 — What You Can and Cannot Claim

The pandemic normalized WFH, but India's Income Tax Act has not added specific WFH deductions for salaried employees. Here's the honest picture of what's available, what's not, and how freelancers have a much better deal.

---

## The Hard Truth: Salaried WFH Employees Have Limited Tax Benefits

India's income tax law does not have a specific "home office deduction" or "WFH allowance" for salaried employees — unlike the US or UK where such deductions exist.

**Under the New Tax Regime (default)**: 
- Only deduction is ₹75,000 standard deduction
- No separate WFH expense deduction
- Internet, electricity, furniture — none deductible

**Under the Old Tax Regime**:
- Slightly more flexibility (employer-paid allowances can be structured)
- But still no direct "home office" deduction for salaried employees

---

## What IS Available for Salaried Employees (Old Regime)

### Employer-Paid Allowances (Old Regime Only)

If your **employer pays you specific allowances** for WFH expenses and declares them as:

| Allowance | Exemption Limit | Condition |
|-----------|----------------|-----------|
| **Internet/Telephone reimbursement** | Actual expense, fully exempt | Must be employer-paid reimbursement (not salary hike) + bills submitted to employer |
| **Food allowance (Sodexo/food coupons)** | ₹50/meal × 2 meals × 22 working days = ₹2,200/month | Employer-provided meal vouchers |
| **Books and periodicals** | Actual expenses | Bills submitted |
| **Dress/uniform allowance** | Actual expenses | Only if specific uniform required |

**Key requirement**: These must be **employer-provided allowances** (structured in your CTC as separate reimbursements) — not a salary hike. And you must be on old tax regime.

**Internet reimbursement example**: If your employer pays ₹1,500/month as "internet reimbursement" and you submit bills, this ₹18,000/year is exempt from tax. Under new regime: this is added to taxable salary.

### Employer Salary Restructuring

Ask HR to restructure your salary to include:
- Internet/telephone reimbursement: ₹1,000–2,000/month
- Food coupons: ₹2,200/month
- Books and periodicals: ₹500–1,000/month

These reduce your taxable salary if you're on the old tax regime. But if old regime doesn't benefit you overall (which it often doesn't for salaries above ₹10L without large 80C/home loan), new regime is still better.

## Freelancers and Self-Employed — Much Better Treatment

If you work from home as a **freelancer, consultant, or self-employed professional**, you CAN deduct home office expenses under **Section 37** (general business expenditure):

### What You Can Deduct (Proportional Home Office Use)

If you use 20% of your home for business:

| Expense | Deductible Amount |
|---------|-----------------|
| Rent (if you pay rent) | 20% of annual rent |
| Electricity bill | 20% of annual electricity bills |
| Internet/broadband | 100% (if used exclusively for work) or 50–70% proportional |
| Furniture/equipment | Depreciation at 10–15% per year on cost |
| Computer/laptop | Depreciation at 40% per year (used for work) |
| Mobile phone (used for work) | Proportional % of bill |

**Documentation required**: Keep all bills; maintain a simple log showing business use percentage.

**Section 44ADA (Presumptive)**: If using presumptive taxation (50% profit), you don't need to individually track expenses — the 50% deduction is a lump allowance for all expenses. Don't try to claim home office expenses on top.

### Business IT Deduction for WFH Equipment

If you bought a laptop, monitor, keyboard for work:
- **Depreciation**: 40% per year on written-down value
- Year 1: ₹60,000 laptop → 40% = ₹24,000 deduction
- Year 2: Remaining ₹36,000 → 40% = ₹14,400 deduction

## WFH Internet and Phone — Claiming Through Section 80C or Other Sections?

Common myth: "Internet bill can be claimed under 80C."

**False** — Section 80C only allows specified investments and expenses (LIC, PPF, ELSS, EPF, tuition fees, home loan principal). Internet bills cannot be claimed under 80C.

**For salaried employees (old regime only)**: Internet reimbursement from employer is exempt; self-paid internet is NOT deductible.

**For freelancers**: Internet is a business expense under Section 37.

## Should You Bother Restructuring Salary for WFH Allowances?

Do the math for your situation:

| Situation | Worth Restructuring? |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Salary < ₹10L, old regime | Possibly — every deduction helps |
| Salary ₹10–15L, large 80C/HRA/home loan | Old regime may be better; restructure makes sense |
| Salary > ₹15L, minimal deductions | New regime saves more; restructuring irrelevant |

**If you're already on new regime**: WFH allowance restructuring doesn't help — new regime doesn't allow these exemptions.

## What Companies Are Actually Doing for WFH Employees

Many companies added WFH-specific allowances post-pandemic:
- **Internet allowance**: ₹1,000–2,000/month (most common)
- **Work-from-home setup allowance**: One-time ₹5,000–20,000 (for chair, monitor) — usually a business expense for the company, not income for you
- **Meal allowance**: Food coupons maintained even for WFH employees

Ask HR if your company offers these as reimbursements (not salary additions) — this is the only legal way for salaried employees to get tax benefit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I claim 80C deduction for my laptop bought for WFH?

No — 80C is only for specific savings instruments (PPF, ELSS, LIC, EPF, home loan principal, tuition fees). A laptop purchase doesn't qualify under 80C.

For freelancers: Laptop depreciation is deductible under Section 37. For salaried: Not directly deductible unless employer provides it as a company asset.

### My employer increased my salary by ₹2,000/month "for internet." Is it tax-free?

If it's a salary hike (not a reimbursement against bills), it's fully taxable income. Only "reimbursement against actual bills submitted to employer" is exempt in old regime.

### I'm a salaried employee but do some freelancing on the side. Can I claim home office for freelance work?

Yes — you can claim proportional home office expenses against your freelancing income (reported as business income in ITR). You cannot claim home office against your salary income.

### Does working from abroad while employed in India affect my tax?

If you stay abroad more than 182 days in a financial year, you may become NRI for that year — with different tax treatment for foreign income. Your salary from Indian employer is still taxable in India. Complex situation — consult a CA.
