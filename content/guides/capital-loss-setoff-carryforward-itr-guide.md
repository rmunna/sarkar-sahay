# Capital Loss Set-Off and Carry Forward India 2026 — Complete Rules

When you sell shares, mutual funds, or property at a loss, that loss is not wasted — you can use it to reduce capital gains taxes in the same year or in the next 8 years. The rules for which losses can set off which gains are specific and important.

## Types of Capital Losses

| Loss Type | When It Arises |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL)** | Selling equity (<12 months), or non-equity/property (<24 or <36 months) at a loss |
| **Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL)** | Selling equity (>12 months), or property/non-equity (>24 or >36 months) at a loss |

## The Set-Off Rules — What Can Set Off What

| Loss Type | Can Set Off Against |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **STCL** | ✅ Any STCG (from equity or non-equity) |
| **STCL** | ✅ Any LTCG (from equity or non-equity) |
| **LTCL** | ✅ Only LTCG (from any asset) |
| **LTCL** | ❌ CANNOT set off against STCG |

> **Key rule**: STCL is more flexible — sets off both STCG and LTCG. LTCL is restrictive — can only set off LTCG.

**Also**: Capital losses CANNOT be set off against salary, rental income, or business income. Only capital gains.

## Same-Year Set-Off — Priority of Use

If you have multiple types of gains and losses in the same year, the IT rules require losses to be set off in this order:

1. First, set off loss against **same type of gains** (STCL vs STCG, LTCL vs LTCG)
2. Only after exhausting same-type gains, set off against other type of gains
3. Any remaining loss is carried forward

**Example — Same Year**:
- STCG from equity: ₹50,000
- LTCG from equity: ₹80,000
- LTCL from property: ₹1,20,000

Set-off:
- LTCL vs LTCG first: ₹80,000 of LTCL absorbs ₹80,000 LTCG → LTCG becomes ₹0
- Remaining LTCL: ₹40,000 — CANNOT set off against STCG
- Carry forward LTCL: ₹40,000 for up to 8 years
- STCG ₹50,000 remains taxable (at 20%)

## Equity LTCL — Special Budget 2018 Rule

When LTCG tax on equity was reintroduced in Budget 2018, LTCL from equity (shares, equity MFs sold at long-term loss) also became allowed for set-off. But gains before January 31, 2018 are grandfathered — losses arising from that grandfathering period have some nuances.

**Practical point**: If you sell equity MF at an LTCL (held > 12 months), this LTCL can offset LTCG from other equity or property sales.

## Carry Forward — 8 Years Rule

Any capital loss that isn't fully set off in the current year can be **carried forward for up to 8 assessment years**.

**Example — Carry Forward**:
- FY 2025-26: You made STCL of ₹2,00,000 (stock crash), STCG of ₹50,000
- Net STCL: ₹2,00,000 − ₹50,000 = **₹1,50,000 carry forward** to FY 2026-27
- FY 2026-27: STCG of ₹1,00,000
  - ₹1,00,000 STCG set off against ₹1,50,000 carried STCL
  - Tax on STCG: ₹0 (fully covered by carried loss)
  - Remaining STCL: ₹50,000 — carried forward again

Over 8 years, this ₹1,50,000 loss saves: ₹1,50,000 × 20% = **₹30,000** in STCG tax.

## CRITICAL: Must File ITR on Time to Carry Forward

This is the most commonly missed rule:

> **You can carry forward capital losses ONLY if you file your ITR before the due date (July 31).**

If you miss the July 31 deadline and file a belated ITR (under Section 139(4) by December 31):
- You CANNOT carry forward the capital loss to next year
- The loss is permanently lost

**Exception**: Unabsorbed losses that are merely being brought forward (from a prior year where ITR was filed on time) can still be used even in a belated ITR.

**Moral**: If you have capital losses in a year, DO NOT miss the ITR deadline.

## How to Claim Capital Loss in ITR

1. File **ITR-2** (if salaried with capital gains/losses) or **ITR-3** (if business income)
   - ITR-1 does NOT have a capital gains/loss section — cannot use it if you have capital losses
2. Go to **Schedule CG (Capital Gains)**
3. Enter all your capital gain/loss transactions
4. The system automatically computes set-offs
5. **Schedule BFLA (Brought Forward Losses Adjusted)**: Enter losses from previous years being adjusted
6. **Schedule CFL (Carry Forward of Losses)**: The remaining loss is automatically computed and carried forward

If you have a broker statement (like Zerodha's Tax P&L or MF Capital Gains Statement), that gives you the STCG/LTCG/STCL/LTCL breakdowns to enter in Schedule CG.

## Special Cases

### Loss From Exempt Income — Not Claimable

Long-term capital loss on equity MF sold before Budget 2018 (when LTCG was exempt): losses from that period cannot be carried forward now.

If you sold equity within a **tax-exempt window** (before the LTCG re-introduction), any losses from that period were not eligible for carry forward.

### STT Condition for Equity LTCL

To claim **LTCL on listed equity shares**, the transaction must have been subject to **STT (Securities Transaction Tax)**. This means:
- Buying on recognized exchange (NSE/BSE): ✅ STT applicable → LTCL claimable
- Off-market transactions (you transferred shares manually): Usually no STT → LTCL NOT claimable

### F&O Losses — Not Capital Loss

Futures and Options (F&O) losses are treated as **business losses** (speculative or non-speculative), NOT capital losses. F&O losses:
- Can set off against business income (not salary)
- Can be carried forward for 4–8 years depending on speculative/non-speculative
- Cannot set off against capital gains

## Intraday Trading Losses — Speculative Loss

Intraday stock trading (buying and selling same stock on same day, equity delivery trades excluded) losses are **speculative business losses**:
- Can only set off against **speculative business income** (other intraday gains)
- Cannot set off against capital gains or regular salary
- Carry forward: 4 years only

## Practical Example — Full Year Tax Calculation

**Year**: FY 2025-26

| Transaction | Amount |
|-------------|--------|
| LTCG from equity MF sold (held 2 years) | +₹2,00,000 |
| STCG from equity shares sold | +₹40,000 |
| LTCL from property sold at loss | −₹80,000 |
| STCL from equity MF loss (panic sold) | −₹1,20,000 |

**Set-off calculation**:
1. STCL (₹1,20,000) vs STCG (₹40,000): STCG eliminated; remaining STCL = ₹80,000
2. Remaining STCL (₹80,000) vs LTCG (₹2,00,000): LTCG reduced to ₹1,20,000; STCL fully used
3. LTCL (₹80,000) vs remaining LTCG (₹1,20,000): LTCG reduced to ₹40,000; LTCL fully used

**Taxable LTCG**: ₹40,000 (well below ₹1.25L exemption → ₹0 tax)
**No carry forwards** (all losses used in same year)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I sold Zerodha equity MF at a loss and same-day equity shares at a gain. Can the loss offset immediately?

Yes — same-year set-off happens when you file your ITR, not transaction by transaction. Your total STCL and STCG for the year are netted out in Schedule CG. The timing of individual transactions within the year doesn't matter.

### I had ₹5L capital loss 7 years ago but forgot to claim it. Can I use it now?

No — the 8-year clock runs from the year the loss was incurred. If you filed ITR on time in Year 0 and it's now Year 7, you have one more year (Year 8) to use it. If you didn't file ITR on time in Year 0, the loss was forfeited and cannot be claimed.

### Can I carry forward losses from property sale?

Yes — LTCL from property can be carried forward for 8 years and set off against future LTCG from property (or equity LTCG). File ITR-2 on time to preserve this.

### My equity MF went down so much I want to "book" the loss. Does booking a STCL make sense?

"Loss harvesting" on the short side: yes, it can make sense. If you have a STCL, you can use it to offset other STCG or LTCG in the same year. But remember: you lose your original cost basis and holding period when you sell and re-buy. For long-term holders, selling at an STCL might restart the 12-month clock for LTCG treatment.
