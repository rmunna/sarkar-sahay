# Employer Not Paying Salary India — What to Do (2026 Guide)

Unpaid salary is not just a dispute — it's a violation of the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, which carries criminal penalties for defaulting employers. You don't have to resign to recover it. Here's what to do, step by step.

---

## When Must Employers Pay Salary?

**Payment of Wages Act, 1936** mandates:

| Employee Count | Salary Payment Deadline |
|----------------|------------------------|
| < 1,000 employees | By **7th of following month** |
| 1,000+ employees | By **10th of following month** |

If your company has fewer than 1,000 employees and doesn't pay by the 7th — that's already a violation.

**For IT/software companies**: Even if not explicitly covered under "factory" definitions, the Act applies to all establishments. State-specific Shops and Establishments Acts also mandate timely payment.

---

## Common Reasons Employers Give (and What They Mean)

| What Employer Says | Is It Valid? |
|--------------------|-------------|
| "Client hasn't paid us" | No — client payment issues don't excuse employee salary delays |
| "We're processing salary, give us a week" | If it's past the legal deadline, this is a violation regardless |
| "You'll get it with next month" | Illegal — salary for each month must be paid that month |
| "You've been marked absent for disputed days" | Valid concern, but must be communicated and calculated transparently |
| "We'll pay after you sign the appraisal" | Linking salary to signing documents = coercion; illegal |

## Step-by-Step: How to Recover Unpaid Salary

### Step 1: Send a Formal Email to HR

Write a formal email to your HR head and department manager. State:
- The month/period for which salary is unpaid
- The exact amount owed
- That you're referencing the Payment of Wages Act, 1936
- Request payment within 7 days

**Why email?** Creates documented evidence of your attempt to resolve before escalating. Courts look favorably at this.

### Step 2: Send a WhatsApp/Written Message to Manager

If HR is unresponsive, reach your direct manager on any documented channel. Screenshot these conversations — they're evidence.

### Step 3: File Online Complaint — Shram Suvidha Portal

**For organized sector employees (PF/ESI registered companies)**:
1. Visit [shramsuvidha.gov.in](https://shramsuvidha.gov.in)
2. Register/login → File Grievance → Select category: "Non-payment of wages"
3. Enter employer's PF registration number (on your payslip), company name, amount owed
4. Submit — Labour Inspector is assigned within 5–7 working days

This is the fastest official route and is free.

### Step 4: Approach State Labour Commissioner

**For all employees (including non-PF registered companies)**:
1. Go to your district's Labour Commissioner's office
2. File a written complaint citing Payment of Wages Act Section 15 (Application for recovery of delayed wages)
3. Bring: payslip, offer letter, bank statement showing no credit, employment proof
4. Labour Commissioner issues notice to employer and can order payment + penalty

**Penalty for employer**: Up to ₹7,500 fine per violation + 6% interest on delayed amount under Payment of Wages Act.

### Step 5: Labour Court / Industrial Disputes Tribunal

For disputes >₹1 lakh or if Labour Commissioner is slow, file directly in Labour Court:
- **Claim**: Wages + 6% interest + ₹25/day penalty for each day of delay
- **Time to file**: Within 1 year of the violation
- **Representation**: You can appear yourself (most Labour Courts are designed for employee self-representation)
- **Cost**: ₹500–₹2,000 filing fee; no need for a lawyer for basic cases

## PF and ESI Not Deposited — A Separate Offence

If your payslip shows PF and ESI deductions from your salary but the employer hasn't deposited them to EPFO/ESIC — that's a **criminal offence** under EPF Act Section 14 and ESI Act Section 85.

**How to check**:
- PF: Login at epfindia.gov.in → UAN → Passbook → check if contributions are posted
- ESI: Login at esic.in → Employee Portal → Check contribution history

**If not deposited**:
- File complaint at [epfindia.gov.in](https://epfindia.gov.in) → Grievance Portal
- File complaint at [esic.nic.in](https://esic.nic.in) → Grievance → ESIC Grievance
- EPFO can attach employer's bank account and recover your share

This is the most powerful lever — EPFO and ESIC can take criminal action against company directors.

## What If the Company Is About to Shut Down / Bankrupt?

If your company is in financial trouble (no salary, mass layoffs, assets being sold):

1. **Workmen's dues are first priority** under Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) — you are a "secured creditor" for salary dues
2. File claim with the **Insolvency Resolution Professional (IRP)** appointed by NCLT
3. Dues up to 24 months' salary are given priority over bank debts in liquidation
4. Register on the IRP's claims portal before the deadline (usually 90 days from insolvency declaration)

**In practice**: Recovery in insolvency takes 1–3 years. Prioritize filing the claim early.

## Can You Stop Working While Salary Is Unpaid?

**If salary is unpaid for 2+ months**: Section 2(q) of the Industrial Disputes Act and related provisions can allow suspension of work. However, formal procedures are complex.

**Practical advice**:
- Keep working while escalating legally (don't give employer grounds for "abandonment of job" claims)
- File legal complaint in parallel
- Don't resign until FnF strategy is clear — resignation may forfeit some claims

## Multiple Months of Unpaid Salary — Recovery Amount

Claim the following in your Labour Court application:

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Principal salary unpaid | Full amount × months |
| Interest | 6% per annum on each month's salary from due date |
| Delay penalty | ₹25 per day per violation (old Act; courts may compute differently under new rules) |
| Legal costs | If you've hired an advocate |

**Total claim example**: 3 months unpaid salary of ₹50,000/month:
- Principal: ₹1,50,000
- Interest (3 months at 6%): ~₹2,250
- Total recoverable: ₹1,52,250+

Courts typically process these cases faster than civil suits — 3–12 months vs 3–5 years.

## Form 16 and Payslip Not Given — Your Rights

**Payslip**: Employer must provide monthly payslip under Model Standing Orders and state Shops Acts. Non-provision is a violation. Use your bank statement as proof of salary in its absence.

**Form 16**: Under Income Tax Act, employers with deducting employees must issue Form 16 by June 15th every year. Non-issuance: File complaint on [incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in) → Grievance → "Employer not issuing Form 16."

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My company says I'm a "consultant" not an "employee." Does the Wages Act still apply?

If you work regular hours, follow company rules, have no other clients, and have payslips — courts treat you as an employee regardless of contract labeling. Misclassification to avoid labor law protections is itself a violation.

### My employer said salary will come after we get funding. It's been 4 months.

File the complaint now. Funding timeline is irrelevant to salary payment obligations. The Labour Commissioner's notice often accelerates funding conversations for cash-strapped startups.

### The company has offered to pay in installments. Should I accept?

Only if they put it in writing with specific dates and amounts. Vague promises: don't accept. A written settlement agreement with dates creates legal enforceability if they default again.

### I'm in a senior manager role — does the Wages Act apply to me?

The Payment of Wages Act applies to employees earning ₹24,000/month or less under the Act's current wage ceiling. However, higher-earning employees can file civil suits for breach of contract + claim under Industrial Disputes Act (managers are still "workmen" under IDA if not in supervisory/managerial roles).
