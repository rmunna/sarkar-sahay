# FD Premature Closure — How Much You Lose, Penalty Rates, and When It Makes Sense

Breaking a Fixed Deposit before its maturity date means you receive interest only for the period you actually held it, **minus a penalty**. Understanding exactly how much you'll lose helps you decide whether to break the FD or find another option.

## How Premature FD Closure Works

When you close an FD before maturity:

1. **Applicable rate**: The interest rate applicable is the rate for the **actual tenure you held** (not the rate you booked at)
2. **Penalty**: Deducted from the applicable rate (usually 0.5% to 1%)
3. **You receive**: Interest at (tenure-applicable rate − penalty) for the actual holding period

**Formula**:
```
Premature Interest = Principal × (Rate for actual tenure − Penalty%) × Actual days held / 365
```

**Example**:
- You booked a 3-year FD at 7.5% for ₹5,00,000 in June 2023
- You break it in December 2025 (2 years and 6 months)
- 2.5 year FD rate at the bank (when you broke it): 7.0%
- Penalty: 0.5%
- Applicable rate: 7.0% − 0.5% = 6.5%
- Interest earned: ₹5,00,000 × 6.5% × 2.5 = ₹81,250 (approximate)
- Original expectation (full 3-year): ₹5,00,000 × 7.5% × 3 = ₹1,12,500
- **You lost approximately ₹31,250** by breaking early

## Penalty Rates — Major Banks

| Bank | Premature Closure Penalty |
|------|--------------------------|
| **SBI** | 0.50% for FDs under ₹1 crore; 1% for FDs ₹1Cr–₹5Cr |
| **HDFC Bank** | 1% across all tenures |
| **ICICI Bank** | 1% across all tenures |
| **Axis Bank** | 1% for most tenures |
| **Kotak Mahindra Bank** | 0.5% for FDs under ₹2 crore |
| **Post Office (POTD)** | No premature closure before 6 months; after 6 months at reduced rate |
| **Small Finance Banks (AU, Jana, Equitas)** | Often 1% |
| **Senior citizen FDs** | Same penalty applies |

**Rates change — verify with your bank before breaking the FD.**

## No Premature Closure — Tax-Saving FD (Section 80C)

**Tax-saving Fixed Deposits (5-year FDs under Section 80C) CANNOT be broken before 5 years.** This is a statutory lock-in. No exceptions, no penalty option — premature closure is simply not allowed.

If you need liquidity and have a tax-saving FD, your options are:
- Loan against FD (most banks offer 75–90% of FD value as overdraft)
- Wait it out (5 years total from deposit date)

## Premature Closure: When It Makes Financial Sense

Break your FD if:
1. **You can invest at significantly higher rate**: If FD rate was 6% and you can now get 8% for remaining tenure, even after 1% penalty, re-investing at 8% wins over time.
2. **Urgent need**: Medical emergency, etc. — obviously break it.
3. **Better alternative**: Debt MF or high-yield savings temporarily while deciding.

**When it usually doesn't make sense**:
- If FD matures in less than 3 months — penalty cost often exceeds any benefit
- If you booked a high rate (7.5%+) and current rates are lower — current tenure rate will be lower than your booked rate, and penalty makes it worse

## Loan Against FD — Instead of Breaking

If you need cash urgently but don't want to break the FD:
- Most banks offer **overdraft against FD** (75–90% of FD value)
- Interest rate: FD rate + 1–2% (e.g., if FD is at 7.5%, loan at 8.5–9.5%)
- You keep earning FD interest; only pay loan interest
- When FD matures, it adjusts against the loan

For short-term cash needs (< 6 months), loan against FD is often better than breaking the FD.

## Tax Implications of Premature FD Closure

When you break an FD:
- **Interest earned so far** is taxable as per normal rules (TDS at 10% if interest > ₹40K/year; ₹50K for senior citizens)
- If TDS was already deducted on quarterly credited interest, excess TDS shows in Form 26AS — you get refund at ITR filing if actual tax is less

**No special tax rate** on premature FD closure — same as regular FD interest taxation.

## Post Office FD — Premature Closure Rules

Post Office Time Deposits (POTD) have stricter rules:
- **Before 6 months**: No interest at all — only principal returned
- **After 6 months but before 1 year**: Post Office savings account rate (lower)
- **After 1 year**: Rate applicable for the completed years, minus 2% penalty

These are more punitive than commercial banks. Consider this before locking money in Post Office FDs for non-essential goals.

## Partial Premature Withdrawal

Some banks allow partial premature closure — you can break only a portion of the FD:
- Check with your specific bank whether partial closure is available
- Minimum amount for partial closure varies (often ₹1,000 or ₹10,000)
- Penalty applies only on the broken portion
- Rest of FD continues at original rate

## FD Renewal vs Breaking

When an FD matures and auto-renews at a lower rate:
- You can break the renewed FD at premature closure terms
- Penalty applies only for the time since renewal (not from original deposit date)
- Check if the bank's premature closure policy allows this — most do

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I have an FD at 8% with 1 year remaining. Current rates are 7%. Should I break and re-invest?

No — breaking at 1% penalty means your effective rate becomes ~7% (8% booked, minus 1% penalty = 7% effective). Current rates are already 7%. There's no gain in breaking. Hold till maturity.

### I need ₹50,000 urgently but my FD is ₹5 lakh. Can I break only part of it?

Check with your bank — many offer partial closure. If partial closure isn't available, consider a loan against FD (overdraft) for ₹50,000 at your FD rate + 1-2%. Much cheaper than breaking ₹5L of FD.

### My FD auto-renewed 3 months ago at a lower rate. Can I break and re-invest at higher rates elsewhere?

Yes — the premature closure penalty applies only from the renewal date (3 months). On 3 months of holding at lower rate: penalty is on the renewed rate, not original. Calculate: is the post-penalty return for 3 months worth moving to a higher rate for the remaining period? Usually yes if rate difference is 0.5%+ and remaining tenure is long.
