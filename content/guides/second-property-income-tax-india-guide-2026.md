# Second House Property Tax India 2026 — Complete Guide

Owning two properties creates a tax complexity that surprises many first-time second-home buyers. Here's exactly how it works.

---

## The Core Rule: How Many Self-Occupied Properties?

| Tax Regime | Self-Occupied Properties Allowed | Rest Are |
|------------|----------------------------------|---------|
| **Old regime** | 2 (both can be SOP) | Optional — 3rd onwards is deemed let-out |
| **New regime** | 1 (your choice) | All others are deemed let-out |

**Self-occupied (SOP)**: You or your family use it. Annual Value = ₹0. Only interest deduction (Section 24b) allowed — limited to ₹2L under old regime for SOP.

**Deemed let-out**: You're assumed to be earning rent (even if vacant or used personally). Tax paid on notional rent even without actual rental income.

---

## Old Tax Regime: Both Houses Self-Occupied

Since Budget 2019, **two properties can be declared self-occupied** under old regime. Annual value of both = ₹0. Tax on notional rent = ₹0.

**Home loan interest deduction**: ₹2L per year per property (Section 24b) — so up to ₹4L total if both have loans. This ₹2L is per property and capped — not combined ₹2L for all properties.

**Loss on house property** (interest > income):
- SOP: Loss = ₹0 (annual value) minus interest paid = negative. Loss can offset salary income up to **₹2L/year**. Balance loss carried forward 8 years.
- If both properties are SOP: Both contribute to house property losses, each capped at ₹2L offset.

---

## New Tax Regime: Only 1 SOP Allowed

Under new regime, only ONE property can be self-occupied. The other(s) are mandatorily treated as deemed let-out.

**You choose which one to declare SOP** — typically the one with:
- Higher market rent (so deemed rental income is lower relative to the other)
- Or larger home loan (so interest offset matters more)

**No home loan interest deduction (Section 24b) in new regime for SOP** — this is a major difference. New regime removes Section 24b for self-occupied property entirely.

**Deemed let-out property in new regime**: Interest on home loan IS deductible (unlimited) — offset against the deemed rental income.

## Deemed Let-Out — How to Calculate Tax

### Step 1: Compute Expected Rent (Fair Market Rent)

Fair Market Rent = Higher of:
1. **Municipal Valuation**: Property's ratable value per municipal records
2. **Fair Rent**: Similar property's rent in same locality
3. **Standard Rent** (if applicable under Rent Control Act) — take whichever is lower between fair rent and standard rent

In practice, for most urban properties: **use 3–5% of property's market value per year** as a reasonable approximation. For a ₹80L apartment, expected rent ≈ ₹2.4–4L/year.

### Step 2: Compute Gross Annual Value (GAV)

- If let-out (actually rented): GAV = Actual rent received or receivable (whichever is higher vs fair rent)
- If deemed let-out: GAV = Expected Rent (fair market rent)
- If vacant for part of year: Proportionate adjustment possible

### Step 3: Compute Net Annual Value (NAV)

NAV = GAV − Municipal taxes paid during the year

### Step 4: Standard Deduction (Section 24a)

30% of NAV — flat deduction. No bills needed. Covers maintenance, repairs, etc.

**Taxable NAV = NAV × 70%**

### Step 5: Interest Deduction (Section 24b)

For **let-out / deemed let-out properties**: Interest on home loan is deductible **without any limit** (unlike SOP where it's capped at ₹2L).

**Taxable Income from House Property = Taxable NAV − Interest paid**

This can be negative (loss) if interest > taxable NAV.

## Full Calculation Example

**Property**: Apartment bought for ₹80L in 2022.
**Current market rent**: ₹35,000/month (₹4.2L/year) — deemed let-out (vacant or used for family visits).
**Home loan interest**: ₹6L/year (outstanding loan of ₹60L @ 9%).
**Municipal tax paid**: ₹18,000/year.

**Step 1 — GAV**: ₹4,20,000 (fair rent)
**Step 2 — NAV**: ₹4,20,000 − ₹18,000 = ₹4,02,000
**Step 3 — Standard Deduction (30%)**: ₹4,02,000 × 30% = ₹1,20,600
**Step 4 — Taxable NAV**: ₹4,02,000 − ₹1,20,600 = ₹2,81,400
**Step 5 — Interest Deduction**: ₹6,00,000 (no limit for let-out/deemed let-out)
**Income from House Property**: ₹2,81,400 − ₹6,00,000 = **−₹3,18,600 (loss)**

This loss of ₹3.18L can be **set off against salary income up to ₹2L** in the same year. Balance ₹1.18L is carried forward 8 years.

**Tax saving at 30% bracket**: ₹2,00,000 × 30% = **₹60,000/year**.

## House Property Loss Set-Off Rules

| Loss Amount | Can Set Off Against | How Much |
|-------------|---------------------|----------|
| House property loss | Salary, business, other income | **Max ₹2L per year** |
| Balance loss | Carried forward | For 8 years |
| Carried forward loss | Can set off against future house property income only | (Not against salary in future years) |

**Crucial**: Loss from house property can set off against salary income only up to **₹2L in the year of loss**. The balance must be carried forward — and in future years, can ONLY be set off against house property income (not salary).

This ₹2L limit was introduced by Budget 2017. Before that, unlimited set-off against salary was allowed.

## Which Is Better for Second Property: Old vs New Regime?

| Situation | Old Regime | New Regime |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Second property has heavy home loan (interest > rent) | Better — ₹2L salary offset + carry forward | Loses SOP deduction; deemed let-out loss treatment worse |
| Second property is fully paid up (no loan) | Deemed let-out applies; rental income taxed after 30% deduction | Same |
| Renting out second property | Better if deductions justify | Better if deductions are small (lower rates may win) |

**The calculation depends on**: Size of your home loan interest, expected rent, and your total income + deductions.

## When Second Property Is Actually Rented Out

If you receive actual rent:
- Report under "Income from House Property" → "Let-Out"
- GAV = Actual rent or fair rent, whichever is higher
- All deductions as above apply
- TDS on rent: If monthly rent exceeds ₹50K (₹6L/year), tenant must deduct TDS at 10% under Section 194IB

**For rent receipts and landlord declarations**: You need rent agreement + rent receipts. Even if no TDS, both you and tenant must comply with reporting.

---

## Pre-Construction Interest Deduction

If you took a loan during construction phase:
- Interest paid during construction = **Pre-construction interest**
- This is deductible in **5 equal installments** starting from the year you get possession
- Same ₹2L cap for SOP, no cap for let-out (applied alongside regular annual interest)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I have two houses, both vacant. Do I pay tax on both?

Under old regime: Both can be SOP — no deemed let-out tax. Under new regime: Only one SOP, the other is deemed let-out — you must pay tax on notional rent of the second one even if it's vacant.

### My second property has a big home loan. Should I put it in old or new regime?

If the home loan interest on the second property (deemed let-out) plus interest on first SOP exceeds the benefits of new regime's lower rates, stick with old regime. Calculate: old regime tax with full deductions vs new regime tax at flat rates. Choose the lower one.

### Can I show both properties as let-out even if one is self-occupied?

No. If you actually reside in a property, you cannot fraudulently declare it as let-out to claim unlimited interest deduction. Tax authorities verify this via electricity consumption, ration card, voter ID address, school admissions etc.

### If I'm living abroad, can I claim both Indian properties as SOP?

Only one property per person can be SOP — you cannot claim SOP for a property you're not using (since you're abroad). Properties in India where you don't reside = deemed let-out (unless your family lives there).

### The deemed rent for my property seems too high. Can I dispute it?

You can show that fair market rent (for similar properties in same locality) is lower. Use examples of comparable properties. The Assessing Officer can adjust the deemed rent based on evidence.
