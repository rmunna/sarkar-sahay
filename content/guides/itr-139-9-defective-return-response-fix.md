# ITR 139(9) Defective Return Notice — How to Respond

If you receive an email from the Income Tax Department with subject line **"Notice under Section 139(9) for Defective Return"**, your ITR has been marked defective. You must respond within **15 days** or your return will be treated as if never filed — which means no refund, late filing penalty, and potential interest on unpaid tax.

## Why Was Your Return Marked Defective?

The IT department marks an ITR defective when it detects specific issues. Common reasons:

| Defect Code | Reason | Example |
|-------------|--------|---------|
| **BD001** | Wrong ITR form | Filed ITR-1 but have capital gains (need ITR-2) |
| **BD002** | No TAN/PAN details for TDS | TDS credit claimed but payer details incomplete |
| **BD003** | Schedule mismatch | Schedule BA shows different income than Part B |
| **BD004** | Tax computation error | Tax payable calculation doesn't match |
| **BD005** | Missing bank account for refund | No valid bank account provided for refund |
| **BD006** | Income head mismatch | Income shown in wrong head (e.g., business income in salary) |
| **BD007** | Exempt income without ITR-2/3 | Exempt income claimed but wrong form used |
| **BD008** | Missing name or address | Basic personal details incomplete |

## How to View the Defective Return Notice

1. Login to **[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/)**
2. Go to **"Pending Actions"** → **"e-Proceedings"**
3. Find the notice under "Defective Return under Section 139(9)"
4. Click to view the notice — it explains the specific defect code and what's wrong

You should also receive an email notification at your registered email.

## Two Ways to Respond

### Option 1: Agree with Defect — File Revised ITR (Recommended)

If the defect is valid (wrong form, missing bank account, schedule error):

1. Login to incometax.gov.in → File Income Tax Return
2. Select the **same Assessment Year**
3. Change the form type if that was the issue (e.g., switch from ITR-1 to ITR-2)
4. Correct the specific defect
5. File the revised return
6. After filing, go back to **e-Proceedings** → Find your 139(9) notice → **Submit Response**
7. Select: "Return filed in response to this notice" → Enter the new acknowledgement number

> Note: The revised return must be filed within the 15-day response window. If you need more time, request an extension from the Assessing Officer (AO) in writing.

### Option 2: Disagree with Defect

If you believe your ITR was not actually defective:

1. Go to **e-Proceedings** → Find your 139(9) notice → **Submit Response**
2. Select: **"Return filed was not defective"**
3. Provide reasons and upload supporting documents (screenshots, calculations)
4. Submit the disagreement response

> The Assessing Officer will review your disagreement. If they accept it, the defect is removed. If rejected, you'll need to file a revised return.

## Fixing Common Defects

### Defect: Wrong ITR Form (BD001)

**How to fix:**
1. Determine the correct form for your income type:
   - ITR-1 (Sahaj): Salary, one house property, other sources (income ≤ ₹50L)
   - ITR-2: Salary + capital gains / more than one house property / foreign income
   - ITR-3: Business/profession income + salary or other income
   - ITR-4: Presumptive business (Section 44AD/44ADA/44AE)
2. File a new return using the correct form
3. Submit response to the 139(9) notice linking the new return

### Defect: Missing Bank Account (BD005)

**How to fix:**
1. File revised ITR with valid bank account number, IFSC code, and account type
2. Ensure the bank account is pre-validated on the income tax portal (incometax.gov.in → Profile → Bank Account)
3. The pre-validated account is used for refunds

### Defect: Schedule Mismatch

**How to fix:**
1. Open your original ITR XML (download from portal → Filed Returns → Download ITR)
2. Identify where the mismatch is (e.g., Part B-TI total doesn't match schedule totals)
3. File revised ITR with corrected figures

## What Happens If You Don't Respond in 15 Days?

If you **ignore the 139(9) notice**:
- Your original ITR is treated as **not filed** under Section 139(9)
- You're liable for:
  - Late filing interest under Section 234A
  - Late filing fee under Section 234F
  - **Loss of refund** if applicable
- You must then file a belated return if within the due date, or request condonation of delay

**Request for Extension**: If you genuinely need more time (e.g., employer error in Form 16), write to your **Jurisdictional Assessing Officer** before the 15-day deadline expires.

## Find Your AO's Contact

1. Login to incometax.gov.in → Profile → My Profile
2. Go to **"Jurisdiction"** → Your AO's details (name, designation, ward)
3. Contact them by email or visit the office

## Timelines

| Action | Deadline |
|--------|---------|
| Respond to 139(9) notice | 15 days from notice date |
| File revised return (same AY) | By December 31 of Assessment Year |
| Appeal if AO rejects your disagreement | 30 days after rejection order |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I received 139(9) notice but already filed revised ITR. Do I still need to respond?

Yes — you must **submit a formal response** to the 139(9) notice through e-Proceedings even if you've already filed the revised ITR. Link the response to your new ITR acknowledgement number.

### My return was filed correctly but still marked defective. What to do?

Choose "Disagree" option in e-Proceedings and provide evidence. Upload relevant schedules, form 16, or AIS data showing your return was correct. If the AO agrees, defect is removed without you needing to refile.

### Can I extend the 15-day deadline?

You can write to your Jurisdictional AO requesting an extension with reasons. The AO may grant extension at their discretion. Do this before the 15 days expire — don't wait.

### I'm filing my first ITR. Can I get a 139(9) notice?

Yes, even first-time filers can get it — typically if they chose the wrong form or left mandatory fields blank. Most 139(9) notices are resolved by filing a revised return with the correct details.
