# Income Tax Demand Notice Under Section 156 — How to Respond

A **Notice of Demand under Section 156** of the Income Tax Act is the formal bill the Income Tax Department sends after completing an assessment. It states the **exact amount of tax, penalty, or interest** you must pay. Unlike the automatic 143(1) intimation (routine for all filers), a Section 156 notice is raised specifically when an assessment results in an outstanding demand.

> **Key action**: You have **30 days** from the date of the notice to either pay the demand or file a request for stay/dispute. Ignoring the notice leads to recovery proceedings.

## When Is Notice 156 Issued?

| Assessment Type | Section | Why 156 Is Issued |
|----------------|---------|------------------|
| Regular assessment | 143(3) | Scrutiny found additional income |
| Best judgment assessment | 144 | You didn't respond to notices; AO assessed income |
| Reassessment | 147 | IT department reopened past years' assessment |
| Penalty order | 271/274 | Penalty imposed for concealment/mis-statement |
| TDS default | 201 | Employer/deductor didn't deposit TDS on time |
| After CIT(A) order | — | Appeals resulted in enhanced demand |

A **143(1) intimation** can also state a demand — but that's an automatic processing notice. Section 156 comes after a **human assessment** (AO/Commissioner), making it more serious and with larger amounts typically.

## What to Do When You Receive Notice 156

### Step 1: Understand the Demand

Open the notice and identify:
1. **Assessment Year** the demand is for
2. **Amount** of demand (may be broken into tax + interest + penalty)
3. **Section** under which the assessment was made (143(3), 144, 147, etc.)
4. **Date of notice** — your 30-day clock starts here

### Step 2: Decide: Pay or Dispute

**Pay if you agree** (demand is valid): Fastest resolution. Interest stops accruing after payment.

**Dispute if you disagree**: File appeal within 30 days of the assessment order. The 156 demand may still need to be paid or stayed (see below).

## How to Pay Notice 156 Demand Online

1. Visit **[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/)** and login
2. Go to **"Pending Actions"** → **"Response to Outstanding Demand"**
3. Find the specific demand for the relevant Assessment Year
4. Click **"Pay Now"**
5. System redirects to Challan 280 with pre-filled details
6. Pay via net banking, UPI, debit card
7. Keep the challan receipt

Alternatively:
1. Go to **e-Pay Tax** → Select Challan 280
2. Type of payment: **Self-Assessment Tax (300)** or **Tax on Regular Assessment (400)**
   - Use **400** if the demand arose from a formal assessment order (143(3)/144/147)
   - Use **300** for self-assessment payments
3. Enter correct Assessment Year and amount

> **Important**: Always pay "Tax on Regular Assessment (400)" for Section 156 demands — NOT Self-Assessment Tax. The wrong challan type causes reconciliation issues.

## How to Dispute Notice 156 (If Demand Is Wrong)

### Option 1: Rectification (Minor Errors)

If the demand is due to a clerical/computational error or facts already on record:
1. Login to incometax.gov.in → **e-File** → **"Rectification"**
2. Select Assessment Year
3. Choose grounds: "Mistake apparent from record" — factual error in the assessment
4. Provide correct details and supporting documents
5. Submit — AO must respond within 6 months

### Option 2: Appeal to CIT(A) (Commissioner of Income Tax - Appeals)

If you disagree with the AO's assessment findings:
1. File **Form 35** (online via incometax.gov.in) within **30 days** of the assessment order
2. Pay **20% of the disputed demand** as pre-deposit (mandatory since Budget 2023)
3. Appeal is heard by CIT(A)
4. During appeal, file a **Stay Application** with the AO requesting stay of balance demand

### Option 3: Stay Application

Even if you file an appeal, the IT department can still pursue recovery of the demand. To stop recovery:
1. Write a **Stay Application** to the Assessing Officer
2. State grounds: appeal filed, demand is excessive/disputed, payment would cause hardship
3. AO can grant stay of demand while appeal is pending
4. If AO rejects, appeal the stay to CIT(A) or High Court

## What Happens If You Ignore Notice 156

The Tax Recovery Officer (TRO) can initiate recovery after the 30-day window:

| Recovery Method | What Happens |
|----------------|-------------|
| **TRO Notice to Bank** | Bank account attached; balance frozen |
| **Certificate to TRO** | IT Dept issues a certificate to TRO to recover as "arrears of land revenue" |
| **Employer attachment** | TRO instructs employer to deduct from salary |
| **Property attachment** | Your property can be attached (rare, for large demands) |
| **Adjustment of refunds** | Future refunds automatically adjusted against outstanding demand |

> **Refund adjustment** is the most common — if you're owed a refund in a future year, the IT department deducts the outstanding demand from it automatically under Section 245 (and sends you a 245 notice before doing so).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My 143(1) intimation shows a demand. Is that also a Notice 156?

No — a 143(1) intimation is separate from a Notice 156. A 143(1) demand is from automatic processing; a Notice 156 follows a human assessment. Both require payment, but the options and appeal routes differ. For 143(1) demands, use rectification. For 156 demands, use formal appeal (Form 35).

### I received Notice 156 for a year I already filed and got a refund for. How?

This happens when your account is picked for scrutiny (143(2)) after your return was processed (143(1) refund). The AO may have found additional income and raised a fresh demand via 143(3) assessment. The earlier refund may be adjusted or the demand raised despite it.

### Can the demand be wrong because of TDS mismatch?

Yes — if your employer or bank didn't deposit TDS on time, 26AS may show less TDS credit than you should have, and the AO may have raised demand on that gap. The correct remedy is to get your employer to pay the TDS and file a rectification request showing the updated 26AS.

### The demand is for AY 2019-20. Is it still valid?

Yes — the IT department can recover demands going back years. There is no short statute of limitation on TRO recovery. However, the time limit for issuing the **original assessment order** matters (4-6 years typically), so the demand itself must have been raised within that window.

### After paying the demand, do I need to inform the IT department?

No separate intimation needed — the tax portal automatically reconciles the payment via challan against the outstanding demand. You can verify by checking "Response to Outstanding Demand" → the demand status should show "Demand Paid" within 5-7 working days. If not, upload the challan manually (Update Challan).

## Timelines at a Glance

| Action | Deadline |
|--------|---------|
| Pay demand without interest | 30 days from notice date |
| File appeal (Form 35) to CIT(A) | 30 days from assessment order |
| File rectification | Within period of limitation (generally 4 years) |
| Stay application | Before 30 days expire (before recovery starts) |
