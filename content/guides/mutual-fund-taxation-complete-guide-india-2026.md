# Mutual Fund Taxation India 2026 — Complete Guide After Budget 2024 Changes

Budget 2024 (effective July 23, 2024) significantly changed mutual fund tax rates. If you're planning a redemption or received a tax notice on mutual fund gains, this guide explains exactly what you owe.

## Budget 2024 Changes — Quick Summary

| Type | Before July 23, 2024 | After July 23, 2024 |
|------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Equity STCG (<12 months) | 15% | **20%** |
| Equity LTCG (>12 months) | 10% above ₹1L | **12.5% above ₹1.25L** |
| Debt STCG/LTCG | Slab rate (Budget 2023) | Slab rate (unchanged) |
| Gold funds (non-equity) | 20% with indexation (LTCG >3yr) | 12.5% without indexation (>2yr) |

## Equity Mutual Funds (Domestic)

**Equity funds** = Funds with ≥65% allocation to Indian equities (equity diversified, ELSS, index funds, large/mid/small cap, sectoral funds, Nifty 50, etc.)

| Holding Period | Tax | Threshold |
|---------------|-----|-----------|
| ≤ 12 months | **STCG at 20%** | From ₹1 (no threshold) |
| > 12 months | **LTCG at 12.5%** | First ₹1.25L exempt per year |

**STCG example**: Redeemed equity fund after 8 months with ₹30,000 gain → Tax: ₹6,000 (20%)

**LTCG example**: Redeemed after 14 months with ₹2,00,000 gain → Exempt: ₹1,25,000; Taxable: ₹75,000 → Tax: ₹9,375 (12.5%)

## Debt Mutual Funds (Post-Budget 2023)

**Debt funds** = Funds with ≤65% equity allocation (liquid funds, ultra-short, short-duration, corporate bond, gilt, banking & PSU, etc.)

Budget 2023 removed the LTCG + indexation benefit for debt funds. From April 1, 2023:

| Holding Period | Tax | Rate |
|---------------|-----|------|
| Any holding period | STCG = Slab Rate | 0%–30% |

There is no LTCG for debt mutual funds anymore. All gains are added to your income and taxed at your slab rate.

**Exception**: Debt funds purchased before April 1, 2023 and sold now: Still get the old treatment? No — the Budget 2023 change applies to all debt fund redemptions from April 1, 2023 regardless of when purchased. There was a transition, but as of FY 2025-26, all debt fund gains are at slab rate.

## Hybrid Mutual Funds

Hybrid funds mix equity and debt. Tax treatment depends on the actual allocation:

| Fund Type | Equity Allocation | Tax Treatment |
|-----------|------------------|--------------|
| Aggressive Hybrid / Equity Savings | ≥65% equity | **Equity tax** (STCG 20%, LTCG 12.5%) |
| Balanced Advantage (Dynamic Asset Allocation) | Varies | Check fund-specific; most maintain ≥65% equity |
| Conservative Hybrid | <65% equity | **Debt tax** (slab rate) |
| Multi-Asset Allocation | Depends on allocation | Check annually |
| Arbitrage Funds | Equity + derivatives | **Equity tax** (classified as equity for tax) |

**How to check**: Your fund house provides the exact equity allocation percentage. SEBI requires funds to maintain consistent classifications.

## International Mutual Funds and FOFs

**International funds** (fund of funds investing in overseas equities):
- Prior to October 1, 2023: STCG at slab, LTCG at 20% with indexation (if held >3 years)
- From October 1, 2023 (Budget 2023 clarity): Taxed as **debt funds** (slab rate for all gains, regardless of holding period)

**Exception**: International funds with ≥65% in domestic equity-oriented funds are taxed as domestic equity funds. Rare — most global/international funds are taxed at slab rate.

## ELSS Funds — Lock-In and Tax

**ELSS (Equity Linked Saving Scheme)** = Equity fund with 3-year lock-in; premium gives 80C deduction.

- Lock-in: 3 years (mandatory)
- After 3 years: Taxed as **equity LTCG** — 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25L
- ELSS gives 80C deduction up to ₹1.5L (old regime only)
- The LTCG tax on redemption partially offsets the 80C benefit — but net benefit remains positive for most

## SIP Taxation — Each SIP Installment Has Its Own Clock

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) adds units every month. **Each SIP unit has its own purchase date for tax purposes**.

**Example**: You invested ₹10,000/month SIP in a Nifty 50 index fund for 3 years (36 installments). When you redeem all units at year 3:
- Units bought in Month 1: Held >12 months → LTCG rate
- Units bought in Months 25-36 (last 12 months): Held ≤12 months → STCG rate

This means SIP redemptions have a mix of LTCG and STCG — the broker/fund house typically provides a capital gains statement that breaks down the gain by category.

**Rule of thumb**: For a 3-year SIP, approximately 2/3 of units are LTCG; 1/3 is STCG at redemption.

## Dividend vs Growth Option — Tax Difference

| Option | Dividend | Growth |
|--------|---------|--------|
| What happens | Fund pays out periodic dividends | Gains accumulate in NAV |
| Tax treatment | Dividend taxable at slab rate (after Budget 2020) | Tax only when you redeem |
| Reinvestment option | Dividend reinvestment (IDCW Reinvestment) — units added | N/A |
| Efficiency | Less tax-efficient for high-bracket investors | More tax-efficient (defer tax) |

**Always choose Growth option** (unless you need regular income) — dividend is taxable in your hands at slab rate. Growth defers tax until redemption and gets LTCG treatment after 12 months.

## TDS on Mutual Fund Redemption

**For resident individuals**: No TDS is deducted at source when you redeem mutual fund units. You pay tax when filing ITR.

**For NRIs**: TDS is deducted by the AMC at 20% on LTCG, 30% on STCG for equity funds. Claim credit in ITR.

## Capital Gains Statement for ITR

When you redeem mutual fund units:
1. Your AMC or CAMS/KFintech provides a **Capital Gains Statement**
2. Log in to CAMS (camsonline.com) or KFintech for a consolidated statement across all AMCs
3. The statement shows: Amount invested, redemption amount, gain/loss, STCG, LTCG, per fund
4. Use this statement to fill Schedule CG in your ITR

**AIS (Annual Information Statement)** also shows mutual fund transaction data — verify it matches your records.

## Setting Off Mutual Fund Losses

| Loss Type | Can Be Set Off Against |
|-----------|----------------------|
| STCG loss (equity fund) | STCG gains, LTCG gains |
| LTCG loss (equity fund) | Only LTCG gains |
| Debt fund loss (slab rate) | Any "Income from Other Sources"? — Actually treated as "capital gains" for carry-forward purposes, even though taxed at slab |

**Carry forward**: STCG and LTCG losses can be carried forward **8 years** (must file ITR on time to preserve carry-forward).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I started a SIP in 2022 and redeemed everything in 2026. Is my gain LTCG or STCG?

Mixed. Units bought before January 2025 (held >12 months by 2026 redemption) are LTCG. Units bought after January 2025 (held <12 months) are STCG. Your fund's capital gains statement will split this automatically. Typically, in a 4-year SIP redeemed at the end, most units are LTCG.

### My equity fund gave a ₹10L gain (LTCG). How much tax do I pay?

LTCG: ₹10,00,000. Exempt: ₹1,25,000. Taxable: ₹8,75,000. Tax at 12.5%: ₹1,09,375. Note: 87A rebate does NOT apply to LTCG — you cannot use your rebate to reduce this.

### My arbitrage fund gave ₹50,000 gain. Is it LTCG or STCG?

Arbitrage funds are classified as equity funds (>65% equity exposure through cash + futures positions). If held >12 months: LTCG at 12.5%. If held ≤12 months: STCG at 20%. Arbitrage funds are often held for short-term (3–12 months) as near-debt alternatives — STCG at 20% makes them less attractive than pre-Budget 2024 (when STCG was 15%).
