Investing in US markets (S&P 500, Nasdaq 100) from India lets you diversify beyond the Indian rupee, participate in global tech growth, and hedge against INR depreciation. Here's how to do it through Indian mutual funds — and the current regulatory status.

## Current Regulatory Status — RBI Overseas MF Limit

**Important note**: In February 2022, RBI asked AMCs to stop accepting fresh investments in overseas mutual funds when the industry-wide limit of $7 billion was breached.

**Current status (as of June 2026)**:
- Some international funds reopened selectively when domestic redemptions created headroom
- A few new FOFs (Fund of Funds investing in foreign ETFs) are accepting investments
- Check each fund's AMC website for current SIP/lumpsum status before investing

**For fresh investors**: Most Motilal Oswal, Mirae, DSP international funds are now accepting SIPs. Verify on AMC website before investing — limits open and close without notice.

## Types of International Funds Available in India

### 1. Fund of Funds (FOF) — Most Common

An Indian mutual fund that invests in a foreign ETF or foreign mutual fund.

**Example**: Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 FOF → invests in Nasdaq 100 ETF (QQQ) listed on US exchanges.

**Tax**: Taxed as **debt fund** (since the Indian fund itself isn't 65%+ Indian equity) — slab rate after 3 years, 20% with indexation for LTCG. This is the major downside vs direct equity.

**Expense ratio**: 0.2–0.5% (Indian FOF fee) + underlying ETF expense. Total 0.5–1%.

### 2. Feeder Funds

Indian fund that "feeds" into a foreign parent fund managed by the same AMC globally.

**Example**: Franklin India Feeder — Franklin US Opportunities Fund → invests in Franklin's US equity strategy.

**Tax**: Same as FOF — debt fund treatment.

### 3. International ETFs Listed in India (Rare)

Some international ETFs are listed on Indian exchanges — but these are typically FOF structures at the AMC level.

## Tax on International Mutual Funds India

This is the biggest catch with international funds in India:

**Taxed as DEBT funds** (not equity funds, because <65% Indian equity):

| Holding Period | Tax |
|----------------|-----|
| <3 years | Short-term capital gain → taxed at slab rate (up to 30%) |
| >3 years | Long-term capital gain → 12.5% without indexation (Budget 2024 change) |

**Budget 2024 change**: From July 2024, LTCG on debt funds is 12.5% WITHOUT indexation (previously 20% WITH indexation). This hurt international funds that previously benefited from indexation against rupee depreciation.

**Compare to equity fund**: LTCG taxed at 12.5% after 12 months (not 36 months) for equity funds. International funds need 3 years for LTCG treatment — much longer lock-in.

**The Parag Parikh exception**: PPFCF has international holdings but is taxed as equity (12 months LTCG) because >65% in Indian stocks. Best of both worlds — but you're getting curated US stocks via the fund manager, not pure index.

## LRS — How Much Can You Invest Overseas?

**LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme)**: Every Indian resident can remit up to **$250,000 per year** for overseas investments (including foreign direct equity, ETFs).

**For domestic international MFs**: No LRS needed — you're buying an Indian mutual fund in INR. LRS applies only if you buy US stocks/ETFs directly via platforms like Vested, INDmoney, or international brokers.

**TCS on LRS**: From October 2023, 20% TCS (Tax Collected at Source) applies on LRS remittances above ₹7 lakh in a financial year. TCS is creditable in your ITR (not a final tax), but impacts cash flow.

For domestic international mutual funds: **No TCS, no LRS limit** — you just buy the Indian FOF in rupees like any other MF.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I claim US dividend tax credit in India?

Yes — under DTAA (Double Tax Avoidance Agreement) between India and US. If US withholds 25% on dividends, you can claim credit against your Indian tax liability. Requires filing Form 67 with ITR. But for FOF investors in India, dividends are reinvested in NAV — you don't receive dividends separately.

### Are international MFs safe from US regulatory risk?

The Indian FOF holds foreign ETFs through custodians. If the underlying US ETF is fine, the Indian fund is fine. Indian SEBI regulations protect you from domestic AMC fraud. There's no scenario where you lose money because of a US regulatory issue — the underlying ETF (like QQQ) continues to exist.

### What happens if RBI restricts overseas MF investments again?

Existing SIP/holdings are not affected. You may be prevented from adding *new* investments. Historical precedent: 2022 restriction — existing investors could hold and redeem, but no new purchases. Rare and temporary.

### Can NRIs invest in international MFs in India?

Yes — NRIs with Indian bank accounts (NRO/NRE) can invest in Indian international MFs. NRI tax rules apply for redemption (TDS deducted at source). Check repatriation rules for NRO-funded investments.
