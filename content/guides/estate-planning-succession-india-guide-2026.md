# Estate Planning India 2026 — Complete Guide: Will, Nomination, Succession

Most Indians don't plan for asset transfer after death. The result: family disputes, legal delays (probate can take years), and assets frozen in courts. A simple will + proper nominations prevents all of this.

---

## What Happens If You Die Without a Will (Intestate Succession)

Dying without a will means the **Hindu Succession Act, 1956** (or Muslim Personal Law / Indian Succession Act for others) decides who gets what.

### For Hindus (including Sikhs, Jains, Buddhists)

**Class I heirs** (inherit equally, first priority):

| Heir | Share |
|------|-------|
| Sons | Equal share |
| Daughters | Equal share (since 2005 amendment — daughters have equal right as sons) |
| Widow/wife | Equal share |
| Mother | Equal share |

**If all Class I heirs are absent**: Class II heirs (father, brothers, sisters, etc.) inherit.

**Example**: You die without a will. Survivors: wife, 2 sons, 1 daughter, mother.  
**Each gets 1/5 (20%)** of your assets. Your wife gets 20%, not "everything."

This creates co-ownership problems: your home is now owned by 5 people. Selling requires all 5 to agree and sign.

### Key Rules for Daughters (2005 Amendment)

Hindu Succession Act was amended in 2005: daughters now have equal coparcenary rights in ancestral property as sons. This applies even if the daughter was born before 2005 and even if the father died before 2005 (Supreme Court 2020 ruling). Daughters cannot be excluded from ancestral property by a will — but self-acquired property can be willed to anyone.

## The Four Pillars of Estate Planning

### Pillar 1: Write a Will

A will is a legal document specifying who gets what after your death.

**What it can cover**:
- Self-acquired property (flat, land bought by you)
- Bank accounts, investments, jewelry
- Business interests
- Guardianship of minor children

**What it cannot override**:
- Ancestral/coparcenary property claims by daughters/sons (to some extent)
- Gifts already made during lifetime
- Trusts already created

**How to write a valid will**:
1. Must be in writing (no oral wills for Hindus with property)
2. Signed by you (testator) in presence of 2 witnesses
3. Witnesses must sign in your presence (witnesses cannot be beneficiaries)
4. No stamp duty required for will creation
5. **Registration optional but strongly recommended**

**Will registration**: At Sub-Registrar office in your jurisdiction. Cost: ₹500–2,000 stamp paper + registration fee. A registered will is harder to challenge in court.

**Keep will updated**: After major life events (marriage, divorce, new property purchase, birth of children).

---

### Pillar 2: Nominations in All Financial Accounts

Nomination and inheritance are **two separate things**:

| Account | Nomination Means | Inheritance Means |
|---------|-----------------|------------------|
| **Bank account** | Nominee receives the money temporarily as custodian | Legal heirs (per will or succession law) are ultimate owners |
| **Demat/stocks** | Nominee can operate/receive shares | Legal heirs are ultimate owners |
| **EPF** | Nominee is legal owner (special rule — EPF nominee > legal heir) | — |
| **PPF** | Nominee receives amount; legal heirs are owners | — |
| **Life insurance** | Nominee is absolute owner of claim money | — |

**Key distinction**: In EPF and life insurance, the nominee is typically the final recipient (not just a custodian). In bank accounts and mutual funds, the nominee is a custodian who must later distribute to legal heirs as per will or succession law.

**Where to add nominations immediately**:
- Bank accounts (savings/FD) — branch or net banking
- Demat account — broker settings
- EPF — EPFO portal (epfindia.gov.in) under member profile
- PPF — at bank/post office where account is held
- Mutual funds — via folio in AMC website or RTA (CAMS/KFintech)
- Life insurance — insurer's portal or branch
- NPS — CRA (eNPS portal)

**Failure to nominate**: Your family will need to go through succession certificate or probate to access funds. Takes months to years.

---

### Pillar 3: Joint Holding with Survivorship

In jointly held assets, the surviving holder gets the asset automatically — **no probate, no succession certificate needed**:

| Asset | How to Set Up Joint Holding |
|-------|---------------------------|
| **Bank account** | Open as "joint account, either or survivor" |
| **Demat account** | Add second holder with "or survivor" clause |
| **Property** | Register with spouse as co-owner (sale deed with two names) |
| **Mutual fund** | Add second holder to the folio |

**Warning on property joint ownership**: Adding someone as co-owner is a one-time transfer. To remove them later, both must cooperate. Use survivorship for spouse; not advisable for casual co-ownership.

---

### Pillar 4: Trust Formation (For Large Assets)

A **private family trust** is useful when:
- Estate is large (₹1Cr+) and you want to control distribution
- You have minor children who can't inherit directly
- You want to protect assets from future litigation
- You want to specify conditions (e.g., children inherit at age 25)

**Types of trust relevant for estate planning**:
- **Revocable living trust**: You remain trustee during life; transfers on death without probate
- **Irrevocable trust**: Assets transferred permanently; provides tax and asset protection benefits

**Trust setup process**: Requires a trust deed (legal document), trust registration under Registration Act or Indian Trusts Act 1882, and appointment of trustees. Cost: CA/legal fees ₹20,000–1,00,000+.

**For most families with assets under ₹2Cr**: A clear will + proper nominations is simpler and sufficient. Trust becomes worthwhile for ₹5Cr+ estates or complex family situations.

## Probate — What It Is and When Needed

**Probate** = court-issued certificate confirming your will is valid and executor has authority to distribute assets.

| State | Probate Requirement |
|-------|-------------------|
| Maharashtra, West Bengal, Chennai (Madras HC jurisdiction) | Mandatory for immovable property in will |
| Rest of India | Optional — but highly advisable if will is contested |

**Without probate (in mandatory states)**: Banks and registrars will not transfer immovable assets based on the will alone.

**Process**: File petition in District Court / High Court with original will, death certificate, and list of assets. Takes 6 months to 2 years. Hire a probate lawyer.

## Inheritance Tax in India — There Is None

India abolished Estate Duty (inheritance tax) in 1985. **There is no inheritance tax** — if you receive property through succession, gift from parents/siblings/spouse, or will, you do NOT pay tax on receipt.

However, **income from inherited assets is taxable**:
- Rent from inherited property: taxable
- Interest from inherited FD: taxable
- Capital gains on selling inherited property: taxable (cost basis = original purchase price of deceased, or FMV as of April 1, 2001, whichever higher)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My father made a will giving everything to one son. Can daughters challenge it?

For **self-acquired property**: Father has full right to will it to anyone — including one son only. Daughters cannot challenge this. But if he did NOT write a will, daughters get equal share as Class I heirs.  
For **ancestral property**: Post-2005 amendment, daughters have coparcenary rights that cannot be fully removed by will.

### Can I write a will to disinherit my wife?

Hindu law protects the spouse partially: even if a will disinherits the wife, she retains right to **maintenance** from the estate. A will cannot completely cut off a spouse's right to basic maintenance. However, specific property can be willed to others.

### I have FDs in multiple banks. What's the simplest way to ensure my wife gets them?

Add your wife as **joint holder with "either or survivor"** clause to all FDs. This way, the bank transfers the FD to her immediately on your death without any paperwork beyond a death certificate. No need for succession certificate.

### What if I die abroad as an NRI?

Your worldwide assets (including India assets) are governed by the laws of the country you were residing in (domicile) — complicated for NRIs. A separate Indian will (specifically for Indian assets) alongside your country-of-residence will is the cleanest solution. Consult a lawyer specializing in cross-border succession.
