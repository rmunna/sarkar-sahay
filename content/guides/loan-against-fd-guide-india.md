# Loan Against FD — Better Than Breaking Your Fixed Deposit

Most people break their FD the moment they need cash — paying a premature closure penalty and losing the locked-in interest rate. Almost no one knows about **loan against FD** — a facility that lets you borrow against your FD without disturbing it.

The math almost always favors the loan.

---

## What Is a Loan Against FD?

A loan against FD (also called **Overdraft against FD** or **OD against FD**) is a secured loan where your Fixed Deposit acts as collateral. The bank lends you money against your FD's value — you keep the FD intact and pay only for what you borrow.

**How it works:**
- FD continues earning interest throughout the loan period
- You pay loan interest only on the amount you withdraw
- Loan interest rate = FD interest rate + 1% to 2%
- Once you repay, your FD is released and continues as before

---

## Loan Against FD — Key Parameters

| Feature | Typical Terms |
|---------|--------------|
| **Loan amount** | 75%–90% of FD value |
| **Interest rate** | FD rate + 1% to 2% (varies by bank) |
| **Loan tenure** | Up to remaining FD tenure |
| **Processing fee** | Usually Nil or minimal |
| **Repayment** | Flexible — as overdraft (pay when you can) |
| **Pre-closure** | Anytime, no penalty |
| **Minimum FD** | ₹5,000 – ₹10,000 (varies by bank) |

**Example:**
You have a ₹2,00,000 FD at 7% interest. You need ₹1,50,000 urgently.
- Loan amount available: ₹1,80,000 (90% of ₹2,00,000)
- Loan interest rate: 7% + 1% = **8%**
- Your FD continues earning 7%
- Net cost of borrowing: 8% − 7% = **1% effectively**

## Loan vs Premature FD Closure — Real Comparison

**Situation:** ₹5,00,000 FD at 7% — 2 years remaining. Need ₹3,00,000 for 6 months.

### Option A: Break the FD (Premature Closure)

| | |
|--|--|
| FD amount | ₹5,00,000 |
| Premature penalty | −0.5 to 1% → ~₹3,500 |
| Interest lost (remaining 2 years at 7%) | ~₹70,000 lost |
| Lower rate applied (bank uses rate for actual holding period) | Rate reduction loss ~₹5,000 |
| **Total loss** | **~₹8,000–₹12,000** |

### Option B: Loan Against FD

| | |
|--|--|
| FD continues | Earns 7% on ₹5,00,000 = ₹35,000/year |
| Loan: ₹3,00,000 at 8% for 6 months | Interest paid: ₹12,000 |
| FD interest earned during 6 months | ₹17,500 |
| **Net cost** | **₹12,000 − ₹17,500 = −₹5,500 (you EARNED net)** |

**Result: Loan against FD costs you NOTHING — FD interest covers the loan interest and more.**

The loan almost always wins mathematically, especially when:
- You need money for a short period (under 1 year)
- FD has significant time remaining
- Your FD interest rate is close to the loan rate

## How to Get Loan Against FD

### SBI — Loans Against Fixed Deposit

1. Visit SBI branch (home branch where FD is held)
2. Fill Loan against FD application
3. Submit: FD receipt + KYC documents
4. **Overdraft account created same day** (linked to your savings account)
5. You can withdraw up to the OD limit anytime
6. Interest charged only on the amount withdrawn

**Or via YONO:** YONO app → Loans → Loan Against FD (available for select FDs)

### HDFC Bank

1. Visit branch / HDFC Net Banking
2. **Borrow → Loan Against FD / OD Against FD**
3. Online process — instant for existing FDs in HDFC
4. OD limit set to 90% of FD value

### ICICI Bank

1. iMobile → **Loans → Overdraft Against FD**
2. Or ICICI Net Banking → **Borrow → OD Against FD**
3. Instant online for existing ICICI FDs

## Overdraft vs Loan Against FD — What's the Difference?

Both use your FD as collateral:

| Feature | Overdraft (OD) | Term Loan |
|---------|---------------|-----------|
| **Access** | Like a current account — draw and repay anytime | Single disbursement |
| **Interest** | Only on amount used × days | On full loan amount |
| **Flexibility** | Maximum | Fixed EMI |
| **Best for** | Uncertain cash needs, working capital | One-time large need |

**Overdraft is almost always better** — you pay interest only on what you actually use. If you need ₹1L but take OD limit of ₹2L — you pay interest only on ₹1L used.

## When Does Breaking FD Make More Sense?

Despite the math favoring loan against FD, breaking the FD is better when:

1. **You need the money long-term** — If you need cash for 2+ years, loan interest accumulates significantly
2. **FD matures soon anyway** — If FD matures in 1–2 months, just wait or break (penalty is minimal)
3. **You won't be able to repay the loan** — An unpaid loan against FD results in bank liquidating your FD
4. **Better investment available** — Rare, but if you have a 5% FD and can invest at 15% — break and reinvest

## Can You Get Loan Against Post Office FD?

Yes — Post Office Term Deposits (TD) can be pledged for loans at post offices.
- Available after 6 months of holding
- Loan amount: Up to 85% of deposit
- Interest: 2% above TD rate
- Apply at the post office where the TD is held

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does taking a loan against FD affect my CIBIL score?**
A loan against FD is a secured loan and is reported to credit bureaus. Timely repayment improves your CIBIL score. Non-repayment damages it. However, the bank will eventually liquidate your FD to recover the loan — so there's no default risk from the bank's side.

**Can I take loan against a joint FD?**
Yes — loan can be availed on a joint FD. Typically all joint holders must sign the loan application. The loan is usually in the name of the primary holder.

**My FD is at one bank — can I get a loan at a different bank?**
No — loan against FD must be from the same bank where the FD is held. The FD is pledged (lien marked) in that bank's system.

**What happens if I can't repay the loan against FD?**
The bank invokes its lien on the FD and recovers the outstanding loan amount from the FD. The remaining amount (if any) is returned to you. This is why loan against FD has almost no default risk for the bank — and why the rates are so low.

**Can senior citizens also avail loan against FD?**
Yes — absolutely. In fact, loan against FD is an excellent option for senior citizens with SCSS or FDs who need temporary liquidity without disturbing their regular interest income.
