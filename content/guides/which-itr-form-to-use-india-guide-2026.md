# Which ITR Form to File India 2026 — Complete Guide

Filing in the wrong ITR form is a common mistake that can lead to defective return notices. The Income Tax Department may ask you to re-file in the correct form. Here's a clear guide to choosing correctly.

## Quick Decision Tree

```
Have any income from business/profession?
  ↓ Yes
  Use presumptive scheme (44AD/44ADA)?
    ↓ Yes → ITR-4 (Sugam)
    ↓ No  → ITR-3
  ↓ No
  Income > ₹50L OR foreign assets/income?
    ↓ Yes → ITR-2
    ↓ No
  Any capital gains (stocks, MF, property)?
    ↓ Yes → ITR-2
    ↓ No
  Multiple house properties or residential status NR/RNOR?
    ↓ Yes → ITR-2
    ↓ No → ITR-1 (Sahaj)
```

## ITR-1 (Sahaj) — Simplest Form

**Who can use**:
- Resident individuals only (not NRI, not RNOR)
- Total income ≤ ₹50 lakh
- Income only from: Salary/pension, ONE house property, Other sources (interest, dividend, family pension)
- Agricultural income ≤ ₹5,000
- **No capital gains of any kind**
- No brought-forward losses
- No foreign income or foreign assets

**Who CANNOT use ITR-1**:
- Anyone with capital gains (even ₹100 in LTCG from equity MF)
- Anyone with business/professional income
- Anyone with more than one house property
- Income > ₹50L
- NRI / RNOR
- Director of a company
- Anyone who holds unlisted shares

## ITR-2 — For Capital Gains

**Who should use**:
- Any individual/HUF who cannot use ITR-1
- Has capital gains (LTCG/STCG from equity shares, MF, property, gold, etc.)
- Income > ₹50L (salary or other income)
- More than one house property
- Foreign income or foreign assets
- NRI / RNOR residents
- Director of a company
- Agricultural income > ₹5,000 (with non-agricultural income)
- Brought-forward capital losses to claim

**Who CANNOT use ITR-2**:
- Anyone with business/professional income (even ₹1 in side income)
- Presumptive income filers (they use ITR-4)

## ITR-3 — For Business/Professional Income (Full Books)

**Who should use**:
- Individuals or HUFs with income from business or profession
- AND maintaining full books of accounts (not using presumptive scheme)
- Cases where profit is declared lower than 44AD/44ADA thresholds (opting out of presumptive)
- Business with losses to carry forward
- Director in a private company with salary income
- Freelancer with actual expenses tracked
- Anyone who opted out of Section 44AD and is now in the 5-year lock period

**Also use ITR-3 if you have**:
- Business income AND capital gains (ITR-3 covers everything)
- Salary + freelance with actual books

## ITR-4 (Sugam) — For Presumptive Taxation

**Who should use**:
- Resident individual, HUF, or firm (not company/LLP)
- With business income under Section 44AD (turnover ≤ ₹3 crore, declaring 6%/8%)
- With professional income under Section 44ADA (receipts ≤ ₹75L, declaring 50%)
- With transport business under Section 44AE

**Who CANNOT use ITR-4**:
- Anyone with capital gains of any kind (must use ITR-3 instead)
- Anyone with more than one house property
- Anyone with foreign assets/income
- NRI / RNOR
- Income > ₹50L from salary (if also having business income, combined check applies)

**If you have both freelance income (44ADA) AND capital gains**: Cannot use ITR-4. Must use ITR-3.

## Common Mistakes and Correct Form

| Situation | Wrong Choice | Correct Form |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Salaried, sold MF units with ₹5,000 LTCG | ITR-1 | **ITR-2** (any capital gains = ITR-2) |
| Freelance consultant, declaring 50% under 44ADA | ITR-3 | **ITR-4** (Sugam — simpler) |
| Salary + freelance income but actual expenses tracked | ITR-4 | **ITR-3** (can't use ITR-4 if maintaining actual books) |
| Freelancer with capital gains from MF | ITR-4 | **ITR-3** (ITR-4 doesn't have Schedule CG) |
| NRI with only rental income | ITR-1 | **ITR-2** (NRIs cannot use ITR-1) |
| Business with losses | ITR-4 | **ITR-3** (losses need actual books; ITR-4 only for 44AD/ADA) |

## ITR for Special Cases

### Salary + Capital Gains (Most Common Confusion)

**Sold equity MF or shares?** Even ₹1 in gains (LTCG or STCG) means you must file **ITR-2**, not ITR-1.

The IT portal pre-fills your AIS data, which shows all capital gains. If you file ITR-1 but have capital gains in your AIS, you may receive a defective return notice.

### Salary + Minor Freelance Income

If you did consulting on the side (even ₹50,000), you have "business/professional income." You cannot use ITR-1 or ITR-2. Use:
- **ITR-4** if declaring 50% under Section 44ADA (professional income ≤ ₹75L)
- **ITR-3** if tracking actual expenses or if income is from trading (business)

### Senior Citizens

Senior citizens follow the same rules as above. Just because Form 16 shows pension doesn't change the form — if they have capital gains, use ITR-2; if they have business income, use ITR-3/4.

## How to Check If Your ITR Form Is Correct

**On the Income Tax portal**:
1. Pre-fill your return — it auto-selects the applicable form based on your AIS data
2. The portal may flag errors if you try to file the wrong form with your data
3. When in doubt: Choose the higher-complexity form (ITR-3 covers more situations than ITR-2)

Going "too broad" in form selection (e.g., using ITR-3 when ITR-2 would suffice) is not an error. Going too narrow (e.g., using ITR-1 when you have capital gains) is a defective return.

## Due Dates — Same for All Forms

| Category | Due Date (AY 2026-27) |
|----------|----------------------|
| Individuals, HUF, firms (no audit) | July 31, 2026 |
| Individuals/firms requiring tax audit | October 31, 2026 |
| Transfer pricing audit | November 30, 2026 |

The form type doesn't change the due date — all individual non-audit returns are due July 31.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I'm salaried (₹12L CTC). I invested in index funds but didn't sell anything. Which ITR?

If you didn't redeem/sell any mutual fund units, there are no capital gains. Use **ITR-1** if other conditions are met (income ≤ ₹50L, one house, no business income). Merely holding units doesn't require ITR-2.

### I'm a freelancer and also have LTCG from selling my flat. Which form?

**ITR-3** — because you have both professional income (freelance, even if under 44ADA) AND capital gains from property sale. ITR-4 doesn't accommodate capital gains. ITR-3 covers all income types.

### My income is ₹95L from salary. Can I use ITR-2?

Yes — ITR-2 handles salary income above ₹50L. Use ITR-2 if you don't have business income. If you also have capital gains, ITR-2 handles that too.
