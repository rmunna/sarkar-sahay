# Term Insurance: How Much Cover Do You Actually Need?

Buying term insurance is Step 1. Buying the RIGHT amount is Step 2 — and most people either under-insure (risk to family) or over-insure (unnecessary premium expense).

Here are three methods to calculate adequate term insurance coverage, with India-specific context for 2026.

## Method 1: 10–15x Annual Income Rule (Simple)

**Formula**: Sum Assured = 10 to 15 × Annual Take-Home Income

| Annual Income | Suggested Cover (10x) | Suggested Cover (15x) |
|--------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| ₹5 lakh | ₹50 lakh | ₹75 lakh |
| ₹10 lakh | ₹1 crore | ₹1.5 crore |
| ₹20 lakh | ₹2 crore | ₹3 crore |
| ₹50 lakh | ₹5 crore | ₹7.5 crore |

**Why 10–15x?** The idea: if you die, your family invests the sum assured at ~7–8% return. The interest income replaces your salary.

₹1 crore invested at 7% = ₹7L/year income. If your salary was ₹10L, the family needs the corpus to generate ₹10L (or more, with inflation). Hence 15x is safer.

**Use 10x if**:
- You're close to debt-free
- Spouse is earning
- Children are grown up
- Retirement is close (financial obligations falling)

**Use 15x if**:
- Young children who need 15+ years of support
- Large home loan outstanding
- Non-earning spouse fully dependent on you
- High-liability decade (education, weddings ahead)

## Method 2: DIME Formula (More Comprehensive)

**DIME = Debt + Income + Mortgage + Education**

| Component | How to Calculate |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **D (Debt)** | Total outstanding: home loan + car loan + credit card + personal loans |
| **I (Income)** | Annual income × years family needs support (children youngest age to 25 = years) |
| **M (Mortgage)** | Outstanding home loan principal |
| **E (Education)** | Cost of children's education from current age to professional degree |

**Example**:
- Annual income: ₹15L; Youngest child age 5 (needs 20 years of support)
- Home loan outstanding: ₹60L
- Car loan: ₹5L
- Children's education estimate: ₹25L per child × 2 children = ₹50L
- Emergency buffer: ₹10L

DIME = ₹65L (all debt) + ₹3Cr (₹15L × 20) + ₹60L (mortgage) + ₹50L (education) = **₹4.75 crore**

This may seem high — but this is the actual financial need if you were to die today, leaving your family with no primary income.

## Method 3: HLV (Human Life Value) Method

HLV calculates the present value of your **future earnings** that your family would lose if you died.

**Formula**: HLV = (Annual income − Annual expenses) × PVIFA (Present Value Interest Factor of Annuity)

**Simplified**:
- Annual income: ₹18L
- Annual personal expenses (spent on yourself, not family): ₹4L
- Family's dependency on your income: ₹14L/year
- Working years remaining: 25 years
- Discount rate: 8% (inflation + investment adjustment)
- PVIFA for 25 years at 8%: ~10.67
- **HLV = ₹14L × 10.67 = ₹1.49 crore**

Add existing liabilities (home loan, etc.) and subtract existing assets (EPF, other investments):
- Existing assets: ₹20L
- Home loan: ₹40L
- **Final insurance need**: ₹1.49Cr + ₹40L − ₹20L = **₹1.69 crore**

## Common Mistakes in Term Insurance Buying

| Mistake | Better Approach |
|---------|----------------|
| Buying endowment/ULIP instead of term | Pure term: no investment component, maximum cover for minimum premium |
| Under-insuring (₹50L cover on ₹20L salary) | Use 10–15x rule; at least ₹1Cr for most salaried professionals |
| Buying for short tenure (20 years) | Cover should last till retirement or youngest child's financial independence |
| Not adding riders | Critical illness rider + accidental death benefit worth considering |
| Waiting till 40 to buy | Premium roughly doubles from 30 to 40 for same cover |
| Not disclosing health conditions | Non-disclosure voids the claim; always disclose honestly |

## How Long Should the Policy Term Be?

**Minimum tenure**: Till your youngest child turns 25 (or completes education/marriage).

**Recommended tenure**: Till age 60–65 (your planned retirement age).

If you're 30 today and plan to retire at 60: 30-year term policy.

**After retirement**: At retirement, ideally you have:
- EPF/NPS corpus generating income
- No major liabilities (home loan closed)
- Children financially independent
- So term insurance need reduces or disappears post-retirement

## Tax Benefit on Term Insurance

- Premium paid on term insurance: **Deductible under Section 80C** up to ₹1.5 lakh (old regime only)
- Claim received by nominee: **Tax-free under Section 10(10D)** — no income tax on the sum assured received

## Single vs Joint Life Term Policy

If both spouses are earning and contributing to family finances:
- **Consider term policies for both**: Each covers the other's dependents
- **Joint term policy**: Usually covers both lives but pays only on first death — check whether second life continues to be covered

For dual-income households with children: Both partners should ideally have separate term covers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I have ₹30L EPF and ₹50L in MFs. Should I reduce my term cover?

Yes — existing assets reduce the gap your family needs to fill. If your cover need (from DIME/HLV) is ₹2Cr and you have ₹80L in assets, you technically need ₹1.2Cr term. However, don't count assets that are locked (PPF till maturity, SSY till daughter's age 21) as liquid replacement.

### I'm single with no dependents. Do I need term insurance?

No — term insurance is for income replacement for **dependents**. If no one depends on your income, term insurance isn't necessary. When you get married or have children, buy immediately.

### My company gives ₹1 crore life insurance as group cover. Is that enough?

Group insurance is a benefit but has limitations: it covers you only while employed, and you lose coverage if you change jobs. Don't rely on group cover as your primary insurance. Buy personal term in addition.
