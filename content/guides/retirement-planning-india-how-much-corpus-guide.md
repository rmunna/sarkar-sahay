# Retirement Planning India 2026 — How Much Corpus You Need and How to Build It

Retirement planning is simple in concept: accumulate enough that returns on your corpus sustain your lifestyle forever, without eating into the principal. Here's a practical India-specific guide.

## How Much Corpus Do You Need?

### The 25x Rule (Safe Withdrawal Rate)

**Rule**: Your retirement corpus should be **25 times your annual expenses** at retirement.

This is based on the "4% withdrawal rate" — historically, withdrawing 4% of your corpus annually has sustained portfolios for 30+ years.

| Monthly Expenses at Retirement | Annual Expenses | Corpus Needed (25x) |
|-------------------------------|----------------|---------------------|
| ₹30,000/month | ₹3,60,000 | **₹90 lakh** |
| ₹50,000/month | ₹6,00,000 | **₹1.5 crore** |
| ₹75,000/month | ₹9,00,000 | **₹2.25 crore** |
| ₹1,00,000/month | ₹12,00,000 | **₹3 crore** |
| ₹2,00,000/month | ₹24,00,000 | **₹6 crore** |

**Adjustment for India**: 4% withdrawal rate assumes a roughly 50:50 equity-debt portfolio. Indian equity historically returns 12–14%; Indian debt 7–9%. With proper allocation, a 3.5–4% withdrawal rate is sustainable.

### Inflation Factor

Your current ₹50,000/month expenses will be more at retirement due to inflation. At 6% annual inflation:
- ₹50,000 today
- After 20 years (retiring at 60 if you're 40 now): ₹1,60,357/month
- Annual expenses at retirement: ₹19.2L
- Corpus needed: 25x ₹19.2L = **₹4.8 crore**

**Always plan for inflated expenses at retirement**, not today's expenses.

## Age-Wise Investment Strategy

### Age 22–35: Aggressive Equity Phase

At 25, you have **35 years** of compounding before retirement. Equity's volatility is irrelevant over 35 years.

**Target allocation**: 80–90% equity, 10–20% debt

**Where to invest**:
- NPS (Tier 1): 75% equity (E) + 25% bonds — tax benefit + very low cost
- ELSS: For 80C limit + equity exposure
- Index funds: Nifty 50 + Nifty Midcap 150 (via Zerodha/Groww direct)
- EPF: Automatic via employer (8.15% guaranteed + equity-like role in retirement)

**Monthly target**: Save 20–25% of take-home for retirement at this age.

### Age 35–50: Balanced Phase

At 40, you have 20 years. Still enough for equity growth, but risk capacity is moderate.

**Target allocation**: 60–70% equity, 30–40% debt

**Where to invest**:
- Continue NPS (switch to Conservative Lifecycle fund if risk-averse)
- Continue EPF (don't withdraw on job changes — transfer)
- PPF: Max ₹1.5L/year for tax-free debt component
- Index funds: Continue SIP
- Consider SGB (Sovereign Gold Bond) for 5–10% gold allocation

**Monthly target**: 25–30% of take-home

### Age 50–60: Capital Preservation Phase

At 55, 5 years to retirement. Equity can recover short-term dips, but risk appetite must drop.

**Target allocation**: 40–50% equity, 50–60% debt

**Where to invest**:
- NPS: Shift to lower equity allocation (50% or below)
- PPF: Continue (15-year blocks; last extension if needed)
- SCSS: Start contributing after 60 — but plan ahead
- Start building annuity/SWP strategy for post-retirement income

## The Three Retirement Income Sources

A strong retirement plan has three legs:

### Leg 1: Guaranteed / Near-Guaranteed Income
- EPF pension (EPS): Monthly pension from EPFO (typically small — ₹2,000–₹7,500/month)
- NPS Annuity: 40% of NPS corpus mandatorily buys an annuity
- PPF maturity: Lump sum to be systematically withdrawn
- SCSS: Quarterly interest payments (currently 8.2%/year)

### Leg 2: Equity Portfolio (Growth + Inflation Protection)
- Index fund corpus
- NPS equity portion (60% you receive as lump sum tax-free)
- Stocks/direct equity (for experienced investors)

This is your **SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan)** source — monthly withdrawal from equity MF.

### Leg 3: Real Estate Rental Income (Optional)
If you own a property, rental income provides inflation-linked cash flow. But it requires management effort (tenant issues, maintenance). Not essential if you have a large corpus.

## The SWP Strategy for Monthly Income After Retirement

**SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan)**: Redeem a fixed amount from your mutual fund corpus every month. The remaining corpus continues to grow.

**Example**: ₹1.5 crore corpus, 60% in balanced index fund portfolio
- Monthly withdrawal (4% annual SWP): ₹50,000/month
- At 10% annual return on corpus: Corpus grows enough to sustain the ₹50K withdrawal

As inflation rises, step up withdrawal by 5–6% annually. The corpus should still last 30+ years if initial allocation is reasonable.

## EPF — Don't Withdraw Between Jobs

Your EPF at retirement can be significant:

If you contributed ₹5,000/month (employee + employer) from age 25 to 60 (35 years) at 8.15% interest:
- Monthly contribution: ₹9,600 (employee ₹4,800 + employer ₹4,800 to EPF portion — excluding EPS)
- Corpus at 60: ~**₹1.8 crore** (at 8.15% for 35 years)

**Never withdraw EPF between jobs** (transfer instead). This single decision can mean the difference between ₹50L and ₹1.8 crore.

## NPS at Maturity — How It Works

At age 60:
- 60% of NPS corpus: **Withdrawn as tax-free lump sum**
- 40%: Mandatorily used to buy **annuity** (pension) from a life insurance company
  - Monthly pension: ~5–7% annual return on the 40% corpus
  - This pension is taxable as salary income

**Example**: NPS corpus ₹80L at 60:
- Tax-free lump sum: ₹48L (60%)
- Annuity purchase: ₹32L (40%) → Monthly pension: ~₹14,000–₹18,000/month

The annuity income is low — which is why you need the other retirement legs too.

## Financial Independence / Early Retirement (FIRE) India

**FIRE** = Financially Independent, Retire Early. Popular among high-income tech professionals.

**Lean FIRE**: ₹50K/month expenses → ₹1.5 crore corpus
**FIRE**: ₹75K/month expenses → ₹2.25 crore corpus
**Fat FIRE**: ₹1.5L/month expenses → ₹4.5 crore corpus

Achievable in India at 40–45 with disciplined saving (40–50% of income) from 25–40.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I'm 35 and have ₹0 saved for retirement. Is it too late?

You have 25 years left. Not too late — but you need to be aggressive. If you invest ₹30,000/month in a 70% equity portfolio at 12% CAGR from age 35 to 60: ₹3.8 crore at retirement. Adequate for ₹1.2–1.5L/month expenses. Start now — every year of delay costs significantly in compounding.

### My parents say PPF is the best for retirement. Is that right?

PPF is excellent for the debt/guaranteed component of retirement savings. But at 7.1% return, it barely beats inflation (6%) for the corpus to grow meaningfully. You need equity (NPS, ELSS, index funds) for growth. PPF is one leg of the stool — not the whole strategy.

### How much should I put in NPS vs equity mutual fund?

Use NPS for tax optimization (₹50K extra via 80CCD(1B), employer contribution via 80CCD(2)). Beyond that, direct index funds are more flexible (no lock-in, no mandatory annuity requirement). NPS first for tax benefits, then index funds for additional corpus building.
