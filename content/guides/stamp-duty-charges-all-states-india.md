## What is Stamp Duty?

Stamp duty is a tax levied by state governments on property transactions — buying a house, flat, plot, or any immovable property. It is mandatory under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 (and respective state stamp acts) and must be paid before or during property registration.

**Registration charges** are a separate fee paid to the Sub-Registrar's office for officially recording the property transfer in government records. Both stamp duty and registration charges must be paid to make the sale deed legally valid.

> **Important:** A property document that is not stamped or is under-stamped is not admissible as evidence in court.

## Concessions and Exemptions

### Women Buyers
Most states offer **1-2% lower stamp duty** for women buyers or joint ownership with a woman as first holder. States with concessions:
- **Delhi:** 4% for women vs 6% for men (saves up to ₹2 lakh on a ₹1 crore property)
- **UP:** 6% for women vs 7% for men
- **Haryana:** 5% for women vs 7% for men
- **Maharashtra:** 5% for women vs 6% for men (in urban areas)
- **Rajasthan:** 5% for women vs 6% for men

### First-Time Buyer Concessions
- **PMAY Beneficiaries:** Some states offer reduced stamp duty for Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana beneficiaries
- **Affordable Housing:** Properties under certain value thresholds may get concessional rates in states like Maharashtra and Rajasthan

### SC/ST Concessions
- **Karnataka:** ₹5,000 concession for properties below ₹20 lakh
- **Madhya Pradesh:** Reduced rates for SC/ST buyers on first property
- **Rajasthan:** Concessional rates available for SC/ST and EWS category
- **UP:** Some concessions available — check with local Sub-Registrar

### Senior Citizens
- Limited states offer specific concessions; check with local Sub-Registrar office

## How to Calculate Stamp Duty

Stamp duty is calculated on **whichever is higher**:
1. **Actual sale consideration** (price mentioned in agreement), OR
2. **Circle rate / Guideline value / Ready reckoner rate** (government-determined minimum value)

### Formula

```
Stamp Duty = Rate (%) × Higher of (Agreement Value, Circle Rate Value)
Registration Charges = Rate (%) × Higher of (Agreement Value, Circle Rate Value)
Total = Stamp Duty + Registration Charges + Any Surcharge/Cess
```

### Example (Maharashtra — Male Buyer)

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Property Agreement Value | ₹80,00,000 |
| Circle Rate Value | ₹75,00,000 |
| **Stamp Duty Base** | **₹80,00,000** (higher) |
| Stamp Duty (6%) | ₹4,80,000 |
| Registration (1%, max ₹30,000) | ₹30,000 |
| **Total** | **₹5,10,000** |

> For a detailed understanding of circle rates, see our [Circle Rate / Guideline Value guide](/guide/circle-rate-guideline-value-property).

## How to Pay Stamp Duty Online

### Method 1: e-Stamping (SHCIL)
1. Visit the Stock Holding Corporation (SHCIL) portal or authorized bank
2. Fill the stamp duty details (property value, type, parties)
3. Pay via net banking, NEFT, or at counter
4. Receive an e-stamp certificate with a unique identification number (UIN)
5. Verify the e-stamp at [shcilestamp.com](https://www.shcilestamp.com)

### Method 2: State Portal — Maharashtra (IGRS)
1. Visit [igrs.maharashtra.gov.in](https://igrs.maharashtra.gov.in)
2. Register/login → Select "Pay Stamp Duty"
3. Enter property details (area, type, location, value)
4. System calculates stamp duty based on ready reckoner rates
5. Pay via net banking/debit card
6. Download payment receipt and e-stamp certificate

### Method 3: State Portal — UP (IGRS UP)
1. Visit [igrsup.gov.in](https://igrsup.gov.in)
2. Login → Select property type and enter details
3. Pay stamp duty + registration charges online
4. Book appointment for document registration

### Method 4: State Portal — Tamil Nadu
1. Visit [tnreginet.gov.in](https://tnreginet.gov.in)
2. Select "E-stamping" option
3. Enter details and pay online
4. Collect e-stamp from authorized center

> For the complete property registration process, see our [Property Registration Online guide](/guide/property-registration-online) and [IGRS Maharashtra guide](/guide/igrs-maharashtra-property-registration).

## Documents Required for Stamp Duty Payment

- **Sale deed / Agreement to sell** (draft copy)
- **PAN card** of buyer and seller
- **Aadhaar card** of buyer and seller
- **Property documents** (previous chain of title)
- **NOC from society/builder** (if applicable)
- **Circle rate / Ready reckoner valuation** printout
- **Passport-size photographs**
- **Power of Attorney** (if applicable)

## Important Tips

1. **Register in a woman's name** to save 1-2% stamp duty in most states — this can mean savings of ₹50,000 to ₹2,00,000 on a ₹1 crore property
2. **Never under-report property value** — if caught, you face penalty of up to 10x the deficit stamp duty plus prosecution
3. **Claim stamp duty deduction** under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act (up to ₹1.5 lakh) in the year of purchase
4. **Verify circle rates** before finalizing the agreement — the stamp duty is on the higher of agreement or circle rate value
5. **Keep original e-stamp certificates safely** — you'll need them for future sale, loan, or disputes

## Stamp Duty Refund

If a property transaction is cancelled:
- **Full refund** if stamp paper is unused and within 6 months of purchase (varies by state)
- **Partial refund** (usually minus 10%) if agreement is cancelled before registration
- Apply to the Collector/District Registrar with proof of cancellation
- Processing time: 3-6 months typically

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who pays stamp duty — buyer or seller?
The **buyer** pays stamp duty and registration charges in most states. However, parties can mutually agree on the split in the sale agreement.

### Is stamp duty same across India?
No. Stamp duty is a **state subject** — each state sets its own rates. It ranges from about 4% (Gujarat) to 8% (Kerala). Always check your specific state's current rates.

### Can I claim stamp duty as tax deduction?
Yes. Stamp duty and registration charges paid on a **residential property** can be claimed under **Section 80C** of the Income Tax Act, subject to the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit, in the year of payment.

### What happens if I don't pay stamp duty?
The document becomes **legally invalid** and is not admissible as evidence in any court. You may also face a penalty of up to 2-10 times the deficit amount (varies by state) plus potential prosecution.

### Is stamp duty applicable on gifted property?
Yes. Even gift deeds and property transfers require stamp duty payment. Some states offer reduced rates for family transfers (e.g., blood relatives in Maharashtra and Karnataka).

### What is the difference between stamp duty and registration charges?
**Stamp duty** is a tax on the transaction. **Registration charges** are a fee for recording the transaction in government records. Both are mandatory and separate payments.

### Can stamp duty be paid in installments?
Generally, no. Stamp duty must be paid in full **before or at the time of document registration**. Some states allow partial advance payment for under-construction properties.
