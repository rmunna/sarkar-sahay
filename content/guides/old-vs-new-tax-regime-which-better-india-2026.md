# Old vs New Tax Regime India 2026 — Which Should You Choose?

The most common tax decision for Indian salaried employees: Should you pick the **new tax regime** (default from AY 2024-25) or stick with the **old tax regime**?

The answer depends on your specific deductions. Here's the complete analysis.

## The New Tax Regime (Default 2026)

**New regime is the default**. If you don't tell your employer which regime to use, they apply the new regime for TDS.

### New Regime Slabs (FY 2025-26, AY 2026-27)

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|------------|---------|
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | 0% |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

**Key benefit**: Section 87A rebate of ₹60,000 → **Zero tax up to ₹12L income** (₹12.75L for salaried after ₹75K standard deduction).

### What You CAN Get in New Regime
- Standard deduction: ₹75,000 (salaried)
- NPS employer contribution deduction: 80CCD(2) — up to 14% of basic salary
- HRA: NOT exempt (taxable fully)
- No 80C, 80D, 80G, 80E deductions

## The Old Tax Regime

### Old Regime Slabs (FY 2025-26)

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|------------|---------|
| Up to ₹2,50,000 | 0% |
| ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹10,00,000 | 30% |

**87A rebate**: Up to ₹12,500 for income ≤ ₹5L → Zero tax up to ₹5L.

### What You CAN Deduct in Old Regime

| Deduction | Max Amount |
|-----------|-----------|
| Standard deduction (salaried) | ₹50,000 |
| Section 80C (PPF, ELSS, LIC, etc.) | ₹1,50,000 |
| Section 80D (health insurance) | ₹25,000 (₹50,000 for parents 60+) |
| Section 24b (home loan interest) | ₹2,00,000 (self-occupied) |
| Section 80CCD(1B) (extra NPS) | ₹50,000 |
| HRA exemption | Varies (up to actual rent paid less 10% basic) |
| LTA exemption | Up to actual travel cost |
| Section 80E (education loan interest) | Unlimited (8 years) |
| Section 80TTA (savings interest) | ₹10,000 |
| Section 80G (donations) | Varies |

**Total possible deductions**: ₹4.5L–₹6L+ for someone with home loan + HRA + full 80C + NPS + health insurance

## Break-Even Analysis — When Is Old Regime Better?

**The old regime is better ONLY if your total deductions exceed the difference in tax between regimes.**

### Comparison at ₹10L Gross Salary

**New regime (₹10L salary)**:
- Taxable income: ₹10L − ₹75K standard deduction = ₹9,25,000
- Tax: 0 + ₹20,000 (5% of ₹4L) + ₹12,500 (10% of ₹1.25L) = ₹32,500
- Wait: ₹4L-₹8L at 5% = ₹20K; ₹8L-₹9.25L at 10% = ₹12,500; Total = ₹32,500

**Old regime (₹10L salary, no deductions)**:
- Taxable: ₹9,50,000 (after ₹50K standard deduction)
- Tax: 0 + ₹12,500 (5% of ₹2.5L) + ₹90,000 (20% of ₹4.5L) = ₹1,02,500

With maximum deductions (old regime):
- 80C: ₹1,50,000
- 80D: ₹25,000
- Standard deduction: ₹50,000
- Total deductions: ₹2,25,000
- Taxable income: ₹10L − ₹2,25,000 = ₹7,75,000
- Tax: ₹12,500 + 20% of ₹2,75,000 = ₹12,500 + ₹55,000 = ₹67,500

**New regime still wins** at ₹10L (₹32,500 vs ₹67,500 — even with full 80C + 80D).

### Adding Home Loan Interest

With home loan (old regime, ₹10L salary):
- Add Section 24b: ₹2,00,000 deduction
- Total deductions: ₹4,25,000
- Taxable income: ₹10L − ₹4,25,000 = ₹5,75,000
- Tax: ₹12,500 + 20% of ₹75,000 = ₹12,500 + ₹15,000 = ₹27,500 (before 87A)
- 87A rebate: Income is ₹5,75,000 > ₹5L → No full rebate, but 87A gives partial relief

With 80D, HRA, NPS:
- Total deductions could reach ₹5,75,000+
- Taxable income: ₹10L − ₹5,75,000 = ₹4,25,000
- Tax (old regime): 5% of ₹1,75,000 = ₹8,750 (after ₹2.5L exemption)
- 87A rebate: Income ₹4,25,000 < ₹5L → Full 87A rebate of ₹12,500 → **Zero tax**

**Old regime can give zero tax at ₹10L** if you have enough deductions (home loan + HRA + full 80C + NPS + health insurance).

But new regime also gives ₹32,500 tax (not zero) — so old regime wins IF deductions are large enough.

## Quick Decision Table

| Salary Range | Deductions | Better Regime |
|-------------|-----------|--------------|
| Below ₹8L | Any | **New Regime** (zero tax up to ₹7L or lower effective rate) |
| ₹8L–₹12L | Minimal (<₹2L deductions) | **New Regime** |
| ₹8L–₹12L | Large (home loan + HRA + 80C + NPS) | **Old Regime** |
| ₹12L–₹15L | Minimal | **New Regime** |
| ₹12L–₹15L | Home loan ₹2L + HRA ₹1.5L + 80C ₹1.5L + NPS ₹50K = ₹5.5L+ | **Old Regime** |
| Above ₹15L | Large deductions (₹5L+) | Depends — calculate both |
| Above ₹15L | No deductions | **New Regime** |
| Very high salary (₹30L+) | Even with max deductions (₹5L) | **New Regime** (slab difference too large) |

## The "Magic Formula" for Old Regime Benefit

**Old regime is better when**: (Deduction-driven old regime tax) < (New regime tax)

Roughly: You need deductions of at least ₹2–3.75L beyond the standard deduction to break even, depending on your income level.

**Minimum deductions to consider old regime**:
- At ₹10L salary: Need >₹3.5–4L in deductions beyond standard deduction
- At ₹15L salary: Need >₹4–4.5L in deductions
- At ₹20L salary: Need >₹4.5–5L in deductions

## How to Declare Regime Choice

**To employer**: Submit Form 10-IE declaration or use employer's payroll system to select regime before April of the financial year. Your employer applies TDS accordingly.

**In ITR**: If you haven't submitted Form 10-IE, you can still choose your regime while filing ITR. The default is new regime — to choose old regime, select it in the ITR portal when filing.

**Switching every year**: Salaried individuals can switch between old and new regime each year when filing ITR. Business owners have a one-time irrevocable choice (with 5-year consequences for switching).

## The Verdict for 2026

**Most people should use the new regime** — especially if:
- You don't have a home loan
- You're a renter in the new regime (can't claim HRA)
- You haven't fully utilized 80C + NPS + health insurance
- Salary below ₹12L (zero tax up to ₹12.75L in new regime)

**Old regime is worth it if**:
- Home loan interest > ₹1.5L/year AND you have HRA benefit
- You max out: 80C (₹1.5L) + 80CCD(1B) NPS (₹50K) + 80D (₹25–50K) + home loan (₹2L) + HRA
- Total deductions exceed ₹5L beyond standard deduction

**When in doubt**: Calculate both and pick lower tax. The income tax portal shows both calculations when you file ITR.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I have a home loan (₹1.8L interest), HRA (₹1.2L exempt), and max 80C (₹1.5L). My salary is ₹14L. Which regime?

Old regime total deductions: ₹50K standard + ₹1,50,000 (80C) + ₹1,80,000 (24b) + ₹1,20,000 (HRA) = ₹5,00,000 deductions. Taxable: ₹9,00,000. Old regime tax: ₹12,500 + 20% of ₹4,00,000 = ₹92,500.

New regime: ₹14,00,000 − ₹75,000 = ₹13,25,000. Tax: ₹20K + ₹40K + ₹48,750 = ₹1,08,750.

**Old regime saves ₹16,250** in this case. Old regime wins.

### My employer deducted TDS under new regime but I want to switch to old regime in ITR. Can I?

Yes — you can select old regime when filing your ITR, even if employer deducted TDS under new regime. The difference (if old regime gives lower tax) is refunded. Or if old regime gives higher tax, pay the difference as self-assessment tax. Salaried individuals can switch between regimes at ITR filing time.
