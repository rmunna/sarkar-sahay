# State PSC Exam Guide — State Civil Services Overview

While UPSC conducts the Civil Services Exam for central services (IAS, IPS), each state in India has its own Public Service Commission (PSC) that recruits officers for state-level administrative, police, and allied services. State PSC exams are an excellent pathway to prestigious state government positions.

This guide covers the major state PSCs, their exam patterns, and how to prepare for them.

## What are State PSCs?

State Public Service Commissions are constitutional bodies established under Article 315 of the Indian Constitution. They conduct exams to recruit candidates for:

- **State Administrative Service** (equivalent to IAS at state level)
- **State Police Service** (equivalent to IPS at state level)
- **State Finance Service, Education Service, Forest Service**
- Various other Group A and Group B state government posts

## General Eligibility

Most state PSC exams have similar eligibility criteria:

- **Education:** Bachelor's degree from a recognised university
- **Age:** 21-40 years (varies by state; some states have higher limits)
- **Nationality:** Indian citizen
- **Domicile:** Some states require domicile of that state; others are open to all
- **Attempts:** Varies by state and category

## Common Exam Pattern

Most state PSCs follow a three-stage process similar to UPSC:

### Stage 1: Preliminary Exam (Objective)

- **Paper 1:** General Studies (100-150 questions)
- **Paper 2:** CSAT/General Aptitude (qualifying in many states)
- Negative marking applies in most states

### Stage 2: Mains Exam (Descriptive)

The Mains pattern varies significantly by state:

**UPPSC Mains:** 8 papers including General Hindi, Essay, and 4 GS papers + Optional
**BPSC Mains:** General Hindi + Essay + GS Paper 1 + GS Paper 2 + Optional
**MPPSC Mains:** 6 papers — GS 1-4 + Hindi Essay + Hindi Language
**RPSC (RAS) Mains:** 4 papers — GS 1-3 + General Hindi & English

**Key difference from UPSC:** Many state PSCs do not have an optional subject paper. UPPSC and BPSC are exceptions that still require optionals.

### Stage 3: Interview/Personality Test

- Usually 100-200 marks
- Tests personality, state-specific knowledge, and communication
- Some states (like TNPSC) have reduced or no interview weightage

## State-Specific Syllabus Focus

While the general syllabus overlaps with UPSC, state PSCs give more weightage to:

1. **State history and culture** — Freedom movement in the state, art, architecture, literature
2. **State geography** — Rivers, forests, minerals, climate, agriculture
3. **State economy** — Major industries, economic indicators, government schemes
4. **State polity** — Governor, state legislature, Panchayati Raj, local governance
5. **State current affairs** — Government policies, schemes, and events

## Preparation Strategy

### Step 1: Build UPSC-Level Foundation

Many topics overlap with UPSC. Start with:
- NCERTs for history, geography, polity, economy
- Standard reference books (Laxmikanth, Ramesh Singh)
- This foundation helps for both UPSC and state PSC

### Step 2: Add State-Specific Content

- Study your state's history, geography, and economy in detail
- Get a state-specific GK book (many publishers offer state-wise books)
- Follow state government schemes and policies
- Read state budget highlights

### Step 3: Previous Year Papers

- Solve previous 10-15 years' papers of your state PSC
- Analyse the pattern — which topics get more questions?
- State PSCs tend to repeat themes and question styles

### Step 4: Answer Writing (for Mains)

- Practice descriptive answers regularly
- Some states emphasise Hindi/regional language — prepare accordingly
- Join a state PSC-specific test series

### Step 5: Newspapers and Current Affairs

- Read one national newspaper (The Hindu/Indian Express)
- Read one state newspaper in the local language
- Follow Yojana and Kurukshetra for government scheme coverage

## State PSC vs UPSC — Key Differences

| Factor | UPSC CSE | State PSC |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Posts | IAS, IPS, IFS (central) | SDM, DSP, BDO (state) |
| Age limit | 21-32 (General) | 21-40 (most states) |
| Attempts | 6 (General) | More attempts in most states |
| Competition | ~10 lakh applicants | 2-5 lakh (varies by state) |
| Syllabus focus | National/International | State + National |
| Language | English/Hindi | Often state language |
| Optional | Yes (in Mains) | Many states removed optional |
| Salary | Same pay scale | Same pay scale (state cadre) |

## How to Apply for State PSC Exams

1. Visit your state PSC's official website
2. Register when the notification is published
3. Fill the application form with personal and educational details
4. Upload photo and signature as per guidelines
5. Pay the application fee (varies: ₹40-₹200 typically)
6. Submit and download confirmation

For general application tips, read our [guide on applying for government jobs online](/guide/how-to-apply-government-jobs-online).

## Career Growth in State Services

State civil services offer excellent growth:

- **Entry level:** SDM, BDO, Tehsildar, Naib Tehsildar, DSP
- **Mid-career:** ADM, SP, Joint Director
- **Senior level:** DM (District Magistrate), SSP, Secretary
- **Deputation:** State officers can go on deputation to central government

With promotions, state service officers can reach IAS-equivalent positions at the senior level.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I appear for multiple state PSC exams?

Yes, you can appear for as many state PSC exams as you are eligible for. There is no restriction. Many candidates prepare for UPSC and 2-3 state PSCs simultaneously.

### Is domicile certificate mandatory for state PSC?

It depends on the state. Some states (like BPSC, UPPSC) require domicile for certain posts. Others are open to candidates from any state. Check the specific notification for details.

### Can I prepare for UPSC and state PSC together?

Yes, this is a common strategy. About 70-80% of the syllabus overlaps. Add state-specific content on top of your UPSC preparation. Many UPSC aspirants also clear state PSCs.

### Which state PSC is the easiest to crack?

Difficulty varies by year and number of vacancies. States with more vacancies relative to applicants (like BPSC, CGPSC, JPSC) may have relatively lower cut-offs. However, no state PSC is "easy" — all require serious preparation.

### What is the salary of a state civil service officer?

Starting salary for state PCS officers is approximately ₹56,100 (Pay Level 10) for SDM-level posts and ₹35,400-₹44,900 for lower posts. This is the same 7th Pay Commission structure used centrally.

### How do I prepare for the state-specific portion?

Get a dedicated state GK book, read the state government's annual budget and economic survey, follow local newspapers, and study the state's history, geography, and cultural heritage in detail.
