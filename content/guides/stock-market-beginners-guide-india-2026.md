# Stock Market Beginners Guide India 2026 — Everything to Get Started

Buying your first stock in India takes 3 steps: open a demat account, fund it, and place a buy order. The entire process is digital and takes 15–30 minutes. Here's everything you need to know.

---

## Step 1: Open a Demat Account (15 minutes)

You need three accounts to invest in stocks:
- **Demat account**: Holds your shares electronically (like a digital locker)
- **Trading account**: Used to place buy/sell orders
- **Bank account**: Source of funds (already have this)

Most brokers bundle demat + trading account together.

**Top brokers for beginners in India**:

| Broker | Account Opening | Brokerage on Delivery | Annual Maintenance |
|--------|----------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **Zerodha** | Free | Zero | ₹300/year (waived year 1) |
| **Groww** | Free | Zero | ₹0 |
| **Angel One** | Free | Zero | ₹0 |
| **HDFC Securities** | ₹999 | 0.25–0.50% | ₹750/year |
| **ICICI Direct** | ₹975 | 0.25–0.50% | ₹700/year |

**For beginners: Groww or Zerodha** — both have zero brokerage on delivery trades, intuitive apps, and are SEBI-regulated.

**How to open**:
1. Download Groww or Zerodha app
2. Enter mobile number + OTP
3. PAN card verification (enter PAN number; fetches name/DOB automatically)
4. Aadhaar e-KYC: Link Aadhaar mobile OTP
5. Bank account linking (IFSC + account number)
6. Upload photo of your signature
7. Done — account usually activated within 24–48 hours

---

## Step 2: Transfer Funds to Trading Account

After demat activation:
1. Go to broker app → Add Funds / Deposit
2. Transfer via UPI, NEFT, or IMPS from your bank account
3. Funds appear in your trading account within minutes (UPI) or 1–4 hours (NEFT)

**Minimum**: ₹500 (though you can buy shares with as little as ₹100)

---

## Step 3: Buy Your First Share

1. Open broker app → Search for company name or NSE/BSE ticker
2. Select the stock (e.g., search "Infosys" → shows INFY as NSE symbol)
3. Tap "Buy"
4. Order type: Select **Market** (buy at current price) or **Limit** (set your target price)
5. Quantity: How many shares
6. Exchange: NSE or BSE (NSE is default and fine for most stocks)
7. Product type: **Delivery** (buy and hold for days/months) or **Intraday** (buy and sell same day)
8. Confirm order

**Your shares appear in Demat** (next working day — T+1 settlement for equities).

## NSE vs BSE — Which Exchange to Use?

| NSE (National Stock Exchange) | BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange) |
|------------------------------|----------------------------|
| Founded 1992 | Founded 1875 (Asia's oldest) |
| 2,000+ listed companies | 5,000+ listed companies |
| Higher volume/liquidity | Lower volume for most stocks |
| Nifty 50 index | Sensex (30 stocks) |
| NSE ticker: RELIANCE, INFY | BSE ticker: 500325, 500209 |

**For beginners**: Always use NSE. All major companies (Nifty 500) have better liquidity on NSE. NSE uses alphabetic tickers (RELIANCE, TCS, INFY); BSE uses 6-digit numeric codes.

**Both are equally regulated by SEBI**. If you buy on NSE and sell on BSE, it's the same demat account — but you need to be consistent per trade (buy NSE, sell NSE). Don't mix exchanges within the same trade.

## Market Timings India

| Session | Time (IST) |
|---------|-----------|
| Pre-open session | 9:00 AM – 9:15 AM |
| Regular trading | **9:15 AM – 3:30 PM** |
| Post-close session | 3:40 PM – 4:00 PM |
| After-market orders (AMO) | 3:45 PM – 8:57 AM (next day) |

**Markets are CLOSED on**: Saturdays, Sundays, and national holidays (NSE publishes holiday calendar annually).

**Pre-open session** (9:00–9:15): Only market and limit orders placed. Price discovery happens. Opening price set. You can place orders — they execute at the opening price.

## Key Stock Market Terms for Beginners

### Market Cap
Total value of all the company's shares.
- **Market Cap = Share Price × Total Shares Outstanding**
- Large-cap (>₹20,000 crore): TCS, Infosys, Reliance — stable
- Mid-cap (₹5,000–20,000 crore): More growth potential, moderate risk
- Small-cap (<₹5,000 crore): High risk, high potential

### P/E Ratio (Price to Earnings)
How much you're paying for ₹1 of company earnings.
- **P/E = Share Price / Earnings Per Share (EPS)**
- Nifty 50 average P/E: ~22–24
- P/E 30+ = expensive (growth expected to justify it)
- P/E 10–15 = possibly undervalued (or declining business)
- **Context matters**: Compare P/E to sector average and historical P/E, not just absolute number

### Earnings Per Share (EPS)
- **EPS = Net Profit / Total Shares**
- Higher EPS over time = business growing

### Dividend Yield
- Annual dividend paid as % of share price
- 3% dividend yield = ₹3 dividend for every ₹100 share price

### Book Value
- What the company is worth on its books (assets minus liabilities per share)
- P/B (Price to Book) = share price / book value per share
- P/B < 1 = trading below book value (rare for good companies)

## Delivery vs Intraday — The Critical Difference

| | Delivery | Intraday |
|--|----------|---------|
| **Holding period** | Days to years | Buy and sell on same day |
| **Tax** | LTCG (>1 year: 12.5%) or STCG (20%) | Speculative income (slab rate) |
| **Brokerage** | Zero (on most discount brokers) | ₹20/order flat typically |
| **Leverage** | None (invest own money) | Available (borrowing from broker) |
| **Risk** | Business risk only | Much higher — positions must close by 3:20 PM |
| **For beginners** | YES | NO — avoid until experienced |

**Beginners should only do delivery trading** until they understand markets deeply. Intraday has no overnight buffer; a stock stuck below purchase price MUST be sold at a loss if it doesn't recover same day.

## Your First Stock — What to Buy

**For absolute beginners: Start with Nifty 50 index fund, not individual stocks.**

Why: Individual stocks require research. An index fund (like Nippon Nifty 50 BeES or Motilal Nifty 50 ETF) gives exposure to India's 50 largest companies in one purchase. Zero company-specific risk.

**If you want individual stocks**:
- Stick to large-cap, profitable companies in sectors you understand
- Look for companies with consistent revenue + profit growth for 3+ years
- Avoid penny stocks (below ₹10), operators, or companies with massive debt
- Start with 1–2 companies, not 20

**Never invest in a stock because:**
- A friend/YouTube/Telegram group recommended it
- Price is going up fast and you want to catch the wave
- It's cheap (₹5 stock is not "affordable" — price is irrelevant without earnings)

## How Much to Start With

**Minimum**: You can buy 1 share of most large companies.
- TCS: ~₹3,900 per share
- Infosys: ~₹1,800 per share
- HDFC Bank: ~₹1,900 per share
- SBI: ~₹830 per share

**Realistic starter**: ₹5,000–₹10,000 to buy 2–3 shares and understand the process.

**Philosophy**: Don't start with large amounts until you understand market behavior through experience. Start small to learn — not to make large returns.

## Taxes on Stock Gains

| Type | Tax Rate | When |
|------|---------|------|
| LTCG (>12 months) | 12.5% above ₹1.25L annual exemption | Delivery held >12 months |
| STCG (≤12 months) | 20% | Delivery sold within 12 months |
| Speculative (intraday) | Slab rate | Intraday profit |
| Dividends | Slab rate | Anytime dividend received |

TDS is deducted on dividends (10% if >₹5,000/year from a company). Capital gains from equity are NOT TDS-deducted — you self-calculate and pay in ITR.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much money do I need to start investing in stocks?

No minimum. You can buy 1 share of any company. Practically, start with ₹5,000–₹10,000 to get meaningful exposure. Avoid spreading too thin across many stocks initially.

### Can I lose all my money in stocks?

In theory, a company can go bankrupt → shares worthless. In practice, large Nifty 50 companies rarely go to zero. Diversifying across 10+ companies significantly reduces this risk. Index funds spread across 50 companies have near-zero bankruptcy risk.

### NSE or BSE — which is better for delivery trading?

NSE for most stocks. Better liquidity = tighter bid-ask spread = slightly better execution price. For penny stocks or BSE SME-listed companies, BSE may be the only option.

### How do I know when to sell a stock?

Your original thesis for buying matters. If you bought because of strong earnings growth and earnings continue to grow — hold. If the reason you bought is no longer true (management change, industry disruption, regulatory issues) — reconsider. Never sell just because price went down (short-term volatility is normal).

### Is it safe to link my bank account to Zerodha/Groww?

Yes — both are SEBI-registered brokers. Your bank account link is read-only for deposit confirmations; they cannot debit without your explicit action. Shares are held in your own demat account, not the broker's.
