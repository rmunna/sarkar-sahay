# Land Conversion – Agricultural to Non-Agricultural (NA) Land in India

Agricultural land conversion (also called land use change or NA conversion) is the legal process of converting agricultural land to non-agricultural purposes — residential, commercial, or industrial use. This is mandatory before constructing any non-agricultural structure on farmland. The process varies by state but involves revenue and planning authorities.

---

## What is Land Conversion?

Land conversion is the official change of land classification from "agricultural" to "non-agricultural" (NA) in government revenue records. Key points:

- **Why needed:** Agricultural land cannot be legally used for building houses, shops, factories, etc.
- **Who grants it:** State government revenue department (Collector/DC/Tehsildar)
- **Legal basis:** State-specific land revenue acts and land reform laws
- **Also called:** NA order, land use change, Section 44/45 (varies by state)
- **Without conversion:** Any construction is illegal and can be demolished; property cannot be financed by banks

---

## Who Can Apply?

- The **registered owner** of the agricultural land
- **Power of Attorney holder** authorized by the owner
- **Legal heirs** (with succession/heir certificate)
- **Developer/builder** (with landowner's consent and valid agreement)

### Restrictions
- Ceiling limit compliance (land ceiling acts)
- Some states restrict non-agriculturalists from buying agricultural land
- Irrigated/fertile land may face stricter conversion rules
- Land in protected zones (forests, wetlands, green belts) cannot be converted

---

## Documents Required

- **Application form** (prescribed by state revenue department)
- **7/12 extract / RTC / Pattadar passbook** (land ownership document)
- **Mutation entry** reflecting current ownership
- **Encumbrance certificate** (10–15 years)
- **Land survey map / village map** showing the plot
- **Zone certificate** from town planning authority
- **NOC from Gram Panchayat** or local body
- **NOC from Irrigation Department** (if land is irrigated)
- **Environmental NOC** (for industrial conversion or large plots)
- **Identity proof:** Aadhaar, PAN of applicant
- **Passport-size photographs**
- **Proposed building plan / site plan** (for residential/commercial)
- **Tax paid receipts** (land revenue/cess)

See our [encumbrance certificate guide](/guide/encumbrance-certificate-ec) for obtaining an EC.

## State-wise Process

### Maharashtra (Section 44 — MLRC)

1. Apply to the **District Collector / Tahsildar** with all documents
2. Pay conversion premium (known as "NA tax")
3. Town Planning Department verifies zoning compatibility
4. Collector's office inspects the land
5. If approved, NA order issued
6. Revenue records updated from agricultural to NA
7. Timeline: 60–120 days

**Online:** Apply through the Maharashtra revenue portal or SatBara portal

### Karnataka (Section 95 — KLR Act)

1. Apply to the **Deputy Commissioner (DC)** of the district
2. DC forwards to Tahsildar for inquiry
3. Town planning report obtained
4. DC issues conversion order
5. RTC (Pahani) updated to reflect NA status
6. Timeline: 60–90 days

**Online:** Apply through [bhoomi.karnataka.gov.in](https://bhoomi.karnataka.gov.in/)

### Uttar Pradesh

1. Apply to **District Magistrate (DM)** through Tehsildar
2. Tehsildar conducts local inquiry
3. Town planning verification
4. DM approves and issues order
5. Khatauni updated
6. Timeline: 60–180 days

### Andhra Pradesh / Telangana

1. Apply to **Joint Collector** or **RDO (Revenue Divisional Officer)**
2. MRO (Mandal Revenue Officer) conducts inquiry
3. Planning authority issues no-objection
4. Conversion order issued
5. Pattadar passbook updated

### Tamil Nadu

1. Apply to the **District Collector**
2. Revenue Divisional Officer inspects
3. Town planning verification
4. Collector approves — Patta updated from wet/dry to house-site/commercial

## Fees & Premium

Conversion fees vary significantly by state, location, and purpose:

| State | Fee Structure |
|-------|-------------|
| **Maharashtra** | Premium based on market value: 2.5% (residential) to 10% (commercial) of ready reckoner value |
| **Karnataka** | ₹200–₹400 per hectare (government fee) + development charges |
| **UP** | Based on circle rate and purpose |
| **Gujarat** | Premium: 10% to 20% of jantri value |
| **Rajasthan** | Conversion charges per sq mt based on zone |
| **Tamil Nadu** | Based on guideline value |

> **Note:** In addition to conversion fees, you may need to pay betterment charges, development fees, and stamp duty on the NA order. Total cost can be significant for large plots.

## What Happens After Conversion?

1. **Revenue records updated** — 7/12 extract, RTC, or Khatauni reflects NA status
2. **Property tax changes** — land is now assessed at non-agricultural rates (higher)
3. **Building permission** — you can now apply for building plan approval from the local planning authority
4. **Bank loans** — banks will accept the property for mortgage/construction loans
5. **Higher market value** — NA land is significantly more valuable than agricultural land

---

## Common Reasons for Rejection

- Land falls in a restricted zone (green belt, forest, flood zone)
- Outstanding land revenue dues
- Land ceiling violations
- Missing NOC from irrigation/forest department
- Proposed use doesn't match the zoning/master plan
- Encumbrances or litigation on the land
- Tribal/schedule area restrictions

---

## Important Tips

1. **Check zoning first** — verify with the town planning authority that your land falls in a zone where conversion is permitted
2. **Clear all dues** — land revenue, cess, and taxes must be fully paid
3. **Get all NOCs in advance** — irrigation, gram panchayat, environment (if applicable)
4. **Apply through a lawyer** for large or commercial conversions — the process has many legal nuances
5. **Don't start construction** before receiving the official NA order — it's illegal and the structure can be demolished

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I build a house on agricultural land without conversion?
No. Constructing any non-agricultural structure on agricultural land is illegal. You must first obtain the NA conversion order. Some states allow a small farmhouse for agricultural use, but rules are strict.

### How much does land conversion cost?
Costs vary widely by state and purpose. It can range from a few thousand rupees (Karnataka government fee) to lakhs (Maharashtra premium based on market value). Factor in stamp duty, development charges, and professional fees.

### Can I convert any agricultural land?
Not all land can be converted. Restrictions apply to irrigated land, land near reservoirs, forest land, land in green/no-development zones, and land exceeding ceiling limits. Check with the local revenue office.

### Is land conversion permanent?
Yes. Once the NA order is issued and records updated, the land permanently retains its non-agricultural classification. It cannot be reverted to agricultural status (in most states).

### Can NRIs apply for land conversion?
NRIs who legally own agricultural land in India can apply for conversion. However, note that many states prohibit NRIs from purchasing agricultural land. If the NRI already owns it (inherited or purchased before going abroad), conversion is possible through a Power of Attorney holder.

### What is the difference between NA conversion and layout approval?
NA conversion changes the land classification from agricultural to non-agricultural. Layout approval is a separate process where the planning authority approves the subdivision of land into plots with roads, drainage, etc. Both are required for developing a residential layout.
