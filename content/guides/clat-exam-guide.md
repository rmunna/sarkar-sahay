## What is the CLAT Exam?

The Common Law Admission Test (CLAT) is a centralized national-level entrance exam for admission to undergraduate (UG) and postgraduate (PG) law programs offered by 24 National Law Universities (NLUs) across India. CLAT is conducted by the Consortium of NLUs on a rotational basis.

CLAT-UG is for 5-year integrated LLB programs (BA LLB, BBA LLB, BSc LLB), while CLAT-PG is for 1-year LLM programs. Over 70,000 students appear for CLAT-UG every year.

## Who is Eligible?

### CLAT-UG (5-Year LLB)
- Passed or appearing in Class 12 from any recognized board
- **No minimum percentage** requirement for appearing (individual NLUs may have their own cut-offs)
- **No upper age limit** (removed from the eligibility criteria)

### CLAT-PG (LLM)
- LLB / 5-year integrated law degree from a recognized university
- **No minimum percentage** for appearing in the exam

## CLAT-UG Exam Pattern

| Section | Questions | Marks |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| English Language | 22-26 | 22-26 |
| Current Affairs & General Knowledge | 28-32 | 28-32 |
| Legal Reasoning | 28-32 | 28-32 |
| Logical Reasoning | 22-26 | 22-26 |
| Quantitative Techniques | 10-14 | 10-14 |
| **Total** | **120** | **120** |

- **Duration:** 2 hours (120 minutes)
- **Marking:** +1 for correct, -0.25 for wrong
- **Mode:** Offline (pen-and-paper based)

## CLAT Syllabus

### English Language
- Reading comprehension passages
- Grammar and vocabulary in context
- Sentence correction and rearrangement
- Inference and critical analysis of text

### Current Affairs & General Knowledge
- Events from the last 12 months
- National and international affairs
- Arts, culture, sports, and science
- Questions are passage-based (not fact-recall)

### Legal Reasoning
- Legal principles and rules applied to fact situations
- No prior legal knowledge required
- Constitutional law concepts and landmark judgments
- Rights, duties, and legal maxims in context

### Logical Reasoning
- Analogies, series, and classification
- Syllogisms and logical sequences
- Strengthening and weakening arguments
- Assumptions and conclusions from passages

### Quantitative Techniques
- Basic arithmetic and algebra
- Percentages, ratios, and averages
- Data interpretation (tables, graphs)
- Class 10 level mathematics

## Preparation Strategy

### 6-Month Plan
- **Months 1-2:** Build fundamentals — grammar, math basics, start reading newspapers daily
- **Months 3-4:** Start subject-wise practice, focus on legal reasoning and current affairs
- **Months 5-6:** Full-length mock tests (2-3 per week), revision, and error analysis

### Recommended Resources
- **Current Affairs:** The Hindu, Indian Express, monthly magazines
- **Legal Reasoning:** AP Bhardwaj's CLAT books, previous year papers
- **English:** Word Power Made Easy, reading editorials daily
- **Logical Reasoning:** RS Aggarwal, practice sets

## Important Tips

- **Read newspapers daily** — current affairs is the highest-weighted section
- **Focus on comprehension** — CLAT is passage-based; practice reading and analyzing passages
- **Legal reasoning needs practice, not memorization** — understand how to apply legal principles to facts
- **Don't ignore quant** — it's the smallest section but easy to score if you know basics
- **Time management** — practice completing 120 questions in 120 minutes (1 minute per question)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is CLAT only for NLU admission?
CLAT scores are primarily used by 24 NLUs, but many private law colleges also accept CLAT scores for admission.

### Can I appear for CLAT after graduation?
CLAT-UG is for candidates who have completed or are appearing in Class 12. If you already have a graduation, you can still apply for the 5-year program, or take CLAT-PG after completing LLB.

### Is there negative marking in CLAT?
Yes, there is a deduction of 0.25 marks for each wrong answer. Avoid random guessing but don't leave questions if you can eliminate even one option.

### How is CLAT different from AILET?
AILET is conducted separately by NLU Delhi for its own admission. CLAT covers 24 other NLUs. Both exams have different patterns and difficulty levels.

### Can I prepare for CLAT without coaching?
Yes, self-study is effective for CLAT. The exam tests comprehension and reasoning, not rote knowledge. Consistent newspaper reading and regular mock tests are the keys.

### What career options are available after NLU?
NLU graduates work as advocates, corporate lawyers, legal consultants, judges (after qualifying exams), and in policy research. Top NLU graduates earn ₹15-30 lakh per annum from campus placements.
