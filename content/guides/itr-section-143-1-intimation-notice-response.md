# Income Tax Intimation Under Section 143(1) — Guide for ITR Filers

If you filed your Income Tax Return and received an email from **CPC Bangalore (Centralised Processing Centre)** with subject line **"Intimation under Section 143(1)"**, this is what you need to know.

> **Quick answer**: Section 143(1) is a **normal, automatic processing intimation** — NOT a scrutiny notice. Most people can ignore it if it says "No Demand No Refund." Action needed only if it shows a demand.

## What Is Section 143(1) Intimation?

After you file your ITR, the Income Tax Department's computer system (CPC, Bengaluru) automatically processes your return. It checks:
- Arithmetic correctness (did your calculations add up?)
- Tax paid vs. tax due
- TDS credits (Form 26AS match)
- Basic adjustments (disallowed losses, incorrect deductions claimed)

The **Section 143(1) Intimation** is a message telling you the outcome of this automatic check. It's NOT:
- A scrutiny assessment
- A reason to worry
- An allegation of fraud

## How to Read the 143(1) Intimation

The intimation comes as a **PDF password-protected** email. Password: your PAN in lowercase + date of birth in DDMMYYYY format.

**Example**: PAN = ABCDE1234F, DOB = 15 July 1985 → Password = abcde1234f15071985

### Three Possible Outcomes

| Outcome | What It Means | Action Needed |
|---------|--------------|---------------|
| **"No Demand No Refund"** | Everything matched perfectly | ✅ None — you're done |
| **"Refund Payable of ₹X"** | You paid excess tax; IT will refund | ✅ Verify bank account is correct at e-filing portal |
| **"Demand Raised of ₹X"** | CPC found a discrepancy; you owe more tax | ⚠️ Action required — see below |

## When a "Demand" Is Raised: What to Do

A demand means the computer found a difference between what you claimed and what it computed. Common reasons:

| Reason | Explanation |
|--------|------------|
| **TDS mismatch** | Your Form 16 / TDS certificate shows different amount than Form 26AS |
| **Income mismatch** | You didn't include an income that shows in your AIS/Form 26AS |
| **Incorrect deduction claimed** | A deduction (like 80C) was adjusted due to missing proof |
| **Advance tax shortfall** | 234B/234C interest added automatically |
| **Late filing fee (234F)** | ₹1,000–₹5,000 for filing after due date |
| **Incorrect exemption claimed** | HRA or LTA claimed without sufficient details |

### Step 1: Read the Comparison Table

Open the 143(1) PDF and find the table comparing:
- Column A: "Income/Deductions as returned by you"
- Column B: "Income/Deductions as computed by CPC"
- Difference: The adjustment

This tells you exactly what was changed.

### Step 2: Decide — Pay or Dispute

**If you agree with the demand** (e.g., it's a late filing fee or you made an error):
1. Pay at incometax.gov.in → E-Pay Tax → Self-Assessment Tax (AY 2025-26)
2. Payment must be made within **30 days** of the intimation date
3. After payment, update your paid challan in your ITR (if required)

**If you disagree with the demand** (e.g., TDS credit missed, income already included):
1. File a **Rectification Request** at incometax.gov.in

### Step 3: How to File Rectification Against 143(1) Demand

1. Login to **[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/)**
2. Go to **"e-File"** → **"Income Tax Returns"** → **"e-Proceedings"** → **"Rectification"**
3. Select **Assessment Year** (the year for which you got the intimation)
4. Select **Reason**: "Data correction" or "Tax credit mismatch"
5. Provide the correct details and supporting documents
6. Submit

> Or go to: e-File → e-Proceedings → **"Pending Actions"** → Find your 143(1) intimation → "Submit Response"

## Common 143(1) Scenarios and What to Do

### Scenario 1: Demand for ₹5,000 — It's Late Filing Fee

Late filing fee under Section 234F:
- ₹1,000 if income ≤ ₹5 lakh
- ₹5,000 if income > ₹5 lakh
- Filed after July 31 but before December 31: ₹1,000–₹5,000

**Action**: If you filed late, pay this demand. It's valid and there's no way to avoid it.

### Scenario 2: TDS Credit Mismatch

CPC shows less TDS credit than you claimed. Example: Your employer deducted ₹80,000 TDS, you claimed it in ITR, but 26AS shows only ₹70,000 deposited.

**Action**: 
- Check Form 26AS for the actual TDS credit
- If employer hasn't deposited TDS on time, it's their liability, not yours legally — but the intimation may still show a demand
- File rectification requesting recognition of the TDS as per your Form 16 (attach Form 16 as document)
- If 26AS is correct and Form 16 is different, the employer made an error — contact employer's accounts team

### Scenario 3: Income Not Reported

AIS/26AS shows income you didn't report. Example: Bank FD interest not included in ITR.

**Action**:
- If the income is real and you missed it: Pay the tax on the missed income via self-assessment tax, then file a revised ITR (before December 31 of the assessment year)
- If the income isn't yours (e.g., wrong account/PAN): File a response on the AIS portal disputing the transaction

### Scenario 4: "No Demand, No Refund" but You Expected a Refund

This can happen if:
- Your TDS credit didn't match what you claimed
- A deduction was disallowed

Check the comparison table to see what was adjusted. If you believe the adjustment is wrong, file rectification.

## How to Pay the 143(1) Tax Demand

1. Login at [incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/) → **e-Pay Tax**
2. Select **Assessment Year**: FY 2025-26 = AY 2026-27
3. Tax type: **Self-Assessment Tax (300)**
4. Enter amount: The demand amount shown in the intimation
5. Pay via net banking, UPI, debit card

> Keep the **payment challan** — you'll need the BSR Code and Challan Serial Number for your records.

## Deadline to Respond to 143(1) Demand

**30 days from the date of intimation** to either pay or dispute.

If you miss the deadline, the demand becomes final and the IT department can:
- Adjust against future refunds
- Deduct from your bank account (if linked via CBDT)
- Issue a further notice

## 143(1) vs Other Income Tax Notices

| Notice Type | What It Means | Severity |
|-------------|--------------|---------|
| **143(1) Intimation** | Automatic processing result | Low — normal for all filers |
| **143(2) Notice** | Scrutiny — your ITR selected for review | High — detailed audit |
| **148 Notice** | Tax escaped assessment — IT reopening your file | High |
| **139(9) Defective Return** | Your ITR has missing/incorrect data | Medium — fix within 15 days |
| **245 Notice** | IT adjusting your refund against old dues | Medium |

Section **143(1)** is the only one that's "routine" — almost every filer gets it. The others are selective and need careful attention.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I got a 143(1) email but no demand. Do I need to do anything?

No. If the intimation says "No Demand No Refund" or "Refund of ₹X," no action is needed from your side. Check that your bank account is correct for refund credit.

### How long after ITR filing does 143(1) come?

Typically **3–6 months** after filing. During peak season (Aug-Dec), CPC processes millions of returns and processing takes time. Some filers receive it in 30 days; others wait 6+ months.

### The demand in my 143(1) is ₹100. Should I pay?

Technically yes, but demands below ₹100 are often not actioned by the IT department. If you disagree, file a rectification. If you agree, ₹100 is worth paying just to close it.

### I paid the demand but the income tax portal still shows it as outstanding. What to do?

Upload the challan: incometax.gov.in → e-File → Update Challan Details. Provide BSR Code, challan serial number, date of payment. The demand status should update within 7–10 working days.
