# Income Tax Appeal to CIT(A) — File Form 35 Online Within 30 Days

If the Assessing Officer (AO) has raised a tax demand you believe is wrong — whether from a 143(3) scrutiny assessment, 144 best judgment assessment, or 147 reassessment — you can appeal to the **Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)** [CIT(A)]. This is the first level of appeal in the income tax system.

> **Deadline**: File Form 35 online within **30 days** of the assessment order (or 143(1) demand order for automatic adjustments). Missing this deadline requires a condonation request, which may or may not be granted.

## When to File a CIT(A) Appeal

| Situation | Appeal? |
|-----------|---------|
| AO disallowed an expense you claimed | ✅ Yes |
| AO added income not disclosed (you disagree) | ✅ Yes |
| Penalty imposed under Section 271 | ✅ Yes |
| 143(1) intimation demand (auto processing error) | ❌ File Rectification instead |
| 143(2) ongoing scrutiny (not yet ordered) | ❌ Wait for assessment order |

**For 143(1) demands** (routine auto-processing): First try rectification. If AO rejects rectification, then appeal.

## Budget 2023 Change — 20% Pre-Deposit Mandatory

From October 1, 2023, filing a CIT(A) appeal requires **20% of disputed demand** as pre-deposit (mandatory payment before appeal is heard).

**Example**: AO assessed ₹5,00,000 as additional tax demand. To appeal:
- Pay **20% = ₹1,00,000** immediately
- This is held in abeyance during appeal
- If appeal succeeds → ₹1,00,000 refunded (with interest)
- If appeal fails → adjusted against the remaining demand

**Before October 2023**: Only a "reasonable" amount needed; often just 20% was "suggested." Now it's statutory.

**Financial hardship exception**: If paying 20% causes hardship, apply to the AO for stay of demand (remaining 80%). AO grants stay of balance demand once pre-deposit is paid.

## Step-by-Step: File Form 35 Online

### Step 1: Login to Income Tax Portal

1. Go to **[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/)**
2. Login with PAN and password

### Step 2: Navigate to Appeal Filing

1. Go to **"e-File"** → **"Income Tax Forms"** → **"File Income Tax Forms"**
2. Search for **Form 35** or navigate to Appeals section
3. Select Assessment Year (the year for which AO passed the order)

### Step 3: Fill Form 35

Key sections:
1. **Assessment Year** and **PAN**
2. **Section under which appeal is made**: Section 246A (for assessments under 143(3)/144/147)
3. **Date of demand notice**: Date on Section 156 notice
4. **Amount of assessed income**: As per AO's order
5. **Amount of relief sought**: Your disputed amount
6. **Grounds of appeal**: This is the most critical part — state exactly why the AO's addition/disallowance is wrong

### Step 4: State Your Grounds of Appeal

Grounds of appeal should be specific and legally sound. Good examples:

- "The AO disallowed ₹X as unexplained expenditure. The expenditure was incurred for business purpose and supported by bills/vouchers as provided during assessment. Disallowance is arbitrary."
- "The AO added ₹X as rental income. The amount represents a loan repayment from a relative and is not income. Loan agreement and bank transfer evidence was provided."
- "The AO applied Section 271(1)(c) penalty. The assessee's omission was due to a bona fide error and not concealment. The income has now been voluntarily offered."

Vague grounds like "the AO erred" are technically acceptable but weaker.

### Step 5: Attach Documents

Upload:
- Copy of the Assessment Order (or 143(3) order)
- Section 156 demand notice
- Copy of your original ITR
- Documents supporting your appeal grounds (bills, bank statements, loan agreements, etc.)

### Step 6: Pay Pre-Deposit

Before submitting Form 35:
1. Pay 20% of the disputed demand via E-Pay Tax (Self-Assessment Tax, Challan 280)
2. Upload the payment challan receipt with the appeal

### Step 7: Submit and Get Acknowledgement

Submit Form 35 → You receive an **Appeal Registration Number**. Keep this for tracking.

## How Long Does CIT(A) Appeal Take?

| Stage | Timeline |
|-------|---------|
| Filing | Immediate |
| CIT(A) hearing scheduling | 6–18 months typically |
| Decision | After hearing + 30-90 days for order |
| Total resolution | 12–24 months (can vary widely) |

**Faceless Appeal Scheme**: Most CIT(A) proceedings are now faceless — conducted entirely online through the portal. You submit written submissions and documents; no in-person appearance required in most cases.

## What to Expect During the Appeal

1. **Notice for hearing**: CIT(A) sends a notice with date/time for hearing (virtual/in-person)
2. **Your submission**: Submit written arguments + supporting documents
3. **Departmental representative**: AO/department advocate presents department's case
4. **Order**: CIT(A) may confirm, reduce, or delete the AO's addition

**Possible outcomes**:
- **Appeal allowed in full**: AO's demand deleted, you get full pre-deposit refund
- **Partly allowed**: Some addition confirmed, some deleted
- **Dismissed**: AO's order confirmed; appeal failed

## What If CIT(A) Rejects Your Appeal?

You can appeal further to **ITAT (Income Tax Appellate Tribunal)** — a higher quasi-judicial body. ITAT has no 20% pre-deposit requirement, but court/legal costs are higher. If still dissatisfied, appeals can go to High Court and Supreme Court on questions of law.

## Tips for a Strong Appeal

1. **File on time**: 30-day deadline is strict. If you miss it, immediately file a condonation request with reasons.
2. **Pay the 20% first**: Appeal is not valid without pre-deposit.
3. **Specific grounds**: Each ground of appeal should identify the specific addition/disallowance and the legal/factual error.
4. **Attach comprehensive documents**: CIT(A) decides based on documents — more is better.
5. **Engage a CA or tax advocate**: For demands above ₹5 lakh, professional representation significantly improves outcomes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I file an appeal if I haven't paid the assessment demand?

You must pay the **20% pre-deposit** to file an appeal. You don't need to pay 100% — only 20%. After paying 20%, apply to the AO for stay of the remaining 80% demand.

### The 30-day deadline passed. Can I still appeal?

File a **Condonation of Delay** application along with Form 35, explaining why you missed the deadline (illness, not receiving notice, CA's error, etc.). CIT(A) has discretion to condone delays. There's no hard outer limit, but longer delays need stronger reasons.

### My appeal succeeded but CIT(A) took 2 years. Do I get interest on the refund?

Yes — the pre-deposit amount (and any additional tax paid) earns **6% p.a. interest** under Section 244A from the date of payment until the refund date.

### CIT(A) partly allowed my appeal but I think more should be allowed. What next?

File further appeal to ITAT (Income Tax Appellate Tribunal) within **60 days** of the CIT(A) order. The process is similar but at a higher forum.
