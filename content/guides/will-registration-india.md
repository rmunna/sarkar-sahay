## What Is a Will?

A **will** (also called a **testament**) is a legal document in which a person (the **testator**) declares how their property and assets should be distributed after their death. In India, wills are governed primarily by the **Indian Succession Act, 1925**.

A will allows you to:
- **Decide who inherits** your property, money, and belongings
- **Appoint a guardian** for minor children
- **Name an executor** to carry out your wishes
- **Prevent family disputes** over inheritance
- **Donate to charity** or distribute assets unequally if desired

> **Important:** Any person of **sound mind** who is **18 years or older** can make a will. The will takes effect only after the testator's death.

## Types of Wills in India

### 1. Unprivileged Will (General Will)

This is the most common type. Under **Section 63** of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, it must:

- Be **signed** by the testator (or by another person in the testator's presence and direction)
- Be **attested by at least two witnesses**, each of whom must sign in the presence of the testator

There are no restrictions on format — a will can be handwritten, typed, or printed.

### 2. Privileged Will

Under **Sections 65–67** of the Indian Succession Act, a privileged will can be made by:

- Members of the **armed forces** (Army, Navy, Air Force) engaged in active service
- **Mariners** at sea

A privileged will has relaxed requirements — it need not be signed or attested by witnesses. It can even be made **orally** in the presence of two witnesses.

### 3. Other Types

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Joint Will** | Made by two or more persons (e.g., husband and wife) jointly |
| **Mutual Will** | Two persons make wills in favour of each other |
| **Conditional Will** | Takes effect only when a specified condition is fulfilled |
| **Holographic Will** | Entirely handwritten and signed by the testator |

## Is Will Registration Compulsory in India?

**No.** Under Indian law, registration of a will is **not mandatory**. An unregistered will is legally valid as long as it meets the requirements of the Indian Succession Act.

However, **registration is strongly recommended** because:
- It serves as **strong evidence** of genuineness in court
- It **reduces chances of fraud**, tampering, or forgery
- It makes the **probate process smoother**
- A registered will is stored safely with the **Sub-Registrar's office**

## How to Register a Will — Step-by-Step Process

### Offline Registration (At Sub-Registrar's Office)

1. **Draft the will** — Prepare the will clearly stating your wishes, asset details, beneficiaries, and executor. You may consult a lawyer.

2. **Visit the Sub-Registrar's office** — Go to the office in the jurisdiction where the testator resides or where the property is located.

3. **Carry required documents** — Bring the original will, identity proof, and two witnesses.

4. **Pay the registration fee** — Fees vary by state (see below).

5. **Testator and witnesses sign** — The testator signs the will in the presence of the Sub-Registrar. Two witnesses also sign and provide their identity proof.

6. **Sub-Registrar verifies and registers** — The Sub-Registrar satisfies themselves about the testator's identity and sound mind, then registers the will under the **Registration Act, 1908 (Section 18)**.

7. **Receive certified copy** — The original is stored in the Sub-Registrar's records. You receive a certified copy.

### Online Registration (State-Specific Portals)

Several states offer **online appointment booking** or partial online registration for wills:

- **Uttar Pradesh:** [igrsup.gov.in](https://igrsup.gov.in)
- **Maharashtra:** [igregistration.maharashtra.gov.in](https://igregistration.maharashtra.gov.in)
- **Tamil Nadu:** [tnreginet.gov.in](https://tnreginet.gov.in)
- **Andhra Pradesh:** [registration.ap.gov.in](https://registration.ap.gov.in)
- **Rajasthan:** [igrs.rajasthan.gov.in](https://igrs.rajasthan.gov.in)

> **Note:** Even with online portals, **physical presence** of the testator and witnesses at the Sub-Registrar's office is typically required for final registration.

## Documents Required for Will Registration

| Document | Details |
|----------|---------|
| **Original will** | Printed or handwritten, with all pages signed |
| **Identity proof of testator** | Aadhaar card, PAN card, passport, or voter ID |
| **Address proof** | Aadhaar, utility bill, or passport |
| **Passport-size photographs** | Of the testator (usually 2) |
| **Identity proof of two witnesses** | Aadhaar, PAN, or voter ID |
| **Property documents** (if applicable) | Sale deed, title deed, or property tax receipts |
| **Medical certificate** (recommended for senior citizens) | Certifying the testator is of sound mind |

> **Tip:** Stamp duty is generally **not required** for will registration, as a will is not a transfer of property during the testator's lifetime. However, some states may charge a nominal stamp fee. Check with your local Sub-Registrar.

## What Is Probate?

**Probate** is the legal process by which a court certifies a will as valid after the testator's death. It is essentially the court's seal of approval on the will.

### When Is Probate Required?

- **Mandatory** in the jurisdictions of **Kolkata, Mumbai, and Chennai** (under the Indian Succession Act) for wills made by Hindus, Buddhists, Sikhs, and Jains
- **Required** when property disputes arise or institutions (banks, registrars) demand it
- **Optional** in other areas, but recommended for high-value estates

### Probate Process

1. File a **petition** in the District Court or High Court having jurisdiction
2. Submit the **original will**, death certificate of the testator, and list of legal heirs
3. Court issues **notices** to all legal heirs and interested parties
4. After hearing objections (if any), the court **grants probate**
5. The executor can then **distribute assets** as per the will

Probate typically takes **6 months to 2 years** depending on the complexity and whether the will is contested.

## What Is a Codicil?

A **codicil** is a supplementary document that **amends, modifies, or adds to** an existing will without replacing it entirely. Under **Section 2(b)** of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, a codicil is treated as part of the will.

### Key Points About Codicils

- Must be executed with the **same formalities** as a will (signed, attested by two witnesses)
- Can be **registered** just like a will
- Multiple codicils can be attached to one will
- If a codicil contradicts the will, the **codicil prevails** for those specific provisions

> **Tip:** For major changes, it is usually better to **revoke the old will and create a new one** rather than adding multiple codicils.

## Can a Will Be Revoked or Changed?

Yes. A testator can **revoke or amend** their will at any time during their lifetime. Methods of revocation include:

- **Making a new will** that expressly revokes all previous wills
- **Destroying the will** with the intention of revoking it (burning, tearing)
- **Adding a codicil** to modify specific clauses

A registered will can also be revoked — registration does not make it irrevocable.

## Important Tips for Making a Will

1. **Use clear language** — Avoid ambiguity. Name beneficiaries with full names and relationships.
2. **List all assets** — Include property, bank accounts, investments, jewellery, digital assets, and any debts.
3. **Appoint an executor** — Choose a trustworthy person to carry out the will.
4. **Include a residuary clause** — Cover any assets not specifically mentioned (e.g., "all remaining property to…").
5. **Get a medical certificate** — Especially for testators above 65 years, to pre-empt challenges on mental capacity.
6. **Update regularly** — Review your will after major life events (marriage, divorce, birth of children, property purchase).
7. **Keep it safe** — Store the original in a secure place and inform your executor of its location.
8. **Consult a lawyer** — While not mandatory, legal advice helps avoid errors that could invalidate the will.

## Will Registration vs. Legal Heir Certificate

If a person dies **without a will** (intestate), the family needs a [Legal Heir Certificate](/guides/legal-heir-certificate-apply) to claim the deceased's property and assets. A registered will simplifies inheritance significantly and can reduce the need for lengthy legal proceedings.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### 1. Can an unregistered will be challenged in court?

Yes. Both registered and unregistered wills can be challenged. However, a **registered will carries stronger evidentiary value** because the Sub-Registrar verifies the testator's identity and witnesses at the time of registration.

### 2. Can a will be made on plain paper?

Yes. A will can be written on **plain paper** (stamp paper is not required in most states). It can be handwritten, typed, or printed. What matters is that it is properly signed and attested by two witnesses.

### 3. Who cannot be a witness to a will?

While the law does not explicitly disqualify anyone from being a witness, **beneficiaries named in the will should not be witnesses**. Under Section 67 of the Indian Succession Act, a bequest to an attesting witness is void (the witness loses their gift but the will remains valid).

### 4. Can NRIs (Non-Resident Indians) register a will in India?

Yes. NRIs can make and register a will in India for their **Indian assets**. They can visit the Sub-Registrar's office in person or grant a **Power of Attorney** to a trusted person in India. The will should be governed by Indian law for Indian property.

### 5. Is a will valid for all religions in India?

The Indian Succession Act, 1925 applies to **Hindus, Buddhists, Sikhs, Jains, Christians, and Parsis**. **Muslims** are governed by their personal law (Islamic law of inheritance), which restricts testamentary disposition to only **one-third** of the estate. A Muslim can make a will (wasiyat) but cannot bequeath more than one-third without the consent of legal heirs.

### 6. How long is a registered will valid?

A will has **no expiry date**. It remains valid until it is **revoked by the testator** or superseded by a new will. The most recent valid will prevails.

### 7. Can two people jointly own a will?

Two people can make a **joint will** (e.g., spouses). However, after the death of one, the surviving testator **cannot revoke** the part that has already taken effect. Joint wills can create complications and are generally not recommended.
