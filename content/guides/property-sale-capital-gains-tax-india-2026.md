# Property Sale Capital Gains Tax India 2026 — Complete Guide

Selling a house? The tax implications are significant — potentially lakhs in capital gains tax. But the exemption under Section 54 can bring it to zero if you reinvest in another property. Budget 2024 changed indexation rules. Here's everything you need to know.

---

## Short-Term vs Long-Term Capital Gain on Property

| Holding Period | Capital Gain Type | Tax Rate |
|----------------|------------------|---------|
| ≤ 24 months | Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG) | Slab rate (5/10/15/20/30%) |
| > 24 months | Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG) | See Budget 2024 section below |

**Holding period**: From date of purchase/registration to date of sale agreement. If inherited: from original purchase date (not date you inherited).

---

## Budget 2024 Change — Indexation Option for Property

**Before July 23, 2024**: LTCG on property = 20% with indexation (CII-based cost inflation adjustment).

**After July 23, 2024**: Two options available for properties purchased before July 23, 2024:

| Option | Rate | Applicable To |
|--------|------|--------------|
| **New rate**: 12.5% WITHOUT indexation | 12.5% | All LTCG property sales after July 23, 2024 |
| **Old rate**: 20% WITH indexation | 20% | Properties acquired BEFORE July 23, 2024 — taxpayer can CHOOSE this if beneficial |

**For properties purchased after July 23, 2024**: Only 12.5% without indexation applies (no choice).

---

## Which Option Saves More Tax? — The Indexation Choice

For pre-July 2024 properties, the better option depends on:
- **When you bought**: Older the purchase, more the indexation benefit (CII was lower earlier)
- **Your purchase price**: Lower the original cost, more indexation helps

**General rule**:
- Properties bought **before 2001**: Old rate (20% with indexation) almost always wins
- Properties bought **2001–2010**: Usually old rate wins
- Properties bought **after 2015**: New rate (12.5% without indexation) may win

**Calculate both and pick lower**. Your tax software (ClearTax) does this automatically.

**Example (property bought in 2010, sold in 2025)**:
- Purchase price: ₹30 lakh (2010)
- Sale price: ₹90 lakh (2025)
- CII 2010: 167; CII 2025: 363

*Option 1 (12.5%, no indexation)*:
LTCG = ₹90L - ₹30L = ₹60L
Tax = 12.5% of ₹60L = **₹7.5 lakh**

*Option 2 (20%, with indexation)*:
Indexed cost = ₹30L × (363/167) = ₹65.21L
LTCG = ₹90L - ₹65.21L = ₹24.79L
Tax = 20% of ₹24.79L = **₹4.96 lakh**

**Old regime (20% with indexation) saves ₹2.54 lakh** in this case.

## How to Calculate LTCG on Property — Step by Step

**LTCG = Sale Price − Indexed Cost of Acquisition − Indexed Cost of Improvement − Transfer Costs**

| Component | What to Include |
|-----------|----------------|
| Sale Price | Actual sale consideration (or stamp duty value, whichever is higher — per Section 50C) |
| Cost of Acquisition | Original purchase price + stamp duty + registration at time of purchase |
| Cost of Improvement | Major renovations, additions, modifications (with invoices) |
| Transfer costs | Brokerage, legal fees, advertisement for sale |

**Section 50C**: If you sell at less than stamp duty value (circle rate), IT Dept assumes circle rate as sale price. Protects against underreporting.

**Inherited property**: Cost of acquisition = original purchase price paid by the person you inherited from + indexation from their purchase year.

## Section 54 — Save ENTIRE LTCG Tax by Reinvesting

Section 54 allows full exemption from LTCG tax on residential property sale if you:

**Condition 1**: The property sold must be a "long-term capital asset" (held >24 months)

**Condition 2**: Invest the LTCG amount (not sale price — just the gain) in a new residential property:
- **Purchase**: Within 1 year BEFORE or 2 years AFTER the sale date
- **Construction**: Within 3 years after the sale date

**Condition 3**: New property must be in India

**Condition 4**: Don't sell the new property within 3 years of purchase (else exemption is reversed)

**Exemption amount**: The lower of (LTCG amount) or (cost of new property)

### Section 54 Example

Sale proceeds: ₹90 lakh | Original cost (indexed): ₹65 lakh | LTCG: ₹25 lakh

If you buy a new house for ₹25 lakh or more within 2 years: **LTCG tax = ₹0**

If you buy a house for ₹15 lakh (less than LTCG): Exemption = ₹15L, taxable LTCG = ₹10L, tax = ₹1.25L

## Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS) — What to Do If New Property Not Ready

If you've sold a property but haven't yet identified/purchased the new one (and want to avoid tax this year):

1. Deposit the LTCG amount in **Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS)** at a nationalized bank before the ITR filing deadline (July 31)
2. You must invest the CGAS amount in new property within the 2/3-year window
3. If not used within the window: Amount becomes taxable + interest in that year
4. CGAS Account: Open at SBI, PNB, Bank of Baroda, etc. (Savings or Term Deposit type)

**Why use CGAS**: Locks the money for property investment; proves to IT Dept you intend to reinvest; protects from tax this year.

## TDS on Property Sale (Section 194-IA)

**When applicable**: Sale value > ₹50 lakh

**Who deducts**: The BUYER (not seller) must deduct TDS @ **1% of sale value**

**Process for buyer**:
1. Deduct 1% from payment to seller
2. Pay via challan 26QB at incometax.gov.in
3. Download Form 16B (TDS certificate) from TRACES within 15 days
4. Give Form 16B to seller

**For seller**:
- The 1% TDS appears in your Form 26AS
- This is credited against your total capital gains tax liability
- If LTCG tax < TDS deducted: Claim refund in ITR

**Penalty for buyer if TDS not deducted**: 1% interest/month + possible prosecution under 276B.

## NRI Property Sale — Special Rules

For NRIs selling property in India:
- TDS rate: 20% on LTCG (not 1%) — buyer must deduct 20% for NRI seller
- NRI can apply for lower TDS certificate from IT Dept (if claiming Section 54 exemption)
- Repatriation: After paying tax, NRI can repatriate sale proceeds up to $1 million/year from NRO account

## Other Exemptions for Property LTCG

### Section 54EC — Invest in Capital Gain Bonds

If you don't want to buy another property, invest LTCG in **NHAI/REC bonds (54EC bonds)**:
- Investment limit: **₹50 lakh maximum per year**
- Lock-in: 5 years
- Interest: ~5% taxable
- Exemption: Full LTCG exempted up to ₹50L invested

**Best for**: Sellers who don't want another property but have LTCG up to ₹50L.

### Section 54F — Sale of Non-Residential Asset

If you sell LAND, commercial property, or shares and want to save LTCG: Section 54F allows exemption if you invest the entire SALE PROCEEDS (not just LTCG) in residential property.

## Property Sale in ITR — Which Form and Schedule

| Income Source | ITR Form |
|--------------|---------|
| Salary + property sale (LTCG) | **ITR-2** |
| Business income + property sale | **ITR-3** |
| Only property sale (no salary/business) | **ITR-2** |

**Schedule in ITR-2**: Schedule "Capital Gains" → "Long Term Capital Gains" → "Sale of House Property (Section 54)"

Enter: Full value of consideration (sale price), Cost of acquisition (indexed or unindexed), improvement costs, transfer expenses, Section 54 exemption amount.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I sold a property at ₹80L but stamp duty value is ₹1 Cr. What's my capital gain?

Your "full consideration" = ₹1 Cr (stamp duty value under Section 50C, since it's higher than actual price). LTCG = ₹1 Cr − indexed cost. This prevents underreporting.

### Both my spouse and I are on the property. How is LTCG split?

Each co-owner is taxed proportionally on their ownership share. If 50-50: each declares 50% of LTCG in their ITR. Both can claim Section 54 if both jointly buy the new property.

### I'm reinvesting in property under construction. When does the 3-year clock start?

3 years from the date of sale of the original property (not from the start of construction or booking date).

### Can I claim Section 54 twice in a lifetime?

No restriction — you can claim Section 54 every time you sell a long-term residential property and reinvest. There's no lifetime limit.
