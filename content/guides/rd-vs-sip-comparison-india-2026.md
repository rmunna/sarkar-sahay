# RD vs SIP India 2026 — Which Is Better?

The classic comparison for monthly savers. Both RD and SIP deduct a fixed amount monthly — but the returns, risk, tax treatment, and ideal use cases are very different. This guide cuts through the confusion.

---

## What Is RD?

A Recurring Deposit (RD) is a fixed-income product where you deposit a fixed amount monthly for a chosen tenure. At maturity, you receive principal + accumulated interest at the promised rate.

**Key features**:
- **Return**: 6.5–7.5% per annum (bank RD); 7.5% (Post Office RD)
- **Guaranteed**: Rate is fixed at the time of opening — won't change
- **Safe**: Principal is insured up to ₹5L per bank (DICGC)
- **Tenure**: 6 months to 10 years
- **Premature closure**: Allowed with 1% interest penalty

---

## What Is SIP?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a method of investing a fixed amount monthly in a mutual fund. Returns depend on the fund's performance — they are NOT guaranteed.

**Key features**:
- **Return**: Historical 10–14% CAGR for equity mutual funds (not guaranteed)
- **Market-linked**: Value goes up and down daily
- **Tax**: LTCG at 12.5% after 1 year (equity funds)
- **Flexible**: Can pause, increase, decrease, or stop anytime
- **No DICGC insurance**: Mutual fund investments are not bank-insured

---

## Head-to-Head Comparison

| Feature | RD | SIP (Equity MF) |
|---------|----|----|
| Return | 6.5–7.5% (fixed) | 10–14% CAGR (historical, not guaranteed) |
| Risk | Zero (bank insured up to ₹5L) | Market risk — can fall 30–40% in downturns |
| Tax on returns | Slab rate (interest = income) | LTCG 12.5% after 1 year |
| Inflation-beating | Barely — real return ~1–2% (post inflation + tax) | Yes — 6–8% real return historically |
| Tenure flexibility | Fixed at opening | No tenure — invest as long as you want |
| Liquidity | Penalty for early closure | Redeem anytime (most funds — 1% exit load if within 1 year) |
| Monthly monitoring needed | No | Minimal — but unit values change daily |
| Best for | Short-term (1–3 years), capital protection | Long-term (5+ years), wealth building |

## Tax Comparison — Important for High-Income Earners

**RD Interest**:
- Taxable as "Income from Other Sources" at your slab rate
- TDS at 10% if total RD interest across a bank exceeds ₹40,000/year (₹50K for senior citizens)
- In 30% tax bracket: 30% tax + 4% cess = effective 31.2% tax on interest
- Real return after tax: 7% × (1 - 0.312) = ~4.8%

**SIP on Equity Fund** (held 12+ months):
- LTCG at 12.5% (above ₹1.25L cumulative gains)
- In 30% bracket: Only 12.5% tax on GAINS (not on principal returned)
- Real return after tax: If CAGR = 12%, after 12.5% LTCG = ~10.5% effective

**The tax gap**: At 30% slab rate, equity SIP is far more tax-efficient than RD. For 5–10% bracket investors, the difference is smaller.

## When RD Is Better

**Choose RD over SIP when**:

1. **Short-term goal (< 3 years)**: House down payment in 2 years, wedding fund in 18 months. You can't risk equity volatility eating into principal.

2. **You're conservative and can't sleep with market fluctuations**: Psychological comfort matters. A guaranteed ₹3.59L is better than potential ₹4.07L that could become ₹2.8L if markets crash before your goal date.

3. **Emergency fund building**: Emergency fund should be in a liquid/safe instrument. RD or liquid fund, not equity SIP.

4. **Low income bracket (5–10% tax)**: The tax advantage of SIP over RD is smaller at lower tax rates.

5. **Very close to goal**: If you're 1 year away from a goal, shift from SIP to RD/FD — don't risk sequence of returns.

---

## When SIP Is Better

**Choose SIP over RD when**:

1. **Long-term goal (5+ years)**: Retirement corpus, child education (10+ years away), wealth building.

2. **High income bracket (20–30% tax)**: Tax advantage of equity SIP is 17.5–18.5 percentage points better than RD interest.

3. **Inflation protection**: Over 20 years, ₹1 crore will be worth ₹50 lakh in today's money (at 7% inflation). Only equity returns have historically beaten inflation significantly.

4. **Discipline without the emotional commitment to manage risk**: SIP auto-debit builds habits without requiring you to choose "when to invest."

5. **Young investors (20–35)**: Time horizon is long enough to absorb market cycles. Even a 40% crash in year 5 is recoverable over 15 more years.

## Best of Both — Hybrid Strategy

For most Indian salaried investors, the answer is BOTH:

| Goal | Instrument |
|------|-----------|
| Emergency fund (6 months expenses) | Liquid fund or RD (safe, accessible) |
| Short-term goals (<3 years) | RD, FD, or debt mutual fund |
| Medium-term goals (3–7 years) | Balanced advantage fund or aggressive hybrid |
| Long-term goals (7+ years) | Equity index fund SIP |
| Retirement corpus (15+ years) | 100% equity SIP (shift to balanced as you approach retirement) |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I have ₹3,000/month to save. Should I do RD or SIP?

At ₹3,000/month: depends on your goal. For first 6 months (emergency fund building): RD or liquid fund. After emergency fund is built (₹18K–₹36K): start SIP for long-term wealth.

### Can I do SIP in a debt mutual fund as an alternative to RD?

Yes — short-duration debt funds or liquid funds offer 7–7.5% returns like RD, but with flexible redemption, no TDS until you withdraw, and slightly more tax-efficient (slab rate but only on gains, not full amount). For amounts above ₹50K/month, debt funds are often better than RD.

### My company allows ₹2,000 voluntary PF contribution. Is that better than RD?

Voluntary PF earns 8.1% (current EPF rate) and is tax-free under 80C. For amounts within your 80C limit, voluntary PF beats both RD and most SIPs on a post-tax basis. Maximize EPF before choosing RD vs SIP for remaining savings.

### RD matures in 5 years but I need money in 2 years. Is it flexible?

RD premature closure: possible with 1% interest penalty and you get interest only up to actual date of closure. Better to open a shorter-tenure RD (1-year RD, renew) for money you might need sooner.
