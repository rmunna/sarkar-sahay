# Agricultural Income Tax India 2026 — Exempt Under Section 10(1), But Know the Full Rules

**Agricultural income** in India is **completely exempt from income tax** under **Section 10(1)** of the Income Tax Act. This is one of the oldest and most significant income tax exemptions — agricultural income doesn't form part of total income for tax calculation purposes.

However, there's an important nuance: the **Partial Integration (Aggregation) Rule** that can increase your tax on non-agricultural income if you have both types of income.

## What Is Agricultural Income?

Agricultural income includes:
- Rent or revenue from agricultural land in India
- Income from agriculture (growing crops, horticulture, nurseries)
- Income from a farmhouse on agricultural land
- Income from activities directly related to agriculture (threshing, storing, processing before first sale)

**Does NOT include**:
- Income from processing agricultural products beyond first sale (e.g., cotton textile factory — not agricultural income)
- Income from poultry farming (not agricultural)
- Income from dairy, bee-keeping, forestry (generally not agricultural)
- Income from selling agricultural land (capital gains — separate rules)

**Agricultural land must be in India**: Income from agricultural land outside India is NOT exempt.

## Section 10(1) — The Exemption

Agricultural income is completely exempt — it's not included in gross total income at all. No tax is computed on it.

**If all your income is agricultural**: You have zero taxable income. No ITR required (unless other conditions trigger filing requirement).

**Example**: Farmer with ₹15L agricultural income, no other income → Zero tax, no ITR needed.

## The Partial Integration Rule — When It Affects You

**Triggered when BOTH conditions are met**:
1. Non-agricultural income exceeds the basic exemption limit (₹2,50,000 old regime; ₹3,00,000 new regime)
2. Agricultural income exceeds ₹5,000 per year

**If both conditions met**: The following aggregation calculation applies:

**Step 1**: Compute tax on (Non-agricultural income + Agricultural income) combined

**Step 2**: Compute tax on (Agricultural income + Basic Exemption Limit)

**Step 3**: Your actual tax = Step 1 result minus Step 2 result

**What this effectively does**: Your agricultural income "pushes" your non-agricultural income into higher tax slabs, even though the agricultural income itself isn't taxed.

**Example**:
- Salary: ₹8,00,000
- Agricultural income: ₹5,00,000
- Basic exemption (old regime): ₹2,50,000

**Step 1**: Tax on ₹8,00,000 + ₹5,00,000 = ₹13,00,000 → Tax ~₹2,62,500 (old regime slabs)

**Step 2**: Tax on ₹5,00,000 + ₹2,50,000 = ₹7,50,000 → Tax ~₹1,12,500

**Actual tax**: ₹2,62,500 − ₹1,12,500 = **₹1,50,000**

Without partial integration, tax on ₹8L salary alone = ~₹1,12,500.
With partial integration = ₹1,50,000.

**You pay ₹37,500 MORE because agricultural income pushed your salary into higher slabs.**

The agricultural income itself is still exempt — you didn't pay tax on ₹5L agricultural income. But your salary was taxed at higher rates.

## Does Partial Integration Apply Under New Tax Regime?

No — the partial integration rule applies **only under the old tax regime**. Under the new tax regime (no deductions), agricultural income does not trigger partial integration.

This is one case where the new regime may be more favorable for mixed agricultural/salary income taxpayers.

## How to Declare Agricultural Income in ITR

Even though agricultural income is exempt, you must declare it in your ITR if:
- Your total income (salary + agricultural) requires ITR filing
- Your agricultural income is above ₹5,000 (to trigger the partial integration calculation if applicable)

**In ITR**:
- Agricultural income is declared in **Schedule EI (Exempt Income)**
- Select "Agricultural Income"
- Enter the amount

The ITR portal automatically applies the partial integration calculation.

## SFT (High-Value Transaction) — Agricultural Income and Cash Deposits

If you deposit large amounts of agricultural income cash into a bank, the bank reports it as SFT (Statement of Financial Transactions) if total deposits exceed ₹10L in a year.

The IT Department may send you a notice asking to explain the source of large cash deposits.

**If source is agricultural income**: Respond with:
- Land documents (7/12 extract, Khasra, land revenue records)
- Agricultural produce sale bills (mandi receipts, crop sale receipts)
- Agricultural income certificate from local authorities
- Bank account showing regular agricultural income pattern

Agricultural income is a legitimate source, but you must document it.

## Capital Gains on Sale of Agricultural Land

**Urban agricultural land** (within municipal limits or near urban areas as defined by notification): Treated as capital asset — gains from sale are taxable.

**Rural agricultural land** (as per Income Tax Act definition): NOT a capital asset — gains from sale are completely exempt from capital gains tax.

**Definition of rural agricultural land** (simplified): Land outside the jurisdiction of a municipality with population > 10,000, and further than specified distances from urban centers (varies by population).

For land near cities (within 8 km of town with 10K–1L population, within 6 km for 1L–10L, within 3 km for 10L+), capital gains on sale ARE taxable.

## State-Level Taxes on Agricultural Income

Income Tax (central) doesn't tax agricultural income, but some states impose agricultural income tax at the state level:
- Kerala: Agricultural income tax (if agricultural income > certain threshold)
- Karnataka: Agricultural income tax (though rates are generally low)
- Most other states: No agricultural income tax

Check your specific state's agricultural income tax provisions.

## ITR Filing Requirement With Agricultural Income

You must file ITR if:
- You have non-agricultural income above the basic exemption limit
- Your agricultural income exceeds ₹5,000 (even if non-agricultural income is below exemption limit — you should file to document the exempt income)

**ITR-1 (Sahaj)**: Can be used if agricultural income ≤ ₹5,000. If more, use **ITR-2**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I'm a salaried employee. My ancestral land generates ₹2L in crop rent annually. How is it taxed?

Your ₹2L agricultural income is exempt. But if your salary is above ₹2.5L (old regime), partial integration applies. Your salary will be taxed at higher slabs because of the ₹2L agricultural income push. Declare both in ITR-2 (use Schedule EI for agricultural income).

### I sold my farm land near Pune. Is the capital gain taxable?

It depends on whether the land is classified as "rural agricultural land" under the IT Act definition. Land near a city like Pune likely falls within the taxable zone (within specified km of urban center). If so, LTCG tax applies. Check the notification and distance criteria for Pune's municipal area.

### My family receives ₹20L in crop income. No salary. Do we file ITR?

If total income is ONLY agricultural and below the threshold: No ITR required. But:
- If receiving high-value payments (SFT threshold crossed)
- If any family member has non-agricultural income
- If you want to document income for loan/visa purposes
You should voluntarily file ITR with agricultural income in Schedule EI.

### The IT Department sent me a notice about ₹15L cash deposit. All from my wheat crop. What do I need?

Respond with: mandi receipts showing wheat sale (APMC receipt with buyer details), land records (7/12 extract confirming your agricultural land ownership), bank passbook showing this is a pattern of seasonal agricultural income. Agricultural income is legitimate — the department just wants documentation, not additional tax.
