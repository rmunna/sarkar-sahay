# Gold ETF vs SGB vs Physical Gold India 2026 — Complete Comparison

Gold is the most trusted asset in Indian households — but the way you buy it makes a huge difference in returns, tax, liquidity, and safety.

## The Three Ways to Invest in Gold

| Method | What You Actually Own |
|--------|----------------------|
| **Physical Gold** | Jewellery, coins, bars — you hold gold |
| **Gold ETF** | Units on stock exchange backed by 99.5% pure gold |
| **Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB)** | RBI-issued bond with gold-linked return + 2.5% annual interest |

## Head-to-Head Comparison

| Feature | Physical Gold | Gold ETF | SGB |
|---------|--------------|----------|-----|
| **Purity guarantee** | No (jewellery has making charges, alloying) | Yes (99.5% pure) | Yes (gold price linked) |
| **Minimum investment** | ₹3,000–₹5,000 (1g coin) | ₹1 unit (~₹80–₹100 = 0.01g) | 1g per bond |
| **Storage** | Your locker / bank locker cost | None (demat) | None (demat/certificate) |
| **Making charges** | 8–25% for jewellery | None | None |
| **Liquidity** | Slow (sell to jeweller at a discount) | High (sell on exchange during market hours) | Low — secondary market thin; RBI buyback only at Year 5/6/7 |
| **Lock-in** | None | None | 8 years (with early exit at Year 5/6/7) |
| **Annual interest** | None | None | **2.5% per year on initial gold value** |
| **Capital appreciation** | Yes (minus making charges) | Yes | Yes (based on gold price at maturity) |

## Tax Treatment — The Biggest Differentiator

### Physical Gold
- Hold > 2 years: **LTCG at 12.5%** (Budget 2024 change — previously 20% with indexation)
- Hold ≤ 2 years: STCG at slab rate
- **Cost**: No indexation benefit post-Budget 2024

### Gold ETF
- Hold > 2 years: **LTCG at 12.5%**
- Hold ≤ 2 years: STCG at slab rate
- No indexation benefit
- No TDS

### SGB (Sovereign Gold Bond) — The Tax Advantage
- **At maturity (8 years via RBI)**: **Completely tax-free** under Section 47(viic). Zero tax on the entire gain.
- **Early redemption via RBI (Year 5/6/7)**: Also tax-free (government-facilitated exit)
- **Secondary market sale**: LTCG at 12.5% if held > 12 months (treated as listed security)
- **Interest (2.5%)**: Taxable at your slab rate every year; no TDS

**The SGB tax advantage is huge**: If you hold 8 years and gold doubles, the entire gain is tax-free. With Gold ETF, you'd pay 12.5% LTCG.

Assuming gold appreciates 8% annually (roughly historical average):

*Note: Gold ETF has no making charges; physical jewellery loses 8–25% upfront on making charges.*

## SGB New Issue Problem — 2024 onwards

The Indian government **stopped issuing new SGBs** from 2024 onwards (RBI announced no new tranches). Existing SGBs continue to trade on exchanges, but buying fresh SGBs at gold price (without premium) is no longer possible.

**As of 2026**: You can only buy SGBs on the secondary market (NSE/BSE). The secondary market price may trade at a **premium or discount to gold price** depending on remaining tenure and interest yield.

**Implication**: Fresh SGB investment is not straightforward. Gold ETF has become the default "clean digital gold" option.

## Gold Mutual Fund vs Gold ETF

**Gold Mutual Fund** = a fund that invests in Gold ETF. You don't need a demat account.
**Gold ETF** = holds physical gold directly. Needs demat.

| | Gold ETF | Gold Mutual Fund |
|-|----------|-----------------|
| Demat required | Yes | No |
| Expense ratio | 0.3–0.5% | 0.5–0.8% (includes ETF + MF charges) |
| SIP available | No (must buy units) | Yes |
| Liquidity | Exchange hours | T+1 (any time) |
| Tax | Same | Same |

**Verdict**: If you already have a demat account, Gold ETF is cheaper. If you want SIP-based gold investing, Gold Mutual Fund is simpler.

## What About Digital Gold (Paytm, PhonePe, Amazon Pay)?

Digital gold sold through apps (by MMTC-PAMP, SafeGold, Augmont) is NOT a regulated investment product. It's stored gold with these providers — not backed by SEBI regulation, no insurance, and the storage fees eat into returns.

**Avoid digital gold for investment** — use Gold ETF or SGB instead.

## The Right Strategy — Who Should Buy What

| Investor Type | Recommended Gold Option |
|--------------|------------------------|
| Long-term (8+ years), tax-conscious, OK with lock-in | **SGB** (if available at/near gold price in secondary market) |
| Medium-term (2–5 years), wants liquidity | **Gold ETF** (clean, liquid, regulated) |
| Want to SIP into gold, no demat | **Gold Mutual Fund** |
| Buying for jewellery/gifting | **Physical gold** — but accept the making charge cost |
| Below ₹10,000 small amounts | **Gold ETF** (any unit) |

## Key Factors When Buying SGB from Secondary Market

Since new SGBs aren't issued, secondary market purchase matters:
1. **Remaining tenure**: SGB with 3 years left vs 7 years left — different interest income
2. **Price vs gold**: If SGB trades at premium (above gold price), the effective yield falls
3. **Accrued interest**: Some tranches pay interest semi-annually — check the next interest date
4. **Tax on exit**: If you sell in secondary market before maturity, it's LTCG 12.5% (not tax-free)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I sell my physical gold jewellery and buy Gold ETF?

Only if the jewellery is purely for investment (not emotional/cultural value). Selling jewellery at a jeweller gives you only the gold value (no making charge recovery). If you're using it purely as investment, Gold ETF is more efficient going forward. But the tax event on sale + new making charge on re-purchase often makes conversion not worth it.

### SGBs aren't available fresh. What's the best thing to buy now?

**Gold ETF** is now the cleanest option for new gold investment. Low expense ratio (0.3–0.5%), liquid, LTCG-taxed (12.5% after 2 years), no making charges, fully regulated by SEBI.

### I have physical gold inherited. How is it taxed if I sell?

Inherited gold: the holding period includes the deceased's period. Cost basis = original purchase price (or FMV on April 1, 2001 if purchased before that). If total holding > 2 years: LTCG at 12.5%. No tax at the time of inheritance — only when you sell.
