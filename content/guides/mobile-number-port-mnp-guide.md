# Mobile Number Portability (MNP): Complete Guide India

Mobile Number Portability (MNP) allows you to switch your telecom operator (Jio, Airtel, Vi, BSNL, etc.) **without changing your mobile number**. This guide covers the complete MNP process, UPC generation for all operators, TRAI rules, and common rejection reasons.

## What is MNP?

MNP (Mobile Number Portability) is a TRAI-regulated service that lets you keep your existing mobile number while switching to a different telecom operator. For example, you can move from Vi to Jio and keep the same 10-digit number.

## TRAI MNP Rules (Key Points)

- MNP is available **across India** — you can port within your circle or to another circle
- Your number must be **at least 90 days old** with the current operator
- No **outstanding dues** on your current connection
- Porting must be completed within **4 working days** (within circle) or **7 working days** (between circles — known as inter-circle or national MNP)
- You can port again after **90 days** from the last port
- **Corporate connections** require a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the company

## Step-by-Step MNP Process

### Step 1: Generate UPC (Unique Porting Code)

Send an SMS from the number you want to port:

**SMS Format:** `PORT <your 10-digit mobile number>` to **1900**

**Example:** `PORT 9876543210` → Send to **1900**

You'll receive a **UPC (Unique Porting Code)** via SMS within minutes. This code is valid for **4 days** (within circle) or **15 days** (national/inter-circle porting).

> This is the same process for **all operators** — Jio, Airtel, Vi, BSNL, MTNL.

### Step 2: Visit the New Operator

1. Visit a **retail store or website** of the operator you want to switch to
2. Carry:
   - **Aadhaar card** (original + copy) — See our [Aadhaar guide](/guide/aadhaar-card-apply-online)
   - **Passport-size photo** (1-2 copies)
   - **UPC code** (received via SMS)
3. Fill the **Customer Application Form (CAF)** and select "Porting" option
4. Choose your desired **plan/recharge**
5. Complete **Aadhaar biometric verification** (fingerprint/iris)

### Step 3: Receive New SIM

1. The new operator will provide a **new SIM card**
2. **Do not insert it immediately** — wait for the porting to complete
3. You'll receive an SMS on your **old SIM** confirming the port date and time (usually between 10 PM and 5 AM)

### Step 4: Porting Activation

1. On the scheduled porting date, your **old SIM will stop working**
2. Insert the **new SIM card**
3. Your number is now active on the new operator
4. Porting happens during **off-peak hours** (usually late night) to minimize disruption

## Common MNP Rejection Reasons

Your porting request may be rejected if:

1. **Number is less than 90 days old** with the current operator
2. **Outstanding dues** — Unpaid bills on postpaid connections
3. **Active contractual obligation** — Lock-in period with current operator
4. **Sub-judice** — Number involved in legal proceedings
5. **UPC expired** — The Unique Porting Code has passed its validity
6. **Incorrect UPC** — Typo while submitting the code
7. **Change of ownership pending** — Name change request in process
8. **Number deactivated** — SIM is inactive/suspended

### How to Fix Rejection
- Clear pending dues, wait for lock-in to expire, or generate a new UPC
- Contact the **new operator** to understand the specific rejection reason

## Corporate MNP (Company-Owned Numbers)

For numbers owned by a company/organization:

1. Obtain a **No Objection Certificate (NOC)** from your employer/company
2. The NOC must be on **company letterhead** with authorized signatory
3. Submit the NOC along with the regular MNP documents
4. Some companies may require an internal approval process — start early
5. The rest of the process is the same as individual MNP

## MNP for Postpaid Connections

1. Clear **all outstanding bills** before generating UPC
2. If you have a **handset EMI** or device contract, settle it first
3. Request a **final bill** from the current operator after porting
4. Any **security deposit** will be refunded by the old operator (usually within 30-60 days)

## Important Tips

1. **Backup your contacts** from SIM before porting
2. Generate UPC only when you're ready to port — it has limited validity
3. Update your number in **banking apps, UPI, and two-factor authentication** after porting (number stays same, but some services may need re-verification)
4. Keep your old SIM active until the porting is complete — don't remove it early
5. If porting fails, your number remains with the old operator — you don't lose it

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does MNP cost?
MNP is **free**. There is no porting charge. You only pay for the new operator's SIM/plan as per their pricing.

### Can I port my number from one state to another?
Yes. **National MNP (inter-circle porting)** allows you to port your number to an operator in a different telecom circle. It takes up to 7 working days.

### Will I lose my balance/validity when I port?
Yes. Any remaining talktime, data, or validity with your current operator will be **forfeited** after porting. Use up your balance before initiating MNP.

### Can I port a prepaid number to postpaid (or vice versa)?
Yes. When applying for MNP with the new operator, you can choose either prepaid or postpaid, regardless of your current plan type.

### What happens to my old SIM after porting?
Your old SIM **stops working** once porting is complete. You can discard it. Your number is now on the new operator's SIM.

### Can I cancel an MNP request?
Yes. Contact the **new operator** before the porting date to cancel. Once porting is executed, it cannot be reversed (you'd need to port back after 90 days).

### How do I check MNP status?
Contact the new operator's customer care or visit their store. They can check the status of your porting request using the UPC code.
