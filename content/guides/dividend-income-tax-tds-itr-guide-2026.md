# Dividend Income Tax India 2026 — Complete Guide

Dividend income in India has been **fully taxable at slab rate since April 1, 2020** (FY 2020-21). Before this, dividends were tax-free in the hands of investors because companies paid a Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT). DDT was abolished in Budget 2020, shifting the tax burden to investors.

> **Current rule**: Every rupee of dividend you receive (from shares, equity MF, debt MF) is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate (5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, or 30%).

## What Is Dividend Income?

| Source | Is It Dividend? |
|--------|----------------|
| **Shares of Indian companies** | ✅ Yes — annual/quarterly dividend declared by company |
| **Equity Mutual Funds (IDCW option)** | ✅ Yes — IDCW (Income Distribution Cum Withdrawal) = dividend |
| **Debt Mutual Funds (IDCW option)** | ✅ Yes |
| **Preference shares** | ✅ Yes |
| **REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)** | ✅ Partially (some REIT distributions are dividends) |
| **Capital gains from selling shares** | ❌ No — that's capital gains, not dividend |

## TDS on Dividend — When Is It Deducted?

| Situation | TDS Rate |
|-----------|---------|
| Dividend from Indian company > ₹5,000 per company in a year | **10% TDS** (Section 194) |
| Dividend from equity MF > ₹5,000 per fund house | **10% TDS** (Section 194K) |
| Resident individual earning dividend | 10% TDS above threshold |
| Non-resident receiving dividend | 20% TDS + surcharge |

**The ₹5,000 threshold**: TDS is per company or per fund house. If you get ₹4,000 dividends from Infosys and ₹4,000 from TCS, no TDS on either (below ₹5,000 each). But if you get ₹8,000 from Infosys alone, TDS is deducted: 10% × ₹8,000 = ₹800.

## How Dividend Tax Is Calculated

**Example**:
- Annual salary: ₹12,00,000
- Dividend received from shares: ₹80,000
- Dividend from equity MF: ₹30,000

**Total income**: ₹12,00,000 + ₹80,000 + ₹30,000 = ₹13,10,000

Tax is calculated on ₹13,10,000 (at applicable slabs after deductions). The dividend income is NOT separately taxed at a different rate — it flows into total income.

**TDS credit**: If companies deducted 10% TDS on dividend, say ₹11,000 total TDS, this is credited in Form 26AS and reduces your final tax payable.

## How to Declare Dividend in ITR

### Step 1: Check Form 26AS / AIS

All dividends appear in your:
- **AIS**: Under "Dividend Income" section
- **Form 26AS**: Under Part A2 (TDS on dividend)

### Step 2: File in Schedule OS

In your ITR:
1. Go to **Schedule OS (Other Sources)**
2. Under "Dividend Income" → Enter total dividend received during the year
3. The system adds this to your total income

**Note**: You do NOT file dividend separately in capital gains schedule. It's "Other Sources" even for equity dividends.

### Step 3: TDS Credit

The TDS deducted on dividend automatically shows in Schedule TDS in your ITR. It's credited against your tax payable.

## Dividend From Foreign Companies / ADRs

If you hold ADRs (American Depositary Receipts) or foreign company shares and receive dividends:
- Foreign dividend is also fully taxable in India at slab rate
- Foreign company does NOT deduct Indian TDS — you pay the full tax
- DTAA (Double Tax Avoidance Agreement) benefit may apply if foreign tax was deducted — claim credit in ITR Schedule FSI and DTAA

## Timing — When Is Dividend Taxable?

Dividend is taxable in the year it is **received or credited to your demat/bank account**, not when it's declared.

**Example**: Company declares dividend in March 2026, but it's credited to your account in April 2026 → taxable in FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28), not FY 2025-26.

## Equity MF IDCW — Important Note

If you chose the **IDCW (dividend) option** in an equity mutual fund:
- The fund pays out a portion of your investment back as "dividend"
- This reduces your NAV (you're getting your own money back, essentially)
- You pay full slab rate tax on what you receive
- The GROWTH option is more tax-efficient — no tax until you sell, and then only LTCG/STCG

**For most investors**: GROWTH option > IDCW option for tax efficiency.

## Advance Tax on Dividend

If you receive significant dividends regularly, you should include them in advance tax calculations:

- Add expected annual dividend income to salary/other income
- Pay advance tax on the combined income quarterly
- Not paying advance tax leads to 234B/234C interest

However, if dividends are irregular or unpredictable, Section 234C has a relaxation — advance tax on dividend can be paid in one shot in the quarter it's received.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I received ₹3,000 dividend from Reliance and ₹3,000 from HDFC. No TDS. Do I still declare in ITR?

Yes — you must declare all dividend income in ITR under Schedule OS regardless of TDS. The ₹5,000 TDS threshold doesn't mean below ₹5,000 is tax-free — it just means no TDS was deducted. You still owe slab rate tax on ₹6,000 total dividend.

### Are REIT distributions (dividends from REITs) taxable the same way?

Partially. REIT distributions have three components: (1) dividend from special purpose vehicles — taxable at slab rate; (2) interest income — taxable at slab rate; (3) capital repayment — not taxable (reduces your cost basis). The exact split is disclosed by the REIT in their distribution statement.

### I re-invested my dividends back into shares. Is it still taxable?

Yes — dividend is taxable when received, regardless of what you do with it. Re-investing doesn't change the tax character. You pay slab rate tax when received; separately, your new share purchase has its own cost basis for future capital gains.

### My equity MF is on IDCW option with dividend re-investment. Is TDS deducted?

Yes — even for IDCW re-investment options, 10% TDS is applicable on the dividend amount > ₹5,000 per fund house. The reinvestment happens after TDS.
