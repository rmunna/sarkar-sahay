# Corporate Bonds and G-Sec Investing India 2026 — Complete Guide

Bonds fill the gap between FDs (safe, low yield) and equity (high return, high risk). For ₹1L+ investments targeting 7–9% returns with lower volatility than equity, bonds deserve serious consideration in 2026.

---

## Bond Basics — What You're Buying

When you buy a bond, you're lending money to the issuer (government or company) in exchange for:
- **Regular interest payments** (coupon) — typically semi-annually
- **Principal repayment** at maturity

| Bond Type | Issuer | Risk | Yield (Approx 2026) |
|-----------|--------|------|---------------------|
| **G-Sec (Government Securities)** | Central Government of India | Zero credit risk | 6.9–7.2% (10-year) |
| **SDL (State Development Loans)** | State Governments | Near-zero credit risk | 7.2–7.5% |
| **PSU Bonds** | NHAI, NABARD, PFC, REC | Very low | 7.3–7.8% |
| **AAA Corporate Bonds** | HDFC, LIC, Bajaj Finance | Low | 7.5–8.5% |
| **AA Corporate Bonds** | Mid-size companies | Moderate | 8.5–9.5% |
| **A / BBB Bonds** | Smaller companies | High | 10–12%+ |

## G-Sec / Government Bonds — How to Invest

### RBI Retail Direct (Best Option for Retail Investors)

**Portal**: retaildirect.rbi.org.in  
**Commission**: Zero — you buy directly from RBI at auction prices

**How it works**:
1. Open a Retail Direct account (KYC with PAN + Aadhaar)
2. Link your savings bank account
3. Participate in primary auctions (new G-Sec issued by government) OR secondary market purchases
4. Coupon payments and maturity proceeds credited directly to your bank account

**What you can buy**:
- G-Secs (Central government bonds): 2, 5, 10, 14, 30-year maturities
- T-Bills (Treasury Bills): 91-day, 182-day, 364-day (short-term, zero coupon)
- SDLs (State government bonds)
- Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) at primary issue

**Minimum investment**: ₹10,000 per security; bid in multiples of ₹10,000

---

### G-Sec via Zerodha/Groww/Coin

If you're already on a demat platform, G-Secs are now available in secondary market:
- **Zerodha**: G-Secs available under Zerodha Console Coin
- **Groww**: Bond section (secondary market, limited selection)
- **Minimum**: ₹1,000 face value per unit

**Secondary market G-Sec**: Price fluctuates with interest rates (when rates go up, existing bond prices fall). Best held to maturity to lock the original yield.

## Corporate Bonds — How to Invest

### Option 1: Primary Issue (Direct from Company)

Large companies issue bonds directly to retail investors via NSE/BSE:
- Apply through your broker (Zerodha, HDFC Securities, ICICI Direct)
- Fixed rate announced before issue; you apply, allotment done
- Held in demat account

### Option 2: Secondary Market Purchase

Already-issued bonds trading on stock exchanges:
- Buy via Zerodha (Bonds tab), HDFC Securities bond platform
- Price + yield visible; settlement T+1

## Credit Ratings — What They Mean

Before buying a corporate bond, always check the credit rating:

| Rating | Meaning | Safety |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **AAA** | Highest credit quality | Very safe; major PSUs and blue chips |
| **AA+ / AA / AA-** | High quality | Safe; large established companies |
| **A+ / A / A-** | Upper medium grade | Acceptable; some credit risk |
| **BBB+ and below** | Medium/speculative | Increasing default risk |
| **Below BB** | Speculative (junk) | Avoid for conservative investing |

**Always invest in AA or higher for retail bond portfolios.** Credit rating agencies: CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, Brickwork.

## Bond Tax Treatment

| Income Type | Tax |
|------------|-----|
| **Coupon/Interest income** | Added to salary income; taxed at your slab rate (20%/30%) |
| **Capital gain on bond sale (LTCG, listed, 12+ months)** | 12.5% flat (as of Budget 2024) |
| **Capital gain on bond sale (STCG, listed, <12 months)** | Slab rate |
| **T-Bill discount (difference between face value and purchase price)** | Taxed as income at slab rate |
| **Bharat Bond ETF held to maturity** | Indexation + 20% (pre-2023 purchase) or 12.5% (post-2023) |

**Tax comparison vs FD**: Bond interest = same as FD interest (slab rate). Bond capital gain on sale = lower tax. But FD is simpler. Bharat Bond ETF = potentially lower tax if held to maturity.

## Corporate Bond vs FD — Which Is Better?

| Feature | Corporate Bond (AAA) | Bank FD |
|---------|---------------------|---------|
| **Yield** | 7.5–8.5% | 6.5–7.5% |
| **Safety** | Very high (AAA PSU) | Very high (DICGC ₹5L insurance) |
| **Liquidity** | Secondary market (may have low liquidity) | Premature withdrawal with penalty |
| **Tax** | Interest at slab; capital gain lower | All interest at slab |
| **Convenience** | Demat required; secondary market | Very simple |
| **TDS** | TDS if held via NSDL/CDSL (Form 15H/G applicable) | TDS if interest >₹40K/year |

**Verdict**: For amounts > ₹5L and 3–10 year horizon, PSU bonds or Bharat Bond ETF beat FD on return and tax. For short-term or convenience, FD wins.

## Building a Bond Ladder

A bond ladder spreads your investment across bonds maturing at different dates:

**Example**: ₹5L total, spread across:
- ₹1L: G-Sec maturing in 2027 (2-year)
- ₹1L: Bharat Bond ETF maturing in 2030 (4-year)
- ₹1L: AAA PSU bond maturing in 2031 (5-year)
- ₹1L: SDL (State government bond) 2033 (7-year)
- ₹1L: 10-year G-Sec 2034 (8-year)

As each matures, reinvest at prevailing rates. Reduces interest rate risk and ensures periodic liquidity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can bond prices fall? Can I lose money?

Yes — if you sell before maturity. When RBI raises interest rates, existing bond prices fall (inverse relationship). If you hold to maturity, you get exactly the promised yield regardless of price movements. Risk of loss = only if you sell in secondary market when rates rose, or if corporate issuer defaults (AAA risk is minimal).

### What's the difference between yield and coupon?

**Coupon** = fixed interest rate at issue (e.g., 7.5% on face value of ₹1,000 = ₹75/year).  
**Yield** = actual return based on price you paid. If you buy bond at ₹950 (below face value), your yield > 7.5% because you get ₹1,000 at maturity. Bond platforms show yield, which is more useful than coupon.

### Is there a TDS on bond interest?

Yes — TDS applies if interest >₹5,000/year per bond (for resident individuals). If your total income is below taxable limits, submit Form 15G (if age <60) or 15H (if age 60+) to avoid TDS.
