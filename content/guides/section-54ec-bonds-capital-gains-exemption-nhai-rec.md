# Section 54EC Bonds India 2026 — Save LTCG Tax by Investing in NHAI/REC Bonds

If you've sold land or property and made a long-term capital gain, Section 54EC offers a simple way to avoid paying LTCG tax: invest the gains in designated government bonds within 6 months.

## What Is Section 54EC?

Section 54EC exempts **Long-Term Capital Gains** from the **sale of immovable property** (land, building, house) if you invest the capital gains amount in specified bonds:

1. **NHAI (National Highways Authority of India)** — Capital Gains Bonds
2. **REC (Rural Electrification Corporation)** — Capital Gains Bonds

These are also called **"54EC bonds"** or **"Capital Gains Bonds"**.

## Who Can Use Section 54EC?

- Any individual, HUF, or entity
- Who sold **land or building** (immovable property in India)
- That was a **long-term capital asset** (held > 24 months for immovable property)
- And made a **capital gain** on the sale

**Specifically excluded from 54EC**: Equity shares, equity MF units, gold, bonds, foreign assets — 54EC only covers gains from immovable property.

## Key Conditions

| Condition | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Asset type** | Only land/building (immovable property) |
| **Asset held** | > 24 months (long-term) |
| **Investment amount** | Only the **capital gains** amount (not entire sale consideration) |
| **Investment cap** | Maximum ₹50 lakh per financial year |
| **Time limit** | Within **6 months** from date of sale |
| **Bond lock-in** | **5 years** (Budget 2018 increased from 3 years) |
| **Redemption** | Only at maturity (5 years) — no premature exit |
| **Interest rate** | Currently 5% per annum (as of 2026) — fully taxable |

## 54EC vs 54F vs 54 — Which to Use?

| | Section 54 | Section 54F | Section 54EC |
|-|-----------|------------|-------------|
| **What you sold** | Residential house | Any asset (except house) | Land/building |
| **Invest in** | New house | New house | NHAI/REC bonds |
| **Amount to invest** | Capital gains | Entire sale consideration | Capital gains only |
| **Time limit** | 2 years (purchase) / 3 years (construction) | 2 years (purchase) / 3 years (construction) | 6 months |
| **Lock-in** | New house for 3 years | New house for 3 years | 5 years (bonds) |
| **Max exemption** | ₹10 crore (Budget 2023 cap) | ₹10 crore (Budget 2023 cap) | ₹50L/year |

**Which to choose for property sale?**
- If you want to buy a house → Section 54 or 54F (depending on what you sold)
- If you don't want to buy a house but want simpler route → Section 54EC bonds
- If gains > ₹50L and no house purchase → Section 54EC for first ₹50L; pay tax on the rest

## Tax on Interest: Not Exempt

Section 54EC exempts only the **capital gain** from tax. The **interest** earned on the bonds (5% per annum) is **fully taxable as income from other sources** at your slab rate.

On ₹50L at 5%:
- Interest per year: ₹2,50,000
- Tax at 30% slab: ₹75,000/year
- Net interest after tax: ₹1,75,000/year or ~3.5% post-tax yield

This is relatively low — but the capital gains tax saved can be much larger than the opportunity cost of investing in bonds vs other instruments.

## How to Buy Section 54EC Bonds

### NHAI Capital Gains Bonds
- **Issuer**: National Highways Authority of India
- **Face value**: ₹10,000 per bond
- **How to buy**: Through designated banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, etc.) or directly via NHAI
- **Form**: Physical or demat
- Available in tranches — check if current tranche is open

### REC Capital Gains Bonds
- **Issuer**: Rural Electrification Corporation
- **Face value**: ₹10,000 per bond
- **How to buy**: Through branches, select banks, or rec.gov.in
- Also available in tranches

### Process
1. Arrange cash equivalent of capital gains amount
2. Visit a designated bank branch or apply online
3. Fill the bond application form with details of capital gains (mention property sold)
4. Pay via demand draft/NEFT
5. Receive bond certificate (or demat holding)
6. Keep all documentation for ITR

**Important**: Make the investment within 6 months of the property sale date, even if the ITR deadline hasn't come yet.

## ₹50 Lakh Annual Cap — Planning Implications

If your LTCG exceeds ₹50L:
- Section 54EC covers only ₹50L of gains
- Remaining gains are taxable at 12.5% LTCG

**Multi-year sale strategy** (if applicable): If you can split the property transaction across two financial years (staggered payment/transfer), you potentially get ₹50L exemption in each FY. This requires legal structuring and consultation.

For large property sales (above ₹50L in gains), consider:
- Section 54EC for ₹50L
- Section 54 or 54F for the rest (via house purchase)

## Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS) for 6-Month Buffer

If you've sold property but the bond tranche isn't currently open, or you want time to arrange funds:

Deposit in **Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS)** at a nationalized bank before your ITR filing date. You can then withdraw from CGAS when the bond tranche opens.

This preserves the tax exemption even if you can't physically buy the bonds immediately.

## ITR Filing — How to Show 54EC Exemption

In your ITR (Schedule CG — Capital Gains):
1. Show the LTCG from property sale under the appropriate head
2. Under "Deductions from CG" → Section 54EC
3. Enter the amount invested in bonds
4. Attach/note: Date of sale, date of bond purchase, bond details (NHAI/REC tranche, certificate number)

Keep bond certificates safe for scrutiny.

## After 5 Years — Bond Maturity

At the end of 5 years:
- Principal (₹50L) is returned
- The capital gains tax exemption stands permanently (no clawback at maturity)
- Interest received annually is already taxed

You can reinvest the principal in any instrument. If you sell a new property within this period and want another 54EC exemption, you can buy fresh bonds (subject to the ₹50L annual cap).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I sold agricultural land (rural) for ₹80L with ₹40L LTCG. Can I use 54EC?

Rural agricultural land is NOT a capital asset under the IT Act — so no capital gains arise in the first place, and 54EC doesn't apply (there's nothing to exempt). If it's urban agricultural land (within specified distances of city), capital gains do arise and 54EC is available.

### My property sale happened in December. Can I invest in February (next year, same AY)?

Yes — the 6-month window runs from the date of sale (not the ITR deadline). December sale + February investment = 2 months. Well within 6 months. What matters is the 6-month gap, not the financial year.

### I invested in 54EC bonds but sold them before 5 years. What happens?

54EC bonds **cannot be transferred, pledged, or prematurely redeemed** for 5 years. If you somehow manage to exit early (unlikely), the capital gains exemption is revoked and the entire LTCG becomes taxable in that year.

### I have ₹70L capital gains from land sale. 54EC only covers ₹50L. What about the rest ₹20L?

Two options:
1. Pay LTCG tax (12.5%) on ₹20L = ₹2.5L
2. If you're also buying a new house: Use Section 54 for the ₹20L remaining gain (residential property sale only, not land)

For land sale specifically, Section 54F doesn't apply to claim the ₹20L — you'd need to look at Section 54F if the land was sold (54F is for non-house assets), but since the asset IS land (not house), check which section applies. Consult a CA for large amounts.
