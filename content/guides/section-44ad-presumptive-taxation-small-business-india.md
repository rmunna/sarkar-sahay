# Section 44AD Presumptive Taxation India 2026 — Small Business Tax Simplified

Running a small shop, trading business, or small contractor operation? Section 44AD lets you declare a fixed percentage of your turnover as income — no need to maintain full books of accounts, no audit requirement below the threshold.

## What Is Section 44AD?

Section 44AD is a **presumptive taxation scheme for small businesses**. Instead of declaring actual profit (revenue minus all expenses), you declare a minimum **percentage of turnover** as your taxable income:

- **6% of turnover**: If at least 95% of business receipts were via digital modes (bank transfers, UPI, cards, cheques)
- **8% of turnover**: If any portion of receipts was cash

You can declare MORE than 6%/8% (if actual profit is higher), but not less than 6%/8% without opting out of the scheme.

## Who Can Use Section 44AD?

**Eligible**:
- Resident individual, HUF, or partnership firm (NOT LLP)
- Engaged in any business (not profession — that's Section 44ADA)
- Annual turnover ≤ **₹3 crore** (with the digital receipts condition below)

**Turnover limit (Budget 2023 change)**:
- ₹3 crore: If receipts from digital modes are ≥ 95% of total receipts in the current + preceding two FYs
- ₹2 crore: For businesses where cash receipts exceed 5%

**Not eligible**:
- Companies (Pvt Ltd, Ltd)
- LLPs (Limited Liability Partnerships)
- Businesses earning income taxable under other specific sections (shipping, mineral oil, civil construction — they have their own presumptive sections)
- Professionals (they use Section 44ADA)
- Businesses whose turnover exceeds ₹3 crore

## The 6% vs 8% Rule — Digital vs Cash

| Receipt Mode | Presumptive Rate |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Digital (bank, UPI, cheque, RTGS, NEFT, credit card) | **6% of turnover** |
| Cash | **8% of turnover** |

If your business has mixed receipts (both cash and digital), the rates apply proportionately:
- Cash receipts × 8% + Digital receipts × 6% = Minimum declared profit

**Budget 2017** introduced the 6% rate to encourage digital transactions. Before that, only 8% existed.

## Advance Tax Simplified for 44AD

Under regular taxation: 4 advance tax installments (June, September, December, March).

Under Section 44AD: **Single installment — 100% by March 15** of the financial year.

This is a significant cash flow benefit. Assess your year-end income and pay once in March.

## Books of Accounts — Not Required

Under 44AD, you're exempt from:
- Maintaining books of accounts under Section 44AA
- Tax audit under Section 44AB (below the turnover threshold)

**You still need to keep**:
- Basic sale/purchase records (in case of scrutiny)
- Bank statements
- GST returns (if GST registered)
- Invoices issued and received

## 5-Year Lock-In Condition — The Trap

**Critical**: If you opt for Section 44AD in a year but your declared profit is below 8%/6% in the previous year, or if you opt out — you must **wait 5 years** before opting into 44AD again.

More specifically:
- If you opt into 44AD and later opt out (to file with actual books and lower declared income): You cannot use 44AD for the **next 5 financial years**
- During that 5-year block: You must file regular ITR-3 with full books, and must get a tax audit if required

This is unlike Section 44ADA (for professionals) which allows annual opting in/out.

**Practical advice**: Don't opt into 44AD if you think you'll want to switch to actual books next year. The 5-year lock-out is punitive.

## When Section 44AD Is Beneficial

### Business with thin margins that don't have good books
Small traders who operate informally often can't reliably track exact expenses. 44AD is a safe harbor — pay tax on 6%/8% and avoid books + audit.

### Business with actual margin > 6%/8%
If your real profit is 15–20% of turnover, 44AD is NOT beneficial — you'd be overpaying tax (declaring 6% when actual is 15% would reduce your profit declaration, but 44AD minimum is 6%, so you declare 6%). Wait — if actual is 15%, declaring 44AD at 6% is LOWER tax. This is the benefit!

If actual profit = 15% = ₹3,00,000 (on ₹20L turnover), declaring 44AD means declaring only 6% = ₹1,20,000. Much lower tax.

**44AD is beneficial when actual margin is HIGHER than 6%/8%** — you declare less than actual and pay lower tax.

### Business with actual margin < 6%/8%
If actual profit is 2–3% of turnover (thin margin business like wholesale trading, FMCG distribution), 44AD forces you to declare 6–8% — paying MORE tax than actual. In this case, opt out and use actual books.

## Section 44AD vs 44ADA — Quick Comparison

| | Section 44AD | Section 44ADA |
|-|-------------|--------------|
| Who | Business owners | Professionals (doctors, CAs, etc.) |
| Turnover limit | ₹3 crore | ₹75 lakh |
| Presumptive rate | 6% or 8% of turnover | 50% of gross receipts |
| ITR form | ITR-4 | ITR-4 |
| Lock-in if opt out | 5 years | No lock-in |
| Advance tax | 100% by March 15 | 100% by March 15 |

## Worked Example

**Ramesh runs a hardware store (FY 2025-26, new regime)**:
- Turnover: ₹45,00,000
- Digital receipts: ₹40,00,000 (89%) — close to 95% threshold
- Cash receipts: ₹5,00,000 (11%)

Since digital receipts < 95%, full 8% rule applies:
- Declared income: 8% × ₹45,00,000 = ₹3,60,000

Tax (new regime):
- Taxable income: ₹3,60,000
- Tax: Nil (below ₹4L threshold)

**Actual business profit (if he tracked expenses)**: ₹7,20,000 (16% margin)
- Under actual: Tax on ₹7,20,000 = ₹1,12,000

**44AD saves ₹1,12,000 in tax** in this case (he pays tax on only ₹3.6L instead of ₹7.2L).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My retail shop turnover is ₹1.2 crore. Can I use 44AD?

Yes — ₹1.2 crore is well below the ₹2–3 crore threshold. Check what % of your receipts are digital vs cash. If ≥95% digital, you fall under 6% rate on ₹3 crore limit. If cash is >5%, the 8% rate applies on ₹2 crore limit (₹1.2 crore < ₹2 crore, so still eligible).

### I'm a small contractor (civil work). Is this business income or professional income?

Civil construction is NOT "professional income" for 44ADA purposes. It's business income — use Section 44AD (if turnover ≤ ₹2–3 crore). However, there's also Section 44AE (for transport businesses) and Section 44BB (oil sector) — contractors may have specific provisions. Most small civil contractors use 44AD.

### I opted for 44AD last year but want to file actual books this year (because I have losses). Can I?

If you opt out of 44AD (to show actual books with lower profit or losses), you trigger the **5-year lock-out** from 44AD. You can file actual books in the current year, but cannot use 44AD for the next 5 years. If the lock-out cost isn't worth showing losses, reconsider.
