# Section 80TTA and 80TTB India 2026 — Savings Interest Tax Deduction

Most people earn some interest on their savings bank account — and often forget it's taxable. But Sections 80TTA and 80TTB give a deduction to offset this tax, saving a useful amount each year.

## Section 80TTA — For Individuals Below 60

| | Details |
|-|---------|
| **Who can claim** | Individuals and HUFs below 60 years |
| **What is covered** | Interest from savings accounts ONLY (not FD, RD, or post office accounts) |
| **Maximum deduction** | **₹10,000 per year** |
| **Tax regime** | **Old regime only** (NOT available in new regime) |

**Savings accounts that qualify for 80TTA**:
- Savings bank account (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, etc.)
- Savings account at cooperative bank
- Post Office Savings Account
- RuPay-linked savings accounts

**Does NOT include**:
- Fixed deposits (FD)
- Recurring deposits (RD)
- Time deposits
- Company deposits
- Bonds / NCDs

### How 80TTA Works

You have ₹15,000 in savings account interest during FY 2025-26.
- First ₹10,000: Deductible under Section 80TTA
- Remaining ₹5,000: Taxable at your slab rate

**Tax saving** (₹10,000 at 30% slab): ₹3,000.

If you have ₹8,000 savings interest:
- Full ₹8,000 is deductible (below the ₹10,000 limit)
- Zero tax on savings account interest

## Section 80TTB — For Senior Citizens (60+)

| | Details |
|-|---------|
| **Who can claim** | Individuals aged 60 years or above |
| **What is covered** | Interest from ALL bank accounts (savings + FD + RD) AND cooperative banks AND post office deposits |
| **Maximum deduction** | **₹50,000 per year** |
| **Tax regime** | **Old regime only** |

**Why 80TTB is much more valuable**: Senior citizens often have large FD portfolios. FD interest at 7–9% on ₹10L corpus = ₹70,000–₹90,000 interest/year. Section 80TTB makes ₹50,000 of this tax-free.

**What 80TTB covers**:
- Savings account interest
- FD interest (all tenures)
- RD interest
- Post office savings account / NSC / FD interest (from post office)
- Cooperative bank interest

### How 80TTB Works (Senior Citizen Example)

Mrs. Sharma (62 years), FY 2025-26, old regime:
- Savings account interest: ₹8,000
- FD interest (SBI, 7%): ₹70,000
- Post Office NSC interest: ₹12,000
- Total interest income: ₹90,000

80TTB deduction: Maximum ₹50,000
- First ₹50,000 of interest: Tax-free (deducted)
- Remaining ₹40,000: Taxable at slab rate

Tax saving (at 10% slab for senior citizens with income below ₹5L): ₹5,000

## Section 80TTA vs 80TTB — Key Differences

| | 80TTA | 80TTB |
|-|-------|-------|
| Who | Below 60 | 60 years or above |
| FD interest covered | ❌ No | ✅ Yes |
| Savings interest covered | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes |
| Post office deposit interest | ✅ (savings only) | ✅ All deposits |
| Maximum deduction | ₹10,000 | ₹50,000 |
| New regime | ❌ Not available | ❌ Not available |

**Note**: Senior citizens use 80TTB (not 80TTA). They get the better deal — both cannot be claimed simultaneously.

## Important: TDS on FD for Senior Citizens

Banks deduct **10% TDS** on FD interest exceeding **₹50,000** per year (for senior citizens). For non-seniors, TDS threshold is ₹40,000.

If you've already had TDS deducted on FD interest:
- The TDS shows in Form 26AS
- You claim it as a credit when filing ITR
- If total tax after 80TTB deduction is lower than TDS deducted → refund

**Form 15H** (for senior citizens) or **Form 15G** (non-seniors below taxable limit): Submit to bank to avoid TDS if your total income is below the taxable limit. Even with 80TTB, if the TDS will be refunded anyway, submit 15H to avoid advance deduction.

## How to Claim 80TTA or 80TTB in ITR

**In ITR-1 or ITR-2**:
1. Go to "Schedule OS" (Income from Other Sources)
2. Enter savings account interest in "Income from savings account" row
3. Enter FD/other interest separately
4. In "Chapter VI-A Deductions" → Section 80TTA or 80TTB
5. Enter the eligible interest amount (up to the limit)

The portal automatically calculates and applies the deduction.

**Documents to keep**:
- Bank statement showing interest credited
- Form 16A from banks (TDS certificates for FD interest)
- Post office passbook/statement

## New Tax Regime — 80TTA and 80TTB Not Available

If you opt for the **new tax regime**, these deductions are not available. You pay tax on the entire interest income.

For most salaried employees in the new regime, this means their ₹8–12K in savings account interest is fully taxable.

**Comparison (30% bracket)**:
- Old regime + 80TTA: Save ₹3,000 on ₹10,000 savings interest
- New regime: Pay ₹3,000 tax on ₹10,000 interest

This is a minor factor in the old vs new regime decision — but for senior citizens with large FD portfolios, the ₹50,000 80TTB deduction under old regime can save ₹5,000–₹15,000, tipping the scales toward old regime.

## Post Office Savings Account — Special Rule Under 80TTA

For **Post Office savings accounts**, the first ₹3,500 of interest is already exempt from tax (Section 10(15)(i)). This is separate from 80TTA.

So effectively: Up to ₹3,500 of post office savings interest is tax-free under Section 10, and any additional savings interest (from banks + additional PO savings) up to ₹10,000 total is covered by 80TTA.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I'm 55 years old. My FD interest is ₹80,000. Can I claim 80TTA on FD interest?

No — at 55, you're below 60 years. Section 80TTA only covers savings account interest (not FD). Your FD interest of ₹80,000 is fully taxable at your slab rate. You can claim 80TTA deduction only on savings account interest (up to ₹10,000).

### My parent is 63 and earns ₹4.5L from pension + ₹60K FD interest. Which regime is better?

Under old regime:
- Basic exemption for senior citizens: ₹3,00,000
- Standard deduction: ₹50,000
- 80TTB: ₹50,000
- Taxable income: ₹4,50,000 + ₹60,000 − ₹3,00,000 − ₹50,000 − ₹50,000 = ₹1,10,000
- Tax: Nil (below ₹3L, after standard deduction and 80TTB = taxable ₹1.1L but basic exemption for senior citizens is ₹3L)

Actually: ₹5,10,000 gross income − ₹50,000 standard deduction = ₹4,60,000 − ₹50,000 (80TTB) = ₹4,10,000. Tax on ₹4,10,000 (old regime, senior citizen with ₹3L basic exemption): ₹10,000 (5% of ₹1,10,000). With 87A rebate (income ≤₹5L): rebate covers ₹12,500 → Zero tax.

Under new regime: ₹4,60,000 taxable (after ₹75K standard deduction), 10% on ₹4,60,000 − ₹4,00,000 = 10% of ₹60,000 = ₹6,000. No 87A rebate (income > ₹7L... wait, ₹4.6L < ₹7L, so 87A applies → Zero tax).

Both give zero tax in this case. Old regime is marginally better if there are other deductions. Ask a CA for exact calculation.
