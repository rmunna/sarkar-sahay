## What is the CAT Exam?

The Common Admission Test (CAT) is India's premier MBA entrance examination conducted annually by one of the IIMs (Indian Institutes of Management) on a rotational basis. CAT scores are accepted by all 21 IIMs and over 1,200 other B-schools across India for admission to MBA and PGDM programs.

CAT is a computer-based test that evaluates candidates on verbal ability, quantitative aptitude, and data interpretation & logical reasoning. Over 2.5 lakh candidates appear for CAT each year, making it one of the most competitive exams in India.

## Who is Eligible?

### Educational Qualification
- Bachelor's degree with at least **50% marks** (45% for SC/ST/PwD candidates) from a recognized university
- Final-year graduation students can also apply (provisional admission)
- Any discipline — engineering, commerce, arts, science, etc.

### Age Limit
- **No upper age limit** for appearing in CAT

### Work Experience
- Not mandatory but preferred by top IIMs during the selection process

## CAT Exam Pattern

The CAT exam has three sections:

| Section | Questions | Time |
|---------|-----------|------|
| Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension (VARC) | 24 | 40 minutes |
| Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning (DILR) | 20 | 40 minutes |
| Quantitative Ability (QA) | 22 | 40 minutes |
| **Total** | **66** | **120 minutes** |

### Marking Scheme
- **+3 marks** for each correct answer
- **-1 mark** for each wrong answer (MCQs only)
- **No negative marking** for non-MCQ (TITA) questions

## CAT Syllabus

### Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension (VARC)
- Reading Comprehension passages (5-6 passages)
- Para Jumbles and Para Summary
- Sentence completion and correction
- Odd sentence out
- Critical reasoning

### Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning (DILR)
- Tables, bar graphs, pie charts, line graphs
- Caselets and data sets
- Seating arrangements and puzzles
- Blood relations and syllogisms
- Binary logic and games

### Quantitative Ability (QA)
- Arithmetic — percentages, profit & loss, time & work, mixtures
- Algebra — linear and quadratic equations, inequalities
- Geometry — triangles, circles, coordinate geometry
- Number system — divisibility, HCF/LCM, remainders
- Modern math — permutations, combinations, probability

## Step-by-Step Preparation Strategy

### Step 1: Understand the Exam
- Visit the official CAT website at [iimcat.ac.in](https://iimcat.ac.in)
- Study the latest exam pattern and syllabus thoroughly
- Analyze previous years' cut-offs for your target IIMs

### Step 2: Build a Study Plan
- Allocate **4-6 months** for focused preparation
- Dedicate time to each section based on your strengths and weaknesses
- Study at least **3-4 hours daily** consistently

### Step 3: Strengthen Fundamentals
- Cover basic concepts in math, grammar, and logical reasoning
- Use standard textbooks: Arun Sharma, Nishit Sinha, or Sarvesh Kumar Verma
- Focus on NCERT math books (Class 8-10) for fundamental concepts

### Step 4: Practice Regularly
- Solve **50-100 questions daily** across all three sections
- Practice reading comprehension by reading newspapers and editorials
- Work on mental math and calculation speed

### Step 5: Take Mock Tests
- Start taking full-length mocks **3 months before the exam**
- Take at least **30-40 mock tests** before the actual exam
- Analyze each mock thoroughly — review every wrong and unattempted question

### Step 6: Revise and Fine-tune
- Create an error log and review it weekly
- Focus on weak areas but don't neglect strong areas
- In the last month, take 2-3 mocks per week

## Application Process

### How to Register for CAT
1. Visit [iimcat.ac.in](https://iimcat.ac.in) when registration opens (usually August)
2. Click on **"New Registration"** and create an account
3. Fill in personal, academic, and work experience details
4. Upload photograph, signature, and category certificate (if applicable)
5. Select test cities (up to 6 preferences)
6. Pay the application fee online
7. Download and save the confirmation page

## Important Tips

- **Reading habit is key** — read newspapers, magazines, and novels daily to boost VARC
- **Time management matters** — practice completing sections within the allotted time
- **Don't skip DILR** — most students find it unpredictable; consistent practice helps
- **Mock analysis > mock count** — thoroughly analyzing 20 mocks is better than blindly taking 50
- **Stay healthy** — maintain a routine with exercise and adequate sleep during preparation

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is CAT only for engineers?
No, CAT is open to graduates from any discipline — arts, commerce, science, law, medicine, etc. IIMs value diversity and admit students from all academic backgrounds.

### How many times can I attempt CAT?
There is no limit on the number of attempts. You can take CAT every year until you get your desired score.

### Is coaching necessary for CAT preparation?
No, many candidates crack CAT through self-study. However, coaching can provide structure and peer learning. Online courses are a good middle ground.

### What is a good CAT score for top IIMs?
A percentile of **99+** is generally needed for IIM-A, B, C. For other top IIMs, **95-97+** percentile is competitive. For newer IIMs, **85-90+** percentile may suffice.

### Can working professionals prepare for CAT?
Yes, many working professionals clear CAT. The key is consistent daily preparation of 2-3 hours and effective use of weekends for mock tests.

### What happens after clearing CAT?
After CAT, IIMs shortlist candidates for WAT (Written Ability Test) and PI (Personal Interview) based on CAT score, academics, work experience, and diversity factors.
