# ₹12 Lakh Income = Zero Tax in 2025-26? Budget 2025 Tax Changes Explained

Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman's **Union Budget 2025-26** (February 2025) contained one of the biggest income tax changes in years: **if your total income is up to ₹12 lakh, you pay ZERO income tax** under the new tax regime.

This doesn't mean income up to ₹12L is exempt from tax — it means a **Section 87A tax rebate** makes the tax liability zero. Here's exactly who benefits and who doesn't.

## The Quick Answer: Are You Paying Zero Tax?

| Your Income | Tax Under New Regime | After 87A Rebate |
|-------------|---------------------|------------------|
| Up to ₹7 lakh | Calculated per slab | **₹0** (already zero before Budget 2025) |
| ₹7.01–₹12 lakh | Calculated per slab (₹20,000–₹80,000) | **₹0** (NEW in Budget 2025) |
| ₹12.01 lakh and above | Tax on full amount — rebate NOT available | **Pay full tax per slab** |

**For salaried employees**: Standard deduction of ₹75,000 means your **gross salary** can be up to **₹12.75 lakh** with zero tax.

## New Income Tax Slabs 2025-26 (New Regime)

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|-------------|---------|
| Up to ₹4 lakh | 0% |
| ₹4 lakh–₹8 lakh | 5% |
| ₹8 lakh–₹12 lakh | 10% |
| ₹12 lakh–₹16 lakh | 15% |
| ₹16 lakh–₹20 lakh | 20% |
| ₹20 lakh–₹24 lakh | 25% |
| Above ₹24 lakh | 30% |

**Plus**: 4% Health & Education Cess on tax payable.

## How Section 87A Rebate Works

**Without Section 87A** (hypothetical):
- Income: ₹12 lakh
- Tax on ₹12L (per slabs): ₹0 + ₹20,000 + ₹40,000 = **₹60,000**
- Cess: ₹60,000 × 4% = ₹2,400
- **Total payable**: ₹62,400

**With Section 87A Rebate** (Budget 2025):
- Same ₹12L income
- Tax calculated: ₹60,000
- Section 87A rebate: **−₹60,000** (rebate equals tax, so tax becomes zero)
- **Total payable**: **₹0**

The rebate amount equals the tax computed (or ₹60,000, whichever is lower) — making effective tax zero for all incomes up to ₹12L.

## Salaried Employees — ₹12.75 Lakh Zero Tax

If you're a salaried employee, your employer deducts **Standard Deduction** of ₹75,000:
- Gross Salary: ₹12,75,000
- Minus Standard Deduction: −₹75,000
- **Net Income for Tax**: ₹12,00,000
- Tax after 87A rebate: **₹0**

So a salaried person earning **up to ₹12.75 lakh gross salary** effectively pays zero tax.

## Critical Exception: Capital Gains Income

**This is where many people make mistakes:**

The Section 87A rebate **does NOT apply** if your income includes **special rate income**:
- **Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG)** under Section 111A (equity mutual funds/stocks): taxed at 20%
- **Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)** under Section 112A (equity): taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25L
- **Other Capital Gains**: Various rates

**Example**:
- Salary: ₹8 lakh → Rebate applies, zero tax on salary
- STCG from stocks: ₹5 lakh → Taxed at 20% = ₹1 lakh tax
- Total income: ₹13 lakh but capital gains component taxes applies separately

If your income includes capital gains, don't assume zero tax — calculate separately.

## Business/Freelance Income: Does the Rebate Apply?

**Yes, the Section 87A rebate applies to business income too**, as long as total income (including business income) is ≤ ₹12 lakh. Example:
- Freelance income: ₹10 lakh
- After presumptive taxation (44AD/44ADA): taxable income may be lower
- If total taxable income ≤ ₹12L, 87A rebate applies → Zero tax

**Caveat**: If presumptive scheme income is above certain thresholds, consult a CA.

## New Regime vs Old Regime — Which is Better?

Under **Old Tax Regime** (can still choose):
- Tax on ₹12L income with no deductions: ₹1,17,000 (approx)
- With 80C (₹1.5L), HRA, and other deductions: Can reduce significantly
- BUT: No such zero-tax option for ₹12L income under old regime

Under **New Tax Regime** (default from FY2024-25):
- Zero tax up to ₹12L
- No need to invest for deductions
- Simpler filing

**Old regime beats new** only if your deductions (80C + HRA + home loan + NPS + etc.) exceed a threshold. For most people earning ₹12L with few deductions, new regime with zero tax is better.

## ITR Filing — Still Required Even at Zero Tax?

**Yes, you must file ITR even if tax is zero**, if:
- Your income exceeds the basic exemption limit (₹2.5L for old regime; ₹3L for new regime)
- You have foreign income or assets
- You want to carry forward losses
- Your company deducted TDS

Filing with zero tax payable still protects your compliance record and helps with loan applications, visa applications, etc.

## Marginal Relief for Income Just Above ₹12L

If your income is just over ₹12L (say ₹12.1 lakh), you lose the entire 87A rebate and suddenly owe ₹65,000+ in tax. This is why **marginal relief** is important:

**Example of cliff effect (WITHOUT marginal relief)**:
- ₹12L income: ₹0 tax (rebate applies)
- ₹12.1L income: ~₹65,000 tax (rebate doesn't apply, sudden jump)

Budget 2025 introduced **marginal relief** to prevent this cliff: if your income is slightly above ₹12L, the tax payable cannot exceed the income above ₹12L. So the effective jump is gradual, not a cliff.

## Summary: 5 Key Facts

1. **₹12L income under new regime = Zero tax** (via Section 87A rebate)
2. **₹12.75L gross salary for salaried = Zero tax** (after ₹75K standard deduction)
3. **Above ₹12L income = Tax on the entire amount** (not just the excess above ₹12L)
4. **Capital gains income excluded** from this rebate — tax on it separately
5. **Old tax regime unchanged** — you can still choose it if it's better for your situation

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is ₹12 lakh income completely exempt from tax, or just rebated?

It's **rebated**, not exempt. The tax slabs still apply and tax is computed. Then a Section 87A rebate cancels that tax, making the net payable zero. Practically the result is the same — you pay nothing — but the mechanism matters for capital gains and other special income.

### I earn ₹14 lakh. How much tax do I pay?

Under new regime, your tax on ₹14L:
- 0%: First ₹4L = ₹0
- 5%: ₹4–8L = ₹20,000
- 10%: ₹8–12L = ₹40,000
- 15%: ₹12–14L = ₹30,000
- Total tax: ₹90,000
- 87A rebate: NOT applicable (income > ₹12L)
- Cess (4%): ₹3,600
- **Total payable: ₹93,600**

### I have ₹10L salary + ₹3L LTCG from mutual funds. Is my total income ₹13L?

Yes, total income is ₹13L. Tax:
- Salary (₹10L): Tax per slab = ₹60,000. With 87A on salary portion: ₹0? But because your total income exceeds ₹12L, 87A rebate does NOT apply. Tax on ₹10L salary: ₹60,000 taxable.
- LTCG (₹3L): First ₹1.25L free, remaining ₹1.75L × 12.5% = ₹21,875
- Total tax: ~₹81,875 + cess

> This is a complex area — consult a CA for mixed income types.

### Does the zero-tax benefit apply for AY 2025-26 or AY 2026-27?

Budget 2025 changes apply from **FY 2025-26 (April 2025 – March 2026)**, so you'll see the benefit when you file your return for **AY 2026-27** (i.e., July 2026 onwards). For FY 2024-25 (AY 2025-26), the old ₹7L limit applied.
