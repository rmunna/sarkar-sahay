# Telecom Complaint: How to File on TRAI Portal Against Any Operator

If your telecom operator (Jio, Airtel, Vi, BSNL) is not resolving your issue — wrong billing, poor network, failed port, spam, or service denial — you have a structured complaint and escalation process. This guide covers every step from operator-level complaint to TDSAT (the telecom tribunal).

## Common Telecom Complaints

- Overbilling or wrong charges
- Poor network quality or call drops
- Slow internet speed (below promised plan)
- Mobile Number Portability (MNP) issues
- Unwanted VAS (Value Added Services) activation
- Spam calls/SMS despite DND (see our [TRAI DND guide](/guide/trai-dnd-activate-spam-block))
- SIM blocking or service disconnection without notice
- Refund not processed

## Step-by-Step Complaint Escalation

### Level 1: Operator Customer Care

**Always start here.** TRAI/DoT will reject complaints if you haven't first approached your operator.

1. Call your operator's customer care:
   - **Jio:** 199 or 1800-889-9999
   - **Airtel:** 121 or 1800-103-0121
   - **Vi:** 199 or 1800-266-6666
   - **BSNL:** 1503 or 1800-345-1503
2. Clearly state your complaint
3. **Note the complaint/ticket number** — this is critical for escalation
4. Ask for the expected resolution timeline
5. Wait **7 days** for resolution

### Level 2: Nodal Officer (Appellate Level 1)

If the customer care does not resolve your issue within 7 days:

1. Find your operator's **Nodal Officer** details:
   - Check your operator's website under "Grievance Redressal" or "Contact Us"
   - It is also printed on your monthly bill
2. Send a **written complaint via email** to the Nodal Officer
3. Include:
   - Your mobile number and account details
   - Previous complaint ticket number
   - Description of the issue
   - What resolution you expect
4. The Nodal Officer must respond within **3 days**
5. Resolution must happen within **10 days** of nodal officer complaint

### Level 3: Appellate Authority

If the Nodal Officer does not resolve your issue:

1. Escalate to the operator's **Appellate Authority**
2. Find details on the operator's website under "Appellate Authority" or "Grievance Redressal"
3. Submit a written appeal with:
   - All previous complaint reference numbers
   - Nodal Officer response (or lack thereof)
   - Supporting evidence (bills, screenshots, speed tests)
4. The Appellate Authority must dispose the appeal within **30 days**

### Level 4: DoT PG Portal (Government Grievance)

If the operator's internal escalation fails:

1. Visit [PG Portal (CPGRAMS)](https://pgportal.gov.in/)
2. Register with your name, email, and phone number
3. Click **Lodge Grievance**
4. Select **Ministry/Department:** Department of Telecommunications
5. Fill in:
   - **Grievance description:** detailed complaint with dates, ticket numbers
   - **Operator name and your mobile number**
   - Upload supporting documents (bills, screenshots)
6. Submit and note the **registration number**
7. You can track the status on the same portal
8. DoT forwards the complaint to the operator and monitors resolution
9. Expected resolution: **30-60 days**

### Level 5: TDSAT (Telecom Disputes Settlement & Appellate Tribunal)

If all previous levels fail, approach TDSAT — the judicial body for telecom disputes:

1. Visit [TDSAT website](https://tdsat.gov.in/)
2. TDSAT handles disputes where the claim amount exceeds certain thresholds or involves policy violations
3. **Filing process:**
   - File a petition online or in person at TDSAT, New Delhi
   - Pay the prescribed court fee
   - Include all previous complaint history and evidence
4. TDSAT can order:
   - Refunds and compensation
   - Directions to the operator
   - Penalties for non-compliance
5. TDSAT decisions are binding on operators

## What Evidence to Collect

- **Complaint ticket numbers** from every level
- **Screenshots** of billing errors, speed tests, or service issues
- **Call recordings** with customer care (where legally permitted)
- **Speed test results** from TRAI MySpeed app (see our [speed test guide](/guide/internet-speed-test-fix))
- **SMS confirmations** from the operator
- **Email correspondence** with nodal officer/appellate authority

## Important Tips

1. **Always start with Level 1** — regulators reject complaints if you skip operator-level grievance
2. **Document everything** — save ticket numbers, emails, and screenshots at every stage
3. **Be specific in complaints** — mention dates, amounts, ticket numbers, and what resolution you expect
4. **Use TRAI MySpeed for speed complaints** — official app data carries more weight as evidence
5. **PG Portal is powerful** — DoT actively monitors complaints filed here and operators take them seriously

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can I file a TRAI complaint directly without contacting the operator?

No. You must first file a complaint with your operator and wait 7 days. TRAI/DoT requires proof that you attempted resolution with the operator first.

### 2. Is there any fee for filing a telecom complaint?

Complaints at the operator, nodal officer, appellate authority, and PG Portal levels are free. TDSAT may require a court fee depending on the nature of the dispute.

### 3. Can I file a complaint for spam calls?

Yes. Activate DND first (see our [TRAI DND guide](/guide/trai-dnd-activate-spam-block)), then report spam via the TRAI DND app or NCCP portal. For operator-level spam complaints, follow the escalation above.

### 4. What if my operator blocks my SIM without notice?

This violates TRAI regulations. File a complaint at Level 1 and escalate immediately if not resolved. You can also approach the PG Portal directly for urgent service restoration.

### 5. How do I find my operator's Nodal Officer details?

Check your operator's website under "Grievance Redressal" or "Contact Us." It is also printed on your monthly bill or available on the TRAI website.

### 6. Can TDSAT order compensation?

Yes. TDSAT can order operators to pay refunds, compensation for damages, and penalties for regulatory violations.

### 7. Is the PG Portal complaint anonymous?

No. You need to register with your real name, email, and phone number. However, your complaint details are shared only with the relevant department and operator.
