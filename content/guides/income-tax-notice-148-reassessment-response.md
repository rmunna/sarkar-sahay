# Income Tax Reassessment Notice — Section 148 and 148A Explained

If you receive an email with subject **"Notice under Section 148/148A(b) of the Income Tax Act"**, the Income Tax Department is attempting to **reopen a previous year's assessment** because they believe income escaped taxation. This is more serious than a 143(2) scrutiny notice and requires careful attention.

> **Amended procedure (Finance Act 2021)**: Since April 2021, the IT Department must go through a mandatory 148A show-cause process before issuing the final 148 notice. You get an opportunity to respond before any reassessment begins.

## What Does a 148/148A Notice Mean?

| Step | What Happens |
|------|-------------|
| **148A(a)** | IT conducts an inquiry and reviews material suggesting escaped income |
| **148A(b)** | Show-cause notice issued to you — you respond within 30 days |
| **148A(c)** | AO reviews your response |
| **148A(d)** | AO passes an order: either drops the case OR proceeds to reassessment |
| **Section 148** | If AO decides to proceed: formal reassessment notice issued |
| **Assessment** | Similar to 143(3) scrutiny — AO examines the past year's return |

## Why Is Your Return Being Reopened?

Common reasons for Section 148 notices:

| Reason | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **High-value transaction unreported** | Property sale, large cash deposit not declared in ITR |
| **Third-party information** | SFT (Statement of Financial Transaction) data from banks, registrar |
| **Foreign assets** | Overseas investments not reported in Schedule FA |
| **Crypto unreported** | Large crypto transactions flagged by exchanges |
| **Cash business** | Cash purchases or sales out of proportion with declared income |
| **Bogus deductions** | Fake 80C investments or HRA claims flagged |
| **Search/survey findings** | IT raid at your premises or associates |

## Time Limit for Reassessment

| Period | Time Limit |
|--------|-----------|
| 3 years from end of Assessment Year | Standard time limit for reopening |
| 5 years from end of AY (if escaped income > ₹50 lakh) | Extended limit for large amounts |
| 10 years from end of AY (for assets abroad or serious cases) | For foreign assets, undisclosed abroad income |

**Example**: For FY 2021-22 (AY 2022-23), the 3-year standard limit expires March 31, 2026. The department can issue 148A notices up to that date.

## How to Respond to Section 148A(b) Notice

### Step 1: Read the Notice Carefully

The notice explains:
- Which Assessment Year is being reopened
- What specific income/transaction is alleged to have escaped taxation
- The "reasons to believe" (RTB) — the specific evidence they have

### Step 2: Respond Online Within 30 Days

1. Login to **[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/)**
2. Go to **"Pending Actions"** → **"e-Proceedings"**
3. Find your 148A(b) notice
4. Click → **"Submit Response"**
5. Provide your response explaining:
   - Whether the income/transaction was disclosed in your ITR
   - If not, explain why it's not taxable or why it was omitted
   - Attach supporting documents (bank statements, ITR copies, investment proofs)

### What to Write in Your Response

**If the income WAS disclosed**:
- State: "The income of ₹X was disclosed in the ITR filed for AY Y"
- Attach a copy of the relevant ITR and the schedule where it was shown
- Reference Form 26AS / AIS showing the transaction

**If the income was NOT disclosed but has an explanation**:
- State why it's not taxable (e.g., gift from relative, loan repayment, insurance proceeds, inherited money)
- Attach supporting documents (gift deed, loan agreement, insurance policy)

**If you missed reporting the income**:
- Consult a CA immediately
- File a revised ITR if the AY is still open for revision
- Offer to pay the tax + interest voluntarily — this often reduces penalty exposure

## After Your Response — Two Outcomes

### Outcome 1: AO Drops the Case (148A(d) Order — No Reassessment)

If your explanation satisfies the AO, they pass an order under 148A(d) dropping the proceedings. No further action needed.

**If you disagree** with even a dropped case (e.g., the order contains adverse observations), you can appeal within 30 days to CIT(A).

### Outcome 2: AO Proceeds to Reassessment (Section 148 Notice)

If AO is not satisfied, they issue the formal Section 148 notice. Now the full reassessment process begins:
- AO examines the specific Assessment Year in detail
- You provide more documents
- AO passes assessment order under 147 read with 143(3)
- If demand raised: you receive Section 156 demand notice

**To challenge the 148A(d) order** (if you believe reopening is unjustified):
1. Appeal to CIT(A) within 30 days
2. OR file a Writ Petition in High Court challenging the validity of the notice (faster but costly)

## Can You File Revised ITR in Response to 148?

If the 148/148A notice is issued before your **Revised Return deadline** (December 31 of Assessment Year), you can file a revised return correcting the omitted income. This is often the cleanest resolution — it avoids scrutiny, reduces penalty risk, and shows good faith.

If beyond the revised return deadline, you can file a **return in response to notice under Section 148** (special filing option available on the IT portal even after normal deadlines).

## Difference: 143(2) vs 148 Notices

| Aspect | 143(2) Scrutiny | 148 Reassessment |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Timing** | Within 3 months of AY end | Up to 3–10 years later |
| **Scope** | Current year's ITR | Past year's already-filed/processed ITR |
| **Trigger** | CBDT risk parameters | Specific evidence of escaped income |
| **Pre-notice requirement** | None | 148A(b) show-cause mandatory |
| **Severity** | Medium | High |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I got a 148 notice for AY 2019-20 (FY 2018-19). Is that within the time limit?

The 3-year limit from end of AY 2019-20 is March 31, 2023. After that, reopening is only allowed if escaped income > ₹50 lakh (5-year limit = March 31, 2025) or for foreign assets (10 years). If the notice came after March 31, 2023 for amounts < ₹50 lakh, it may be time-barred — challenge it immediately.

### I never received the 148A(b) show-cause but directly got a 148 assessment notice. Is that valid?

No — the Finance Act 2021 made 148A mandatory. If you received a 148 assessment notice without a prior 148A(b), the notice is procedurally invalid. File a writ petition in High Court immediately. Many such cases have been quashed.

### I already paid the tax they're asking for in a previous year. Why am I getting this notice?

Check if the specific transaction flagged was actually reported. If paid as self-assessment, it should reflect in Form 26AS. Bring proof of payment to your 148A response. The AO should drop the case if you can show the tax was paid.
