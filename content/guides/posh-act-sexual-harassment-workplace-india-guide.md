# POSH Act India 2026 — Your Rights Against Sexual Harassment at Workplace

Sexual harassment at the workplace is a violation of your fundamental rights under Articles 14, 15, and 21 of the Indian Constitution. The POSH Act (Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition, and Redressal) Act, 2013) gives you a clear mechanism to seek justice — without going to court first.

---

## What Counts as Sexual Harassment Under POSH?

POSH Act defines sexual harassment broadly. It includes **unwelcome acts or behaviour** (whether directly or by implication):

| Type | Examples |
|------|---------|
| **Physical contact** | Unwanted touching, groping |
| **Sexual demands or requests** | Demanding sexual favours in exchange for promotion, project, positive review |
| **Sexually colored remarks** | Remarks about body, sexual jokes, innuendo |
| **Showing pornography** | Displaying sexual material on screen/phone without consent |
| **Any other unwelcome conduct** | Staring, following, inappropriate messages, repeated date/relationship requests after clear refusal |

**Both in-person and online**: Harassment via email, WhatsApp, social media in a work context falls under POSH.

**Who is covered**:
- All women employees: regular, part-time, temporary, contractual, interns, trainees
- At office premises, during work travel, office events, client meetings, online work interactions

**Note**: POSH currently protects only women as victims. Men experiencing harassment may use other legal provisions (IPC provisions on outraging modesty, etc.).

---

## Employer's Legal Obligations Under POSH

**Every establishment with 10+ employees MUST have**:
1. **Internal Complaints Committee (ICC)**: Chaired by a senior woman employee; at least half members must be women; one external member (from NGO/legal profession experienced in gender issues)
2. **Written anti-harassment policy**: Displayed and communicated to all employees
3. **Regular awareness programs**: Conduct workshops, training
4. **Annual report to District Officer**: Summary of ICC complaints received and resolved

**Failure to comply**: Fine up to ₹50,000 for first offense; ₹1 lakh + license cancellation for repeat offense.

**Small organizations (<10 employees) and individual households (domestic workers)**: Local Complaints Committee (LCC) at district level — contact District Collector's office.

## How to File a POSH Complaint

### Option 1: Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) — Fastest Route

**Deadline**: Within **3 months** of the incident (or 3 months from last incident in ongoing harassment). ICC can extend by 3 more months if reasonable cause shown.

**Process**:
1. File written complaint to the Presiding Officer of ICC (name should be in company policy/notice board)
2. ICC acknowledges within 7 days
3. Option to attempt **Conciliation** (mediation between parties) — your choice, not mandatory
4. If no conciliation or fails: **Inquiry proceeds**
5. ICC completes inquiry within **90 days**
6. ICC submits report to employer within 10 days of completing inquiry
7. Employer acts on ICC recommendations within **60 days**

**What ICC can recommend**:
- Written apology
- Warning, reprimand
- Withhold promotion/increment of accused
- Transfer accused or complainant (with complainant's consent for self-transfer)
- Termination of accused
- Compensation to complainant

### Option 2: SHe-Box Portal (Online, For Government and Private Employees)

**SHe-Box** = Sexual Harassment Electronic Box
- Portal: [shebox.nic.in](https://shebox.nic.in)
- For: Both government employees and private sector employees
- File complaint online; forwarded to relevant ICC/LCC/authority
- Track status online with complaint reference number

**Steps**:
1. Visit shebox.nic.in → Register → File Complaint
2. Select: Central Government / State Government / Private / Domestic Worker
3. Fill incident details, accused details, your details
4. Complaint routed to the appropriate ICC/LCC

### Option 3: Local Complaints Committee (LCC)

For unorganized sector, small establishments, or if ICC is not functioning:
- Contact District Collector's office
- LCC has same powers as ICC
- Same 3-month filing deadline

### Option 4: Police Complaint

If the harassment is severe (assault, criminal intimidation, stalking):
- File FIR at nearest police station under IPC Sections 354 (outraging modesty), 354A (sexual harassment), 509 (words/gesture to insult modesty), 506 (criminal intimidation), 376 (if sexual assault)
- You can pursue both POSH complaint AND police FIR simultaneously

## Protections for Complainant

**Anti-retaliation protection**: Employer CANNOT:
- Terminate, demote, or transfer complainant for filing complaint
- Reduce salary, benefits, or work assignments
- Create a hostile work environment after complaint is filed

**Identity protection**: Complaint contents, identity of complainant, respondent, and witnesses cannot be made public or disclosed to press (penalty: ₹5,000 fine).

**Leave provision**: Employer may grant additional paid leave (up to 3 months) to complainant during inquiry if mental health or safety requires.

---

## Interim Relief — While Inquiry is Pending

During the inquiry, complainant can request ICC for interim measures:
- Transfer of respondent (or yourself) to a different department/location
- Grant temporary leave with pay
- Restrict respondent from interacting with complainant

ICC can recommend these to employer before inquiry concludes.

---

## If ICC Is Not Functioning or Complaint Is Dismissed Unfairly

1. **Appeal to court**: If dissatisfied with ICC inquiry, you can file appeal in civil court within 90 days of receiving ICC order.

2. **Labour Commissioner**: If employer doesn't act on ICC report within 60 days, file complaint.

3. **NHRC / State Human Rights Commission**: For serious harassment where employer/ICC is complicit.

4. **National Commission for Women (NCW)**: [ncw.nic.in](https://ncw.nic.in) → Online Complaint Portal. NCW can investigate and recommend action.

---

## What If I'm Afraid to File — Anonymous Complaint

Anonymous complaints are valid under POSH. However:
- ICC may have difficulty investigating without your cooperation
- Conciliation is not possible anonymously
- SHe-Box allows anonymous complaints but inquiry effectiveness is limited

**Confidential (not anonymous)**: You can file with your name but request strict confidentiality. ICC must maintain it. This is the recommended approach — ICC has information to investigate but your identity is protected from public disclosure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### The harasser is my boss / senior management. Is it safe to file?

Yes — ICC is specifically designed to handle situations where the harasser is in a position of power. ICC must be constituted to function independently of management. Anti-retaliation provisions protect you. If internal ICC is compromised, use SHe-Box or LCC directly.

### Can I file after 3 months?

ICC can extend by another 3 months if you can show "sufficient cause" (fear of retaliation, mental health issues, lack of awareness of rights). If you've missed the 6-month window entirely, you may still approach police or civil court under Indian Penal Code provisions.

### Harassment happened online during WFH. Is it still covered?

Yes — digital harassment in a work context (work email, company Teams/Slack, WhatsApp for work, video calls for work) is covered under POSH even if both parties are working from home.

### My company has less than 10 employees and no ICC. What do I do?

Go directly to the Local Complaints Committee at your District Collector's office. LCC handles complaints from establishments without ICC and unorganized sector workers.
