# NRI Income Tax India 2026 — Residential Status, NRI vs RNOR vs Resident, and Returning NRI Guide

India taxes your income based on your **residential status** — not your citizenship. Whether you are an NRI, returning NRI, or a resident who lived abroad briefly, your tax liability in India is entirely determined by how many days you spent in India during the financial year and the preceding years.

This guide explains the three statuses (Resident, RNOR, NRI), what income is taxable under each, and what returning NRIs need to know about the valuable RNOR transition period.

## The Three Residential Statuses

Under Section 6 of the Income Tax Act, every individual in India has one of three statuses in any given financial year:

| Status | What It Means |
|--------|--------------|
| **Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR)** | You are fully resident — global income taxable in India |
| **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)** | You are a resident, but foreign income is not taxable |
| **Non-Resident Indian (NRI)** | Only India-sourced income is taxable in India |

## Step 1: Are You a Resident or NRI?

**Primary test — days in India in the current year**:

You are **Resident** if you satisfy EITHER of these:
- **182 days or more** in India during the financial year (April–March)
- **60 days or more** in India + **365 days or more** in the previous 4 financial years combined

You are **NRI** if you don't satisfy either condition.

**Special rule for returning Indians**: If you have been an NRI in 9 out of 10 preceding years, or in India for ≤ 729 days in the preceding 7 years, even if you become "Resident" by the day count, you may qualify as **RNOR** (see below).

**Exception for Indian citizens working abroad**: If you are an Indian citizen OR Person of Indian Origin (PIO), the "60 days" threshold in the second test changes to **182 days** (not 60 days). This means an Indian working abroad who visits India for up to 181 days per year stays an NRI. The 60-day rule only applies to foreign citizens visiting India.

**Special rule (Finance Act 2020)**: Indian citizens earning ≥ ₹15 lakh from Indian sources but not taxed in any other country → deemed resident even if they don't spend 182 days in India.

## Step 2: If Resident — Are You ROR or RNOR?

If you qualified as "Resident" in Step 1, now determine if you're **Ordinarily Resident (ROR)** or **Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)**.

You are **RNOR** if you satisfy EITHER condition:
- You were an NRI in **9 out of 10** preceding financial years, OR
- You were in India for **729 days or fewer** in total during the **7 immediately preceding financial years**

If you don't satisfy either RNOR condition → you are **ROR** (full resident).

## What Income Is Taxable — By Status

| Income Type | NRI | RNOR | ROR (Resident) |
|-------------|-----|------|---------------|
| Salary earned in India | ✅ Taxable | ✅ Taxable | ✅ Taxable |
| Salary earned outside India (for services abroad) | ❌ Not taxable | ❌ Not taxable | ✅ Taxable |
| Interest on NRE account | ❌ Not taxable | ❌ Not taxable | ✅ Taxable |
| Interest on NRO account | ✅ Taxable | ✅ Taxable | ✅ Taxable |
| Rental income from India property | ✅ Taxable | ✅ Taxable | ✅ Taxable |
| Capital gains on Indian shares/MF | ✅ Taxable | ✅ Taxable | ✅ Taxable |
| Foreign income (abroad salary, foreign FD, foreign shares) | ❌ Not taxable | ❌ Not taxable | ✅ Taxable |
| Business income from India | ✅ Taxable | ✅ Taxable | ✅ Taxable |
| Business income controlled from India but operated abroad | ❌ Not taxable | ✅ Taxable | ✅ Taxable |

## The RNOR Advantage for Returning NRIs

**RNOR is the most valuable status** for Indians returning from abroad. Here's why:

When you return to India after living abroad for years, you typically have:
- Foreign savings / FDs still earning interest
- Foreign investments (stocks, mutual funds) still generating dividends/capital gains
- Maybe a part-year foreign salary

Under RNOR status:
- All that **foreign income remains tax-free in India**
- Only Indian-sourced income is taxed
- This continues for **2–3 years** typically after your return (depending on when you were last an NRI)

**Practical example**:
- Rakesh worked in the US for 12 years, returned to India in FY 2025-26
- He spent 200 days in India in FY 2025-26 → technically "Resident" (crossed 182 days)
- But he was NRI for 9 of the last 10 years → qualifies as RNOR
- His US salary for the months he worked in the US: ❌ Not taxable in India
- His US bank FD interest: ❌ Not taxable in India
- His India rental income: ✅ Taxable in India
- His Indian salary (if he got a job in India): ✅ Taxable

Without RNOR, all of the US income in the return year would be taxable in India.

## How Long Does RNOR Last?

RNOR is recalculated every financial year. You must re-check each year whether you still qualify.

**Typical scenario**:
- Year 0: Last year abroad (NRI)
- Year 1: Return to India. 200+ days in India → "Resident". But 9/10 years as NRI → RNOR. Foreign income exempt.
- Year 2: Still resident. 8/10 years as NRI (the year you returned now counts as "resident") → Still RNOR (the 9/10 test or 729-day test may still pass).
- Year 3: Depends on how many of the 10 preceding years you were NRI. May flip to ROR.

**Most returning NRIs get 2–3 years of RNOR status** before becoming fully taxable residents.

## What NRIs Must Declare in Indian ITR

NRIs must file ITR in India if any of the following apply:
- Indian income (rent, capital gains, business income) > basic exemption limit (₹2.5L under old regime, ₹3L under new regime)
- TDS was deducted on Indian income and you want to claim refund
- You have Indian assets above specified limits

**What counts as Indian income for NRIs**:
- Rent received from Indian property
- Capital gains on Indian property, shares (listed/unlisted), or MFs
- Interest on NRO FDs (not NRE — NRE is exempt)
- Indian business income
- Salary earned in India (for services in India)

**Not included**:
- Interest on NRE/FCNR accounts (tax-free for NRIs and RNORs)
- Foreign salary for services performed outside India
- Foreign capital gains

## TDS on NRI Income — Special Rates

Banks and others deduct TDS on NRI income at higher rates than resident rates:

| Income | TDS Rate for NRI |
|--------|-----------------|
| Interest on NRO deposits | 30% + surcharge + cess |
| Rent from India property | 30% + surcharge + cess (if tenant is company) |
| Capital gains — STCG on equity | 15% (same as resident) |
| Capital gains — LTCG on equity | 10% above ₹1.25L |
| Capital gains — STCG on property | 30% |
| Capital gains — LTCG on property | 12.5% |

NRIs often get TDS deducted at the slab-applicable rate even when DTAA (Double Tax Avoidance Agreement) reduces the actual tax. Always file ITR to claim DTAA benefit and get a refund.

## NRE vs NRO vs FCNR Accounts

| Account | Who Can Hold | Tax on Interest |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| **NRE (Non-Resident External)** | NRIs only | ❌ Fully exempt for NRIs and RNORs; taxable when you become ROR |
| **NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary)** | NRIs (and residents) | ✅ Taxable in India (TDS at 30%) |
| **FCNR (Foreign Currency Non-Resident)** | NRIs only | ❌ Fully exempt for NRIs and RNORs; taxable when ROR |

**Important for returning NRIs**: Once you become ROR (typically after 2–3 years of RNOR), your NRE and FCNR accounts must be converted to resident accounts (or closed). Interest earned after you become ROR is fully taxable.

## Which ITR Form for NRIs?

| Situation | ITR Form |
|-----------|---------|
| NRI with India salary only | ITR-1 (if income ≤ ₹50L, no capital gains) |
| NRI with India income + capital gains | ITR-2 |
| NRI with India business income | ITR-3 |
| RNOR or ROR with foreign income | ITR-2 (must file Schedule FSI and DTAA) |

NRIs cannot use ITR-1 if they have capital gains from Indian property, shares, or MFs. ITR-2 is the safe choice for most NRIs.

## DTAA — Avoid Double Taxation

India has DTAAs with most countries (US, UK, UAE, Singapore, Germany, Australia, etc.). DTAA prevents the same income from being taxed twice.

**How it works**:
- You earned interest in the US, paid 10% tax there
- India also wants to tax it (if you're ROR)
- DTAA: credit 10% paid abroad against Indian tax; pay only the difference to India

Claim DTAA credit using:
- **Schedule FSI** (Foreign Source Income) in ITR-2/ITR-3
- **Schedule TR** (Tax Relief for DTAA)
- Attach proof: tax paid certificate from the foreign country

## Returning NRI Checklist

When you return to India permanently:
1. **Convert NRE/FCNR accounts** to Resident/RFC accounts within 6 months of becoming resident
2. **NRO account**: Can keep as is (already taxable in India)
3. **Demat account**: Open resident demat account; NRI demat account cannot hold Indian stocks once you're resident
4. **Mutual funds**: Update residential status with your AMC/folio
5. **PAN update**: Update resident status on PAN (if PAN was issued as NRI)
6. **ITR filing**: File as RNOR for first 2–3 years after return; declare only India income
7. **FEMA compliance**: Maintain records of foreign assets declared under FEMA

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I was in the US for 10 years. I returned in December 2025. Am I an NRI or RNOR for FY 2025-26?

FY 2025-26 is April 2025 – March 2026. You were in India from December 2025 → about 120 days. That's less than 182 days → you are an **NRI** for FY 2025-26. Your US income is not taxable in India. From FY 2026-27, when you're in India 182+ days, you'll become RNOR (having been NRI for 9 out of 10 preceding years).

### I have a US 401(k) / stock options that will vest after I return to India. How are they taxed?

US 401(k) withdrawals: Tax treatment depends on your residential status when you receive the money, and DTAA provisions between India and US. Generally taxable in India once you're ROR, but DTAA credit applies for US tax paid. Stock options: The income is typically taxed when vested/exercised; if you're RNOR and the income relates to services performed outside India, it may be exempt. Complex area — consult a CA with international tax expertise.

### My NRE FD matures after I become ROR. Is all the interest taxable?

Interest earned during the period you were NRI/RNOR: exempt. Interest earned after you become ROR: taxable. You may need to apportion the interest if the FD spans the transition. Your bank should provide a break-up on request.

### I visit India for 5 months each year (working remotely for a foreign employer). What's my status?

5 months = ~150 days. Less than 182 days → NRI for that year. Your foreign employer salary is not taxable in India. However, if you have any India-sourced income (rental income, NRO FD interest), those are taxable. Ensure your Indian income is declared in ITR.
