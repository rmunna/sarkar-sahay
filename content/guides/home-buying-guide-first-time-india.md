## Why This Guide?

Buying your first home is likely the **biggest financial decision** of your life. In India, the process involves navigating RERA portals, home loans, stamp duty, registration, and countless documents — and mistakes can cost lakhs. This guide walks you through every step, from budgeting to possession.

## Step 1: Budget Planning

### How Much Can You Afford?

A safe rule: your **total EMI should not exceed 40% of your monthly take-home salary**.

| Monthly Income | Max EMI (40%) | Approx. Loan (20 yrs, 8.5%) | Property Budget (80% LTV) |
|---------------|--------------|------------------------------|--------------------------|
| ₹50,000 | ₹20,000 | ~₹22 lakh | ~₹27 lakh |
| ₹1,00,000 | ₹40,000 | ~₹44 lakh | ~₹55 lakh |
| ₹1,50,000 | ₹60,000 | ~₹66 lakh | ~₹82 lakh |
| ₹2,00,000 | ₹80,000 | ~₹88 lakh | ~₹1.10 crore |

### Hidden Costs (Budget Extra 10-15%)

| Cost | Typical % |
|------|-----------|
| Stamp duty | 4-8% |
| Registration charges | 1-4% |
| GST (under-construction) | 5% (affordable) / 12% (luxury) |
| Brokerage | 1-2% |
| Home loan processing fee | 0.5-1% |
| Maintenance deposit | 1-2 years advance |
| Interior/furnishing | ₹3-10 lakh |
| Moving costs | ₹10,000-50,000 |

## Step 2: Home Loan Pre-Approval

Get **pre-approved** before house hunting — it gives you a clear budget and stronger negotiating power.

### What Banks Check
- **CIBIL score** — 750+ for best rates; 650+ minimum
- **Income stability** — Salaried (2+ years) or self-employed (3+ years ITR)
- **Existing EMIs** — Total EMI burden including new loan
- **Age** — Loan tenure + age should not exceed 60-65 years
- **Property type** — Banks have approved builder lists

### Documents for Home Loan
- **Identity:** Aadhaar, PAN
- **Income (Salaried):** Last 6 months salary slips, 2 years Form 16, bank statements
- **Income (Self-employed):** 3 years ITR, profit & loss, balance sheet, bank statements
- **Property:** Agreement to sell, builder approvals, RERA certificate

## Step 3: Location Research

### Factors to Evaluate
- **Connectivity** — Metro/bus routes, highways, distance to workplace
- **Social infrastructure** — Schools, hospitals, malls, parks
- **Future development** — Upcoming metro lines, IT parks, expressways (check local master plan)
- **Water and power supply** — Critical in many Indian cities
- **Neighbourhood safety** — Visit at different times (day/night/weekend)
- **Resale value** — Areas near infrastructure projects appreciate faster

### Smart Research Methods
1. Check **Google Maps** for commute times during peak hours
2. Visit **local municipal website** for master plan/development plans
3. Talk to **existing residents** in the neighbourhood
4. Check **property portals** for price trends in the area
5. Verify **land use** — residential, commercial, or mixed (check zoning)

## Step 4: RERA Verification

**Never buy a project that isn't RERA registered** (if applicable — projects with 8+ units or 500+ sq. m.).

### Quick RERA Check
1. Ask the builder for the **RERA registration number**
2. Visit your **state RERA portal** and search the number
3. Verify: project name, promoter, completion date, approved plans
4. Check for **complaints** filed against the project
5. Verify the **broker** is also RERA-registered

> For a detailed state-wise RERA verification guide, see our [RERA Registration Check guide](/guide/rera-registered-project-check-verify).

## Step 5: Builder Verification

### Background Checks
- **Previous projects** — Have they been delivered on time? Visit completed projects.
- **Financial health** — Publicly listed builders have accessible financials
- **Litigation check** — Search the builder's name on [ecourts.gov.in](https://ecourts.gov.in) for pending cases
- **RERA track record** — Check all projects on RERA portal
- **Customer reviews** — Talk to buyers in previous projects (don't rely only on online reviews)

### Red Flags
🚩 Builder not RERA registered
🚩 Asking for cash component
🚩 No clear land title documentation
🚩 Refusing to show approved plans
🚩 History of delayed projects
🚩 Demanding more than 10% before agreement (violates RERA)

## Step 6: Documents to Verify Before Buying

### Property Documents (Must Check)

1. **Title deed** — Establishes ownership chain (verify at least 30 years)
2. **Encumbrance certificate (EC)** — Confirms property is free from legal dues/mortgages
3. **RERA registration certificate** — Valid and active
4. **Approved building plan** — From local municipal authority
5. **Commencement certificate** — Permission to start construction
6. **Occupancy certificate (OC)** — For ready-to-move properties; confirms building is habitable
7. **Completion certificate (CC)** — Building completed as per approved plan
8. **Land use certificate** — Confirms residential use is permitted
9. **NOC from relevant authorities** — Fire, water, pollution, airport (if near one)
10. **Property tax receipts** — Up to date

### Builder Documents
- **Company registration** certificate
- **PAN and GST** registration
- **RERA agent registration** (for the broker)

> **Tip:** Hire a **property lawyer** to verify documents. Cost: ₹5,000-25,000 — a small price to avoid lakhs in losses.

## Step 7: Negotiation and Agreement

### Negotiation Tips
- Research **circle rates** and recent sale prices in the area
- Negotiate on **total cost** including parking, club membership, maintenance deposit
- Ask for **payment plan flexibility** — construction-linked plans are safer
- Get everything **in writing** — verbal promises mean nothing
- If resale, negotiate based on **age of property** and pending maintenance

### Agreement to Sell
- RERA mandates a **model agreement** — don't accept one-sided builder agreements
- Key clauses to check: possession date, penalty for delay, carpet area, specifications, cancellation terms
- Pay only up to **10% as booking amount** before the agreement is signed

## Step 8: Registration Process

1. **Pay stamp duty** — Online (e-stamping) or through authorized stamps
2. **Pay registration charges** — Along with stamp duty at the Sub-Registrar office
3. **Book appointment** at the Sub-Registrar office (online in most states)
4. **Both parties appear** with 2 witnesses at the Sub-Registrar office
5. **Biometric verification** and document submission
6. **Receive registered deed** — Usually within 15-30 days (many states give it same day now)

> For detailed registration process, see our [Property Registration Online guide](/guide/property-registration-online).

## Step 9: Possession Checklist

When taking possession of your new home, verify:

### Structural
- [ ] Walls — No cracks, seepage, or dampness
- [ ] Flooring — Level, no hollow sounds when tapped
- [ ] Doors and windows — Open/close smoothly, proper locks
- [ ] Plumbing — Run all taps, check water pressure, flush toilets
- [ ] Electrical — Test all switches, sockets, and MCBs
- [ ] Waterproofing — Check bathrooms, balconies, and terrace

### Documentation
- [ ] **Occupancy certificate (OC)** received from municipal authority
- [ ] **Completion certificate (CC)** obtained
- [ ] **Registered sale deed** in your name
- [ ] **Society formation** initiated by builder (mandatory under RERA)
- [ ] **Maintenance agreement** signed
- [ ] **Parking allotment** letter received
- [ ] **Water, electricity, gas connections** transferred to your name

### Financial
- [ ] All payments made as per agreement — get **receipts**
- [ ] No pending dues shown by builder
- [ ] Home loan disbursement complete
- [ ] Property insurance arranged

## Important Tips

1. **Never pay in cash** — Always use banking channels for traceability and tax compliance
2. **Get property insurance** — Covers fire, natural disasters, and structural damage
3. **Start an emergency fund** — Keep 6 months of EMI as buffer before buying
4. **Check water and electricity** supply physically, not just on paper
5. **Don't stretch beyond 40% EMI-to-income** — You need to live, not just pay EMI

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the minimum CIBIL score needed for a home loan?
Most banks require a minimum of **650-700** CIBIL score. For the best interest rates, aim for **750+**. You can check your score for free on the CIBIL website once a year.

### Should I buy under-construction or ready-to-move?
**Ready-to-move** is safer — no delay risk, you can inspect the actual property, and there's no GST. Under-construction is cheaper (5-15%) but carries delay and quality risks. Choose based on your risk appetite and timeline.

### Is it better to buy or rent in India?
Use the **price-to-rent ratio**: If annual rent is less than 3-4% of property price, renting may be financially better. But home ownership offers stability, forced savings, and tax benefits. It's a personal decision.

### How much down payment do I need?
Banks finance up to **75-90%** of property value (LTV ratio). You need **10-25% down payment** from your own funds. For properties above ₹75 lakh, the down payment requirement is typically 20-25%.

### What is carpet area vs built-up area?
**Carpet area** = usable area inside the walls (what you can actually lay carpet on). **Built-up area** = carpet area + wall thickness + balcony. **Super built-up** = built-up + proportionate common areas. Under RERA, sale must be on **carpet area** basis only.

### Should I buy in my name or jointly?
**Joint ownership with spouse (especially wife)** is often beneficial — lower stamp duty in many states, better home loan terms for joint applications, and inheritance simplification.

### What is PMAY and am I eligible?
Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana provides **interest subsidy of 3-6.5%** on home loans for first-time buyers in EWS/LIG/MIG categories. Check eligibility at [pmay-urban.gov.in](https://pmay-urban.gov.in).
