## What is Electricity Bill Name Transfer?

Electricity bill name transfer (also called **electricity connection transfer** or **consumer name change**) is the process of changing the registered consumer name on an electricity connection. The existing connection remains — only the account holder name changes in the electricity board's records.

This is needed when:
- **Property is sold** — new owner needs the connection in their name
- **Inheritance** — property owner has passed away and legal heir takes over
- **Death of account holder** — surviving family member needs the connection transferred
- **Tenant change** — landlord wants to transfer connection to/from a tenant
- **Divorce or family settlement** — ownership changes within a family

> **Note:** Name transfer does NOT mean getting a new connection. Your meter number, consumer number, and load remain the same.

## Documents Required

### For Property Sale / Purchase

| Document | Details |
|----------|---------|
| Sale deed / registry copy | Registered copy of property sale deed |
| Identity proof | Aadhaar card, Voter ID, or Passport of new owner |
| Address proof | Same as property address (Aadhaar preferred) |
| NOC from previous owner | No Objection Certificate signed by old consumer |
| Latest electricity bill | Paid bill of the connection to be transferred |
| Passport-size photos | 2 photos of the new applicant |
| Application form | From the electricity board office or website |

### For Inheritance / Death of Account Holder

| Document | Details |
|----------|---------|
| Death certificate | Of the previous account holder |
| Legal heir certificate / Succession certificate | Issued by Tehsildar or court |
| Identity proof of heir | Aadhaar card, Voter ID, or Passport |
| Latest electricity bill | Of the existing connection |
| Indemnity bond | On stamp paper (some states require this) |
| Affidavit | Stating you are the rightful legal heir |

### For Tenant Transfer

| Document | Details |
|----------|---------|
| Rent agreement | Valid registered rent agreement |
| NOC from property owner | Landlord's consent letter |
| Identity proof of tenant | Aadhaar card, Voter ID |
| Latest electricity bill | Paid copy |

## Step-by-Step Process — Online (State-Wise)

Online name transfer is available in some states. Here's how it works for major electricity boards:

### MSEDCL (Maharashtra)

1. Visit [MSEDCL website](https://www.mahadiscom.in) and log in
2. Go to **"Apply Online"** → **"Change of Name / Ownership"**
3. Enter your consumer number and verify details
4. Upload scanned copies of sale deed, ID proof, and NOC
5. Pay the processing fee online (₹100–₹500 depending on category)
6. Note the application number for tracking
7. A field officer may visit for physical verification

### UPPCL (Uttar Pradesh)

1. Visit [UPPCL portal](https://www.upenergy.in)
2. Navigate to **"Consumer Services"** → **"Name Change / Ownership Transfer"**
3. Enter consumer account number
4. Fill in the application form and upload required documents
5. Submit and note the reference number
6. Track status online using the reference number

### KSEB (Kerala)

1. Visit [KSEB Self-Service Portal](https://wss.kseb.in)
2. Register/login with your consumer number
3. Select **"Ownership Transfer"** from services menu
4. Upload sale deed, ID proof, and other documents
5. Pay applicable fees online
6. Track application status on the portal

### TANGEDCO (Tamil Nadu)

1. Visit [TANGEDCO website](https://www.tangedco.gov.in)
2. Go to **"Online Services"** → **"Change of Name"**
3. Enter service connection number
4. Upload documents and submit application
5. Visit the local section office with originals for verification if asked

### BESCOM (Karnataka)

1. Visit [BESCOM portal](https://bescom.karnataka.gov.in)
2. Navigate to **"Consumer Services"**
3. Select **"Change of Ownership"**
4. Fill in application details with consumer number
5. Upload required documents
6. Pay processing fee and submit

> **Tip:** If online submission is not available for your state, you must apply offline at the local electricity office.

## Step-by-Step Process — Offline

This works for **all states and electricity boards**:

1. **Visit your local electricity subdivision office** — Find the office that handles your area (mentioned on your electricity bill)
2. **Collect the application form** — Ask for the "Name Transfer" or "Ownership Change" form (some boards call it Form-C or similar)
3. **Fill in the form** with:
   - Old consumer number
   - Old consumer name
   - New consumer name
   - Property address
   - Reason for transfer
4. **Attach all required documents** (photocopies + originals for verification)
5. **Pay the processing fee** — Typically ₹100 to ₹500
6. **Get acknowledgment receipt** — Keep it safe for tracking
7. **Physical verification** — An electricity board officer may visit your premises
8. **Name updated** — Once approved, the next bill will show the new name

## Processing Time

- **Typical timeline:** 15 to 30 working days
- **If all documents are in order:** Can be done in 7–15 days in some states
- **If physical verification is required:** May take up to 30 days
- **If application has errors:** Will be delayed until corrections are submitted

## Application Rejected? Common Reasons & Fixes

### 1. Pending Electricity Bills
- **Problem:** Outstanding dues on the connection
- **Fix:** Clear ALL pending bills before applying. The electricity board will not transfer a connection with unpaid dues. Get a **"No Dues Certificate"** from the billing section

### 2. NOC Not Provided
- **Problem:** No Objection Certificate from previous owner is missing
- **Fix:** If the previous owner is unavailable or uncooperative, submit a **legal heir certificate** (for death cases) or **court order** (for disputed property). Some boards accept a **registered sale deed** as sufficient proof

### 3. Property Document Mismatch
- **Problem:** Name on sale deed doesn't match the applicant's ID proof
- **Fix:** Ensure your name is spelled **exactly the same** across all documents. If there's a mismatch, get an **affidavit** explaining the variation

### 4. Incomplete Documents
- **Problem:** Missing one or more required documents
- **Fix:** Check the specific document list for your electricity board. Common missing items: passport photos, stamp paper affidavit, original bill copy

### 5. Property Under Dispute
- **Problem:** Multiple people claiming ownership
- **Fix:** Provide a **court order** or **family settlement deed** that clearly establishes ownership. The electricity board cannot transfer during active legal disputes

### 6. Commercial Connection Transfer
- **Problem:** Additional documents needed for commercial/industrial connections
- **Fix:** For commercial transfers, you may also need: **trade licence**, **GST registration**, and **partnership deed / company registration** documents

## Pending Bills — Who Pays?

This is one of the most common concerns during name transfer:

- **All pending dues must be cleared BEFORE transfer** — This is a universal rule across all electricity boards
- **The previous owner is legally liable** for bills during their tenure
- **In practice:** The electricity board requires all dues to be cleared regardless of who pays. Negotiate with the seller during property purchase
- **Pro tip for buyers:** Before buying a property, always **check the electricity bill status**. Ask for the last 6 months' paid bills or get a "No Dues Certificate" from the electricity office
- **If the previous owner has died:** The legal heir applying for transfer will need to clear the outstanding bills

## Important Tips

1. **Check bill status before property purchase** — Unpaid electricity bills become your problem after ownership transfer
2. **Keep original documents ready** — Even for online applications, officers may ask for originals during verification
3. **Apply within 30 days of property registration** — Some states have a time limit; delays can attract late fees
4. **Get everything in writing** — Always take acknowledgment receipts and keep copies of submitted documents
5. **Track your application** — Use the reference number to check status online or call the customer care number on your electricity bill

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I transfer an electricity connection online?
Yes, major state electricity boards like MSEDCL, UPPCL, KSEB, TANGEDCO, and BESCOM offer online name transfer. However, physical verification may still be required. If online is not available in your area, visit the local subdivision office.

### How long does electricity name transfer take?
Typically **15 to 30 working days**. If all documents are correct and bills are clear, some states process it within 7–15 days.

### What if the previous owner refuses to give NOC?
You can submit the **registered sale deed** as proof of ownership. Some boards also accept a **notarized affidavit** stating that the previous owner is not cooperating. In extreme cases, approach the consumer grievance forum.

### Do I need to pay the previous owner's pending bills?
The electricity board requires **all dues to be cleared** before transfer, regardless of who pays. Legally, the previous owner is responsible, but practically you may need to pay and adjust the amount with the seller.

### Can a tenant get the electricity bill in their name?
Yes, with a **valid rent agreement** and **NOC from the property owner**. When the tenant leaves, the connection should be transferred back to the owner's name.

### Will my meter number change after name transfer?
No. The **meter number, consumer number, and sanctioned load remain the same**. Only the registered consumer name changes in the records.

### What happens if I don't transfer the electricity connection after buying property?
You may face issues with bill payments, getting duplicate bills, applying for load enhancement, or lodging complaints. The connection may even be disconnected if the previous owner requests disconnection.

### Is name transfer required after the death of the account holder?
Yes. The legal heir must apply for name transfer with the **death certificate** and **legal heir certificate**. Until then, bills continue in the deceased person's name.
