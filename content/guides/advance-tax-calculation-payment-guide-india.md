# Advance Tax India 2026 — Complete Guide to Calculation, Due Dates, and Payment

Most salaried employees never think about advance tax — their employer deducts TDS every month. But if you have **income beyond salary** (freelance, capital gains, rental income, business income, dividends), you may need to pay advance tax yourself — or face interest penalties.

## What Is Advance Tax?

**Advance tax** = paying your estimated income tax in installments during the financial year (April–March), rather than all at once when filing ITR in July.

Think of it as: instead of paying your entire year's tax on July 31, you pre-pay in chunks — the government gets money earlier, and you spread the burden.

## Who Must Pay Advance Tax?

**Mandatory if**: Your total estimated tax liability for the year exceeds **₹10,000** after subtracting TDS.

| Taxpayer Type | Situation |
|--------------|-----------|
| **Salaried** | Usually no advance tax needed (employer TDS covers it). But if you have freelance income, capital gains, or FD interest with tax liability >₹10K beyond TDS → advance tax required |
| **Freelancer / Consultant** | Usually must pay advance tax (no TDS on most freelance income) |
| **Business owner** | Must pay (no automatic TDS mechanism) |
| **Investor with capital gains** | Must pay if total capital gains tax + other tax beyond TDS > ₹10K |
| **NRI with India income** | Must pay on income where TDS isn't deducted |

**Exempt**: Senior citizens (60+ years) with **no business/profession income** are exempt from advance tax. They pay all tax at ITR filing time.

## Advance Tax Due Dates 2026 (FY 2025-26)

| Installment | Due Date | Cumulative % of Total Tax |
|------------|----------|--------------------------|
| 1st | **June 15, 2025** | 15% |
| 2nd | **September 15, 2025** | 45% |
| 3rd | **December 15, 2025** | 75% |
| 4th (Final) | **March 15, 2026** | 100% |

**For FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28)**:
- 1st: June 15, 2026
- 2nd: September 15, 2026
- 3rd: December 15, 2026
- 4th: March 15, 2027

**Important**: The March 15 deadline is the complete advance tax payment. If you pay 100% by March 15, no Section 234C interest applies.

## How to Calculate Advance Tax

### Step 1: Estimate Your Total Income for the Year
Include all income sources:
- Salary (use your CTC breakdown)
- Freelance / consulting income (estimate for the year)
- Rental income (annual rent received)
- Interest income (FD, savings account)
- Capital gains (already realized + expected before March 31)
- Dividends received

### Step 2: Reduce by Deductions
- Section 80C, 80D, 80G, 80E, etc.
- HRA exemption
- Standard deduction (₹75K for new regime)
- Leave travel allowance, etc.

### Step 3: Calculate Tax on Taxable Income
Apply the appropriate tax slabs (new or old regime).

### Step 4: Subtract TDS Already Deducted / Expected
- Salary TDS (Form 16 projection)
- TDS on FD interest
- TDS on consulting/freelance income (10% on professional payments)

### Step 5: If Remaining Tax > ₹10,000 → Pay Advance Tax

**Example**: Salaried person with ₹12L salary + ₹3L freelance income (FY 2025-26, new regime)
- Total tax (new regime): ~₹1,17,500
- TDS on salary (employer deducts): ~₹90,000
- TDS on freelance (client deducts at 10%): ₹30,000
- Remaining tax: ₹1,17,500 - ₹90,000 - ₹30,000 = **–₹2,500** (refund)
- No advance tax needed (remaining tax < ₹10K)

Another example: Same person with ₹5L freelance (no TDS deducted by client):
- Total tax: ~₹1,72,500
- TDS on salary: ~₹90,000
- TDS on freelance: ₹0 (client didn't deduct)
- Remaining: **₹82,500** → Must pay advance tax in 4 installments

## Installment Amounts

For the example above (₹82,500 total advance tax due):

| Installment | % | Amount | Cumulative |
|------------|---|--------|-----------|
| June 15 | 15% | ₹12,375 | ₹12,375 |
| September 15 | 30% more | ₹24,750 | ₹37,125 |
| December 15 | 30% more | ₹24,750 | ₹61,875 |
| March 15 | 25% more | ₹20,625 | ₹82,500 |

You don't need to pay exact installments — the requirement is cumulative percentage thresholds.

## How to Pay Advance Tax Online (Challan 280)

### Method 1: Income Tax Portal (Recommended)

1. Go to incometax.gov.in → **e-Pay Tax**
2. Enter PAN, confirm
3. Select **Income Tax (Major Head 0021)**
4. Under Type of Payment: **Advance Tax (100)**
5. Assessment Year: **2026-27** (for FY 2025-26 payments)
6. Enter the amount and pay via net banking / debit card / UPI / NEFT-RTGS

### Method 2: NSDL Portal

1. Go to tin.tin.nsdl.com → Challan 280
2. Select Income Tax (other than companies) — TAN, PAN as applicable
3. Select **Advance Tax (100)** under type
4. Fill assessment year and amount
5. Pay

**Keep the payment acknowledgement**: It shows Challan Identification Number (CIN) — needed when filing ITR.

### After Payment: Credit in AIS/26AS
Your advance tax payment appears in **Form 26AS** and **AIS** (Annual Information Statement) within 3–5 days. Verify before filing ITR to ensure it's reflected.

## Interest for Not Paying Advance Tax (Section 234C and 234B)

### Section 234C — Installment Shortfalls

If you don't meet cumulative thresholds by each due date:

| Missed Threshold | Interest |
|-----------------|---------|
| Less than 12% by June 15 | 1% per month for 3 months on shortfall |
| Less than 36% by September 15 | 1% per month for 3 months on shortfall |
| Less than 75% by December 15 | 1% per month for 3 months on shortfall |

### Section 234B — Shortfall by Year-End

If total advance tax paid is less than 90% of total assessed tax liability by March 31:
- 1% per month from April 1 of AY to date of payment/assessment

**The good news**: For capital gains (both LTCG and STCG) that occur in Q4 (January–March), Section 234C does not apply if you pay the full amount in the March 15 installment. Capital gains are recognized when they occur — if they all happen in Q4, you're not penalized for not paying in June/September.

## Special Rule for Capital Gains

**Capital gains arising after December 15**: You may pay the FULL remaining advance tax in the **March 15 installment** without 234C interest — even if earlier installments missed the capital gains portion.

Example: You sold equity in February 2026 (Q4) with ₹50L LTCG. Your earlier installments (June, September, December) didn't include this because the sale hadn't happened. You can pay the entire tax on this gain in March 15 — no 234C interest.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I'm salaried. Do I need to pay advance tax for dividend income of ₹50,000?

If the tax on ₹50,000 dividend income (at your slab rate) after any existing TDS credits > ₹10,000: Yes, you must pay advance tax. For someone in the 20% bracket, ₹50K × 20% = ₹10,000 — right at the threshold. If in 30% bracket: ₹15,000 → must pay advance tax.

### I started freelancing in October. Do I need to pay advance tax for June/September?

If your freelancing income wasn't expected earlier, you pay for the remaining installments on revised estimates. Section 234C interest applies only to income that was "assessable" at the time of the installment. Newly started income in October → the December and March installments cover it.

### I paid no advance tax and paid everything at ITR filing in July. What interest do I owe?

- Section 234C: Interest on each installment shortfall (calculated quarter-by-quarter)
- Section 234B: 1% per month from April 1 (start of AY) to July 31 (date of filing)

These are automatically calculated by the IT portal when you file. The interest is added to your total tax due.
