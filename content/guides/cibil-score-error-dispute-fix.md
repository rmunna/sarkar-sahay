# CIBIL Score Error — How to Dispute and Fix

Your CIBIL score directly impacts your ability to get loans, credit cards, and even rental agreements. But what happens when your credit report contains errors — a loan you already closed still showing as active, or an account that isn't even yours? These mistakes are more common than you think, and they can silently destroy your creditworthiness.

This guide walks you through how to check your CIBIL report for free, identify errors, raise disputes, and get them corrected.

---

## How to Check Your CIBIL Report for Free

Every individual is entitled to **one free CIBIL report per year** from TransUnion CIBIL. You can also check it via several other platforms:

### Official CIBIL Website

1. Visit [cibil.com/freecibilscore](https://www.cibil.com/freecibilscore)
2. Sign up or log in with your PAN, email, and mobile number
3. Complete OTP verification
4. View your credit score and detailed credit report

### Other Free Options

- **RBI mandate:** All four credit bureaus (CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, CRIF High Mark) must provide one free report per year
- **Banking apps:** Many banks like SBI, HDFC, ICICI show your CIBIL score in their mobile apps
- **Fintech platforms:** Paytm, PhonePe, BankBazaar, and Paisabazaar offer free score checks (these are soft inquiries and don't affect your score)

> **Tip:** Download the full credit report, not just the score. Errors hide in the details — account history, personal information, and enquiry sections.

## Common Types of Errors in CIBIL Report

### 1. Wrong Personal Information

- Name misspelled or mismatch
- Wrong date of birth
- Incorrect PAN number
- Old or wrong address still listed
- Someone else's phone number or email

### 2. Account-Level Errors

- **Closed loan showing as open:** You repaid a loan in full, but it still appears as active or outstanding
- **Wrong outstanding balance:** EMI payments not reflected, balance higher than actual
- **Incorrect payment status:** Timely payments marked as late or defaulted
- **Wrong account type:** Home loan shown as personal loan, etc.

### 3. Duplicate Entries

- Same loan appearing twice (common when a lender merges with another bank)
- Same credit card account listed multiple times
- Duplicate entries inflate your total outstanding debt, lowering your score

### 4. Fraudulent or Unknown Accounts

- Accounts you never opened (could indicate identity theft)
- Loans taken using your PAN by someone else
- Unauthorized credit card enquiries

### 5. Settlement/Write-Off Errors

- Loan marked as "settled" or "written off" when it was fully repaid
- Old settled accounts not updated after payment of the remaining amount

---

## How Errors Affect Your Loan Eligibility

Even small errors can have serious consequences:

| Error Type | Impact on Score | Impact on Loans |
|---|---|---|
| Closed loan showing open | Increases debt-to-income ratio | Loan amount sanctioned may reduce |
| Late payment wrongly marked | Can drop score by 50-100 points | Higher interest rates or outright rejection |
| Duplicate accounts | Inflates total debt | Banks may consider you over-leveraged |
| Unknown accounts | Severe score drop | Red flag — loan applications rejected |
| Settled/written-off status | Major negative mark | Most banks reject applicants with settlement tags |

> **Real impact:** A score drop from 750 to 650 due to an error can mean the difference between a home loan at 8.5% and rejection at the doorstep.

## How to Raise a Dispute on the CIBIL Portal

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Log in** to your CIBIL account at [cibil.com](https://www.cibil.com)
2. Go to **"My Credit Report"** section
3. Click on the **"Dispute"** or **"Raise a Dispute"** option next to the incorrect entry
4. Select the **type of dispute:**
   - Personal information correction
   - Account information correction
   - Enquiry dispute
5. **Describe the error** clearly — what is wrong and what it should be
6. **Upload supporting documents** (loan closure letter, payment receipts, bank statement)
7. **Submit the dispute** — you will receive a Dispute ID and confirmation email

### Documents You May Need

- Loan closure certificate / NOC from the lender
- Bank account statements showing EMI payments
- Payment receipts or challan copies
- PAN card copy (for personal info corrections)
- Written communication from the bank confirming the error

### What Happens After You File

1. **CIBIL forwards your dispute** to the concerned lender/bank (called the "credit institution" or CI)
2. The lender verifies records and responds to CIBIL
3. If the lender confirms the error, CIBIL updates your report
4. If the lender disagrees, the dispute is marked as "no change"
5. You receive an email notification about the outcome

---

## Timeline for Dispute Resolution

| Stage | Expected Duration |
|---|---|
| Dispute acknowledgment | 1–3 days |
| Lender verification | 15–30 days |
| CIBIL report updated | Within 30 days of dispute |
| Maximum resolution time (RBI guideline) | **30 days** from date of complaint |

- Under **RBI's Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005**, credit bureaus must resolve disputes within **30 days**
- If the lender doesn't respond to CIBIL within 30 days, the disputed entry should be flagged or removed
- In practice, some cases take 45-60 days — escalation is your remedy in such cases

## What to Do If Your Dispute Is Rejected

If CIBIL closes your dispute with "no change," you still have options:

### 1. Contact the Lender Directly

- Visit the bank branch or call customer care
- Get written confirmation of the error
- Ask the bank to send updated data to CIBIL directly
- Banks are **required** to send corrected data to all four credit bureaus

### 2. Re-raise the Dispute with Additional Proof

- File the dispute again on the CIBIL portal with stronger documentation
- Attach the lender's written acknowledgment of the error

### 3. File a Complaint with CIBIL's Grievance Redressal

- Email: **info@cibil.com**
- Mention your dispute ID and provide all supporting documents
- CIBIL must respond within **30 days**

### 4. Escalate to RBI Ombudsman

If CIBIL and the lender both fail to resolve your issue:

- File a complaint on the **RBI Integrated Ombudsman portal** at [cms.rbi.org.in](https://cms.rbi.org.in/)
- You can complain against both the credit bureau and the bank
- RBI Ombudsman can direct the credit bureau to correct the entry
- No fees or lawyer required — the process is free

### 5. Approach Consumer Court

- For persistent, unresolved disputes causing financial damage
- You can claim compensation for mental harassment and financial loss
- File in the **District Consumer Disputes Redressal Forum**

## RBI Guidelines on Credit Bureau Corrections

The Reserve Bank of India has laid down clear rules for credit information companies:

### Key RBI Directives

- **Free annual report:** Every individual is entitled to one free credit report per year from each bureau
- **30-day resolution:** All disputes must be resolved within 30 days
- **Mandatory data accuracy:** Lenders must report accurate, updated data to credit bureaus monthly
- **Borrower notification:** When a lender reports a default or negative entry, the borrower should be informed
- **Correction obligation:** If a lender sends incorrect data, they are obligated to send corrected data to **all** credit bureaus, not just one
- **No penal action for bureau errors:** If a loan is rejected due to a credit bureau error, and the error is later corrected, the lender should reconsider the application

### RBI Circular on Credit Information (2023)

RBI directed all credit bureaus to:

- Set up robust grievance redressal mechanisms
- Display dispute resolution turnaround time on their websites
- Allow consumers to flag incorrect data points directly
- Implement automated alerts when credit report data changes

## Tips to Keep Your CIBIL Report Error-Free

1. **Check your report at least once a year** — use your free annual report
2. **Keep loan closure certificates** for every loan you close — NOC, final payment receipt
3. **Verify the report after closing any loan** — ensure it reflects as "closed" within 30-45 days
4. **Monitor for unknown accounts** — especially if your PAN was ever compromised
5. **Don't ignore "settled" status** — if you fully repaid a loan, ensure it shows "closed," not "settled"
6. **Track credit enquiries** — multiple hard enquiries by unknown lenders is a red flag

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does it take for CIBIL to update after a dispute?

Once the lender confirms the correction, CIBIL typically updates the report within **10–15 days**. The entire process from filing to resolution usually takes **30 days**.

### Can I dispute errors on other credit bureaus too?

Yes. You can raise disputes on all four bureaus — CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, and CRIF High Mark. Each bureau has its own dispute portal. It's the lender's responsibility to correct data across all bureaus.

### Will raising a dispute affect my CIBIL score?

No. Filing a dispute does **not** impact your credit score. It is your right, and it's treated as a correction request, not a negative action.

### What if a loan I never took appears on my report?

This could indicate **identity theft**. Immediately:
- Raise a dispute on the CIBIL portal
- File an FIR with the police
- Inform the lender whose account is showing
- File a complaint with RBI Ombudsman if the lender doesn't cooperate

### My bank says they sent corrected data but CIBIL still shows the error?

Ask the bank for the **exact date** they submitted the correction and the **control number**. Share this with CIBIL while re-raising the dispute. Data transmission issues between banks and CIBIL are not uncommon.

### Is there a fee to raise a CIBIL dispute?

No. Raising a dispute is **completely free**. Neither CIBIL nor any credit bureau can charge you for correcting errors in your report.
