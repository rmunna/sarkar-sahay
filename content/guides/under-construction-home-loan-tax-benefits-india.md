# Under-Construction Home Loan Tax Benefits India 2026

Buying an apartment under construction is common in India — builders sell projects 2–4 years before completion. During this period, you pay a "pre-EMI" (only interest on disbursed amount). The tax treatment for this period is different and often confusing.

## The Core Difference: Ready Property vs Under Construction

| | Ready Property | Under Construction |
|-|---------------|-------------------|
| **When does Section 24b start** | From purchase date | Only from **possession date** |
| **When does Section 80C principal deduction start** | From purchase date | Only from **possession date** |
| **Pre-EMI interest (during construction)** | N/A | Claimed in **5 equal installments** AFTER possession |
| **When does GST apply** | No GST (secondary sale) | 5% GST (under construction) |

## What Is Pre-EMI?

When a builder draws down the loan in tranches (as construction progresses), the bank charges interest on the disbursed amount. But since the full home loan isn't disbursed yet, the EMI structure hasn't started. You pay only **interest** on whatever is disbursed.

**Example**: ₹50L home loan, disbursed in 3 tranches of ₹16.67L each over 18 months.
- After tranche 1: Interest on ₹16.67L at 8.5% = ₹11,810/month (pre-EMI)
- After tranche 2: Interest on ₹33.33L = ₹23,610/month (pre-EMI)
- After full disbursement: EMI begins — ~₹43,391/month

During this pre-EMI period, you're paying out ₹11,810–₹43,391/month but **cannot claim any deduction in that year's ITR**.

## Pre-EMI Interest — The 5 Equal Installment Rule

All the interest paid during the **construction period** (before possession) is accumulated.

After possession, this total accumulated interest is deductible in **5 equal installments** — one per year, starting from the year of possession.

**Example**:
- Construction period: April 2022 – March 2025 (3 years)
- Total pre-EMI interest paid: ₹4,20,000
- Possession: April 2025

**In ITR**:
- FY 2025-26 (Year of possession): Claim 1/5th = ₹84,000
- FY 2026-27: Claim 1/5th = ₹84,000
- FY 2027-28: Claim 1/5th = ₹84,000
- FY 2028-29: Claim 1/5th = ₹84,000
- FY 2029-30: Claim 1/5th = ₹84,000

The ₹84,000 annual pre-EMI deduction is added to the current year's home loan interest for computing the ₹2L cap under Section 24b.

## Section 24b — Deduction Limit Applies to Combined Interest

The ₹2 lakh annual limit under Section 24b (for self-occupied property) applies to the **combination** of:
1. Current year's actual EMI interest
2. 1/5th of accumulated pre-EMI interest

**Example (Year of Possession, FY 2025-26)**:
- EMI interest in FY 2025-26 (from April 2025): ₹4,00,000
- Pre-EMI installment (1/5th of ₹4,20,000): ₹84,000
- Total deductible interest claim: ₹4,84,000
- **But cap is ₹2L for self-occupied** → Deduction capped at ₹2,00,000

If the property is let out: Both the current EMI interest AND pre-EMI installment are fully deductible (no ₹2L cap). Subject to ₹2L set-off limit per year for house property loss against salary.

## Section 80C — Principal Deduction Starts After Possession

Before possession, your "EMI" has no principal component (only interest in pre-EMI phase). Even when you start full EMI, **Section 80C principal deduction is not available until possession**.

After possession: The principal component of your EMI becomes deductible under Section 80C (up to ₹1.5L per year, within the overall 80C limit).

**Also eligible at possession**:
- Stamp duty paid: Deductible under Section 80C in the year of payment (only if claimed in the year of possession or the year stamp duty was paid — cannot be claimed later)
- Registration charges: Part of Section 80C eligible costs

## What You CAN Claim During Construction Period

Very little. During the pre-EMI/under-construction period:
- **No Section 24b deduction** (neither for interest nor principal)
- **No Section 80C** on EMI principal
- **BUT**: Stamp duty and registration (paid at time of sale deed) can be claimed under 80C **in the year they're paid**, even before possession

## GST on Under-Construction Property (Not Income Tax)

Under-construction property attracts **5% GST** on the purchase price (3% for affordable housing). This GST is paid to the builder and is not refundable or income-tax deductible for buyers (unlike in some cases for businesses).

Stamp duty is paid at the time of registration of the sale deed — usually after possession or at initial agreement (varies by state).

## 3-Year Condition for Section 24b

Section 24b allows the full ₹2L deduction for self-occupied property only if:
- The house was **acquired or constructed within 3 years** from the end of the financial year in which the loan was taken

If construction takes more than 3 years from the year the loan was sanctioned: The deduction drops to only **₹30,000** per year (not ₹2L).

**Example**:
- Loan sanctioned: FY 2019-20
- 3-year clock runs until: March 31, 2023
- If possession happens after March 31, 2023: Only ₹30,000 deduction per year

Builder delays are common — this can significantly reduce your tax benefit. Many buyers are unaware that their deduction dropped from ₹2L to ₹30K because of builder delays.

## How to Record Pre-EMI for ITR

Keep a record of:
1. All pre-EMI interest payment statements (from bank, monthly)
2. Date of possession (builder's completion certificate / possession letter)
3. Calculate total pre-EMI interest paid from first disbursement to possession
4. Divide by 5 to get annual deduction amount

In ITR under Schedule HP (House Property):
- Enter address of property
- For under-construction: Property type = "Self-Occupied" (after possession)
- Interest deduction includes current year's interest + 1/5th pre-EMI installment
- Note: The portal may not automatically handle pre-EMI — enter the computed amount manually

## Practical Timeline Example

**Amit buys a flat in Pune (under construction)**:
- Booking: April 2021
- Loan sanctioned: June 2021 (₹60L, 8.5%, 20 years)
- Disbursements: June 2021 (₹20L), Dec 2021 (₹20L), June 2022 (₹20L)
- Possession: March 2024

**Pre-EMI paid**:
- FY 2021-22: ₹85,000 (interest on ₹20L for 10 months, then ₹40L for 3 months)
- FY 2022-23: ₹1,72,000 (full year on ₹60L disbursed)
- FY 2023-24: ₹1,86,000 (full year before possession in March)
- **Total pre-EMI: ₹4,43,000**
- No deduction claimed in any of FY 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24

**After possession (FY 2023-24 — possession March 2024)**:
- Pre-EMI installment per year: ₹4,43,000 ÷ 5 = ₹88,600
- EMI interest (one month, April 2024 = first month in next year): 0 for FY 2023-24
- In FY 2024-25: EMI interest + ₹88,600 pre-EMI installment (claim both, subject to ₹2L cap)

**Loan was sanctioned June 2021. 3 years = June 2024. Possession March 2024 = within 3 years.** Full ₹2L deduction applies.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My builder is delayed by 2 years beyond the contract date. My loan has been running for 4 years now. Will I get ₹2L or ₹30K deduction?

If possession is delayed beyond 3 years from the financial year the loan was taken, you get only ₹30,000 deduction — not ₹2L. However, the 3-year limit can be extended in certain cases if the delay was due to circumstances beyond the buyer's control (like builder insolvency, litigation, government approvals). You can claim that the delay was not attributable to you and was caused by the developer. Keep documentation of the delay cause for potential scrutiny.

### I paid stamp duty and registration before possession (at initial agreement). Can I claim 80C now?

If you paid stamp duty and registration before possession (at sale agreement stage), you can claim it under Section 80C **in the year it was paid** — not deferred to possession. If you missed claiming it in that year, you unfortunately cannot claim it retrospectively (only via revised return if within deadline).

### Can I take a home loan for under-construction property and rent it out after possession?

Yes — this is common. If you rent it out after possession, you can claim the full EMI interest (no ₹2L cap, since it's let-out property). You must declare the rental income and can deduct standard deduction (30% of annual value), municipal taxes, and unlimited interest. Pre-EMI installments also deductible in full for let-out property.
