# LTCG Tax Harvesting India 2026 — Book ₹1.25 Lakh Gains Tax-Free Every Year

Every financial year, you can book up to **₹1.25 lakh in long-term capital gains** on equity (stocks and equity mutual funds) completely tax-free. If you don't use this exemption, you lose it — it doesn't accumulate.

**Tax harvesting** is the technique of deliberately selling equity investments every year to book up to ₹1.25L in LTCG, then immediately re-buying. You reset your cost basis to a higher level, deferring future tax while paying zero tax now.

## Why This Works — The ₹1.25L LTCG Exemption

Under Section 112A, LTCG on equity shares and equity mutual funds:
- Rate: **12.5%** on gains above ₹1.25 lakh
- Tax on first ₹1.25 lakh of LTCG: **₹0**

If you hold ₹50 lakh worth of equity MFs with large unrealized LTCG and never harvest, eventually when you sell everything, you'll pay 12.5% on ALL gains above ₹1.25L in that one year.

If instead you harvest ₹1.25L of gains every year, over 10 years you get ₹12.5L of gains tax-free.

**Tax saved per year** (at full 12.5%): ₹1,25,000 × 12.5% = **₹15,625** per year.

## How Tax Harvesting Works — Step by Step

**Step 1**: In March (or any time before March 31), identify equity holdings held > 12 months with unrealized LTCG.

**Step 2**: Calculate how much gain you can book this year to stay within ₹1.25L LTCG total.

**Step 3**: Sell enough units/shares to book exactly that gain.

**Step 4**: Immediately re-buy the same shares/fund units.

**Step 5**: Your cost basis (average purchase price) resets to the current higher market price.

**Result**: You've "used" the ₹1.25L exemption for this year, paid zero tax, and now your new cost basis is higher — so future gains from this higher base will be smaller.

## Worked Example

**Your portfolio**:
- 1,000 units of an equity MF bought in 2022 at ₹100/unit (total cost ₹1,00,000)
- Current NAV in March 2026: ₹230/unit
- Current value: ₹2,30,000
- Unrealized LTCG: ₹1,30,000

**Without tax harvesting**:
- You hold for 3 more years, NAV grows to ₹400
- You sell in 2029: Gains = ₹3,00,000 above ₹1,00,000 = ₹2,00,000
- Tax on ₹2,00,000: (₹2,00,000 − ₹1,25,000) × 12.5% = ₹9,375

**With tax harvesting in March 2026**:
- Sell 1,000 units at ₹230 = ₹2,30,000 received
- LTCG booked: ₹2,30,000 − ₹1,00,000 = ₹1,30,000
- Tax: (₹1,30,000 − ₹1,25,000) × 12.5% = **₹625** (tiny tax on ₹5K excess)
- Immediately re-buy 1,000 units at ₹230

**New cost basis**: ₹230/unit (total ₹2,30,000)

**When you sell in 2029 at ₹400**:
- Gains = ₹4,00,000 − ₹2,30,000 = ₹1,70,000
- Tax: (₹1,70,000 − ₹1,25,000) × 12.5% = ₹5,625
- **Plus ₹625 from 2026 harvest = total ₹6,250**

Without harvesting: ₹9,375 total tax.
With one harvest: ₹6,250 total tax. **Saving: ₹3,125** (and this grows with more harvesting years).

## Key Rules for Tax Harvesting

### 1. Must Hold > 12 Months to Qualify as LTCG

For equity shares and equity MFs, the holding period for LTCG is **more than 12 months**. If you sell before 12 months, it's STCG taxed at 20%.

Harvest only holdings you've held for 12+ months.

### 2. Track Your Total LTCG for the Year

The ₹1.25L exemption is total across all your LTCG for the year (all stocks + all equity MFs combined). If you already have ₹80,000 of LTCG from selling shares earlier, you can only harvest ₹45,000 more before hitting ₹1.25L.

### 3. Re-purchase Timing — Same Day Is Fine

Unlike the US (where "wash sale" rules disallow immediate re-purchase), **India has NO wash sale rule**. You can sell in the morning and re-buy in the afternoon. The cost basis resets immediately.

However, for mutual funds, note:
- Exit load: If there's an exit load period (most equity MFs have 1% exit load for 1 year), selling triggers the exit load. Check your fund's exit load schedule.
- STT (Securities Transaction Tax): Paid on both the sale and re-purchase, but STT is typically 0.1% for equity MF sales.

### 4. Track with FIFO

When you have multiple purchase batches, Indian tax law uses **FIFO (First In, First Out)** for calculating which units are sold. The oldest units are sold first.

This matters when you have a mix of old and recent purchases — the oldest (most likely LTCG-eligible) units are used up first in FIFO.

### 5. Budget 2024 Change — New Rates

Budget 2024 increased LTCG rates:
- Old rate (pre-July 23, 2024): 10% above ₹1 lakh
- New rate (post-July 23, 2024): **12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh**

Grandfathering: Gains on equity held before January 31, 2018 have a special cost-basis calculation (FMV as on Jan 31, 2018 is the deemed cost). This was part of the original 2018 LTCG reintroduction.

## Who Should Do Tax Harvesting

Tax harvesting is most beneficial if:
- You have equity MFs or stocks held for 12+ months with unrealized gains
- Your overall income is in the 20% or 30% slab (tax saving is real and significant)
- You're a long-term investor (buy-and-hold) who otherwise might face a large LTCG tax bill on eventual redemption

It's less useful if:
- Your total LTCG from all sources is likely to stay below ₹1.25L anyway
- You're investing via SIP where each installment creates a different purchase lot (more complex tracking)
- Your fund has significant exit load within 1 year

## Mutual Fund vs Direct Stock Harvesting

| | Equity Mutual Funds | Direct Stocks |
|-|--------------------|--------------|
| Exit load? | Often 1% for 1 year | No exit load |
| Bid-ask spread? | NAV-based (no spread) | Small spread on market |
| STT | 0.001% (sale) + 0.001% (buy) | 0.1% (equity sell) |
| FIFO applies? | Yes (per folio) | Yes (per company) |
| Complexity | Medium (track each SIP lot) | Low for direct stock |

For direct stock investors, tax harvesting is cleaner — just sell the position and re-buy. For MF investors with many SIPs, tracking which lots are eligible (12+ months) adds complexity.

## Tax Harvesting Does NOT Apply to

- **Debt MFs**: No ₹1.25L exemption — debt MF gains are at slab rate regardless of holding period
- **Gold ETFs/Sovereign Gold Bonds**: Different rules
- **STCG**: The ₹1.25L exemption is only for LTCG; short-term gains are taxed at 20%
- **Non-equity**: Property, fixed deposits, bonds — different capital gains rules

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I did SIPs for 3 years. Which units are eligible for LTCG?

Only units purchased 12+ months ago. In March 2026, units purchased before March 2025 are eligible. Your March 2025 SIP installment is exactly 12 months — check whether it's "more than" 12 months. FIFO means your oldest SIP lots are sold first, which is beneficial (more of your holding will be LTCG-eligible).

### Does tax harvesting make sense for someone in the 5% tax bracket?

At 5% slab, LTCG tax (12.5%) is higher than your slab rate. But you still pay 12.5% on LTCG above ₹1.25L — you can't pay "slab rate" on LTCG. So harvesting ₹1.25L still saves you 12.5% × ₹1.25L = ₹15,625 per year. Yes, it makes sense.

### Can my wife and I each harvest ₹1.25L separately?

Yes — the ₹1.25L exemption is **per taxpayer**. If you and your spouse each hold equity independently, you can each harvest ₹1.25L per year = ₹2.5L combined tax-free LTCG per family per year.

### What if I accidentally book more than ₹1.25L LTCG?

Not a disaster — you just pay 12.5% on the excess. For example, if you book ₹1.5L of LTCG, you pay 12.5% × ₹25,000 = ₹3,125. It's a small tax. The point of harvesting is to use up the ₹1.25L exemption, not to avoid tax entirely.
