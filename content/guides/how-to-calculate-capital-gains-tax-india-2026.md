# Capital Gains Tax Calculation India 2026 — Complete Guide

Budget 2024 (effective July 23, 2024) significantly changed capital gains tax rates. This guide has the updated rates and step-by-step calculation examples for every asset class.

---

## Quick Reference: Capital Gains Tax Rates 2026

| Asset | Holding Period | Tax Rate |
|-------|---------------|----------|
| **Listed equity / equity MF** | ≤12 months (STCG) | **20%** (was 15%) |
| **Listed equity / equity MF** | >12 months (LTCG) | **12.5%** above ₹1.25L exemption (was 10% above ₹1L) |
| **Unlisted shares** | ≤24 months | Slab rate |
| **Unlisted shares** | >24 months | **12.5%** (no indexation) |
| **Property (residential/commercial)** | ≤24 months (STCG) | Slab rate |
| **Property** | >24 months (LTCG) | **12.5%** without indexation (Budget 2024 choice) |
| **Debt mutual funds** | Any | Slab rate (Budget 2023 removed LTCG/indexation) |
| **Gold (physical)** | ≤24 months | Slab rate |
| **Gold (physical)** | >24 months | **12.5%** without indexation |
| **Gold ETF** | >12 months | **12.5%** (equity fund rules apply if >65% equity; otherwise debt) |
| **SGB (Sovereign Gold Bond)** | Held to maturity | Tax-free |
| **F&O / Intraday** | Any | Slab rate (business income) |

## Budget 2024 Changes — What Changed from July 23, 2024

| | Before July 23, 2024 | After July 23, 2024 |
|--|---------------------|---------------------|
| Equity STCG | 15% | **20%** |
| Equity LTCG exemption | ₹1,00,000 | **₹1,25,000** |
| Equity LTCG rate | 10% above ₹1L | **12.5%** above ₹1.25L |
| Property LTCG | 20% with indexation | **12.5% without indexation** (see exception below) |

**Property exception**: Properties purchased before July 23, 2024 — you can choose the better of: (a) 12.5% without indexation or (b) 20% with old indexation. This was a relief provision added after backlash.

## Equity Capital Gains Calculation (Shares & Equity MF)

### Example 1: LTCG on Equity Shares

You bought 100 shares of a company at ₹800/share in March 2023 (total ₹80,000).
You sold them in June 2026 at ₹1,500/share (total ₹1,50,000).

- **Holding period**: 3+ years → LTCG
- **Gain**: ₹1,50,000 − ₹80,000 = **₹70,000**
- **Annual LTCG exemption**: ₹1,25,000
- **Taxable LTCG**: ₹70,000 − ₹1,25,000 = **₹0** (below exemption limit)
- **Tax**: ₹0

Now suppose you made ₹2,00,000 LTCG from another stock in the same year:
- Total LTCG = ₹70,000 + ₹2,00,000 = ₹2,70,000
- Taxable LTCG = ₹2,70,000 − ₹1,25,000 = **₹1,45,000**
- Tax = ₹1,45,000 × 12.5% = **₹18,125** + 4% cess = **₹18,850**

### Example 2: STCG on Equity Shares

You bought 200 shares at ₹500 (total ₹1,00,000) in Jan 2026.
You sold them at ₹650 (total ₹1,30,000) in May 2026.

- **Holding period**: 4 months → STCG
- **Gain**: ₹1,30,000 − ₹1,00,000 = **₹30,000**
- **No exemption for STCG**
- **Tax**: ₹30,000 × 20% = ₹6,000 + 4% cess = **₹6,240**

### Equity LTCG Tax Harvesting

The ₹1.25L annual exemption on LTCG resets every financial year (April 1). Strategy:
- Before March 31 each year, sell equity holdings worth enough to book LTCG up to ₹1.25L
- Re-buy the same shares/units next day (or wait T+2 for settlement)
- Cost basis resets to new higher price; future LTCG is calculated from new basis

Over 20 years, this can save significant LTCG tax. See our [LTCG harvesting guide](/guide/ltcg-tax-harvesting-equity-india-guide) for detailed calculations.

## Property (Real Estate) Capital Gains Calculation

### Long-Term (>24 months): Budget 2024 Options

**Option A: 12.5% without indexation** (new default)
**Option B: 20% with indexation** (only for property purchased BEFORE July 23, 2024)

Use whichever gives lower tax.

### Example: Property Sale LTCG

- Property purchased: July 2018 for ₹50,00,000
- Property sold: June 2026 for ₹90,00,000
- Holding period: ~8 years → LTCG

**Option A — 12.5% without indexation:**
- Gain = ₹90,00,000 − ₹50,00,000 = ₹40,00,000
- Tax = ₹40,00,000 × 12.5% = **₹5,00,000**

**Option B — 20% with indexation:**
- Cost Inflation Index (CII) FY 2018-19: 280; FY 2025-26: ~363 (approximate)
- Indexed cost = ₹50L × (363/280) = **₹64,82,143**
- Gain = ₹90L − ₹64.82L = ₹25,17,857
- Tax = ₹25,17,857 × 20% = **₹5,03,571**

**In this case**: Option A is slightly better (₹5L vs ₹5.03L). Choose Option A.

> The CII values are notified by IT department each year. For FY 2026-27, check the latest notification at incometax.gov.in.

### Property LTCG Exemptions (Reinvestment)

You can avoid/reduce LTCG tax on property by reinvesting:

- **Section 54**: Buy another residential house → LTCG exemption (full exemption if entire LTCG reinvested; ₹10 crore cap from Budget 2023)
- **Section 54F**: Sell non-residential asset (land, commercial, gold) → buy residential house → exemption (full amount, not just gains)
- **Section 54EC**: Invest in NHAI/REC bonds within 6 months → up to ₹50L exemption; 5-year lock-in

**Property STCG** (held ≤24 months): Added to income, taxed at slab rate. No exemptions available.

## Debt Mutual Fund Capital Gains (Post Budget 2023)

Debt funds (liquid, gilt, dynamic bond, credit risk, short duration, etc.) no longer have LTCG/STCG distinction or indexation benefit.

**All redemptions taxed at slab rate** — regardless of holding period.

This change applies to debt funds purchased after April 1, 2023. Units bought before that date retain their original LTCG (20% with indexation) treatment.

**Practical impact**: For someone in 30% tax bracket, debt MF returns are now taxed at 30% (same as bank FD). The advantage over FD is: tax is paid only on redemption (FD TDS is annual), giving you deferral benefit.

## Gold Capital Gains

| Gold Type | LTCG (>24 months) | STCG (≤24 months) |
|-----------|------------------|------------------|
| Physical gold | 12.5% | Slab rate |
| Gold ETF | 12.5% (equity rules if >65% in gold equity; typically debt rules) | Slab rate |
| Gold MF FOF | Slab rate (debt fund rules) | Slab rate |
| SGB (held to maturity) | Tax-free | N/A |
| SGB (sold before maturity on exchange) | 12.5% LTCG if >12 months | 20% STCG |

**Best structure for gold taxation**: SGB held to maturity = completely tax-free. Physical gold = worst structure (also has making charges + GST).

## Capital Loss Set-Off Rules

Capital losses can be set off against capital gains (not salary/business income):

| Loss Type | Can Set Off Against |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Short-term capital loss | Any capital gain (STCG or LTCG) |
| Long-term capital loss | LTCG only (not against STCG) |

**Carry forward**: Unabsorbed capital losses can be carried forward for **8 years** (must file ITR by due date to carry forward).

**Example**: You have ₹50,000 STCG from equity + ₹30,000 STCL from another stock.
- Net STCG = ₹50,000 − ₹30,000 = ₹20,000
- Tax = ₹20,000 × 20% = ₹4,000

## Reporting Capital Gains in ITR

Capital gains must be reported in **ITR-2** (not ITR-1). If you have any capital gains in a financial year, you cannot file ITR-1.

Schedules to fill:
- **Schedule CG**: Long-term capital gains (LTCG)
- **Schedule SI**: Special rate income (shows LTCG/STCG separately)
- **Schedule BFLA**: Brought forward losses to set off
- **Schedule CFL**: Losses to carry forward

Capital gains data comes pre-filled in ITR from AIS — verify it matches your broker's P&L statement. Common errors: Missing SIP SFT data, wrong cost basis for old units, dividend reinvestment treated as fresh purchase.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Equity STCG tax rate 2026 — is it 15% or 20%?

**20%** (raised from 15% in Budget 2024, effective July 23, 2024). Applies to listed equity shares, equity mutual funds (65%+ equity), ETFs.

### Is there tax on equity LTCG below ₹1.25L?

No. The first ₹1.25 lakh of LTCG from equity/equity MF in a financial year is completely exempt. Tax at 12.5% applies only above ₹1.25L. You must still report it in ITR-2 if you have capital gains.

### Do I need to pay tax on MF SIP profits?

Yes — each SIP installment's units are treated separately. Units held more than 12 months → LTCG (12.5% above ₹1.25L). Units sold within 12 months → STCG (20%). Your AMC's CAMS/KFintech statement shows this breakup.

### Property sold after 2 years — which option is better, 12.5% or 20% with indexation?

Calculate both and choose the lower tax. For properties bought when inflation was high (2012–2020), indexation sometimes gives a better result. For recently purchased properties in a low-inflation environment, 12.5% without indexation is usually better. Always compute both.

### Can I set off LTCG from property against LTCL from stocks?

Yes — LTCL from equity can be set off against LTCG from property. LTCG from property can also be set off against LTCL from equity.
