# NRI Tax Guide India 2026 — Complete Reference

Indians living abroad often have India-sourced income — property rent, interest, capital gains from Indian investments. Understanding when you're an NRI for tax purposes, what's taxable in India, and how to avoid double taxation is essential. Here's the complete guide.

---

## Are You an NRI? Residency Test

Your residential status for tax determines how much India can tax you:

| Status | Condition |
|--------|-----------|
| **Resident (Ordinary)** | In India 182+ days in the financial year; OR 60+ days in FY AND 365+ days in preceding 4 years |
| **Not Ordinarily Resident (NOR)** | Was NRI for 9 of last 10 years; OR in India ≤729 days in last 7 years |
| **Non-Resident Indian (NRI)** | In India <182 days in the financial year (for those with Indian employment/business) OR <60 days |

**Simple rule for most working abroad**: If you stayed less than 182 days in India in FY 2025-26 (April 2025–March 2026) → you are NRI for that year.

**Returning NRI**: When you come back to India permanently, you may be NOR (Not Ordinarily Resident) for 1–3 years before becoming Resident — this is beneficial as NOR status exempts foreign income.

---

## What is Taxable in India for NRIs?

NRIs are taxed in India **only on income that accrues or arises in India**:

| Income Type | Taxable in India? |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Salary from Indian employer (paid in India) | Yes |
| Rental income from Indian property | Yes |
| Interest on NRO account | Yes (TDS 30%) |
| Interest on NRE account | **NO** — fully exempt |
| Capital gains on Indian stocks/mutual funds | Yes |
| Capital gains on Indian property | Yes |
| Salary earned abroad | No (foreign income, not taxable in India) |
| Interest from foreign bank accounts | No |
| Dividends from foreign companies | No |

---

## NRO vs NRE Account — Key Differences

| Feature | NRO Account | NRE Account |
|---------|------------|------------|
| **What goes in** | Indian income (rent, salary from Indian company, pension) | Foreign earnings (salary from abroad) |
| **Currency** | INR | INR (but funded from foreign currency) |
| **Interest rate** | Similar to regular savings (3–7%) | Similar to regular savings (3–7%) |
| **Interest taxable in India** | **Yes** — TDS at 30% | **No** — fully exempt |
| **Repatriation (send money abroad)** | Up to USD 1 million/year (with documentation) | Fully repatriable (no limit) |
| **Joint account** | With NRI or resident Indian (Indian relative) | Only with NRI |

**NRE account is king**: If you're earning abroad, deposit into NRE — tax-free interest, fully repatriable. Perfect for parking funds you want to bring back or invest in India.

## TDS on NRI Income

Banks and companies deduct TDS on NRI income at higher rates:

| Income | TDS Rate for NRI |
|--------|----------------|
| NRO account interest | 30% + surcharge + cess |
| Rental income | 30% + surcharge |
| Short-term capital gains (equity) | 15% (STCG) |
| Long-term capital gains (equity) | 12.5% + surcharge |
| Long-term capital gains (property) | 12.5% without indexation (Budget 2024) |
| Dividend income from Indian stocks | 20% |

**High TDS on NRO**: 30% TDS on NRO interest is why many NRIs avoid NRO FDs. You can reduce it via DTAA (see below).

---

## DTAA — Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement

India has DTAA with 90+ countries including USA, UK, UAE, Canada, Singapore, Australia, Germany, Netherlands.

**What DTAA does**: Prevents the same income from being taxed twice (in India AND in your country of residence).

**How to use DTAA for lower TDS**:

For NRO interest, dividend income, etc.:
1. Submit **Tax Residency Certificate (TRC)** from the country where you reside to your bank/company
2. Along with Form 10F (declaration form) at incometax.gov.in
3. Bank applies DTAA rate instead of default 30% TDS

**DTAA tax rates on interest** (examples):
- USA-India DTAA: Max 15% on bank interest (vs 30% default)
- UK-India DTAA: Max 15% on interest
- UAE-India: No income tax in UAE; India may still tax the income; file ITR and claim credit

**Claim DTAA credit in your country**: Your country of residence may also allow credit for taxes paid in India — reducing your tax burden there.

## Capital Gains on Indian Property (NRI)

### Selling Indian Property as NRI

**Capital gains tax**:
- **Long-term** (held >2 years): 12.5% (without indexation, Budget 2024 rule for properties sold after July 23, 2024)
- **Short-term** (held ≤2 years): As per slab rates (can be 30% for NRIs at high incomes)

**TDS by buyer**: When an NRI sells Indian property, the buyer must deduct TDS at **20% on LTCG amount** (or 30% if short-term). This is often the full sale price × 20% until Form 13 Lower Deduction Certificate is obtained.

**To reduce buyer TDS**:
1. File for Lower Deduction Certificate at incometax.gov.in (Form 13)
2. Income tax officer may approve lower TDS based on actual capital gain
3. Without this, buyer deducts 20% (or more) of the entire sale amount as TDS

**Repatriating sale proceeds**:
- Route proceeds through NRO account
- Repatriation: Up to USD 1 million/year from NRO account with proper documentation
- Form 15CA and 15CB (CA certificate) required for large remittances

## Capital Gains on Indian Stocks/Mutual Funds (NRI)

NRIs can invest in Indian equities via **PIS (Portfolio Investment Scheme)** account through a designated bank:

| Type | Tax | Notes |
|------|-----|-------|
| STCG (equity, ≤12 months) | 20% | Increased from 15% (Budget 2024) |
| LTCG (equity, >12 months) | 12.5% above ₹1.25L per year | ₹1.25L exemption limit |
| Mutual fund LTCG | 12.5% (equity funds) | Same as direct equity |
| Debt mutual fund | Slab rate | No indexation (Budget 2023 change) |

TDS at source applies for NRIs:
- Broker deducts TDS on capital gains
- File ITR to reconcile and claim refund if TDS > actual tax liability

## Which ITR Form for NRIs?

**ITR-2**: For NRIs with income from:
- Capital gains (property, stocks)
- Rental income from Indian property
- Interest income
- Salary from Indian company

**ITR-1 (Sahaj)**: NRIs CANNOT use ITR-1 — it's only for Resident Indians.

**ITR-3**: If NRI has Indian business income.

---

## NRI Filing Deadline and Process

**Deadline**: July 31, 2026 for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27). Same as resident Indians.

**How to file**:
1. Log in at incometax.gov.in using PAN
2. Verify NRI status in the Personal Information section
3. Fill income details: Indian income only (not foreign)
4. Claim TDS credits from Form 26AS
5. Claim DTAA credit/exemption where applicable
6. e-Verify using Aadhaar OTP (if Aadhaar linked to Indian mobile) or DSC or EVC via net banking

**PAN is mandatory** for NRIs with any Indian income. Apply at NSDL or UTIITSL portals.

## Returning to India — Tax on Foreign Income

When you return to India and become Resident:
- **RNOR status (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident)**: Foreign income still not taxable for 1–3 years
- NRE FD interest: Continues tax-free as long as you're RNOR
- After you become Ordinarily Resident: Global income becomes taxable in India

**Planning tip**: If you're returning to India, maintain RNOR status (stay <182 days in year of return) to protect foreign income and NRE FD interest for 1–2 more years.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I'm in the UAE (no income tax there). Am I still taxed in India on my Indian income?

Yes — India taxes NRI income that arises in India (rental income, NRO interest, capital gains). UAE's zero-tax status protects you from UAE tax, but India's income tax still applies to Indian-source income.

### My NRE FD interest is ₹5 lakh. Is it taxable?

No — NRE account interest is fully exempt from Indian income tax for as long as you are NRI (non-resident). Declare it in your ITR but it's exempt.

### I sold my flat in India while being NRI. The buyer deducted 20% TDS on full sale price. Can I get it back?

Yes — if actual capital gains tax is lower than 20% TDS deducted, file ITR showing the actual LTCG (purchase price vs sale price), and the excess TDS will be refunded to your NRO account. A CA can help calculate actual LTCG.

### Do NRIs get the ₹12 lakh zero-tax benefit under Budget 2025?

The ₹12L zero-tax rebate (Section 87A) is NOT available to NRIs. NRIs are taxed at flat rates without the rebate. Even ₹5L income for an NRI is taxed at the applicable slab rate.

### Can my NRE account be jointly held with my India-resident spouse?

NRE accounts can only be held jointly with another NRI — not with a resident Indian. However, you can add a resident Indian as a nominee (not a joint holder).
