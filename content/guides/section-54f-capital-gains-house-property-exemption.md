# Section 54F — Save LTCG Tax by Investing Sale Proceeds in a House

If you sell any long-term capital asset (gold, shares, mutual funds, land, commercial property, bonds) and reinvest the **entire net sale proceeds** in a new residential house, you can get full exemption from Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax.

This is **Section 54F** of the Income Tax Act — one of the most significant capital gains exemption provisions.

## Section 54F vs Section 54 — Know the Difference

| | Section 54 | Section 54F |
|-|-----------|------------|
| **What you sold** | Residential house property | ANY long-term capital asset EXCEPT house property |
| **What you invest in** | New residential house | New residential house |
| **Investment amount** | Capital gains only | Entire net sale consideration (not just gains) |
| **Exemption calculation** | Proportionate if partial investment | Proportionate to amount invested |

**54F applies when you sell**: Gold, equity shares, equity MF, debt MF, land (agricultural/commercial), bonds, foreign assets, unlisted shares, intellectual property — anything except your residential house.

## Section 54F — Core Conditions

### Condition 1: Asset Must Be Long-Term
- Equity shares/MF: Held > 12 months
- Land, gold, bonds: Held > 24 months
- Unlisted shares: Held > 24 months

### Condition 2: You Must NOT Already Own More Than One Residential House
**At the time of sale**: You cannot own more than **one other residential house** (other than the new one you're buying with the exemption).

If you already own **two or more houses** at the time of sale → Section 54F exemption is NOT available.

This is a common disqualifying condition many people miss.

### Condition 3: Invest in New Residential House
- **Purchase**: Within **1 year before** or **2 years after** the date of sale
- **Construction**: Within **3 years after** the date of sale

**Construction option**: You can construct a new house. The construction must be completed within 3 years.

### Condition 4: The New House Must Be in India
Foreign residential property does not qualify for Section 54F exemption.

### Condition 5: Invest the "Net Consideration" (Not Just Gains)
**Net consideration** = sale price minus any expenses of transfer (brokerage, transfer charges, registration of sale deed).

To get **full exemption**: Invest the entire net consideration.
If you invest **less**: Exemption is proportionate.

### Condition 6: Don't Sell the New House Within 3 Years
If you sell the new house within **3 years** of purchase/construction completion:
- The previously claimed Section 54F exemption is revoked
- Capital gains are added back as income in the year of sale

## How to Calculate Section 54F Exemption

**Formula for partial investment**:
Exemption = (LTCG × Amount Invested) ÷ Net Consideration

**Example**:
- Sold gold (held 5 years) for ₹50,00,000
- LTCG on gold: ₹30,00,000 (after indexed cost — wait, no indexation post Budget 2024)
- LTCG: ₹30,00,000
- Net consideration (sale price minus transfer costs): ₹49,50,000
- Invested in new house: ₹40,00,000

**Exemption = (₹30,00,000 × ₹40,00,000) ÷ ₹49,50,000 = ₹24,24,242**

Remaining LTCG taxable = ₹30,00,000 − ₹24,24,242 = **₹5,75,758**

To get full exemption: Must invest the entire ₹49,50,000 net consideration.

## What About the Remaining Amount? — Capital Gains Account Scheme

If you've sold the asset and can't complete the house purchase by the ITR filing date (July 31 of AY), deposit the **unspent proceeds** in a **Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS)** with a bank.

- Open CGAS at any nationalized bank (SBI, Bank of Baroda, etc.) — both Term Account (Type A: savings-like) and Term Account (Type B: FD-like)
- Deposit the amount before the ITR filing date
- Show this in your ITR under Section 54F/CGAS
- Money must be used for house purchase/construction within the original timeline (2 years for purchase, 3 years for construction)

If unused after the deadline: The amount is treated as LTCG in the year when the deadline passes.

## Budget 2023 Change — ₹10 Crore Cap on Section 54F

**Budget 2023 (effective AY 2024-25 onwards)**: Section 54F exemption is capped at **₹10 crore** of investment. Even if you invest ₹25 crore in a house, the maximum exemption is on investment up to ₹10 crore.

For most investors, this cap isn't an issue — but for high-net-worth individuals selling large asset portfolios, this limits the exemption.

## Practical Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Sold Equity MF (Long-Term), Buying First House
- Sold ₹60L of equity MF held 3 years, LTCG = ₹20L
- Buying first house for ₹55L (within 2 years)
- Own no other house
- You invest entire sale proceeds (₹60L) in the house
- **Full LTCG exemption** — zero tax on ₹20L LTCG

### Scenario 2: Sold Agricultural Land, Buying Apartment
- Sold agricultural land (held 5 years) for ₹80L, LTCG = ₹50L
- You already own one house (no more than 1 → condition satisfied)
- Plan to buy apartment for ₹1.2 crore (2 years timeline)
- Invest ₹78L (net consideration) into apartment down payment + CGAS for balance
- **Full exemption on ₹50L LTCG**

### Scenario 3: Already Own Two Houses → Section 54F Not Available
- Sold ₹40L of gold (LTCG = ₹15L)
- You already own 2 houses at time of gold sale
- Section 54F NOT available — must pay LTCG tax (12.5% = ₹1.875L)

## Section 54EC — Alternative Bond Route

If you don't want to buy a house but want to save LTCG tax on land/property sale:

**Section 54EC**: Invest LTCG amount (up to ₹50L) in **REC/NHAI bonds** within 6 months of sale. Bonds lock for 5 years.

**Key difference**: 54EC is on LTCG amount only (not entire sale consideration). Also only for land/property, not for equities/gold.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I want to use Section 54F but the house will take 3 years to build. What do I do till then?

Deposit the sale proceeds in CGAS (Capital Gains Account Scheme) at a nationalized bank within the ITR filing date. Under-construction house construction within 3 years of the sale date qualifies. Withdraw from CGAS as construction payments are made.

### Can a joint purchase qualify for Section 54F?

Yes — if you're buying jointly, your portion of the investment should cover your net consideration. Courts have generally held that joint ownership of new house counts as long as the assessee has a share in the new property.

### I already own one house. I'm selling equity MF to buy another. Does 54F apply?

Yes — owning **one** house at the time of sale is fine. Section 54F disqualifies only if you own **more than one** house at time of sale. Selling equity MF and buying a second house (with one already owned) qualifies for 54F.

### The gold I'm selling was received as a gift. Does 54F apply?

Yes — if the gift was received from a relative (or through Will/inheritance), and the total holding period (your period + previous owner's period for inherited assets) exceeds 24 months, it's LTCG and Section 54F applies normally.

### Budget 2024 changed gold LTCG to 12.5% without indexation. Does 54F still save tax?

Yes — Section 54F exempts the LTCG entirely (up to full net consideration reinvested). Even without indexation, if you invest the whole sale amount in a new house, LTCG tax is zero.
