# GST on Rent India 2026 — Commercial Property, Residential Rental, and Reverse Charge Rules

Rental income is subject to GST in India — but only in specific situations. Most individual landlords renting out their single property don't need to worry about GST. But commercial landlords, business owners renting their premises, and certain residential arrangements are subject to GST.

## Quick Summary — When GST Applies to Rent

| Situation | GST? |
|-----------|------|
| Commercial property (office/shop/warehouse) rented to business | ✅ 18% GST — landlord must charge |
| Residential property rented to individual for personal residence | ❌ Exempt from GST |
| Residential property rented to **registered business** for employee use | ✅ 18% GST under RCM (tenant pays) |
| Any rent below ₹20L/year from an unregistered landlord | ❌ No GST |

## Commercial Property Rent — 18% GST

If you are a **landlord** renting commercial property (office space, shop, warehouse, factory):

### When Does GST Registration Apply to You?

**Threshold**: If your total annual rental income (all commercial properties combined) exceeds **₹20 lakh**, you must register under GST.

- Below ₹20L/year: No GST registration needed (and no GST on rent)
- Above ₹20L/year: Register for GST, charge 18% GST on rent, file monthly/quarterly GST returns

**Special states threshold**: ₹10 lakh for North-Eastern and some special category states.

### How GST on Commercial Rent Works

You (landlord) add 18% GST to your rent invoice:
- Monthly rent: ₹1,00,000
- GST @ 18%: ₹18,000
- Total invoice: ₹1,18,000

Tenant pays ₹1,18,000. You collect ₹18,000 GST and deposit it with the government.

**ITC benefit for commercial tenants**: The tenant (business) can claim Input Tax Credit (ITC) of ₹18,000 against their own GST liability — effectively reducing the GST burden on the tenant.

### GST Registration Even Below ₹20L?

If you **voluntarily register** for GST (even below ₹20L), you charge 18% and the tenant gets ITC. Some landlords do this if tenants specifically want GST invoices for ITC purposes.

## Residential Property Rent — Generally Exempt

**Pure residential rent to individuals for personal use**: Exempt from GST since 1947 (and under GST post-2017).

If you rent your flat to an individual (salaried person, family) for their residence:
- No GST charged
- No GST registration needed (solely for this residential rental)
- Rent is NOT subject to GST even if rent is ₹2L/month

**Condition for exemption**: The use must be for **residential purposes** by the tenant.

## The Key Exception — Residential Rent to Registered Business (RCM)

**October 2022 amendment** — A major change that confused many:

If a **GST-registered entity** (company, firm, LLP, even proprietor with GST) rents a **residential property** (flat, bungalow, apartment) for their employees' use:

→ **18% GST applies under Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM)**

**Who pays in RCM?**: The **tenant (business entity)** pays the GST directly to the government — NOT the landlord. The landlord doesn't need to charge or collect GST.

**ITC on this RCM payment**: The business can claim ITC on the GST it paid under RCM (if it's for business employees, ITC may be blocked under Section 17(5) — check with CA).

**If tenant is an individual (NOT a GST-registered business)**: RCM does NOT apply. Normal exemption continues.

**Example**:
- ABC Pvt Ltd (GST registered) rents a flat for ₹50,000/month for its outstation employee
- GST under RCM: ₹50,000 × 18% = ₹9,000/month — ABC pays this directly to government
- Landlord: Not affected, receives ₹50,000 rent, doesn't need to be GST registered for this

## Commercial vs Residential — How Is "Use" Determined?

| Property | Tenant Use | GST? |
|----------|-----------|------|
| Residential flat rented to doctor for clinic | Commercial use → **18% GST** by landlord if registered |
| Commercial office rented to IT company | Commercial → **18% GST** |
| Residential flat rented to IT company for employee | Business tenant → **RCM 18% by tenant** |
| Residential flat rented to salaried person for home | Residential → **Exempt** |

The property's classification (residential vs commercial) AND the purpose of use both matter.

## GST vs Income Tax on Rental Income

| Tax | Applies To |
|-----|-----------|
| **Income Tax** | All rental income above basic exemption limit |
| **GST** | Only commercial rent (landlord > ₹20L threshold) or residential rent to registered businesses (RCM by tenant) |

These are independent. You pay income tax on rental income (after deductions) AND separately comply with GST. A commercial landlord above ₹20L files both income tax (rental income in ITR) and GST (monthly GSTR-1, GSTR-3B).

## GST Input Tax Credit on Rent Paid by Businesses

If your business pays commercial rent:
- Rent invoice with 18% GST → You can claim ITC on that 18% GST
- ITC reduces your own GST outflow

This is why commercial tenants prefer landlords who are GST-registered — the ITC benefit makes the effective rent lower.

## Penalty for Not Registering — Landlord

If your commercial rental income exceeds ₹20L and you don't register:
- 100% penalty on tax evaded
- Interest on late payment
- GST authorities can issue a notice

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I own a small shop rented to a grocer at ₹15,000/month (₹1.8L/year). Do I need GST?

No — ₹1.8L annual rental income is below the ₹20L threshold. No GST registration needed, no GST to charge.

### I rent two offices — one at ₹8L/year and another at ₹15L/year (total ₹23L). Do I need GST?

Yes — total commercial rent ₹23L exceeds ₹20L threshold. You must register for GST and charge 18% on both rentals.

### My startup company leases a furnished apartment for its CEO's residence. Does GST apply?

Yes — from October 2022, if your company (GST-registered) rents a residential property for employee/CEO use, GST at 18% applies under RCM. Your company pays ₹18% of monthly rent to the government via RCM. Check with your CA whether ITC is available on this (there are restrictions on employee accommodation ITC under Section 17(5)).

### I rent my flat to an NRI who works for an MNC. Is that residential or commercial?

If the NRI is using the flat as their personal residence (not running a business from it), it's residential use — GST exempt. Even though the tenant may be employed by a multinational, personal residence use keeps it exempt.
