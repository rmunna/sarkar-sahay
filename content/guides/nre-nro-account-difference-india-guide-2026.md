# NRE vs NRO Account India 2026 — Complete Guide

If you're an NRI (Non-Resident Indian) or recently moved abroad, you'll encounter NRE and NRO accounts. They serve completely different purposes. Opening the wrong one for your use case has real tax and repatriation consequences.

---

## Quick Comparison: NRE vs NRO vs FCNR

| Feature | NRE (Non-Resident External) | NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) | FCNR (Foreign Currency Non-Resident) |
|---------|----------------------------|-----------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| **Currency** | INR | INR | USD, GBP, EUR, JPY, AUD, CAD |
| **Purpose** | Parking foreign earnings in India (in INR) | Managing Indian income (rent, dividends) | Parking foreign earnings in foreign currency |
| **Funded from** | Foreign income only | Indian income + foreign income | Foreign income only |
| **Interest in India** | Tax-free | Taxable (30% TDS) | Tax-free |
| **Repatriation** | Fully and freely repatriable | Max $1 million USD per year | Fully repatriable |
| **Joint account** | With another NRI only | With resident Indian allowed | With another NRI only |

---

## NRE Account — For Parking Abroad Earnings in India

**Use NRE when**: You earn in USD/GBP/AED and want to send money to India.

- Your salary/income abroad → convert to INR → deposit in NRE account
- NRE savings/FD interest is **completely tax-free in India** — no TDS
- Full amount (principal + interest) can be **taken back abroad** anytime — fully repatriable
- Cannot receive Indian-source income (rent, dividends, EPF) in NRE account

**Best for**: IT professionals in US/UK/UAE/Canada, NRIs who want to keep savings in India while being able to repatriate freely.

**NRE FD rates** (2026): SBI: 6.5–7.1%, HDFC: 6.6–7.25%, ICICI: 6.7–7.3% (varies by tenure). All interest is tax-free.

### Tax on NRE Interest

Interest on NRE savings and FD = **Tax-free in India** under Section 10(4) of Income Tax Act.

**If you return to India**: Tax-free status continues for 2 years after you become RNOR (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident). After you become fully Resident, NRE interest becomes taxable.

---

## NRO Account — For Indian Income

**Use NRO when**: You have income sources IN India — rent from property, dividends, pension, EPF maturity, sale of property.

- Can receive both Indian income and foreign remittances
- Interest on NRO FD is taxable in India at **30% TDS** (higher of flat 30% or treaty rate — no basic exemption applicable)
- Repatriation: Up to **$1 million USD per financial year** (with CA certificate and Form 15CB/15CA)
- Joint account with a resident Indian is allowed (useful for managing parents' accounts)

**Best for**: NRIs with rental property in India, receiving pension or EPF, getting dividend income from Indian stocks/MF.

### Tax on NRO Interest

Interest on NRO savings and FD: **TDS at 30% (plus cess = 31.2%)** deducted by bank.

Lower TDS under DTAA: If your country has a Double Tax Avoidance Agreement with India (US: 15%, UK: 15%, UAE: None — UAE has no income tax, so no treaty benefit on this), you can submit a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from your country to your bank to reduce TDS.

**File ITR in India**: If NRO interest + other Indian income requires ITR filing, you can claim TDS credit and get refund if actual tax liability is lower.

## Which Account to Open First?

**Scenario 1: Just moved abroad, have Indian salary history**
- Convert existing savings account to **NRO** account immediately (mandatory under FEMA)
- Open a new **NRE** account to receive your foreign salary

**Scenario 2: Getting salary abroad, no Indian income**
- Just **NRE** is sufficient
- Add NRO if you buy property in India (for rental income)

**Scenario 3: Indian rental income + foreign salary**
- Need both: **NRO** for rent deposits, **NRE** for foreign salary
- Transfer from NRO to NRE: Allowed after paying applicable taxes (funds in NRO that have been taxed can be transferred to NRE)

## How to Open NRE / NRO Account

### Online (from abroad)

Most major Indian banks allow NRI account opening online:
- **SBI**: SBI's NRI banking portal or branch in your country
- **HDFC**: HDFC NRI account opening at hdfc.com/nri
- **ICICI**: ICICI NRI Banking online
- **Axis/Kotak/Yes Bank**: Online NRI account opening available

**Documents needed** (varies slightly by bank):
- PAN card (mandatory; [get PAN for NRI](/guide/pan-card-nri-apply-online))
- Passport copy (with visa page)
- Proof of NRI status (work permit, employment visa, or residency permit of the country you're in)
- Overseas address proof (utility bill, bank statement in country of residence)
- India address proof (Aadhaar, voter ID, property document)
- Photograph

### At Indian Bank Branch (while visiting India)

Most convenient — walk-in to any major bank branch in India with documents. Account opened same day.

## FCNR Account — Foreign Currency Option

FCNR (Foreign Currency Non-Resident) account lets you keep money in foreign currency — no exchange rate risk.

- Available in: USD, GBP, EUR, JPY, AUD, CAD
- Fully repatriable (principal and interest)
- Interest tax-free in India
- Tenures: 1–5 years FD only (no savings account)

**Best for**: NRIs who want higher interest rates (FCNR FD rates are often better than keeping money in US savings accounts) without converting to INR.

**Current FCNR rates** (June 2026): USD 1-year FD: 4.5–5.5% at major Indian banks. Compare to US savings: 4.5–5% — broadly similar with the advantage of fully repatriable + tax-free.

## Can NRIs Invest in Mutual Funds?

Yes — NRIs can invest in most Indian mutual funds via NRE or NRO account.

| Route | Account | Repatriation |
|-------|---------|--------------|
| Via NRE | NRE account linked to MF folio | Returns repatriable (fully) |
| Via NRO | NRO account linked to MF folio | Returns repatriable up to $1M/year |

**KYC requirement**: NRI KYC is required. Process: submit PAN, passport, visa, overseas address proof, FATCA declaration to your AMC or through a broker like MF Central.

**US and Canada NRIs**: Some AMCs (HDFC, SBI, ICICI, Franklin) accept US/Canada NRI investments but some don't (due to FATCA compliance burden). Check before choosing AMC.

**Tax on MF returns for NRIs**: Same LTCG/STCG rates as residents — TDS deducted at source by AMC (20% on STCG, 12.5% on LTCG). File ITR in India to claim excess TDS as refund.

## Can NRIs Invest in Indian Stocks?

Yes — via **Portfolio Investment Scheme (PIS)** route.

1. Open PIS account with a designated bank (SBI, HDFC, Axis, etc.)
2. Link your NRE/NRO account
3. Open demat account with broker (Zerodha, HDFC Securities, etc.) linked to PIS
4. All stock purchases and sales go through this linked account
5. PIS has limits: Up to 5% of listed company's paid-up capital by any single NRI

**Tax**: Same as residents — STCG 20%, LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25L.

## Repatriation Rules — How to Send Money Back Abroad

### From NRE account
- Any amount, anytime — no restrictions. No paperwork needed.
- Just wire from your NRE account to your foreign bank account.

### From NRO account
- Up to **$1 million USD per financial year**
- Need: Form 15CB (CA certificate) + Form 15CA (online self-declaration at incometax.gov.in)
- Also need: Bank's own repatriation request form + proof of source of funds

**Form 15CB**: CA certifies that taxes have been paid on the amount being remitted. CA fees: ₹2,000–₹5,000 typically.

**Exceeding $1M**: Prior RBI approval needed. Very unusual for individuals.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I'm an NRI. Do I need to convert my existing savings account?

Yes — under FEMA, once you become an NRI, you CANNOT maintain a regular resident savings account. You must:
1. Convert existing savings to NRO account (inform your bank)
2. Open NRE account for foreign earnings
Non-compliance technically violates FEMA — but most banks will notify you when they detect your NRI status.

### Can I have both NRE and NRO accounts at the same bank?

Yes — most NRIs have both. NRE for foreign salary remittances, NRO for Indian income. Many banks offer a linked combo.

### NRE FD vs FCNR FD — which is better?

If you have no currency risk tolerance: FCNR (no INR/USD exchange fluctuation). If you're bullish on INR appreciating vs USD: NRE FD (you benefit if INR strengthens). Currently, NRE FD rates (7%) > FCNR USD rates (5%) — but exchange rate movement can change the outcome.

### Is interest on NRE account taxable in home country (US, UK, UAE)?

Tax in your country of residence depends on that country's tax laws. In the US, global income is taxable — NRE FD interest is taxable in the US (not India). Report in US tax return. In UAE, there is no income tax. In UK, interest is taxable.

### My parents are in India. Can they operate my NRO account?

Yes — you can add them as a joint holder or grant Power of Attorney. NRO accounts can have joint holder with a resident Indian. NRE accounts can only have joint holder with another NRI.
