# Schengen Visa from India 2026 — Complete Application Guide

A Schengen visa is a single visa that lets you travel across 27 European countries. No separate visa needed for each country — one stamp, one fee, travel across the entire Schengen Area. Here's how to apply from India.

---

## Schengen Area — Which 27 Countries?

| Region | Countries |
|--------|----------|
| Western Europe | France, Germany, Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg, Austria, Switzerland, Liechtenstein |
| Southern Europe | Spain, Portugal, Italy, Greece, Malta, Slovenia, Croatia |
| Northern Europe | Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland, Iceland |
| Eastern/Central Europe | Czech Republic, Poland, Hungary, Slovakia |
| Baltic | Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania |
| Small states | Vatican (de facto) |

**NOT in Schengen** (need separate visa): UK, Ireland, Bulgaria, Romania, Cyprus, Kosovo.

---

## Which Consulate Do You Apply At?

The Schengen rule: Apply at the consulate of the country where you'll spend **the most nights**. If equal nights in multiple countries, apply at the **country of first entry**.

**Example**: Paris 4 nights → Amsterdam 3 nights → Berlin 4 nights (first entry: Paris). Apply at **French consulate** (tie: most nights + first entry).

**Exception**: If you have a connecting flight through a Schengen country but your main destination is another, apply at your main destination's consulate.

---

## Visa Types — Which Schengen Visa Do You Need?

| Type | Validity | Max Stay |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Single Entry (C)** | Up to 90 days | 90 days in any 180-day period |
| **Double Entry** | Up to 90 days | 90 days in any 180-day period |
| **Multiple Entry** | 1–5 years | 90 days in any 180-day period |

**For first-time applicants**: Single or double entry is typical. After a history of clean Schengen travel, you may get 1–5 year multiple-entry.

**The 90/180 rule**: In any rolling 180-day window, you cannot spend more than 90 days in the entire Schengen Area. This applies even with a multiple-entry visa.

## Documents Required — Standard Checklist

### Mandatory for All

| Document | Details |
|----------|---------|
| **Valid passport** | Valid for 3 months beyond return date; at least 2 blank pages |
| **Visa application form** | Filled and signed; download from consulate or VFS website |
| **Photo** | 35×45mm, white background, recent (within 6 months); consulate-specific specs |
| **Travel insurance** | Minimum €30,000 coverage; valid for entire Schengen trip; medical emergencies + repatriation |
| **Round-trip flight itinerary** | Confirmed booking (not final ticket — use dummy ticket service or book refundable) |
| **Hotel bookings** | All accommodation bookings for entire trip duration |
| **Travel itinerary** | Day-by-day plan showing cities, activities |

### Financial Documents (Critical)

| Document | What It Shows |
|----------|--------------|
| Bank statements (last 3 months) | ₹5,000–7,000 per day minimum (unofficial benchmark) |
| Savings account balance | Sufficient funds for entire trip |
| ITR (last 2 years) | Income stability |
| Form 16 / salary slips (3 months) | Employment income |
| Fixed deposits, investments | Financial stability |

**Key benchmark**: Consulates typically want to see approximately €50–€70 per day in your bank account for the trip duration. For a 10-day trip: show ₹50,000–70,000 clearly available.

### Employment/Business Documents

| Applicant Type | Documents |
|---------------|----------|
| **Salaried** | Leave letter, employment letter on letterhead (confirming salary, position, leave approval), NOC to travel |
| **Self-employed** | Business registration (GST certificate/Udyam), CA income certificate, IT returns |
| **Student** | Enrollment letter from college, NOC from college, parent's financial documents |
| **Retired** | Pension documents, bank statements |

## Mandatory Travel Insurance Requirements

**This is non-negotiable** — no insurance = visa rejection.

- **Coverage**: Minimum €30,000 for medical expenses
- **Scope**: Entire Schengen Area (not just the main destination)
- **Period**: Covers your entire travel dates (from first entry to last exit)
- **Additional**: Should cover medical repatriation

**Where to buy**: AXA, Bajaj Allianz, HDFC Ergo, Tata AIG, Religare all offer Schengen travel insurance. Cost: ₹1,500–3,500 for a 10-day trip.

## Where to Apply — VFS Global or Consulate

Most European countries use **VFS Global** as the visa application center:
- Book appointment at [vfsglobal.com](https://www.vfsglobal.com/en/individuals/index.html)
- Select: India → Europe → specific country
- Choose city (Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, etc.)

**Appointment wait times (2026)**: 2–8 weeks depending on country and city. French, German, Italian consulates in Mumbai/Delhi tend to be busiest.

**TLScontact**: Some countries (France for some cities) use TLScontact instead of VFS. Check the specific consulate's page.

## Step-by-Step Application Process

### Step 1: Determine Which Consulate to Apply At

Based on your itinerary (country with most nights, or first entry country).

### Step 2: Book Appointment

At VFS Global (or TLScontact) for your chosen consulate city. Book as early as possible — appointments often fill 3–6 weeks out.

### Step 3: Prepare Documents

Compile all documents in the checklist above. For itinerary and hotel: tentative bookings are fine (refundable), but they should show specific dates and locations.

### Step 4: Submit at VFS Center

Bring originals + self-attested photocopies of everything. VFS staff scans and submits to consulate. **Biometrics** (fingerprints) collected at VFS if not already on file (valid for 59 months).

### Step 5: Wait for Processing

Standard processing: **15 working days** (some consulates state 30). Do not book non-refundable tickets or finalize hotels until visa is stamped.

**Status tracking**: Most VFS portals let you track application status with your reference number.

### Step 6: Passport Collection / Courier

Collect passport (with visa) at VFS, or opt for courier delivery (extra fee). Verify the visa details: dates, number of entries, validity period.

## What the Visa Stamp Shows — Decode It

Example stamp: `VALID: 01.09.2026 — 30.09.2026 / ENTRIES: 01 / DURATION: 15`

| Field | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| VALID FROM/TO | The period within which you must enter and exit |
| ENTRIES | 01 = single; 02 = double; MULT = multiple entry |
| DURATION | Maximum days you can stay (≤ validity window) |
| TYPE: C | Short-stay Schengen visa (up to 90 days) |

**Common mistake**: VALID means the visa is usable within that window — NOT that you can stay the entire period. DURATION = actual stay allowed.

## Common Rejection Reasons for Indian Applicants

| Reason | How to Fix |
|--------|-----------|
| Insufficient funds in bank | Ensure clear funds (no sudden deposits just before applying — it looks suspicious) |
| No travel insurance or inadequate coverage | Buy before applying; ensure €30K minimum |
| Vague itinerary | Provide specific day-by-day plan with hotel names and locations |
| No strong ties to India | Strengthen with property, family, or job documents |
| Too short employment history | 2+ years at same employer is ideal |
| Prior Schengen rejection not disclosed | Must be honest about rejection history |
| Passport too close to expiry | Passport must be valid 3+ months beyond return date |

## Applying for Multiple Countries — Tips

### Entry Strategy

For a multi-country trip (e.g., Germany → France → Italy → Switzerland):
- Apply at **Germany** if first point of entry
- Or apply at **France** if most nights are in France
- Doesn't matter where you enter once you have the visa

### Countries Worth Applying At

Some nationalities find certain consulates more "friendly" — this is somewhat anecdotal, but German and Dutch consulates tend to process straightforwardly with complete documents.

## After the Visa — Travel Rules to Follow

- **Do not overstay**: The 90/180 rule is strictly tracked. Overstay = ban of 1–5 years from Schengen
- **Entry country**: You should enter through the country whose consulate issued your visa (first entry ideally at issuing country, especially for single-entry)
- **Registration**: Some countries require registration if staying >7 days in one place (accommodation registration is usually done by hotels automatically)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I apply for a 2-year or 5-year multiple-entry Schengen visa as a first-time applicant?

Generally no — first-time applicants typically get single or double entry. After 1–2 clean Schengen trips, you qualify for longer multi-entry visas.

### Is VFS appointment mandatory or can I apply directly at the consulate?

For most European countries in India, VFS Global is the only submission point. Direct consulate submission is rarely available.

### I have a US B1/B2 visa. Do I still need a Schengen visa?

Yes — US visa holders from India still need a Schengen visa. The US visa doesn't grant Schengen entry.

### My trip is 10 days. How much money should I show in bank statements?

As a rough guide, ₹5,000–7,000 per day — so ₹50,000–70,000 for 10 days, clearly available (not just accumulated over 3 months). Ideally maintain this as a stable balance, not a sudden transfer.

### Rejection on my last application. How soon can I reapply?

There's no mandatory wait period. You can reapply immediately, but address the exact rejection reason with stronger documentation.
