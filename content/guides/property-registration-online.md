# Property Registration Online: Process, Documents & Fees in India

Property registration is a mandatory legal process when buying or selling property in India. Under the **Registration Act, 1908**, all property transactions must be registered with the **Sub-Registrar of Assurances**. Many states now offer online e-registration to simplify the process. This guide covers the complete property registration process, required documents, stamp duty, and fees.

## What is Property Registration?

Property registration is the legal process of recording the transfer of property ownership with the government. The **sale deed** is executed and registered at the **Sub-Registrar Office (SRO)** of the jurisdiction where the property is located.

**Key points:**
- Registration makes the property transfer **legally valid and enforceable**
- The registered document serves as **proof of ownership**
- Without registration, the sale deed has **no legal value** in court
- Registration must be done **within 4 months** of executing the sale deed

---

## Who Needs to Register Property?

Property registration is required for:
- **Sale/purchase** of land, flat, house, or plot
- **Gift deed** — transferring property as a gift
- **Lease agreements** exceeding 11 months
- **Partition deed** — dividing jointly owned property
- **Exchange deed** — exchanging one property for another
- **Power of Attorney** (in some states)

---

## Documents Required for Property Registration

### Mandatory Documents
- **Sale deed** (drafted on stamp paper of appropriate value)
- **Aadhaar card** of buyer and seller
- **PAN card** of buyer and seller (mandatory for transactions above ₹10 lakh)
- **Passport-size photographs** (2 each of buyer and seller)
- **Identity proof** of two witnesses with photographs
- **Property documents** — previous sale deed, title deed, or allotment letter
- **Encumbrance Certificate (EC)** — proving the property is free from legal dues
- **Property tax receipts** (latest paid)
- **NOC from housing society** (for flats/apartments)

### Additional Documents (if applicable)
- **Power of Attorney** (if registering through an agent)
- **Building plan approval** and occupancy certificate (for new constructions)
- **Mutation certificate** from the municipal corporation
- **Bank NOC** (if property had a home loan)
- **TDS challan** (Form 26QB) if property value exceeds ₹50 lakh

## Online Property Registration (E-Registration) Process

Several states offer e-registration. Here is the general process:

### Step 1: Check if Your State Supports E-Registration

| State | E-Registration Portal |
|-------|----------------------|
| Uttar Pradesh | [igrsup.gov.in](https://igrsup.gov.in/) |
| Maharashtra | [igrmaharashtra.gov.in](https://igrmaharashtra.gov.in/) |
| Karnataka | [kaveri2.karnataka.gov.in](https://kaveri2.karnataka.gov.in/) |
| Tamil Nadu | [tnreginet.gov.in](https://tnreginet.gov.in/) |
| Rajasthan | [registration.rajasthan.gov.in](https://registration.rajasthan.gov.in/) |
| Telangana | [registration.telangana.gov.in](https://registration.telangana.gov.in/) |
| Andhra Pradesh | [registration.ap.gov.in](https://registration.ap.gov.in/) |

### Step 2: Register on the Portal
1. Visit your state's e-registration portal
2. Create an account using your mobile number and email
3. Complete profile with Aadhaar verification

### Step 3: Book an Appointment
1. Log in to the portal
2. Select **"Property Registration"** or **"Document Registration"**
3. Choose the **Sub-Registrar Office** nearest to the property location
4. Select an available **date and time slot**

### Step 4: Pay Stamp Duty & Registration Fee Online
1. Calculate stamp duty using the portal's calculator
2. Pay **stamp duty + registration fee** online via net banking/UPI/demand draft
3. Download the e-stamp certificate or pay via physical stamp paper

### Step 5: Visit the Sub-Registrar Office
1. Both buyer and seller must appear with **2 witnesses**
2. Carry all original documents and 2 sets of photocopies
3. The Sub-Registrar verifies documents and identities
4. **Biometric verification** (fingerprint/photo) of all parties
5. Sign the sale deed in the presence of the Sub-Registrar

### Step 6: Collect Registered Document
1. The Sub-Registrar registers the document and assigns a **registration number**
2. Collect the registered sale deed (usually within **3-7 working days**)
3. Some states provide the registered document on the same day

## Important Tips

1. **Get the property valued** — stamp duty is calculated on the **higher of** the sale price or the government guideline value (circle rate/ready reckoner rate)
2. **Verify the title** thoroughly — check at least 30 years of ownership history and get an [Encumbrance Certificate](/guide/encumbrance-certificate-apply)
3. **Pay TDS** — if property value exceeds ₹50 lakh, the buyer must deduct 1% TDS (Form 26QB) before registration
4. **Women buyers get discounts** — many states offer 1-2% lower stamp duty for female property owners
5. **Keep multiple copies** — get 2-3 certified copies of the registered sale deed for bank loans and future reference

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Q1. Can property registration be done completely online?
No. While appointment booking, stamp duty payment, and document submission can be done online in many states, **physical presence** of buyer, seller, and witnesses at the Sub-Registrar Office is mandatory for biometric verification and signing.

### Q2. What is the penalty for late registration?
If the sale deed is not registered within **4 months** of execution, a penalty of up to **10 times the registration fee** may be charged. The Sub-Registrar may also refuse registration after 4 months.

### Q3. Is stamp duty different for men and women?
Yes, in several states like **UP, Rajasthan, Delhi, and Haryana**, women buyers pay 1-2% less stamp duty. Joint registration with a female co-owner can also reduce stamp duty.

### Q4. What is the difference between stamp duty and registration fee?
**Stamp duty** is a tax paid to the state government on the property transaction. **Registration fee** is the charge for recording the document with the Sub-Registrar. Both are mandatory.

### Q5. Can I register property without an Aadhaar card?
In most states, **Aadhaar is now mandatory** for property registration due to biometric verification requirements. Some states accept passport or voter ID as alternative identity proof.

### Q6. What is guideline value / circle rate / ready reckoner rate?
It is the **minimum value** set by the government for property transactions in a specific area. Stamp duty is calculated on the higher of the actual sale price or this guideline value. You cannot register property below the guideline value.

### Q7. Do I need a lawyer for property registration?
While not legally mandatory, it is **highly recommended** to hire a lawyer to draft the sale deed, verify the title, and ensure all legal requirements are met.
