# How to Change Aadhaar Address Without Address Proof Documents

Moved to a new city for work or studies but don't have address proof documents like utility bills, rent agreement, or property papers? UIDAI provides two official methods to change your [Aadhaar card](/guide/aadhaar-card-apply-online) address even without traditional address proof documents:

1. **Address Validation Letter (AVL)** — UIDAI sends a secret code to your new address via post
2. **Head of Family (HoF) consent** — A family member or landlord with Aadhaar at that address verifies you

## Method 1: Address Validation Letter (AVL)

This is the most popular method for tenants, students, and working professionals who don't have address proof at their new location.

### How It Works

UIDAI sends a physical letter containing a **Secret Code** to your new address. You then enter this code online to verify and update your address.

### Step-by-Step Process

1. Visit [myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in](https://myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in/)
2. Click **Login** and enter your Aadhaar number
3. Enter the OTP received on your Aadhaar-linked mobile number
4. Go to **Update Aadhaar** → **Address Update**
5. Select **Address Validation Letter** option (when asked for address proof, choose "I don't have valid document")
6. Enter your **complete new address** with correct PIN code
7. Verify the address on the map using the **Bhuvan portal integration**
8. Submit the request
9. Pay the fee of **₹50** via online payment
10. A **Service Request Number (SRN)** will be generated — save this
11. UIDAI will send a physical letter with a **Secret Code** to your new address within **7–14 days**

### After Receiving the Letter

1. Visit [myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in](https://myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in/) again
2. Log in with your Aadhaar and OTP
3. Go to **Update Aadhaar** → **Verify Address by Secret Code**
4. Enter your **SRN** and the **Secret Code** from the letter
5. Submit — your address will be updated within **2–5 working days**

> **Important:** The secret code is valid for **30 days** from the date of letter generation. If the letter doesn't arrive or the code expires, you can request a new letter.

## Method 2: Head of Family (HoF) / Introducer Consent

If someone in your household already has Aadhaar registered at that address, they can act as your "Head of Family" to help you update your address.

### Who Can Be Head of Family?

- Your landlord or property owner
- A family member already living at that address with Aadhaar
- Any Aadhaar holder whose address matches your new address

### Step-by-Step Process

1. Visit [myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in](https://myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in/)
2. Log in with your Aadhaar and OTP
3. Go to **Update Aadhaar** → **Address Update**
4. Select **Head of Family / Consent-based Update**
5. Enter the **Aadhaar number of the Head of Family** (the person consenting)
6. Enter your new address
7. Submit the request
8. The Head of Family will receive an **OTP/consent request** on their Aadhaar-linked mobile number
9. They must approve the consent
10. Once approved, your address will be updated within **2–5 working days**

> **Note:** The Head of Family's address details will NOT be shared with you. UIDAI only verifies that the address matches.

## Fees

| Method | Fee |

| Head of Family consent (online) | ₹50 |
| Aadhaar Enrolment Centre (offline with documents) | ₹50 |

## What If Both Methods Fail?

If you cannot use AVL or HoF consent:

1. **Get a rent agreement** — Even an unregistered rent agreement on stamp paper with landlord details can work as address proof at an [Aadhaar enrolment centre](/guide/aadhaar-card-apply-online)
2. **Bank statement** — Open a bank account at your new address and get a statement after 3 months
3. **Employer letter** — Get a letter from your employer on company letterhead mentioning your current address
4. **Gas connection bill** — Apply for a gas connection at your new address

See our complete [Aadhaar address change guide](/guide/aadhaar-address-change-online) for the full list of accepted documents.

## How to Track Your Address Update

1. Visit [myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in](https://myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in/)
2. Click **Track Update Request**
3. Enter your **Aadhaar number** and **SRN** (Service Request Number)
4. View the current status of your address update

## Important Tips

- The Address Validation Letter method works even if you have zero documents at the new address
- The Secret Code letter is sent via **India Post** — make sure your address and PIN code are accurate
- Head of Family consent is the fastest method if someone at the address can help
- Your Aadhaar-linked mobile number must be active to receive OTPs for both methods
- After address update, download your updated e-Aadhaar from [myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in](https://myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in/)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I change my Aadhaar address without any documents?
Yes. UIDAI provides the **Address Validation Letter** method where a secret code is sent to your new address via post. No address proof documents are required.

### How long does the Address Validation Letter take to arrive?
The letter typically arrives within **7–14 days** via India Post. In remote areas, it may take up to 21 days.

### What if the Address Validation Letter doesn't arrive?
You can request a new letter through the myAadhaar portal using your SRN. If repeated attempts fail, visit the nearest Aadhaar enrolment centre.

### Can my landlord help update my Aadhaar address?
Yes. Your landlord can act as **Head of Family** and provide consent for your address update. They will receive an OTP for verification but their details remain confidential.

### Is the ₹50 fee refundable if the update fails?
No. The fee is non-refundable. However, if the letter doesn't arrive, you can request a resend without additional charges (in some cases).

### Can I update my address from another city?
Yes. Both the AVL and HoF methods work online from anywhere. You only need access to your Aadhaar-linked mobile number for OTP verification.

### How many times can I change my Aadhaar address?
There is no official limit on the number of times you can update your Aadhaar address. However, frequent changes may trigger additional verification.
