# Section 80EEA — ₹1.5 Lakh Additional Deduction for First-Time Home Buyers

If you took a home loan between **April 1, 2019 and March 31, 2022** to buy an affordable house, you may be eligible for an **additional ₹1.5 lakh deduction** on home loan interest under **Section 80EEA** — every year until the loan is fully repaid.

This is on top of the ₹2 lakh Section 24b deduction, potentially giving you **₹3.5 lakh total home loan interest deduction** per year.

> **Important**: The scheme has expired for new loans (no new loans after March 31, 2022 qualify). But if you took the loan before that date, you can **continue claiming 80EEA every year** for the entire loan tenure.

## Section 80EEA — Eligibility Conditions

All conditions must be met simultaneously:

| Condition | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Loan sanction date** | Between April 1, 2019 and March 31, 2022 |
| **Stamp duty value of property** | ≤ ₹45 lakh |
| **First-time buyer** | You must NOT own any other residential property on the date the loan is sanctioned |
| **Loan from** | Banks, housing finance companies, or other notified financial institutions |
| **Section 80EE not claimed** | You must not already be claiming the older Section 80EE deduction |

## How Much Can You Claim?

| Deduction | Section | Maximum Per Year |
|-----------|---------|-----------------|
| Home loan interest (regular) | Section 24b | **₹2,00,000** |
| Additional interest (first-time/affordable) | Section 80EEA | **₹1,50,000** |
| **Combined maximum** | | **₹3,50,000** |

**Note**: You claim up to ₹1.5L under 80EEA **only on the portion of interest that exceeds the ₹2L already claimed under Section 24b**.

In practice:
- If your total home loan interest in the year was ₹3,50,000:
  - Section 24b: ₹2,00,000
  - Section 80EEA: ₹1,50,000
  - Total deduction: ₹3,50,000 (full interest claimed)

- If your total interest was ₹2,80,000:
  - Section 24b: ₹2,00,000
  - Section 80EEA: ₹80,000 (remaining interest, not the full ₹1.5L cap)

## The ₹45 Lakh Stamp Duty Value — Not Circle Rate

The ₹45 lakh limit is based on **stamp duty value** (the value the property is registered at for stamp duty calculation) — NOT the actual purchase price or agreement value.

- Agreement value: ₹48 lakh
- Stamp duty value: ₹44 lakh
- → Eligible for 80EEA (since stamp duty value ≤ ₹45L)

Stamp duty value is typically the higher of:
- Actual consideration paid
- Circle rate × area

In many cities (especially Tier-2 towns), stamp duty value of affordable apartments stays within ₹45L even if market value is higher. In metros, most properties now exceed ₹45L stamp duty value — 80EEA primarily benefits Tier-2/3 city buyers.

## First-Time Buyer Condition — What It Means

"You must not own any residential property" on the **loan sanction date**.

- You own commercial property but no house → ✅ Still eligible (only residential property disqualifies)
- You co-own a house with parents → ❌ Not eligible
- You owned a house, sold it before sanction date → ✅ Check with CA — technically the condition is met on sanction date
- Spouse owns a house, you don't → If loan is in your name alone and you don't own property: likely eligible (but verify with lender/CA since joint income may be considered)

## 80EEA Is for Self-Occupied Property

Section 80EEA applies only to:
- **Self-occupied property** (where you or your family reside, or you have no other residential house)

If the affordable housing loan is for a **let-out property**: You already get unlimited home loan interest deduction under Section 24b for let-out property — 80EEA is not applicable in that case.

## Which ITR Form and Schedule

- File in **Schedule VIA** of your ITR (Chapter VI-A deductions)
- Under **"Section 80EEA"**
- Enter the eligible interest amount (max ₹1.5L)

Available in **ITR-1**, **ITR-2**, **ITR-3**, and **ITR-4**.

## Old Regime Only — Not Available Under New Tax Regime

Section 80EEA is a Chapter VI-A deduction. Under the **new tax regime** (Section 115BAC), all Chapter VI-A deductions are disallowed.

If you want to claim 80EEA:
- File under the **old tax regime**
- Compare: old regime (with 80EEA + 80C + 24b deductions) vs new regime (lower slab rates, no deductions)

For people with large home loans on affordable housing, the old regime is often better due to the ₹3.5L total interest deduction.

## Practical Example

**Profile**:
- First-time home buyer in Pune
- Flat bought: ₹42 lakh (stamp duty value: ₹40 lakh) — eligible
- Home loan taken: July 2021 (within April 2019 – March 2022 window)
- Loan amount: ₹35 lakh at 8.5% for 20 years
- EMI: ~₹30,500/month; Year 3 interest component: approximately ₹2,70,000

**Deductions**:
- Section 24b: ₹2,00,000 (capped)
- Section 80EEA: ₹70,000 (remaining interest: ₹2,70,000 − ₹2,00,000 = ₹70,000)
- Total interest deduction: ₹2,70,000

**Tax saving** (at 20% slab): ₹54,000 saved per year.

As the loan matures (interest component decreases below ₹2L), 80EEA won't be needed since Section 24b alone covers the full interest.

## Section 80EEA vs Section 80EE — What's the Difference?

| | Section 80EE (old) | Section 80EEA (new) |
|-|--------------------|--------------------|
| For loans sanctioned | April 1, 2016 – March 31, 2017 | April 1, 2019 – March 31, 2022 |
| Property value limit | ₹50 lakh (stamp duty) | ₹45 lakh (stamp duty) |
| Loan value limit | ≤ ₹35 lakh | No limit on loan amount |
| Additional deduction | ₹50,000 | ₹1,50,000 |
| Can claim both? | No — choose one | No — choose one |

80EEA replaced 80EE and offers a much higher deduction (₹1.5L vs ₹50K).

## Can You Claim 80EEA If You Took the Loan Before March 2022?

Yes — and this is the key point many taxpayers miss. **80EEA continues for the entire loan tenure** as long as:
- Loan was sanctioned before March 31, 2022 ✅
- You are still paying EMIs ✅
- The other eligibility conditions were met at sanction time ✅

The March 2022 deadline was for new loan sanctions — not for existing borrowers. You can claim 80EEA in FY 2026-27 on a loan you took in FY 2021-22.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I took the loan in FY 2021-22 but didn't claim 80EEA in earlier ITRs. Can I claim it now?

For the current year's ITR, yes. For past years, you can file revised ITR for FY 2024-25 (before December 31, 2025) if you missed it. For even earlier years, file a condonation request with your Jurisdictional PCIT — but this is a long process with no guaranteed outcome. Don't miss claiming it going forward.

### My loan is a balance transfer to a new lender. Does 80EEA still apply?

The original loan sanction date was within the window, and your property's stamp duty value was ≤ ₹45L. A balance transfer is restructuring of the same loan — most CAs take the view that 80EEA continues. Check with your new lender whether they issue a certificate and consult a CA.

### Joint home loan — can both co-borrowers claim 80EEA?

Each co-borrower can independently claim 80EEA to the extent of their interest share (max ₹1.5L each). Both must meet the "first-time buyer" condition. If one co-borrower already owns a property, they can't claim 80EEA individually even if the other can.

### My property's stamp duty value was ₹44 lakh but the builder later revised the sale price to ₹52 lakh. Is 80EEA affected?

80EEA eligibility is determined on the **loan sanction date**. If stamp duty value was ₤45L at sanction, you're eligible even if the price changes later (provided the registration also reflects ≤₹45L stamp duty value, not just the agreement value).
